## I. INTRODUCTION

Defendants concede that venue is proper in Cook County (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, p.1, ¶1). They ask the Court to take Plaintiff's chosen forum away from her anyway. To succeed, they must carry a heavy burden. "The burden is on the defendant to show that relevant private and public interest factors 'strongly favor' the defendant's choice of forum to warrant disturbing plaintiff's choice." (Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.7, ¶2; see Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, p.4, §III, ¶3, which quotes the same standard.) The doctrine applies "only in exceptional circumstances." (First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, p.5, ¶3; Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.6, ¶2.) Defendants have not met that burden.

The Appellate Court has already decided a case very close to this one. In *Bradbury*:
- the alleged medical negligence occurred entirely in Kankakee County;
- every individual and corporate defendant resided there;
- the records of the negligent treatment were there; and
- the hospital nurses were there.

The patient was transferred to a Cook County hospital, treated there, and died there. The defendants moved to transfer the case to Kankakee County. The trial court denied the motion, and the Appellate Court **affirmed**. It held that Cook County "has an interest because the decedent died here," and that the defendants had not carried their burden of showing that the factors "strongly favor" transfer. (Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, p.3, ¶4; p.4, ¶¶2, 5; p.5, ¶¶2–3.)

This case has the same structure. Baby Davis was born at Safe Hospital on August 24, 2024. On August 28, 2024, he was transferred to Cook County Hospital. He lived there for the remaining 19 days of his 23-day life and died there on September 16, 2024. (Davis Complaint.docx, p.1, ¶5; p.3, ¶¶27–29.) Five Cook County physicians treated him there. All five live and practice in Cook County, and they will testify about his injuries, his course, his suffering, and the cause of his death. (Exs. 2–6.)

Defendants' Motion does not engage with *Bradbury*'s holding. It also contains several errors:
- it says *Bradbury* "reversed course," when *Bradbury* affirmed denial of transfer;
- it reverses the holding of *Guerine*;
- it relies on a dissent for its court-congestion argument;
- it calls 19 of Baby Davis's 23 days "unrelated"; and
- its affidavits identify no nonparty witness and do not say Cook County is inconvenient to any Defendant.

Under *Guerine*, a trial court "abuses its discretion in granting an intrastate forum non conveniens motion to transfer venue where ... the potential trial witnesses are scattered among several counties, including the plaintiff's chosen forum, and no single county enjoys a predominant connection to the litigation." (First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, p.8, ¶4.) That describes this case. The Motion should be denied.

## III. LEGAL STANDARD

Forum non conveniens "allows a trial court to decline jurisdiction in the exceptional case where trial in another forum with proper jurisdiction and venue 'would better serve the ends of justice.'" (First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, p.2, ¶5.) The court weighs private and public interest factors. (First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, p.3, ¶4.) It does not weigh the private factors against the public ones. Instead, it evaluates "the total circumstances of the case in determining whether the defendant has proven that the balance of factors strongly favors transfer." (First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, p.4, ¶3; Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.7, ¶2.)

Several principles in that standard apply directly here, and the Motion leaves most of them out.

1. **The burden is entirely on Defendants.** They must show that the factors "strongly favor" transfer. (Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.7, ¶2.) "[T]he battle over forum begins with the plaintiff's choice already in the lead." (First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, p.6, ¶2.)

2. **Defendants must prove inconvenience to themselves.** "[T]he defendant must show that the plaintiff's chosen forum is inconvenient to the defendant and that another forum is more convenient to all parties." The defendant "cannot assert that the plaintiff's chosen forum is inconvenient to the plaintiff." (First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, p.4, ¶3; Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.7, ¶2.)

3. **Reduced deference is still deference.** When the plaintiff does not live in the chosen forum, her choice receives less deference. But "the deference to be accorded is only less, as opposed to none." (First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, p.4, ¶1; Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.9, ¶3.)

4. **Intrastate transfer requires little or no connection to the chosen forum.** Intrastate transfer is reserved for litigation with "no practical connection," or no nexus, to the plaintiff's chosen forum. (First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, p.6, ¶2.)

5. **Scattered witnesses defeat transfer.** Where potential witnesses are "scattered among several counties, including the plaintiff's chosen forum, and no single county enjoys a predominant connection to the litigation," granting transfer is an abuse of discretion. (First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, p.8, ¶4; Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.11, ¶3.)

6. **Defendants must support the Motion with evidence.** "Neither this court nor the trial court should be forced to reach a decision by speculating as to the identity, number, and location of witnesses that a party might call to testify." (Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, p.4, ¶2.) A defendant that relies on "unsupported and unverified assertions" fails to meet its burden. (Id., p.5, ¶¶1–2.)

## IV. ARGUMENT

### A. Plaintiff's choice of forum is entitled to deference, and Cook County is the site of the injury and death at issue.

Defendants argue that Plaintiff's choice deserves little deference because, they say, neither her residence nor the "site of the accident or injury" is in Cook County. (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, p.4, §III, ¶4.) The second half of that premise is wrong.

**1. Cook County is where the wrongful-death claim accrued.** In a wrongful-death action, the transaction giving rise to the claim is not complete until death. Death is the last event necessary to make Defendants liable, so the place of the wrong is "the place of the decedent's death." (Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, p.2, ¶¶3–4.) That is why Defendants must concede venue in Cook County. Baby Davis died in Cook County. (Davis Complaint.docx, p.3, ¶29.) For the three wrongful-death counts, the "site of the ... injury" is therefore in this forum.

**2. Most of the survival-count injury occurred in Cook County.** The survival counts seek damages for what Baby Davis suffered "[b]etween the time of his injury and death." (Davis Complaint.docx, p.4, ¶34; p.6, ¶43; p.8, ¶52.) Nineteen of those 23 days were spent in a Cook County hospital. (Id., p.3, ¶¶27–29.)

**3. Plaintiff's Kane County residence does not reduce deference to zero.** Plaintiff discloses her residence by sworn affidavit. (Ex. 1, ¶2.) Defendants offered only an unverified statement that they are "informed" she does not live in Cook County. (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, p.2, ¶9.) Even when the plaintiff is a nonresident, the deference owed is "only less, as opposed to none." (First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, p.4, ¶1.)

*Guerine* is directly on point. The plaintiffs there were Kane County residents who sued in Cook County. The Supreme Court reversed an order transferring the case to Kane County. It relied in part on the plaintiffs' affidavits stating they would suffer no inconvenience in traveling to Cook County. (First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, p.7, ¶4; p.8, ¶4.) Ms. Davis has given the same assurance here. (Ex. 1, ¶¶10–12.)

**4. Defendants' reliance on the decedent's-residence rule does not help them.** Defendants cite *Bradbury* for the rule that the decedent's residence at death controls the deference analysis. (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, p.4, §III, ¶4.) But in *Bradbury*, the absence of residence evidence counted against the movants, because they bore the burden. (Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, p.4, ¶6 – p.5, ¶2.) Baby Davis never lived anywhere other than a hospital. He spent 19 of his 23 days in a Cook County hospital, and nothing in Defendants' Motion establishes any other residence for him.

At a minimum, Plaintiff's choice keeps the lead. The question is whether Defendants have proven that the factors strongly favor DuPage County. They have not.

### B. The private interest factors do not strongly favor transfer.

#### 1. Convenience of the parties

**a. No Defendant has shown that Cook County is inconvenient.** The test requires Defendants to show that Cook County "is inconvenient to the defendant." (First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, p.4, ¶3.)
- Dr. Smith says only that DuPage would be "substantially more convenient." (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, p.9, ¶7.) That is a comparison. It identifies no travel burden, no lost practice time, and no hardship of any kind from a trial in Cook County.
- Safe Hospital's affiant says nothing about any inconvenience to Safe Hospital. (Id., pp.10–11.)
- DuPage Health submitted no affidavit at all. Its location is described only through Dr. Smith's hedged reference to its "principal place of business and/or the office(s) at which I practice," followed by a blank address placeholder. (Id., p.8, ¶3.)

*Langenhorst* affirmed denial of transfer where "no affidavits have been filed stating that St. Clair County would be an inconvenient forum." (Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.10, ¶¶3–4.) The defendants there had argued that the alternative forum "would not be inconvenient." The response was that this is "not the test." (Id., p.3, ¶¶2, 6–7.) The same is true here.

**b. Defendants cannot argue Plaintiff's convenience for her.** The Motion says trial in DuPage "would be more convenient for every party," and that Defendants "are aware of no party for whom Cook County would be more convenient." (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, p.4, §IV.A.) A defendant "cannot assert that the plaintiff's chosen forum is inconvenient to the plaintiff." (First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, p.4, ¶3.) *Langenhorst* rejected the same argument when defendants claimed the transferee county "would be more convenient to the plaintiff." (Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.9, ¶4.) In any event, Plaintiff has sworn that Cook County is convenient for her. (Ex. 1, ¶¶10–12.)

**c. The proposed forum is nearby.** "We live in a smaller world," and convenience "has a different meaning" today. (First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, p.8, ¶3; Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.10, ¶5.) When the competing forums are close together, "the battle over the forum results in a battle over the minutiae." (Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.10, ¶5.) Defendants' own affiants place their staff "in DuPage County or in counties in the immediate vicinity of DuPage County." (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, p.9, ¶6; p.10, ¶4.) That description does not exclude Cook County. Defendants cannot show that coming to a Cook County courthouse is a meaningful burden.

**d. Defendants' attempt to distinguish *Guerine* does not work.** Defendants note that one *Guerine* defendant lived in Cook County. (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, p.4, §IV.A.) But the core of *Guerine* is that Kane County plaintiffs kept their Cook County forum because the witnesses were spread across several counties and no county predominated. (First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, p.7, ¶4; p.8, ¶4.) *Bradbury* is also a case in which every defendant resided in the proposed transferee county. (Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, p.3, ¶4.) Transfer was still denied. (Id., p.5, ¶¶2–3.) Defendants' residence is "the obvious convenience to the defendants if they were to defend this action in their home forum." (Id., p.4, ¶2.) It is not enough.

#### 2. Relative ease of access to sources of proof

Defendants argue that the standard-of-care records are at Safe Hospital and that the Cook County records concern "subsequent, unrelated inpatient treatment." (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, p.2, ¶8; p.4, §IV.B.) The 2-622 report attached to the Complaint says the opposite.

**a. The Cook County records are causation and damages evidence.** The reviewing physician expressly relied on the Cook County Hospital records and on the death certificate and any autopsy, pathology, and diagnostic reports. (Davis Complaint.docx, Reviewing Physician's Report, p.11, items 7–8.) The reviewer then concluded that the delivery injuries "caused or contributed to BABY DAVIS's subsequent medical condition, need for extensive treatment, transfer to Cook County Hospital, deterioration, and death." (Id., p.13, ¶3.) In describing the reviewer's list of materials, Defendants cited items 1 through 6 and described item 7 as "unrelated." They omitted item 8 entirely. (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, p.2, ¶8.)

**b. This case requires more than standard-of-care proof.** Plaintiff must also prove causation, damages, and death. That proof is centered in Cook County:
- the survival counts seek damages for the period between injury and death, and 19 of those 23 days were in Cook County (Davis Complaint.docx, p.4, ¶34; p.6, ¶43; p.8, ¶52);
- the wrongful-death counts depend on the death, which occurred in Cook County (Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, p.2, ¶¶3–4); and
- the family-expense count seeks hospital and medical expenses, most of which were incurred in Cook County (Davis Complaint.docx, pp.8–9, ¶¶56–58; Ex. 1, ¶9).

**c. *Bradbury* already addressed records split between two counties.** The court there found that "medical records relative to the decedent are located in both Cook and Kankakee Counties" and affirmed denial of transfer. (Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, p.4, ¶2.) The same is true here.

**d. Defendants identify no obstacle to producing their records in Cook County.** Fetal-monitoring tracings, charts, and orders can be copied and produced anywhere. Defendants do not identify "any impediments to accessing sources of testimonial, documentary, and real evidence." (Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.10, ¶4.) Defendants also do not suggest a jury view of anything in DuPage County. That distinguishes the case from *Dawdy*, where a possible jury view of the accident scene was a practical consideration. (Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.11, ¶4.)

#### 3. Availability of compulsory process and cost of obtaining witnesses

**a. Defendants identify no witness.** Defendants refer generally to "physicians, nurses, and other personnel" who, "on information and belief," live in DuPage "or the surrounding counties." (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, p.4, §IV.C.) Neither affidavit gives a single name. Neither states where any identified person lives or what that person would say. *Bradbury* rejected exactly this kind of showing: "they have failed to identify any nonparty witnesses ... Neither this court nor the trial court should be forced to reach a decision by speculating as to the identity, number, and location of witnesses." (Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, p.4, ¶2.) So did *Langenhorst*, where defendants listed categories of personnel "but have not identified who these people are, where they live, or what, if any, relevant testimony they might provide." (Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.9, ¶2.)

**b. The witnesses Defendants describe are their own employees and agents.** Safe Hospital's nurses and staff, and Dr. Smith's colleagues, are within Defendants' control. Defendants do not claim that any of them is unwilling to appear. They also concede that compulsory process under Rule 237 is available in either county. (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, p.4, §IV.C; Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, p.4, ¶2.)

**c. Plaintiff has identified her witnesses by name.** Plaintiff submits sworn affidavits from five named Cook County treating physicians: Drs. Vance, Brooks, Montgomery, Linwood, and Hayes. (Exs. 2–6.) Each states:
- that he or she lives and practices in Cook County;
- that he or she treated Baby Davis at Cook County Hospital;
- what his or her testimony will cover;
- that he or she is willing to testify in Cook County; and
- that a trial in DuPage County would be more burdensome.

(Exs. 2–6, ¶¶2–8.) This is a stronger showing than the one that carried the day in *Bradbury*. There, the plaintiff's only support was an affidavit from a nurse at her law firm, who listed 13 Cook County hospital doctors and nurses as likely trial witnesses. (Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, p.3, ¶5.) Here, the treating physicians have sworn to these facts themselves. In *Langenhorst*, the majority likewise relied on the location of the plaintiff's treating physicians outside the transferee county. (Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.9, ¶6.)

**d. *Bland* does not apply.** *Bland* cautions against a plaintiff "selecting a treating physician in an inconvenient forum" to frustrate forum non conveniens. (Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, p.4, ¶3.) That concern does not fit these facts:
- These physicians were not selected for litigation. They are the doctors who cared for a critically injured newborn during the last 19 days of his life.
- He was transferred because he needed a higher level of specialized care, nearly two years before suit was filed. (Ex. 1, ¶¶6–8.)
- The 2-622 reviewer concluded that the transfer itself was caused by the injuries Defendants inflicted. (Davis Complaint.docx, Reviewing Physician's Report, p.13, ¶3.)

As in *Bradbury*, "[n]othing in this record suggests that the decedent's treating physicians in Cook County were chosen to establish a nexus to venue." (Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, p.4, ¶3.) These doctors are occurrence witnesses to the injury and the death. Plaintiff does not offer them as retained standard-of-care experts. Their location is a fact of the case, not something counsel created.

#### 4. No single county has a predominant connection to this case.

Defendants' central argument is that DuPage has a "predominant connection." (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, pp.4–5, §IV.D.) That argument has three problems.

**a. The Motion reverses *Guerine*'s holding.** The Motion says *Guerine* "held that transfer is warranted only where 'the potential trial witnesses are scattered among several counties.'" (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, p.5, §IV.D.) *Guerine* held the opposite. A trial court "abuses its discretion in granting an intrastate forum non conveniens motion to transfer venue where ... the potential trial witnesses are scattered among several counties, including the plaintiff's chosen forum, and no single county enjoys a predominant connection to the litigation." (First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, p.8, ¶4.) Scattered witnesses are a reason to deny transfer, not a requirement for granting it.

**b. The witnesses here are scattered among several counties, including Cook.**
- Plaintiff, the decedent's mother and next of kin, lives in Kane County. (Ex. 1, ¶2.)
- Defendants and their staff are in DuPage "or in counties in the immediate vicinity." (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, p.9, ¶6; p.10, ¶4.)
- Five named treating physicians are in Cook County. (Exs. 2–6.)
- The records are in both DuPage and Cook Counties. (Davis Complaint.docx, Reviewing Physician's Report, p.11, items 1–8.)

That is the pattern in which *Guerine* and *Langenhorst* required the plaintiff's choice to be respected. (First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, p.8, ¶4; Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.11, ¶3.)

**c. Defendants count only the liability witnesses.** Defendants say "virtually every standard-of-care witness" is in DuPage. (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, p.5, §IV.D.) That phrasing concedes that the causation, damages, and death witnesses are somewhere else. Liability under the Wrongful Death Act is not complete until death. (Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, p.2, ¶¶3–4.) A connection based only on the alleged breach is not a "predominant connection" to the whole litigation.

**d. *Dawdy* does not control.** The *Langenhorst* majority distinguished *Dawdy* because "in Dawdy, none of the witnesses resided in the plaintiff's chosen forum." (Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.11, ¶4.) Here, five key witnesses live in the chosen forum.

**e. *Bradbury* is the closest case.** In *Bradbury*:
- Kankakee was "the situs of all of the negligent acts";
- it was "the residence of all of the individual and corporate defendants";
- the relevant treatment records were there; and
- the hospital nurses were there. (Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, p.3, ¶4.)

That is the same showing Defendants make here. Because the decedent was treated and died in Cook County, the Appellate Court held that the defendants had not shown the factors strongly favored transfer. (Id., p.4, ¶¶2, 5; p.5, ¶¶2–3.) The same result should follow.

### C. The public interest factors do not strongly favor transfer.

#### 1. Cook County has a real interest in this controversy.

Plaintiff does not dispute that DuPage County has an interest in medical care delivered within its borders. But the issue is not which county has the greater interest. Transfer is appropriate only if Cook County has "no practical connection" to the case. (First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, p.6, ¶2.)

Defendants try to distinguish *Bradbury* on the ground that the Cook County death there resulted from "Cook County conduct by these Defendants." (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, p.5, §V.A.) *Bradbury* contains no such fact. All of the alleged negligent acts there occurred in Kankakee County. (Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, p.3, ¶4.) The court nonetheless held:

> "[B]oth Cook and Kankakee Counties have an interest in this litigation. Kankakee County has an interest because the action involves alleged acts of medical negligence occurring within its borders, and Cook County has an interest because the decedent died here." (Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, p.4, ¶5.)

That reasoning applies directly here. Cook County's connection also goes further:
- Baby Davis spent most of his life in Cook County and died there. (Davis Complaint.docx, p.3, ¶¶27–29.)
- This Court appointed the Special Administrator of his estate. (Id., p.1, ¶2.)
- The testimony and records of a Cook County hospital and five Cook County physicians will be central at trial. (Exs. 2–6.)

When two counties have a legitimate interest in a case, that shared interest cannot "strongly favor" either one.

#### 2. Asking Cook County residents to serve as jurors is not unfair.

Defendants argue that Cook County jurors should not be burdened with a case that involves no "Cook County resident, any Cook County business, or any conduct occurring in Cook County." (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, p.5, §V.B.) That premise is incorrect:
- the death occurred in Cook County (Davis Complaint.docx, p.3, ¶29);
- a Cook County hospital provided 19 days of care (id., p.3, ¶27);
- five Cook County residents are key witnesses (Exs. 2–6); and
- this Court appointed the estate's representative (Davis Complaint.docx, p.1, ¶2).

*Bradbury* rejected the same argument: "Because neither county can be said to be totally unrelated to this action, we are unprepared to say that it would be unfair to burden the citizens of either county with the obligation of jury service in this matter." (Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, p.4, ¶5.) Similarly, in *Langenhorst* the Supreme Court found that the defendants' claim that the forum had no connection to the case was "factually inaccurate and misleading." (Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.10, ¶2.)

#### 3. Court congestion does not support transfer.

**a. Congestion carries little weight.** Court congestion "is relatively insignificant." (First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, p.3, ¶4.) It is "not dispositive." (Id., p.8, ¶2.) It matters even less "where the record does not show the other forum would resolve the case more quickly." (Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.11, ¶1.) Courts should be "extremely reluctant to dismiss a case from the forum rei gestae merely because that forum's docket has a backlog." (First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, p.3, ¶4.)

**b. Defendants concede they cannot show that DuPage would be faster.** The only time-to-verdict figure in the record is DuPage's: an average of **51.4 months** from filing to jury verdict in Law > $50,000 cases. (2023 Annual Report Statistical Summary.pdf, p.160.) That is essentially the same as the downstate average of 51.9 months. (Id.) Defendants state that they "do not rely on" this figure because no comparable Cook County figure is available. (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, p.6, §V.C.) That concession is fatal. On this record, nothing shows DuPage "would resolve the case more quickly." (Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.11, ¶1.)

**c. Defendants rely on the wrong statistic, and it is affected by the reporting caveat.** Defendants compare clearance rates for all case types: 60.5% for Cook and 99.0% for the 18th Circuit. (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, p.5, §V.C; 2023 Annual Report Statistical Summary.pdf, p.21.) That figure has two problems.
- It combines criminal, traffic, family, juvenile, and other matters that have nothing to do with a Law Division medical-negligence case.
- Defendants say it "does not depend on" Cook County's case-management reporting change. (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, p.6, §V.C.) But the clearance rate is calculated from cases closed and cases filed or reinstated. (2023 Annual Report Statistical Summary.pdf, p.21, note.) The Summary's caveat, which Defendants quoted only in part, goes on to warn that "[t]here will also be noticeable differences in the number of cases filed and closed in 2023." (Id., p.19, note; p.49, note.) So the figure Defendants rely on is directly affected by the reporting change.

**d. The civil-case statistics favor Cook County.** For civil cases, the category relevant here, Cook County's 2023 clearance rate was **97.9%**. The 18th Circuit's was **96.3%**. (2023 Annual Report Statistical Summary.pdf, p.22.) Cook County judges also handled **1,322** new filings per judge, compared with **2,522** per judge in the 18th Circuit. (Id., p.20.) Raw pending-case totals, such as 145,608 versus 10,877, do not account for the difference in court size: 372 judges in Cook County and 47 in the 18th Circuit. (Id., p.20; pp.46, 49.)

**e. Defendants rely on a dissent.** For their reliance on Statistical Summary data, Defendants cite "Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.18." (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, p.5, §V.C.) That page is part of the dissent, which runs from page 12 through page 20. The *Langenhorst* majority deferred to the trial court's assessment of its own docket and held that "[t]ransfer ... is also not required by the court docket." (Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.10, ¶7 – p.11, ¶1.) The "relatively insignificant" language Defendants attribute to page 12 of *Langenhorst* comes from the majority opinion at page 11 and from *Guerine*. In both places, that language weighs against transfer.

**f. This Court is best placed to assess its own docket.** "[T]he trial court is in a much better position than we are to assess the burden on its own docket that the trial of this case might pose." (Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, p.4, ¶5; Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.11, ¶1.) The Law Division can try this case.

### D. The Motion's verification and affidavits do not cure its evidentiary defects.

Defendants describe their Motion as an improvement on the one rejected in *Bradbury*, which they say "reversed course only after observing" a deficient record. (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, p.6, §VI.) *Bradbury* did not reverse anything. It "affirm[ed] the order of the circuit court denying the defendants' motion to transfer." (Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, p.5, ¶3.)

The Motion's own record also has significant gaps.

**1. No verification.** The Motion is titled "Verified," but no verification by any party appears. The document ends with counsel's signature. (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, p.7.)

**2. Dr. Smith's affidavit (Ex. A).**
- His home, practice, and hospital addresses are unfilled placeholders: "[street address], [city] ... [ZIP]." (Id., p.8, ¶¶2–4.)
- His statements about records and staff residences are made on "knowledge, information, and belief." (Id., p.8, ¶5; p.9, ¶6.)
- He calls the Cook County care "treatment unrelated to the standard-of-care conduct." (Id., p.9, ¶8.) That is a conclusion, and the 2-622 report contradicts it. (Davis Complaint.docx, Reviewing Physician's Report, p.13, ¶¶2–3.)

**3. Safe Hospital's affidavit (Ex. B).**
- The affiant is identified only as "Hospital Administrator." (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, p.11.)
- The Motion calls Exhibit B the "Affidavit of [Records Custodian]." (Id., pp.3, 4, 7.)
- The Motion cites "¶6" of that affidavit for witness residences. The residence statement actually appears at ¶4, and it is qualified "to the best of my knowledge." (Id., p.4; p.10, ¶4; p.11, ¶6.)

**4. DuPage Health.** DuPage Health submitted no affidavit.

**5. What the affidavits do not say.** Neither affidavit:
- names any nonparty witness;
- states that Cook County is an inconvenient forum for any Defendant; or
- addresses any causation, damages, or death witness.

On every point that matters to the outcome, the Motion rests on the kind of "unsupported and unverified assertions" that *Bradbury* held insufficient. (Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, p.5, ¶¶1–2.)

### E. Considering all the circumstances, Defendants have not shown that the factors strongly favor DuPage County.

Considered together, the factors fall short of the required showing:
- Plaintiff's choice of forum keeps the lead.
- Cook County is where the wrongful death occurred and where most of the survival-period injury was suffered.
- The witnesses are spread across Kane, DuPage, and Cook Counties, including five named physicians who live and practice in Cook County.
- The records are in two counties.
- Both counties have a legitimate public interest.
- The congestion data either favor Cook County or do not distinguish between the two forums.

Defendants have shown only that DuPage would be more convenient for them. That is "the obvious convenience to the defendants if they were to defend this action in their home forum." (Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, p.4, ¶2.) This is not the "exceptional" case that justifies taking away a plaintiff's chosen forum. (First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, p.5, ¶3.) As in *Guerine*, "[t]he balance of factors must strongly favor transfer of the case before the plaintiff can be deprived of his chosen forum. ... This is not such a case." (First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, p.8, ¶4.)
