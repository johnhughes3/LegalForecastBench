## Plaintiff’s Response in Opposition to Defendants’ Verified Motion to Transfer Venue Pursuant to the Doctrine of Forum Non Conveniens

**Pinpoint convention.** Complaint citations use pleaded paragraph numbers. Page numbers for the reviewing physician’s report refer to its rendered pagination in the supplied DOCX; opinion paragraph pinpoints count prose paragraphs from the top of the cited PDF page, excluding headings. Statistical tables are cited by page and row or table heading because they have no numbered paragraphs.

Plaintiff Sara Davis, individually and as Special Administrator of the Estate of Baby Davis, respectfully requests that the Court deny Defendants’ motion to transfer this action to DuPage County. Defendants have shown that DuPage County is an appropriate and substantially connected forum: the alleged delivery-care departures occurred at Safe Hospital, the defendants are located there, and the principal labor-and-delivery records are maintained there. Plaintiff does not minimize those facts. But the question is whether the total circumstances strongly favor transfer—not whether DuPage has a genuine connection. Cook County is also connected to the action: Baby Davis was transferred there for further treatment, deteriorated there, and died there; the complaint pleads survival and wrongful-death damages arising from the same alleged injuries; and the reviewing physician expressly links the delivery injuries to the later treatment, deterioration, and death. Defendants’ generalized witness evidence and broad congestion statistics do not overcome those connections or establish that DuPage is more convenient for all parties and material witnesses.

## I. Governing standard

The defendant bears the burden to establish that the relevant private and public interest factors, viewed in their totality, strongly favor the proposed forum. The plaintiff’s choice begins with some deference, and transfer is appropriate only when the overall balance strongly favors it. (*Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf*, PDF p. 7, ¶ 2; *First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf*, PDF p. 4, ¶ 2; PDF p. 8, ¶ 4.) The court considers party convenience; access to testimonial, documentary, and real evidence; practical trial problems; local interests; jury burden; and congestion. No single factor is conclusive, and the private and public factors are evaluated together as the total circumstances. (*First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf*, PDF p. 3, ¶ 4; PDF p. 4, ¶ 2.) Defendants must show both that Cook County is inconvenient to them and that DuPage County is more convenient to all parties; they may not rely on a claim that Cook is inconvenient to Plaintiff. (*First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf*, PDF p. 4, ¶ 2.)

The Court should give appropriate, not inflated, weight to Plaintiff’s choice. The complaint does not state Sara Davis’s or Baby Davis’s residence, so Plaintiff does not claim this record establishes Cook County as a home forum. (*Davis Complaint.docx*, rendered pp. 1–2, ¶¶ 1–15.) In a wrongful-death action, *Bradbury* identifies the decedent’s residence at death as significant to forum-choice deference and cautions against supplying a missing residence through unsupported assertions. (*Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf*, PDF p. 3, ¶ 3; PDF p. 4, ¶ 6.) *Guerine* further explains that diminished deference is less deference, not none. (*First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf*, PDF p. 4, ¶ 1.) The absence of residence evidence does not establish that Plaintiff or the decedent resided in DuPage, and it does not erase Cook County’s connection through the treatment and death alleged here. The Court should assess the actual record rather than either side’s assumptions.

## II. Cook County is part of the alleged injury and damages course, not an unrelated forum

The complaint locates the alleged breaches in DuPage County. Sara Davis presented to Safe Hospital for labor and delivery; the alleged failures concern fetal monitoring, recognition and response to distress, communication, escalation, and timely delivery. (*Davis Complaint.docx*, rendered p. 2, ¶¶ 16–24.) Plaintiff acknowledges that those allegations, the defendants, and the core standard-of-care evidence give DuPage a substantial connection to the liability issues.

But Defendants describe Cook County as connected only to later, “unrelated” treatment. That description is contradicted by the pleading and the reviewing physician’s report. The complaint alleges Baby Davis suffered severe injuries from the delayed delivery, required extensive care, was transferred to Cook County Hospital on August 28, 2024, deteriorated despite treatment, and died there on September 16, 2024. It alleges the injuries and death were proximately caused by Defendants’ conduct and seeks survival, wrongful-death, and family-expense damages. (*Davis Complaint.docx*, rendered p. 3, ¶¶ 25–30; rendered pp. 3–7, ¶¶ 31–58.) The reviewing physician considered Cook County Hospital treatment records, transfer records, the death certificate, and diagnostic materials. The physician further opines that the delivery injuries caused or contributed to Baby Davis’s later medical condition, extensive treatment, transfer, deterioration, and death. (*Davis Complaint.docx*, rendered p. 11, Reviewing Physician’s Written Report, list items 6–8; rendered p. 12, ¶¶ 2–4.)

Plaintiff does not contend that the complaint alleges negligent treatment by Cook County providers. It does not. The distinction is that treatment and death in Cook are material parts of the pleaded causal sequence and damages proof, even though the alleged delivery-care departures occurred in DuPage. Defendants’ characterization of those events as irrelevant to the action is too broad.

*Bradbury* is particularly instructive on this point. There, the alleged obstetrical negligence occurred in Kankakee County, the infant was transferred to a Cook County hospital, and the infant died in Cook County. The court recognized a local interest in Kankakee because the alleged negligence occurred there and a separate local interest in Cook because the decedent died there; it concluded neither county was totally unrelated for purposes of jury service. (*Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf*, PDF p. 4, ¶ 5.) The facts here likewise support legitimate interests in both counties. DuPage has the stronger connection to the alleged breach; Cook has a real connection to the resulting treatment and death. That is enough to defeat the motion’s premise that Cook is unrelated, although the Court must still weigh the entire record.

## III. The private-interest evidence does not establish that transfer would make trial more convenient for all parties and material witnesses

### A. Party convenience

The affidavits support Defendants’ position that Dr. Smith and Safe Hospital are based in DuPage, and Dr. Smith states that trial there would be substantially more convenient for him. (*Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf*, PDF pp. 8–9, Smith Aff. ¶¶ 2–4, 7; PDF p. 10, Hospital Administrator Aff. ¶ 2.) Plaintiff recognizes this as a genuine factor favoring DuPage. But the motion’s assertion that DuPage would be more convenient for “every party” is not supported by evidence about Sara Davis’s residence, travel, or trial convenience. The complaint identifies her as Baby Davis’s mother and the estate’s Special Administrator but gives no county of residence. (*Davis Complaint.docx*, rendered p. 1, ¶¶ 1–4.) Dr. Smith’s statement supports his own convenience; it does not establish that DuPage is more convenient to Plaintiff or to all parties. Under *Guerine*, Defendants cannot satisfy their burden by asserting Plaintiff would find DuPage more convenient. (*First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf*, PDF p. 4, ¶ 2.)

### B. Records and other proof

The labor-and-delivery records, fetal-monitoring tracings, orders, nursing documentation, and Safe Hospital neonatal records are maintained in DuPage. (*Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf*, PDF p. 10, Hospital Administrator Aff. ¶ 3; *Davis Complaint.docx*, rendered p. 11, Reviewing Physician’s Written Report, list items 1–5.) Their location favors DuPage. The same report, however, identifies Baby Davis’s Cook County Hospital treatment records, transfer records, and death-related diagnostic materials as sources reviewed. (*Davis Complaint.docx*, rendered p. 11, Reviewing Physician’s Written Report, list items 6–8.) Those materials bear on the later course of injury and death that Plaintiff alleges was caused or contributed to by the delivery injuries. Defendants offer no evidence that accessing or presenting the Cook County records would create a practical obstacle to trial in Cook. On this record, the location of the core delivery records is a DuPage factor, but it does not make the Cook records immaterial or resolve the total balance.

### C. Witnesses, process, and trial logistics

Dr. Smith and the hospital administrator state that delivery and neonatal personnel worked at Safe Hospital’s DuPage facility and, to their knowledge, reside in DuPage County or nearby counties. (*Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf*, PDF p. 9, Smith Aff. ¶ 6; PDF p. 10, Hospital Administrator Aff. ¶ 4.) This supports a DuPage convenience interest for some defense witnesses. It does not support the motion’s stronger claim that the overwhelming majority of material fact witnesses are in DuPage. Neither affidavit names the nurses, technicians, or other staff; identifies how many are material witnesses; gives their individual counties of residence; explains what each would testify about; or describes a specific burden of appearing in Cook. A general reference to DuPage and nearby counties does not permit a reliable witness-by-witness comparison.

Plaintiff identifies the following five Cook County physicians with whom counsel has spoken: **Liam Vance, Savannah Brooks, Caleb Montgomery, Maya Linwood, and Garrett Hayes.** The complaint and report establish that Baby Davis received treatment at Cook County Hospital and that the medical course there is linked to the alleged delivery injuries and death. (*Davis Complaint.docx*, rendered p. 3, ¶¶ 25–30; rendered pp. 11–12, Reviewing Physician’s Written Report, list items 6–8 and ¶¶ 2–4.) The supplied record does not set out each physician’s role, expected testimony, precise residence, willingness to appear, or scheduling burden. Plaintiff offers the names for the Court’s witness comparison without asking the Court to assume unsupported details about any individual’s participation. Their identification, considered with the documented Cook treatment, warrants a comparison with the defense’s unnamed DuPage staff rather than a conclusion that the medical witnesses are overwhelmingly located in DuPage.

The motion concedes that compulsory process is available for in-state witnesses in either county. (*Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf*, PDF p. 4, § IV.C, ¶ 1.) *Bradbury* likewise treated process as available in either forum for Illinois resident witnesses. (*Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf*, PDF p. 4, ¶ 2.) Compulsory process is therefore neutral on this record. The relative cost and convenience of willing witnesses may favor DuPage for Dr. Smith and some hospital staff, but Defendants have not quantified that burden or shown that it outweighs Cook’s documented medical evidence and identified physician roster. No supplied evidence shows that a jury view of the hospital or delivery premises would assist the jury; that factor is neutral. The parties identify no other practical problem or obstacle to a fair trial unique to either county.

*Bradbury* cautions that a court should not decide witness convenience by speculating about witnesses’ identity, number, and location. (*Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf*, PDF p. 4, ¶ 2.) Defendants’ affidavits are materially more helpful than the unsupported motion in *Bradbury*, and Plaintiff does not contend otherwise. But their general staff-location statements still leave the material comparison incomplete. In *Guerine*, the court required a totality-based assessment and rejected transfer where witnesses and evidence were distributed and no county’s asserted predominance was established. (*First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf*, PDF p. 8, ¶ 4.) This case has a stronger DuPage center for the alleged breach than *Guerine* did; it also has a Cook treatment-and-death connection that Defendants’ motion minimizes. The comparison should turn on identified material witnesses and the evidence of actual convenience, not the label “overwhelming majority.”

### D. The supporting affidavits prove important DuPage facts, but not the motion’s broader conclusions

Dr. Smith’s affidavit supplies his DuPage residence and practice location, the location of records to his knowledge, the general location of hospital personnel, and his own convenience assertion. His view that Cook County treatment was “unrelated” is stated as his understanding and is inconsistent with the causal allegations and the reviewing physician’s report. (*Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf*, PDF pp. 8–9, Smith Aff. ¶¶ 2–8; *Davis Complaint.docx*, rendered p. 3, ¶¶ 25–30; rendered p. 12, Reviewing Physician’s Written Report ¶¶ 3–4.) The hospital administrator’s affidavit supports the location of the facility, its records, and its employees, and confirms the transfer to Cook County Hospital. The administrator states that Safe Hospital did not provide treatment at Cook County Hospital and that the administrator is unaware of Cook County records, equipment, or personnel relevant to the standard-of-care allegations. (*Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf*, PDF pp. 10–11, Hospital Administrator Aff. ¶¶ 2–6.) Those statements address where alleged negligence occurred and what records Safe Hospital maintains; they do not negate Cook’s role in the subsequent treatment, causal course, and death pleaded here.

The motion describes itself as verified and says its declarations identify witness locations “with particularity.” The supplied copy shows counsel’s signature and the separately attached affidavits; the Smith affidavit contains bracketed address placeholders, and the hospital personnel are not identified individually. The motion also calls Exhibit B an affidavit of a “[Records Custodian],” while the attached declaration is titled as an affidavit of the Hospital Administrator/President. (*Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf*, PDF pp. 1, 7–11, § VI; Smith Aff. ¶¶ 2–4, 6; Hospital Administrator Aff. ¶ 4.) Plaintiff does not ask the Court to disregard the affidavits or decide the motion on a technical label. The point is that the Court should credit the facts actually established by the declarations without treating them as a particularized comparison of all material witnesses.

## IV. The public-interest factors do not make Cook County an unrelated forum

DuPage has a substantial local interest in the alleged quality of obstetric care delivered at Safe Hospital by a physician and medical group located there. The allegations of breach center on that care. (*Davis Complaint.docx*, rendered pp. 2–3, ¶¶ 16–24; *Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf*, PDF p. 5, § V.A, ¶ 1.) Plaintiff acknowledges that DuPage’s interest in the alleged delivery negligence is strong.

Cook County nevertheless has a legitimate local interest in a case involving treatment and the death of an infant at Cook County Hospital. The pleaded claims include survival damages during Baby Davis’s life, wrongful-death damages arising from his death, and expenses associated with his treatment and death. (*Davis Complaint.docx*, rendered pp. 3–7, ¶¶ 25–30, 31–58.) As *Bradbury* explains in a closely comparable medical-negligence and infant-death setting, the county of the alleged negligence and the county of death each had an interest; neither was wholly unrelated. (*Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf*, PDF p. 4, ¶ 5.) The jury-burden factor therefore does not support Defendants’ assertion that Cook citizens would be burdened with a dispute having no relation to their county. It may favor DuPage comparatively, but it does not eliminate Cook’s interest.

### Court congestion

The 2023 report gives Defendants some support: the all-case clearance rate was 60.5% for Cook County and 99.0% for the 18th Circuit, and Cook reported a much larger absolute pending caseload. (*2023 Annual Report Statistical Summary.pdf*, pp. 19, 21, Cook County and 18th Circuit rows.) Plaintiff does not contend these statistics show Cook is less congested. Their weight is limited, however. The clearance-rate table expressly covers all case types, and the report’s pending-case totals likewise aggregate civil, family, criminal, traffic, juvenile, and other cases. (*2023 Annual Report Statistical Summary.pdf*, pp. 19, 21, table headings and notes.) The report also cautions that changes to Cook County’s case-management system created variances in reported open cases and in 2023 filings and closures. (*2023 Annual Report Statistical Summary.pdf*, pp. 19, 49, Cook County notes.) These broad comparisons do not establish how quickly this medical-malpractice action would be tried in either county.

The report’s per-judge filing comparison is also mixed: the 18th Circuit reported 2,522 cases filed per judge in 2023, compared with 1,322 in Cook County. Those figures also cover all case types and are not a direct measure of this case’s time to trial. (*2023 Annual Report Statistical Summary.pdf*, p. 20, “Number of Cases Filed per Judge” rows for 18th Circuit and Cook County.) For civil jury cases over $50,000, the report records 17 DuPage jury verdicts and an average filing-to-verdict time of 51.4 months. It provides no comparable Cook County time-lapse figure and expressly notes that Cook did not report time-lapse data. (*2023 Annual Report Statistical Summary.pdf*, pp. 159–160, 18th Circuit and Cook rows and note; p. 163, Cook County row and note.) Thus, the record does not show that DuPage would resolve this action more quickly.

*Guerine* and the majority in *Langenhorst* describe congestion as relatively insignificant, particularly absent proof that the proposed forum would resolve the case sooner. (*First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf*, PDF p. 3, ¶ 4; *Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf*, PDF p. 11, ¶ 1.) The motion also cites the discussion of annual statistics at page 18 of *Langenhorst*, but that discussion is in the dissent; the majority’s congestion analysis is at page 11. (*Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf*, PDF p. 18, dissent ¶ 1; PDF p. 11, ¶ 1.) Defendants properly put statistics before the Court, unlike the defendants in *Bradbury*, who had not asked the court to consider them. (*Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf*, PDF p. 4, ¶ 5.) But the current figures establish only a broad docket difference, not a comparative trial-time advantage for DuPage. The congestion factor should receive modest weight and cannot carry the motion.

## V. The total circumstances do not strongly favor transfer

The case’s core alleged negligence, defendants, and standard-of-care records are centered in DuPage. Those points deserve substantial weight, and this record is not the unsupported defense presentation in *Bradbury*. But the totality also includes the ensuing treatment and death in Cook; medical records and diagnostic materials from Cook; the physician’s causal link between the delivery injuries and the Cook treatment and death; five identified Cook County physicians; no individualized defense witness comparison; compulsory process available in either county; no demonstrated practical barrier to trial in Cook; and no proof that DuPage would resolve the case faster. (*Davis Complaint.docx*, rendered p. 3, ¶¶ 25–30; rendered pp. 11–12, Reviewing Physician’s Written Report, list items 6–8 and ¶¶ 2–4; *Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf*, PDF pp. 9–11, Smith Aff. ¶¶ 6–8 and Hospital Administrator Aff. ¶¶ 4–6.)

The comparison is not that Cook County predominates. It is that Defendants have not shown that DuPage’s advantages strongly outweigh Cook’s genuine connection and the evidentiary gaps in their motion. *Bradbury* confirms that a county where the decedent received treatment and died is not unrelated merely because the alleged negligence occurred elsewhere. *Guerine* and *Langenhorst* require a totality analysis and preserve the plaintiff’s forum choice unless the balance strongly favors transfer. (*Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf*, PDF p. 4, ¶ 5; *First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf*, PDF p. 4, ¶ 2; PDF p. 8, ¶ 4; *Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf*, PDF p. 7, ¶ 2.) On this record, the required showing has not been made.

For these reasons, Plaintiff respectfully requests that the Court deny Defendants’ Verified Motion to Transfer Venue to DuPage County and grant such other relief as the Court deems just.
