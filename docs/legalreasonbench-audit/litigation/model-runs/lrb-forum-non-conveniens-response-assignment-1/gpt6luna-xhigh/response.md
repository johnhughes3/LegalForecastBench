## Bottom line

Defendants have a substantial DuPage-centered argument: the pleaded departures occurred during labor and delivery at Safe Hospital; the defendants, Dr. Smith, the hospital, and the direct-care records are tied to DuPage; and the affidavits support that the delivery personnel worked there. This is a materially stronger evidentiary presentation than the unsupported defense motion discussed in *Bradbury*. Do not suggest that the location of the alleged malpractice or the parties’ convenience points toward Cook County.

The best response is that Defendants overstate the proposition that Cook County is irrelevant. Baby Davis received extensive treatment there, deteriorated, and died there; the complaint and reviewing physician’s report connect the delivery injuries to the treatment, deterioration, and death. *Bradbury* recognizes that Cook has a local interest when the decedent dies there, even though the alleged medical negligence occurred in the other county. The motion also lacks a witness-by-witness comparison, and its congestion argument does not establish that this case would be resolved sooner in DuPage. The result is a serious transfer risk, but the motion’s assertion that the total balance “strongly” favors DuPage is open to challenge.

## Argument-by-argument assessment

### 1. Cook venue is proper, but DuPage is the appropriate forum

**What is strong for Defendants.** Defendants concede Cook is technically a proper venue and frame their motion as a convenience question. They have substantial support for DuPage as the site of the alleged negligence: the Complaint places the labor and delivery at Safe Hospital, and the affidavits place the hospital and Dr. Smith in DuPage. (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, PDF p. 1, § I, ¶ 1; PDF pp. 8–10, Smith Aff. ¶¶ 2–5 and Hospital Administrator Aff. ¶¶ 2–4; Davis Complaint.docx, p. 2, ¶¶ 16–24.) That is a real connection to the proposed forum, not merely a venue technicality.

**What is weak or overstated.** The motion characterizes Cook’s “only connection” as later, unrelated treatment and death. The Complaint alleges that Baby Davis sustained severe injuries from the delayed delivery, required extensive treatment, deteriorated, and died at Cook County Hospital, and that the injuries and death resulted from Defendants’ conduct. (Davis Complaint.docx, p. 3, ¶¶ 25–30.) The reviewing physician’s report further links the labor-and-delivery injuries to Baby Davis’s subsequent condition, treatment, transfer, deterioration, and death. (Davis Complaint.docx, rendered p. 12, ¶¶ 2–4.) Thus, Cook treatment is not pleaded as a separate negligent act by Cook providers, but it is part of the alleged causal chain and damages proof. Calling it “unrelated” improperly collapses “not an alleged site of negligent delivery care” into “irrelevant to the action.”

**Target in the response.** Concede that the alleged delivery-care departures occurred in DuPage, but reject the claim that Cook is unrelated. *Bradbury* explains that death is the final event necessary to a wrongful-death claim and recognizes Cook’s interest because the decedent died there. (Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, PDF p. 2, ¶¶ 3–4.) The Complaint pleads both survival damages during Baby Davis’s life and wrongful-death damages after his death, so the Cook treatment and death are material to more than venue. (Davis Complaint.docx, p. 3, ¶¶ 27–30; p. 4, ¶¶ 34–38.) Keep the distinction clear: the location of the alleged negligence is DuPage; the situs of death and a substantial portion of the alleged injury course is Cook.

### 2. Plaintiff’s forum choice and residence

**What is strong for Defendants.** The Complaint does not allege that Sara Davis, Baby Davis, or any next of kin resided in Cook County—or identify a residence anywhere. (Davis Complaint.docx, rendered pp. 1–2, ¶¶ 1–15.) In a wrongful-death action, *Bradbury* identifies the decedent’s residence at death as important to the level of deference. (Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, PDF p. 3, ¶ 3.) On the attached record, Plaintiff cannot support an assertion that Cook is a home forum.

**What is weak or overstated.** An absent residence allegation is not affirmative proof that the decedent or Plaintiff lived in DuPage, nor does a non-home forum choice receive no deference. *Guerine* describes the reduction as “somewhat less” deference and quotes the principle that deference is less, not eliminated. (First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, PDF p. 4, ¶ 1.) The motion uses the Complaint’s silence to suggest that Plaintiff cannot have a Cook connection; that goes too far because the Complaint does allege treatment and death in Cook.

**Target in the response.** Acknowledge that the present pleadings do not establish home-forum deference; do not convert the omission into proof for Defendants. Emphasize that the choice is still entitled to consideration and that the death situs and subsequent treatment provide an independent Cook nexus. If residence is to be relied on in the filed response, support it with evidence in the record rather than an assumption. The governing inquiry remains the total circumstances and whether Defendants have shown the balance strongly favors transfer. (First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, PDF p. 4, ¶ 2; Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, PDF p. 7, ¶ 2.)

### 3. Private interest — convenience of the parties

**What is strong for Defendants.** All three defendants seek transfer, and the record places Dr. Smith, DuPage Health, and Safe Hospital in DuPage. Dr. Smith states that trial in DuPage would be substantially more convenient for him as a party and fact witness. (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, PDF p. 4, § IV.A, ¶ 1; PDF p. 9, Smith Aff. ¶¶ 2–4, 7; PDF p. 10, Hospital Administrator Aff. ¶ 2.) Unlike *Guerine*, there is no Cook-resident defendant who declined to seek transfer. (First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, PDF p. 7, ¶ 4.) This is a genuine point in Defendants’ favor.

**What is weak or overstated.** The motion asserts DuPage is more convenient for “every party” and Cook is convenient to no party, but it provides no evidence of Sara Davis’s or the estate’s residence or travel convenience. Nor does Dr. Smith’s affidavit explain a specific travel, scheduling, or trial-management burden. *Guerine* requires Defendants to show their chosen forum is inconvenient to them and the alternative is more convenient to all parties; Defendants cannot rest on an assertion that Plaintiff would find DuPage more convenient. (First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, PDF p. 4, ¶ 2.)

**Target in the response.** Do not deny that the defendants’ locations favor DuPage. Point out that their party-convenience evidence is one-sided and not enough by itself to satisfy the “strongly favors” standard. Tie it to the material Cook connection and the deficiencies in the witness comparison below.

### 4. Private interest — records and other evidence

**What is strong for Defendants.** The core standard-of-care proof—labor-and-delivery records, monitoring tracings, orders, nursing documentation, and Safe Hospital neonatal records—is identified as maintained at Safe Hospital in DuPage. (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, PDF p. 4, § IV.B, ¶ 1; PDF p. 10, Hospital Administrator Aff. ¶ 3.) Those materials go directly to the pleaded allegations about monitoring, recognizing fetal distress, communication, and timing of delivery. The records’ location gives DuPage a practical evidentiary advantage.

**What is weak or overstated.** The motion treats the Cook records as unrelated to liability and says the records are “overwhelmingly” DuPage materials. The report’s own list includes Baby Davis’s treatment records from Cook County Hospital, death-related materials, and diagnostic materials, and the report links the later medical course to the delivery injuries. (Davis Complaint.docx, Reviewing Physician’s Written Report, rendered p. 11, list items 7–9; rendered p. 12, ¶¶ 2–4.) The motion does not show that the Cook records cannot be produced electronically or transported, or that their location creates a practical obstacle to trial. The physical location of the core delivery records is a factor, not a conclusion on the whole motion.

**Target in the response.** Distinguish the location of the Safe Hospital records from the relevance of Cook treatment records. Argue that the latter bear on causation, deterioration, treatment, and death even if they do not establish a separate breach by a Cook provider. The motion’s assertion that only the Safe Hospital materials matter is inconsistent with the Complaint and its expert report.

### 5. Private interest — witnesses, process, and trial logistics

**What is strong for Defendants.** Dr. Smith identifies himself as a fact witness, and both affidavits state that Safe Hospital staff involved in labor, delivery, and neonatal care worked at the DuPage facility and lived in DuPage or nearby counties. (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, PDF p. 9, Smith Aff. ¶¶ 6–7; PDF p. 10, Hospital Administrator Aff. ¶ 4.) These direct-care witnesses are important to the standard-of-care issues. DuPage is likely more convenient for at least some of them.

**What is weak or overstated.** The affidavits do not identify the nurses, technicians, or other personnel by name; state how many are likely witnesses; give their actual counties of residence; distinguish material witnesses from other employees; or describe availability, willingness, or the concrete burden of appearing in Cook. “DuPage or counties in the immediate vicinity” does not establish that the overwhelming majority of material witnesses live in DuPage. The Complaint and report identify later Cook treatment and medical records, but the defense motion gives no countervailing witness-by-witness account. Defendants themselves acknowledge that compulsory process is available for in-state witnesses in either county. (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, PDF p. 4, § IV.C, ¶ 1; Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, PDF p. 4, ¶ 2.) *Bradbury* cautions against deciding witness convenience by speculation about identity, number, and location. (Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, PDF p. 4, ¶ 2.)

**Target in the response.** Make the evidentiary gap central. Ask the court to compare identified material witnesses and their testimony, not a general category of hospital employees. Develop the Cook treating-witness record with names, residence/work locations, expected testimony, and willingness or scheduling burden. On the present attachments, do not represent those witnesses as already identified or located. Compulsory process is effectively neutral on this record; cost and convenience remain possible DuPage factors but are not quantified. No supplied material establishes that a jury view of the hospital or delivery premises would assist the jury, so the view-of-premises factor is neutral.

### 6. “No predominant connection to Cook” / reliance on *Guerine* and *Langenhorst*

**What is strong for Defendants.** The primary negligence allegations, all defendants, Safe Hospital, and the core delivery evidence converge in DuPage. That distinguishes the geographic spread in *Guerine* and *Langenhorst*, where witnesses and evidence were distributed and no single county predominated. (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, PDF pp. 4–5, § IV.D, ¶ 1; First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, PDF p. 8, ¶ 4; Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, PDF p. 9, ¶ 6; PDF p. 10, ¶ 6; PDF p. 11, ¶ 3.) Defendants’ claim that DuPage has the strongest connection is the motion’s most persuasive point.

**What is weak or overstated.** Defendants’ “virtually every” and “overwhelming majority” formulations exceed the detail in their affidavits. The affidavits establish a DuPage location and a broad “immediate vicinity” for staff, not a complete comparison of the material witness pool. Further, *Guerine* requires totality-of-the-circumstances review; no single factor receives central or conclusive effect, and the plaintiff’s choice begins with some deference. (First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, PDF p. 4, ¶ 2; PDF p. 8, ¶ 4.) Cook has a real—not predominant by itself—connection through treatment and death. *Bradbury* rejected the premise that the death county was totally unrelated when the alleged negligence was elsewhere. (Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, PDF p. 4, ¶ 5.)

**Target in the response.** Be candid that the current materials make DuPage the center of the alleged delivery negligence. Then show why “center of the negligence” is not equivalent to “strongly more convenient for the whole case.” Use *Bradbury* for the Cook death/treatment connection and *Guerine* for the need for an evidence-based, totality analysis. Do not claim that *Guerine* controls the result: the motion has a meaningful distinction because it places all defendants and the initial care in DuPage.

### 7. Public interest — local interest and jury burden

**What is strong for Defendants.** DuPage has a substantial local interest in the quality of obstetric care furnished at a local hospital by a local physician and medical group. The Complaint places the alleged breaches there and names no Cook County defendant or negligent act. (Davis Complaint.docx, p. 2, ¶¶ 16–24; Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, PDF p. 5, § V.A, ¶ 1; § V.B, ¶ 1.) That supports trying the delivery-care controversy in DuPage and gives force to the argument that Cook jurors should not be burdened with a dispute wholly unconnected to their county.

**What is weak or overstated.** Cook is not “unrelated.” The Complaint alleges that Baby Davis was transferred to Cook County Hospital, received treatment there, deteriorated, and died there. *Bradbury* expressly found local interests in both the county of alleged negligence and Cook, where the child died, and declined to treat either as unrelated for jury-duty purposes. (Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, PDF p. 4, ¶ 5.) Defendants’ distinction—that there was no Cook conduct by these defendants—does not erase the local interest in the death and the medical course that the Complaint places there.

**Target in the response.** Frame this as a controversy with legitimate local interests in both counties: DuPage in the delivery care and Cook in the ensuing treatment and death. Acknowledge DuPage’s stronger connection to breach; argue that the jury-burden factor does not make Cook unrelated and cannot override the totality by itself.

### 8. Public interest — court congestion and annual-report statistics

**What is strong for Defendants.** The 2023 report shows a substantially lower all-case clearance rate for Cook than for the 18th Circuit: 60.5% versus 99.0%, with Cook below 100% in each year of the displayed five-year trend. It also reports more pending civil cases in Cook in absolute terms. These data are properly put before the court, unlike in *Bradbury*, where the defendants had not asked the court to consider them. (2023 Annual Report Statistical Summary.pdf, p. 21, clearance-rate table and definition; pp. 19, 46, 49, Cook and DuPage civil caseload rows; Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, PDF p. 4, ¶ 5; Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, PDF pp. 5–7, § V.C.)

**What is weak or overstated.** The rate and absolute case counts are for all case types, not medical-malpractice or comparable jury cases. They do not show that this case would reach disposition faster in DuPage. The report’s own case-filing-ratio table shows 2,522 total cases filed per judge in the 18th Circuit versus 1,322 in Cook; that all-case measure is also not dispositive, but it undercuts the inference from raw pending totals that DuPage necessarily has less judicial workload. (2023 Annual Report Statistical Summary.pdf, p. 20, “Number of Cases Filed per Judge” rows for 18th Circuit and Cook County.) Most importantly, the reported DuPage figure for 17 “Law > $50,000” jury cases is an average 51.4 months to verdict; the report contains no comparable Cook time-to-verdict figure and expressly notes Cook’s missing time-lapse data. (2023 Annual Report Statistical Summary.pdf, pp. 159–160, 163, 18th Circuit and Cook rows/notes.) The motion correctly disclaims reliance on that figure, but consequently has no direct evidence DuPage would resolve this case faster. It also acknowledges the report’s warning about Cook case-management variances. (2023 Annual Report Statistical Summary.pdf, p. 19, Cook caseload note; p. 49, reporting-variance note.)

The motion’s use of *Langenhorst* at page 18 should be corrected: that discussion of annual statistics appears in the dissent, not the majority opinion. The majority states that congestion is relatively insignificant where the record does not show the other forum would resolve the case more quickly. (Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, PDF p. 11, majority ¶ 1; PDF p. 18, dissenting opinion ¶ 1, paragraph discussing congestion statistics.)

**Target in the response.** Treat congestion as a modest factor only. Explain that the annual-report data are broad and mixed, provide the per-judge comparison and the absence of a Cook time-to-verdict comparator, and emphasize that the supplied cases caution against transfer based on congestion alone. (First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, PDF p. 3, ¶ 4; Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, PDF p. 11, majority ¶ 1.) Do not claim the statistics prove Cook is less congested; argue they fail to establish a comparative speed advantage for DuPage.

### 9. Claimed verification and supporting affidavits

**What is strong for Defendants.** Unlike the motion in *Bradbury*, this motion includes declarations from Dr. Smith and a Safe Hospital administrator addressing the defendant and hospital locations, records, and delivery personnel. (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, PDF pp. 8–11, Smith Aff. ¶¶ 1–9 and Hospital Administrator Aff. ¶¶ 1–7.) The response should not characterize the defense record as wholly unsupported.

**What is weak or overstated.** The motion calls itself verified and § VI says “this Motion is verified,” but the supplied copy shows an attorney signature and separate affidavits, not a separate verification of the motion. The text also calls Exhibit B an affidavit of a “[Records Custodian],” while the attached declaration is titled as one from the Hospital Administrator/President. Smith’s address fields are left as bracketed placeholders, and the affidavits do not identify the hospital staff or their specific residences. (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, PDF pp. 1, 7–10, §§ I, VI; Smith Aff. ¶¶ 2–4, 6; Hospital Administrator Aff. ¶ 4.) Smith’s statement that Cook treatment was “unrelated” is phrased as his understanding and conflicts with the causal allegations and expert report. (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, PDF p. 9, Smith Aff. ¶ 8; Davis Complaint.docx, p. 3, ¶¶ 25–30; rendered p. 12, ¶¶ 2–4.)

**Target in the response.** Ask the Court to evaluate the statements actually supported by the attached declarations, not the motion’s broader characterizations. Verify the filed original before making a technical objection to the “verified” label; the separate affidavits do supply some factual support, even if the motion itself appears not to include a verification page in the provided copy. The useful point is lack of specificity and the unsupported “unrelated” characterization, not that no defense evidence exists. Also correct the motion’s description of *Bradbury*: it affirmed denial of transfer because Defendants failed to meet their burden on the record; it did not “reverse course.” (Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, PDF p. 5, ¶¶ 1–2.)

## Recommended response priorities

1. **Lead with the causation and death record.** Use the Complaint’s paragraphs 25–30 and the reviewing physician’s causal opinions to refute “unrelated” Cook treatment, and use *Bradbury* for Cook’s local interest as the place of death.
2. **Confront the DuPage facts rather than minimize them.** The place of delivery, defendants, and core standard-of-care records favor DuPage. Argue that those facts do not by themselves establish that the total convenience factors strongly favor transfer.
3. **Expose the witness-proof gap.** The defense has not named or counted its hospital witnesses or given their actual county residences; the “immediate vicinity” language does not establish the alleged overwhelming DuPage majority. Provide a concrete plaintiff-side witness comparison if supported by admissible evidence.
4. **Correct the congestion analysis.** The attached data are all-case measures, provide no comparative trial-time evidence, and include a per-judge figure unfavorable to the motion’s simple volume comparison. Emphasize the limited role of congestion.
5. **Be candid about residence.** The Complaint is silent on Sara’s and Baby Davis’s residence. Do not claim Cook home-forum deference from these materials; establish residence with record evidence if the point will be made.

The strongest response is therefore a focused totality argument: DuPage is the site of the alleged delivery negligence, but Cook is a meaningful forum for the resulting course of treatment, the death, and the wrongful-death and survival proof. Defendants’ evidence makes DuPage a plausible forum; it does not eliminate Cook’s connection or conclusively establish that transfer would make trial more convenient for all parties and material witnesses.
