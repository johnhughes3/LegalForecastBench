## II. Executive Summary

The motion is better built than the one rejected in *Bradbury*. It purports to be verified, it attaches affidavits, and it puts court statistics before the court. Its main theme is real: every defendant, and all of the alleged negligent conduct, is in DuPage County. Even so, the motion has serious problems, and we can win this on the record if we build our own record now.

1. **The controlling case is on our side, and the motion misstates it.** *Bradbury* is nearly identical to our case. There, the alleged medical negligence and every defendant were in Kankakee County, and the decedent was transferred to a Cook County hospital and died there. The court **affirmed denial** of the transfer. It held that Cook County had a legitimate interest "because the decedent died here." (Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, p.4, ¶5; p.5, ¶¶2–3.) The motion says *Bradbury* "reversed course" (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, p.6, ¶4 (§VI)). That is wrong.
2. **The motion misstates the rule in *Guerine*.** It says *Guerine* held transfer is "warranted only where" witnesses are scattered (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, pp.4–5, §IV.D). *Guerine* actually held that a trial court **abuses its discretion in granting** transfer where witnesses are scattered across several counties, including the plaintiff's chosen forum, and no county has a predominant connection. (First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, p.8, ¶4.)
3. **One of the motion's authorities is a dissent.** For court-congestion statistics, the motion relies on "Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.18." That page is part of Justice Garman's **dissent**. The majority held congestion did not require transfer. (Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.10, ¶7; p.11, ¶1; pp.12–20 (dissent).)
4. **The congestion argument relies on the wrong statistic.** Defendants compare clearance rates for all case types: Cook 60.5% versus 18th Circuit 99.0% (2023 Annual Report Statistical Summary.pdf, p.21). They also say this figure "does not depend on" Cook's case-management reporting change. But the Summary itself warns that Cook's change affects the number of cases **filed and closed** in 2023 (2023 Annual Report Statistical Summary.pdf, p.19, note; p.49, note), and those are the inputs to the clearance rate (p.21, note). For the **civil category**, which is the relevant one, Cook's 2023 clearance rate was **97.9%**, higher than the 18th Circuit's **96.3%** (2023 Annual Report Statistical Summary.pdf, p.22). Defendants also concede that DuPage's average time to jury verdict in Law > $50,000 cases is **51.4 months** (2023 Annual Report Statistical Summary.pdf, p.160). Nothing in the record shows DuPage would resolve this case faster.
5. **The affidavits are weak.** Key statements are made "to the best of my knowledge, information, and belief." Neither affidavit names a single nonparty witness. The hospital affiant is identified only as "Hospital Administrator." Dr. Smith's affidavit has blank placeholders for addresses. No jurat or verification appears. There is no affidavit from DuPage Health, S.C. The motion cites an "Affidavit of [Records Custodian]" that does not exist, and its paragraph citations do not match the affidavit actually attached.
6. **The motion treats 19 of Baby Davis's 23 days of life as irrelevant.** It calls the Cook County care "subsequent, unrelated" treatment. That is contradicted by the 2-622 report: the reviewer relied on the Cook County records, and the report links the delivery injuries to the transfer, the deterioration, and the death. The survival counts seek damages for pain and suffering "between the time of his injury and death" (Davis Complaint.docx, p.4, ¶34; p.6, ¶43; p.8, ¶52). Most of that time was spent in Cook County, and the Cook County physicians are the core damages and causation witnesses.

**Our main exposure:**
- Ms. Davis lives in Kane County, not Cook. Her choice therefore gets "somewhat less deference," although "only less, as opposed to none." (First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, p.4, ¶1.)
- If the court views the case the way the *Langenhorst* dissent describes *Dawdy*, it may find DuPage has a "predominant connection." In that scenario, all liability conduct and all defendants are in the transferee county and the chosen forum has little tie to the case (Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.8, ¶¶2–3; p.19, ¶1).
- Our client file contains facts that, if they come out, cut against us. Chief among them: the memo says the **family** chose the transfer, which raises the *Bland* "plaintiff-selected treater" problem discussed in *Bradbury* (Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, p.4, ¶3).

We must close these gaps with affidavits **before** the response is filed. *Bradbury* turned on who built the better record (Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, p.5, ¶2).

## IV. Argument-by-Argument Analysis

### A. Introduction and Legal Standard (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, p.1, ¶1; pp.3–4, §III)

**What Defendants argue.** They concede venue is proper in Cook County. They invoke forum non conveniens and recite the private and public interest factors, the defendant's "strongly favor" burden, and the reduced deference given when neither the plaintiff's residence nor the injury site is in the chosen forum. They also cite *Bradbury* for the rule that the decedent's residence at death controls in a wrongful-death case.

**Strengths:**
- The factor list and the burden standard are accurately quoted from *Guerine* and *Langenhorst* (First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, p.3, ¶4; Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.7, ¶2).
- The reduced-deference rule is a real problem for us because Ms. Davis lives in Kane County (First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, p.4, ¶1; Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.9, ¶3).

**Weaknesses:**
- **Defendants concede venue.** That concession matters. *Bradbury* holds that in a wrongful-death action the place of the wrong is where the last event necessary for liability occurs, which is "the place of the decedent's death." Venue was proper in Cook County on that basis (Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, p.2, ¶¶3–4). That holding also supports treating Cook County as a **site of the injury** for deference purposes. The rule reduces deference only when "neither the plaintiff's residence **nor the site of the accident or injury**" is in the chosen forum (First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, p.4, ¶1). We should argue that for the wrongful-death counts, the death in Cook County is the legal injury.
- **The standard section leaves out the language that hurts Defendants.** It omits that:
  - forum non conveniens is exercised "only in exceptional circumstances" (First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, p.5, ¶3; Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.6, ¶2);
  - "the battle over forum begins with the plaintiff's choice already in the lead" (First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, p.6, ¶2);
  - intrastate transfer is appropriate "only when the litigation has 'no practical connection' ... no nexus, with the plaintiff's chosen forum" (First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, p.6, ¶2);
  - reduced deference is "only less, as opposed to none" (First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, p.4, ¶1; Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.9, ¶3);
  - defendants "cannot assert that the plaintiff's chosen forum is inconvenient to the plaintiff." The motion quotes this (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, p.4) but then violates it (see §IV.B below).
- **The *Bradbury* citation backfires.** The motion cites *Bradbury* for the decedent's-residence rule. In *Bradbury*, the *defendants'* failure to establish residence by evidence counted **against the movants**, because they carry the burden (Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, p.4, ¶6 – p.5, ¶2). Here too, Defendants' only showing on residence is an unverified "informed" statement (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, p.2, ¶9). That is exactly the kind of "unsupported and unverified assertion[]" *Bradbury* refused to credit (Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, p.5, ¶1).

**Target points:**
- Restore the full standard: exceptional circumstances, plaintiff "in the lead," the "no practical connection" requirement, and "less, as opposed to none."
- Argue that the place of death is a site of injury for the wrongful-death claims.
- **Do not** concede that Ms. Davis's choice deserves minimal deference. Argue that even reduced deference remains meaningful, and that *Guerine* kept Kane County plaintiffs in Cook County.

### B. Private Factor 1: Convenience of the Parties (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, p.4, §IV.A)

**What Defendants argue.** Every defendant is in DuPage County. Cook County is "convenient to no Defendant." They distinguish *Guerine*, where a defendant lived in Cook County, and assert that DuPage would be more convenient for "every party," including Plaintiff.

**Strengths:**
- It is true and undisputed that all three defendants are DuPage-based. This is the motion's strongest factual point.
- The *Guerine* distinction is fair as far as it goes. The *Guerine* court relied in part on the fact that defendant Guerine lived in Cook County and never moved to transfer (First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, p.7, ¶4).
- The *Langenhorst* majority also partly distinguished *Dawdy* because the *Dawdy* defendant sought transfer to the individual defendant's home county, which Defendants seek here (Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.11, ¶4).

**Weaknesses:**
1. **No defendant shows that Cook County is actually inconvenient.** The test is that "the defendant must show that the plaintiff's chosen forum is inconvenient to the defendant **and** that another forum is more convenient to all parties" (First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, p.4, ¶2; Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.7, ¶2).
   - Dr. Smith's affidavit says only that DuPage would be "substantially more convenient" (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, p.9, ¶7). That is comparative. It identifies no concrete burden from a Cook County trial: no travel time, no lost practice days, no hardship.
   - The hospital affidavit says nothing about Safe Hospital being inconvenienced.
   - **DuPage Health, S.C. filed no affidavit at all.** Dr. Smith's statement about its location is hedged as "principal place of business and/or the office(s) at which I practice," with the address left as a placeholder (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, p.8, ¶3).
   - *Langenhorst* rejected a motion where "no affidavits have been filed stating that St. Clair County would be an inconvenient forum," and where defendants had not "claimed any inconvenience whatsoever" (Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.10, ¶¶3–4). The *Langenhorst* defendants' affidavits said only that the transferee county "would not be inconvenient," and plaintiff's counsel successfully argued "that's not the test" (Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.3, ¶2; p.3, ¶¶6–7).
2. **Defendants improperly argue convenience for Plaintiff.** The motion says DuPage "would be more convenient for every party," and "Defendants are aware of no party for whom Cook County would be more convenient." That is an argument that Cook is inconvenient for Plaintiff. *Guerine* and *Langenhorst* forbid it (First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, p.4, ¶2; Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.9, ¶4, which rejects "[d]efendants assert[ion] that trial in Clinton County would be more convenient to the plaintiff").
3. **The "smaller world" principle.** "Convenience ... has a different meaning" in an interconnected world (First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, p.8, ¶3; Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.10, ¶5). Defendants make no showing of meaningful distance or travel burden between their DuPage locations and a Cook County courthouse.
   - The record gives no mileage, but *Langenhorst* says that where counties are adjacent and witness travel is "minimally different," the "battle over the forum results in a battle over the minutiae" (Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.10, ¶5).
   - Defendants' own affidavits say their witnesses live "in DuPage County or in counties in the immediate vicinity of DuPage County" (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, p.9, ¶6; p.10, ¶4). That phrase does not exclude Cook County.
   - Before we argue adjacency, we should support the geography with an affidavit.
4. **The *Guerine* distinction is weaker than it looks.** In *Guerine*, the plaintiffs were Kane County residents who filed in Cook County, just like Ms. Davis. The Supreme Court relied on their **affidavits stating they would suffer no inconvenience traveling to Cook County** (First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, p.7, ¶4). We can supply the same affidavit.

**Target points:**
- No defendant has shown that Cook County is inconvenient; comparative convenience is not the test.
- DuPage Health has no affidavit at all.
- Defendants cannot argue Plaintiff's convenience.
- **Action:** Obtain an affidavit from Sara Davis (and the father, as next of kin, Davis Complaint.docx, p.5, ¶37) stating where she lives and that Cook County is convenient for her and is her chosen forum.

### C. Private Factor 2: Access to Sources of Proof (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, p.4, §IV.B; p.2, ¶8)

**What Defendants argue.** The labor-and-delivery records, fetal-heart-rate tracings, orders, nursing records, and neonatal records are at Safe Hospital. The only Cook County records relate to "subsequent" treatment that is "not alleged to be part of" the standard-of-care conduct.

**Strengths:**
- The liability records are at Safe Hospital (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, p.10, ¶3).
- The Complaint's breach allegations focus on the August 24 labor and delivery (Davis Complaint.docx, pp.2–3, ¶¶17–24; p.4, ¶32; pp.6–7, ¶48).

**Weaknesses:**
1. **The motion mischaracterizes the 2-622 report.**
   - The reviewer expressly relied on "[t]he records concerning BABY DAVIS's treatment at Cook County Hospital from August 28, 2024 through September 16, 2024." The reviewer also relied on the death certificate and any autopsy, pathology, neurological, radiological, or diagnostic reports (Davis Complaint.docx, Reviewing Physician's Report, p.11, items 7–8).
   - The report opines that the delivery injuries "caused or contributed to BABY DAVIS's subsequent medical condition, need for extensive treatment, transfer to Cook County Hospital, deterioration, and death" (Davis Complaint.docx, Reviewing Physician's Report, p.13, ¶3), and that the delay caused hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy, organ injury, and "ultimate death" (id., p.13, ¶2).
   - So the Cook County records are **causation and damages records**, not "unrelated" records.
   - The motion cites only items 1–6 as Safe Hospital records and calls item 7 "unrelated" (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, p.2, ¶8; p.4, §IV.B). It ignores item 8 entirely.
2. **Proof is not limited to liability.** The case has seven counts:
   - three survival counts seeking damages for pain, suffering, disability, and loss of a normal life "[b]etween the time of his injury and death" (Davis Complaint.docx, p.4, ¶34; p.6, ¶43; p.8, ¶52);
   - three wrongful-death counts, in which death is an element (Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, p.2, ¶¶3–4);
   - a family-expenses count for hospital and medical bills, most of which presumably come from Cook County (Davis Complaint.docx, p.8–9, ¶¶56–58).
   Each element requires Cook County proof.
3. **Records exist in both counties.** That was exactly the situation in *Bradbury*, where the court found "medical records relative to the decedent are located in both Cook and Kankakee Counties" and still affirmed denial of transfer (Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, p.4, ¶2).
4. **Documentary evidence is portable.** Electronic fetal-monitoring tracings and medical charts can be produced anywhere. *Langenhorst* faulted defendants for not showing "any impediments to accessing sources of testimonial, documentary, and real evidence" (Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.10, ¶4). Defendants do not claim any impediment to producing Safe Hospital records in a Cook County courtroom.
5. **No jury view.** Defendants do not argue that a jury view of the delivery room is appropriate. The factor is absent here, unlike *Dawdy*, where "the possibility of a jury view of the accident scene was a practical consideration" (Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.11, ¶4).

**Target points:**
- Quote the 2-622 report's causation paragraph against Defendants' "unrelated" characterization.
- Emphasize that 19 of 23 days of survival damages, the death, and the medical expenses arose in Cook County.
- Records exist in both counties (*Bradbury*), and documents are portable.

### D. Private Factor 3: Compulsory Process and Cost of Witness Attendance (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, p.4, §IV.C)

**What Defendants argue.** The labor-and-delivery physicians, nurses, and staff worked at Safe Hospital and live "in DuPage County or the surrounding counties." Compulsory process is technically available statewide, but bringing these providers to Cook County is a greater practical burden.

**Strengths:**
- It is plausible, and supported by an affidavit, that the liability witnesses are DuPage-based (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, p.10, ¶4).
- Defendants are candid that compulsory process is available.

**Weaknesses:**
1. **No witness is identified.** Neither affidavit names one nurse, technician, or physician, gives a residence, or says what the person would testify to.
   - *Bradbury*: "they have failed to identify any nonparty witnesses ... Neither this court nor the trial court should be forced to reach a decision by speculating as to the identity, number, and location of witnesses" (Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, p.4, ¶2).
   - *Langenhorst*: defendants listed hospital and other personnel "but have not identified who these people are, where they live, or what, if any, relevant testimony they might provide" (Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.9, ¶2).
2. **The residence statements are hedged and not based on personal knowledge.**
   - Dr. Smith's ¶¶5–6 are made "[t]o the best of my knowledge, information, and belief" (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, p.8, ¶5; p.9, ¶6).
   - The hospital affiant's residence statement is likewise "to the best of my knowledge," and is based on review of personnel records (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, p.10, ¶¶1, 4).
   - "Or in counties in the immediate vicinity" is wide enough to include Cook County.
3. **The witnesses are Defendants' own employees and agents.** Safe Hospital and Dr. Smith control them. These are not "unwilling" witnesses who would need compulsory process. Defendants concede Rule 237 process is available in either county (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, p.4, §IV.C; Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, p.4, ¶2).
4. **Defendants ignore our witnesses.**
   - According to the client file, the five Cook County treating physicians live in Cook County, are willing to testify, and would be inconvenienced by trial in DuPage.
   - In *Bradbury*, plaintiff's affidavit identifying 13 Cook County hospital doctors and nurses as likely trial witnesses was enough. The court rejected the defendants' *Bland* argument because the defendants arranged the transfer (Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, p.3, ¶5; p.4, ¶3).
   - In *Langenhorst*, the majority relied on "plaintiff's treating physicians" located outside the transferee county (Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.9, ¶6).
5. **The *Bland* risk (our weakness).** *Bland* cautions against plaintiffs selecting "a treating physician or expert in what would, in reality, be an inconvenient forum" (Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, p.4, ¶3; Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.16, ¶1 (dissent)).
   - If the family alone chose the Cook County hospital, Defendants will argue the Cook County treaters deserve less weight.
   - If we present these doctors as **standard-of-care critics** of Dr. Smith, Defendants will call them de facto plaintiff's experts. The *Langenhorst* dissent shows how a plaintiff-retained witness's residence gets "heavily discounted" (Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.16, ¶1).
   - Our response: Baby Davis was transferred by necessity to a higher level of care, years before any litigation. These physicians are **occurrence/treating witnesses** to 19 days of injury and to the death itself. "Nothing in this record suggests that the decedent's treating physicians in Cook County were chosen to establish a nexus to venue" (Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, p.4, ¶3).
   - Our expert is the 2-622 reviewer, not these doctors.

**Target points:**
- Defendants identify no nonparty witness by name.
- Their affidavits are made on information and belief.
- Their witnesses are their own employees and are not "unwilling."
- **Action:** Obtain affidavits from each of the five Cook County physicians covering: name, specialty, Cook County residence and practice, the dates and nature of their treatment, the subject of their testimony (injury course, neurological condition, cause of death, suffering), willingness to testify in Cook County, and the concrete burden of a DuPage trial (patient care and on-call obligations). Have their testimony framed as **treating** testimony. Keep standard-of-care opinions out of the affidavits.
- Also identify Cook County nurses, NICU staff, the pathologist or medical examiner (if any), and the Cook County funeral home. These add to the *Guerine*/*Langenhorst* "scattered witnesses" picture.

### E. "No Predominant Connection to Cook County" (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, pp.4–5, §IV.D)

**What Defendants argue.** Unlike *Guerine* and *Langenhorst*, where witnesses were scattered, here the negligence, the defendants, and "virtually every standard-of-care witness" are in DuPage. DuPage therefore has the "predominant connection."

**Strengths:**
- This is Defendants' best argument. It tracks how *Langenhorst* describes *Dawdy*: transfer was required where "none of the witnesses resided in the plaintiff's chosen forum," the accident happened in the transferee county, the individual defendant lived there, and the transferee county had a "predominant connection" (Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.7, ¶4 – p.8, ¶3; p.19, ¶1 (dissent's account of *Dawdy*)).
- Liability conduct was confined to DuPage.

**Weaknesses:**
1. **The motion misstates *Guerine*'s holding.** It says *Guerine* "held that transfer is warranted only where 'the potential trial witnesses are scattered among several counties ...'" (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, p.5). In fact *Guerine* held that "a trial court **abuses its discretion in granting** an intrastate forum non conveniens motion ... where ... the potential trial witnesses are scattered among several counties, including the plaintiff's chosen forum, and no single county enjoys a predominant connection" (First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, p.8, ¶4). This misquotation reverses the holding and we should point it out directly to the court.
2. **The witnesses here are not concentrated in DuPage.**
   - Plaintiff and her family live in Kane County (client file).
   - Defendants and liability staff are in DuPage "or ... the immediate vicinity" (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, p.9, ¶6).
   - The treating physicians are in Cook County (client file).
   - That is witnesses "scattered among several counties, including the plaintiff's chosen forum," which is the *Guerine* holding (First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, p.8, ¶4) and the *Langenhorst* holding (Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.11, ¶3).
   - *Dawdy* is distinguishable on the ground the *Langenhorst* majority itself identified: "in Dawdy, none of the witnesses resided in the plaintiff's chosen forum" (Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.11, ¶4). Here, our key damages and causation witnesses do.
3. **The motion's "predominant connection" analysis looks only at liability.** It defines the case by the breach. Wrongful-death liability is not complete until death (Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, p.2, ¶¶3–4), and the death happened in Cook County. The motion's own phrase, "virtually every **standard-of-care** witness," concedes that causation and damages witnesses are elsewhere.
4. ***Bradbury* is closer than *Guerine* or *Dawdy*.** The motion never discusses *Bradbury*'s facts on this point:
   - In *Bradbury*, Kankakee was "the situs of all of the negligent acts" and "the residence of all of the individual and corporate defendants." Records were in Kankakee and the nurses were in Kankakee (Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, p.3, ¶4).
   - That is exactly Defendants' "predominant connection" showing here. The court still affirmed denial of transfer, because Cook County had an interest through the death there and through the Cook County treaters (Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, p.4, ¶¶2–5; p.5, ¶¶2–3).
5. **Citation errors.** The motion cites *Langenhorst* "p.10–p.11" for the dispersal of witnesses. That discussion is primarily at p.9, ¶¶2, 6 and p.10, ¶1.

**Target points:**
- Expose the misquotation of *Guerine*.
- Make *Bradbury* the centerpiece: same structure (out-county negligence, out-county defendants, death in Cook after transfer), same result.
- Distinguish *Dawdy* using the *Langenhorst* majority's own first ground: we have Cook County witnesses.
- **Caution:** this factor depends on the strength of our affidavits. If we file nothing, the court will see only Defendants' version, and *Dawdy* becomes the closer analogy.

### F. Public Factor 1: Local Interest (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, p.5, §V.A)

**What Defendants argue.** DuPage has a strong interest in the quality of obstetric care by DuPage providers. Cook County's only tie is that Baby Davis later received treatment and died there. Unlike in *Bradbury*, they say, that "did not result from any Cook County conduct by these Defendants."

**Strengths:**
- DuPage plainly has a legitimate local interest in medical care delivered within its borders. *Bradbury* acknowledges the same interest for Kankakee County (Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, p.4, ¶5).
- Under *Dawdy* as described in *Langenhorst*, the place of the occurrence gives the action a "local interest" (Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.8, ¶2).

**Weaknesses:**
1. **The attempted distinction of *Bradbury* fails on *Bradbury*'s own facts.** In *Bradbury*, the defendants also had no "Cook County conduct." All negligent acts occurred in Kankakee County (Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, p.3, ¶4). The court found Cook County "has an interest because the decedent died here," and it was "unprepared to say that it would be unfair to burden the citizens of either county" (Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, p.4, ¶5). Defendants' asserted distinction does not exist.
2. **The test is whether the forum is "totally unrelated" or has "no practical connection," not which county has a stronger interest** (Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, p.4, ¶5; First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, p.6, ¶2). A forum where the decedent spent most of his life, suffered most of the injuries claimed in the survival counts, and died is not unconnected.
3. **Cook County has an independent interest in its own institutions.** The case will put the treatment, prognosis, and cause-of-death testimony of Cook County physicians and a Cook County hospital before the jury. In *Langenhorst*, St. Clair County's interest arose from conduct and presence connected to that county even though the accident happened elsewhere (Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.10, ¶6). That said, *Langenhorst* is a weaker analogy here, because its tie was a defendant's presence. Use it sparingly.

**Target points:**
- Quote *Bradbury* p.4, ¶5 directly against Defendants' attempted distinction.
- Frame the issue as "shared interest," which defeats "strongly favors."

### G. Public Factor 2: Jury Duty Burden (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, p.5, §V.B)

**What Defendants argue.** It is unfair to impose jury duty on Cook County residents in a case "that does not involve any Cook County resident, any Cook County business, or any conduct occurring in Cook County."

**Strengths:**
- This factor normally follows the local-interest factor, and DuPage's interest is real.

**Weaknesses:**
- **The premise is false on the record.**
  - The death occurred in Cook County (Davis Complaint.docx, p.3, ¶29).
  - Treatment was rendered by a Cook County institution (id., p.3, ¶27).
  - The Cook County treating physicians are Cook County residents (client file; to be supported by affidavit).
  - The Special Administrator was appointed by the Circuit Court of Cook County (Davis Complaint.docx, p.1, ¶2).
- ***Bradbury* rejected this argument on the same facts** (Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, p.4, ¶5).

**Target point:** This argument depends entirely on Defendants' "no connection" framing. When that framing falls, so does this factor. Compare *Langenhorst*: defendants' claim of "no connection" was "factually inaccurate and misleading" (Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.10, ¶2).

### H. Public Factor 3: Court Congestion (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, pp.5–6, §V.C)

**What Defendants argue.** They ask the court to take judicial notice of the 2023 Statistical Summary, and they distinguish *Bradbury*'s forfeiture holding. They rely on:
- all-case-type clearance rates: Cook 60.5% versus 18th Circuit 99.0%, plus five-year trends;
- pending caseloads: Cook 145,608 civil / 498,378 total versus DuPage 10,877 civil / 101,244 total.

They disclaim reliance on time-to-verdict data.

**Strengths:**
- Judicial notice of these statistics is proper, and Defendants cured the *Bradbury* forfeiture problem by putting the numbers before the court (Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, p.4, ¶5).
- The figures are accurately transcribed:
  - all-types clearance rates and five-year trends (2023 Annual Report Statistical Summary.pdf, p.21);
  - Cook ending-open totals (p.49);
  - DuPage totals (p.46);
  - Cook's 145,608 open civil cases is the "Total Civil Cases" ending-open figure (p.49).
- *Guerine* acknowledged that "the Cook County circuit court is more congested than either Kane or De Kalb County circuit courts" (First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, p.8, ¶2).
- The candor about the reporting caveat and the missing Cook time-to-verdict data will earn some credibility with the court.

**Weaknesses (this is the most vulnerable section of the motion):**
1. **Congestion is "relatively insignificant," and especially so where the record does not show the other forum would resolve the case faster** (First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, p.3, ¶4; Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.11, ¶1). It is "not dispositive" (First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, p.8, ¶2). Courts "should be extremely reluctant to dismiss a case from the forum rei gestae merely because that forum's docket has a backlog" (First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, p.3, ¶4).
2. **Defendants concede they cannot show DuPage is faster.** They admit Cook reported no time-to-verdict data and withdraw reliance on DuPage's figure (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, p.6). That figure is **51.4 months** from filing to jury verdict in Law > $50,000 (L) cases in the 18th Circuit (2023 Annual Report Statistical Summary.pdf, p.160, 18th Circuit block, 17 cases). That is essentially the same as the downstate average of 51.9 months (id., p.160, Downstate Total). DuPage's all-case-type average time to jury verdict was 29.1 months, slower than neighboring Kane County at 16.8 months (id., p.163). By Defendants' own admission, the record "does not show the other forum would resolve the case more quickly." Under *Guerine* and *Langenhorst*, that ends the inquiry.
3. **The all-case-type clearance rate is the wrong measure, and it is not immune from the reporting caveat.**
   - Defendants say the clearance rate "does not depend on that reporting change." But the clearance rate is closed cases divided by new-filed plus reinstated cases (2023 Annual Report Statistical Summary.pdf, p.21, note). The Summary expressly warns that because of Cook's system change, "[t]here will also be noticeable differences in the number of cases **filed and closed** in 2023" (id., p.19, note; p.49, note).
   - Defendants quoted only the first sentence of that note (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, p.6). The omitted sentence directly undercuts their claim of independence. We should point this out.
   - The all-types figure includes criminal, traffic, family, juvenile, and other matters, which have nothing to do with a Law Division medical negligence case.
4. **The civil-category data favor Cook County.** The 2023 **civil** clearance rate was **97.9% for Cook County** versus **96.3% for the 18th Circuit** (2023 Annual Report Statistical Summary.pdf, p.22). Cook's civil caseload: 161,064 new filed plus 1,283 reinstated, with 158,903 closed (id., p.49). On the measure most relevant to this case, Cook kept pace better than DuPage in 2023.
5. **Raw pending numbers ignore scale.** Cook has 372 judges versus 47 in the 18th Circuit, and Cook judges received **1,322** filings per judge versus **2,522** per judge in the 18th Circuit (2023 Annual Report Statistical Summary.pdf, p.20). Comparing 145,608 to 10,877 without accounting for court size is misleading.
   - **Caution:** if we divide pending civil cases by judges, Cook's per-judge civil backlog is still higher than DuPage's (roughly 391 versus 231, computed from p.20 and pp.46, 49). So we should use the filings-per-judge and civil clearance figures, not a per-judge pending comparison.
6. **The motion relies on a dissent.** "Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.18" is from Justice Garman's dissent (the dissent runs pp.12–20). The *Langenhorst* majority rejected the congestion argument, deferred to the trial court's assessment of its own docket, and held that "[t]ransfer ... is also not required by the court docket" (Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.10, ¶7 – p.11, ¶1). The motion also cites "Langenhorst ... p.12" for the "relatively insignificant" quotation. That language appears in the majority at p.11, ¶1 and in *Guerine* at p.3, ¶4.
7. **The trial court is in the best position to assess its own docket** (Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.11, ¶1; Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, p.4, ¶5). We should invite the Law Division judge to speak to the calendar's actual ability to try this case, as the *Langenhorst* trial judge did (Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.3, ¶¶3–4).

**Target points:**
- Use the civil clearance rate (p.22) and filings-per-judge figure (p.20).
- Quote the full reporting-change note (p.19; p.49).
- Use DuPage's 51.4-month time to verdict (p.160).
- Expose the reliance on a dissent.
- Rely on the "relatively insignificant" and "not dispositive" rule.
- **Caveat on the Law > $50,000 pending columns:** the column headers are hard to read in the extracted text. Cook shows 11,049 open in one Law > $50,000 column (2023 Annual Report Statistical Summary.pdf, p.49), and DuPage shows 561 and 1,528 in the two Law > $50,000 columns (p.46). These favor Defendants in absolute terms. Verify the columns visually before anyone cites them. We should not raise these figures ourselves, but we should be ready to address them on a per-judge basis.

**Legal misstatements (high-value, low-risk):**
1. *Guerine* misquoted. The holding is that granting transfer is an abuse of discretion where witnesses are scattered (First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, p.8, ¶4).
2. *Bradbury* did not "reverse course." It **affirmed denial** of transfer on facts nearly identical to ours (Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, p.5, ¶3).
3. *Langenhorst* p.18 is the **dissent**. The majority rejected the congestion argument (Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, pp.10–11).

**Burden and standard:**
5. "Exceptional circumstances," the plaintiff "in the lead," "no practical connection," and deference that is "less ... as opposed to none."
6. The defendant must show the chosen forum is inconvenient **to the defendant**. No affidavit does this, and DuPage Health filed none.
7. Defendants cannot argue Plaintiff's convenience.

8. *Bradbury* is our closest case: negligence out of county, all defendants out of county, transfer to and death in Cook County, Cook County treaters. Transfer was denied.

12. Defendants identify no nonparty witnesses, rely on information and belief, and have no verification.

**Congestion:**
13. Civil clearance: Cook 97.9% versus DuPage 96.3% (p.22). Filings per judge: Cook 1,322 versus DuPage 2,522 (p.20). DuPage time to verdict: 51.4 months (p.160). Defendants concede there is no showing that DuPage is faster. Congestion is "relatively insignificant."

## VII. Risk Assessment and Cautions

- **Overall:** On the current record (Defendants' affidavits only), the court could find a *Dawdy*-type predominant connection to DuPage, especially given Ms. Davis's non-Cook residence. With the affidavits listed in Part VI, *Bradbury* and *Guerine* give us a strong basis to argue the factors do not "strongly favor" transfer. Review is for abuse of discretion, and the trial court's ruling will be hard to overturn either way (Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.5, ¶8 – p.6, ¶1). Winning at the trial level is therefore what counts.
- **Forum-shopping optics:** *Guerine* expressly names curbing plaintiffs' forum shopping as a concern (First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, p.5, ¶¶5–6; p.6, ¶1). Our response must not mention verdict-size considerations or DuPage's reputation. In *Langenhorst*, counsel openly admitted verdict motives (Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p.3, last ¶ – p.4, ¶1). Don't do that.
- **Bland exposure:** This depends on who chose the Cook County hospital, and is the main reason to get the transfer records before we draft.
- **Kane County residence:** Defendants will argue that Kane is closer to DuPage. Under *Guerine*, Defendants cannot argue Plaintiff's convenience, and the *Guerine* plaintiffs were also Kane County residents who kept a Cook County forum (First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, p.4, ¶2; p.7, ¶4).
