## 2. Award’s departures from the Final Notice

### Review standard

The arbitrator stated that review was **not de novo**. Because the deciding official had weighed the Douglas factors under 6-B DCMR § 1606.2, he asked whether MPD’s conclusions on those factors fell “within tolerable limits of reasonableness”; if a conclusion on a material factor fell outside those limits, the penalty determination could not stand as issued. (In re Arbitration Between Fraternal Ord. of Police/Metro. Police Dep't Lab. Comm. (Braddock Grievance) & D.C. Metro. Police Dep't, FMCS Case No. 24-51267, Grievance No. 24-0392 (Mar. 14, 2025) (Larrabee, Arb.) (braddock_arbitration_award.pdf), p. 2, ¶ 5.) The arbitrator applied that same standard to both factor-level departures.

### Departure 1 — Factor six (comparability of the discipline)

**MPD’s determination:** MPD found its four comparator terminations sufficiently similar to support termination here. **Arbitrator’s finding:** He held that MPD had not proved that the comparators were similarly situated and that MPD’s factor-six conclusion fell outside tolerable limits of reasonableness. He reasoned that two comparators involved on-duty conduct, one involved a fatality, and one involved an officer with two prior sustained force complaints, whereas Braddock’s misconduct was off duty, caused no fatality, and followed one unrelated reprimand. (In re Arbitration Between Fraternal Ord. of Police/Metro. Police Dep't Lab. Comm. (Braddock Grievance) & D.C. Metro. Police Dep't, FMCS Case No. 24-51267, Grievance No. 24-0392 (Mar. 14, 2025) (Larrabee, Arb.) (braddock_arbitration_award.pdf), p. 2, ¶ 7.) The comparator memorandum identifies the two on-duty cases as Guzman and Sadler, the fatality case as Pietrzak, and Guzman as the officer with two prior sustained excessive-force complaints. (Memorandum from Yolanda Britt, Director, Disciplinary Review Division, Hum. Res. Mgmt. Bureau, D.C. Metro. Police Dep't, to Sandra M. Delgado, Assistant Chief, Professional Development Bureau, re: Comparator Discipline Cases, Adverse Action Case No. IS-23-002841 (Braddock) (Apr. 11, 2024) (comparator_discipline_cases.pdf), p. 1, ¶¶ 2–4.) The applied review standard was the non-de-novo, tolerable-limits-of-reasonableness standard described above; the arbitrator expressly found MPD’s conclusion outside those limits. (In re Arbitration Between Fraternal Ord. of Police/Metro. Police Dep't Lab. Comm. (Braddock Grievance) & D.C. Metro. Police Dep't, FMCS Case No. 24-51267, Grievance No. 24-0392 (Mar. 14, 2025) (Larrabee, Arb.) (braddock_arbitration_award.pdf), p. 2, ¶¶ 5, 7.)

### Departure 2 — Factor twelve (deterrence by alternative sanctions)

**MPD’s determination:** No lesser sanction would adequately deter Braddock or other members; a sanction short of removal would signal that this conduct was survivable. **Arbitrator’s finding:** MPD offered no evidence for its no-alternative-sanction conclusion beyond the deciding official’s assertion. The arbitrator found that a lengthy suspension would be severe—entailing lost pay, lost standing, and a permanent disciplinary record—and likely would have sufficed to deter Braddock and other members. He held MPD’s factor-twelve conclusion outside tolerable limits of reasonableness. (In re Arbitration Between Fraternal Ord. of Police/Metro. Police Dep't Lab. Comm. (Braddock Grievance) & D.C. Metro. Police Dep't, FMCS Case No. 24-51267, Grievance No. 24-0392 (Mar. 14, 2025) (Larrabee, Arb.) (braddock_arbitration_award.pdf), p. 2, ¶ 8.) The applied review standard was the same non-de-novo, tolerable-limits-of-reasonableness standard; the arbitrator found MPD’s conclusion outside those limits. (In re Arbitration Between Fraternal Ord. of Police/Metro. Police Dep't Lab. Comm. (Braddock Grievance) & D.C. Metro. Police Dep't, FMCS Case No. 24-51267, Grievance No. 24-0392 (Mar. 14, 2025) (Larrabee, Arb.) (braddock_arbitration_award.pdf), p. 2, ¶¶ 5, 8.)

### Factors left undisturbed and resulting penalty

The arbitrator left **factors one through five and seven through eleven** undisturbed, finding MPD weighed them within tolerable limits of reasonableness and that the findings were supported by the record. Thus, he disturbed only factors six and twelve. (In re Arbitration Between Fraternal Ord. of Police/Metro. Police Dep't Lab. Comm. (Braddock Grievance) & D.C. Metro. Police Dep't, FMCS Case No. 24-51267, Grievance No. 24-0392 (Mar. 14, 2025) (Larrabee, Arb.) (braddock_arbitration_award.pdf), p. 2, ¶ 6.)

As a consequence of the two factor reversals, the arbitrator weighed the remaining ten factors against the failures on factors six and twelve and substituted a 45-day suspension without pay for termination. (In re Arbitration Between Fraternal Ord. of Police/Metro. Police Dep't Lab. Comm. (Braddock Grievance) & D.C. Metro. Police Dep't, FMCS Case No. 24-51267, Grievance No. 24-0392 (Mar. 14, 2025) (Larrabee, Arb.) (braddock_arbitration_award.pdf), p. 2, ¶ 9.) The Award affirmed the finding of cause, reduced the penalty, and ordered reinstatement and make-whole relief for back pay and benefits from June 21, 2024, less the 45-day suspension and interim earnings. (In re Arbitration Between Fraternal Ord. of Police/Metro. Police Dep't Lab. Comm. (Braddock Grievance) & D.C. Metro. Police Dep't, FMCS Case No. 24-51267, Grievance No. 24-0392 (Mar. 14, 2025) (Larrabee, Arb.) (braddock_arbitration_award.pdf), p. 3, ¶ 10.)
