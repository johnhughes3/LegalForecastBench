## Part 1: MPD's Penalty Determination as Set Out in the Final Notice

### A. Framework, charge, and decision

- **Charge.** MPD sustained one charge, Conduct Unbecoming an Officer under General Order 120.21, Attachment A, Part A-12. The charge was for using deadly force against an unarmed person without an objectively reasonable belief that anyone faced an imminent threat of death or serious bodily injury. The Deciding Official adopted the Use of Force Review Board's finding that Braddock acted unreasonably "from the outset." In her view, he did so by arming himself and confronting Mr. Pryor over a suspected property offense, and then by firing at a man he had no reason to believe was armed. She sustained the charge on a preponderance of the evidence. Final Notice, p. 2, ¶ 12.
- **Penalty framework.** The Deciding Official considered the twelve Douglas factors "as required by 6-B DCMR sec. 1606.2." Final Notice, p. 2, ¶ 13.
- **Decision.** She found that "all twelve Douglas factors, each of which aggravates the penalty," supported termination. She also found that "no lesser sanction serves the interests of the Department or the public." Termination took effect June 21, 2024. Final Notice, p. 4, ¶ 26.

### B. Factor-by-factor reconstruction

**Analyst observations, for later Steps:**

1. MPD labeled **no factor as mitigating**, not even factor 4, which on its face recites nine years of service with satisfactory ratings. Final Notice, p. 3, ¶ 17. Several "aggravating" labels go on factors that are neutral or that only confirm the penalty is permissible. Factor 7 shows only that termination is within the range. Final Notice, p. 3, ¶ 20. Factor 9 is notice. Factor 11 is an absence of mitigation. Final Notice, p. 3, ¶¶ 22, 24. Opposing counsel may describe this uniform labeling as mechanical, so we should anticipate that argument.
2. The factor 6 conclusion rests entirely on the Comparator Memo. That memo states that the Division "expresses no view on the appropriate penalty," and it was limited to 2018–2021 records. Comparator Memo, p. 1, ¶¶ 1, 6.
3. The factor 12 conclusion is supported by the Deciding Official's own reasoning, not by separate evidence. Final Notice, p. 4, ¶ 25.

## Part 2: How the Award Departs from the Final Notice

### A. The standard of review the arbitrator said he was applying

The arbitrator stated a single standard for the penalty. He said his review was "not de novo." Where the deciding official has weighed the Douglas factors under 6-B DCMR sec. 1606.2, he would ask "whether the Department's conclusions on those factors fall within tolerable limits of reasonableness" (citing Douglas, 5 M.S.P.R. at 306). If a conclusion on a material factor falls outside those limits, "the penalty determination cannot stand as issued." Award, p. 2, ¶ 5.

He applied a preponderance standard to the merits, at Award, p. 1, ¶ 4. He did not state any separate standard for choosing the substitute penalty, at Award, p. 2, ¶ 9.

### B. What the Award leaves intact

- **Facts.** The arbitrator found the material facts "not seriously disputed" and took them from the Final Notice (he cites ¶¶ 5–12). Award, p. 1, ¶ 3. He separately found that Braddock knew he had no police authority in Virginia and never saw a weapon. Award, p. 1, ¶ 3; accord Final Notice, p. 2, ¶¶ 7, 11.
- **Cause.** The charge was proven by a preponderance. A reasonable officer would not have perceived an imminent threat of death or serious bodily injury, and the finding of cause was "sustained." Award, p. 1, ¶ 4; p. 2, ¶ 10; accord Final Notice, p. 2, ¶ 12.
- **Douglas factors 1–5 and 7–11 (ten factors) were left undisturbed.** The arbitrator found MPD weighed them "within tolerable limits of reasonableness." The findings were "supported by the record and I do not disturb them." He specifically noted that the offense was grave, that Braddock is a sworn officer, that his record contains a prior sustained reprimand, and that the deciding official "reasonably found no mitigation." Award, p. 2, ¶ 6. He later referred to these as "the sustained findings on the remaining ten factors." Award, p. 2, ¶ 9.

### D. Record discrepancies inside the departures (to flag for the viability analysis)

1. **The on-duty count in the factor 6 finding conflicts with the Comparator Memo.** The Award says "[t]wo of the four" comparators involved on-duty conduct. Award, p. 2, ¶ 7. The memo shows **three** on-duty comparators:
   - Comparator A (Guzman) was on duty. Comparator Memo, p. 1, ¶ 2.
   - Comparator B (Sadler) was on duty. Comparator Memo, p. 1, ¶ 3.
   - Comparator D (Voss) was on duty. Comparator Memo, p. 1, ¶ 5.
   - Only Comparator C (Pietrzak) was off duty. Comparator Memo, p. 1, ¶ 4.

   The arbitrator's other descriptions are accurate:
   - The fatality case is Comparator C. Comparator Memo, p. 1, ¶ 4.
   - The officer with two prior sustained force complaints is Comparator A. Comparator Memo, p. 1, ¶ 2.

   Two comparators the arbitrator did not specifically address:
   - Comparator B was non-fatal, involved no firearm discharge, and the officer had no prior discipline. Comparator Memo, p. 1, ¶ 3.
   - Comparator D was non-fatal and had one prior sustained complaint. Comparator Memo, p. 1, ¶ 5.

   The off-duty comparator that involved a firearm discharge (C) is the one the arbitrator distinguished on the fatality.
2. **The factor 6 reasoning fits uneasily with the factor 3 finding left undisturbed.** In ¶ 7, the arbitrator calls the 2021 reprimand "a single unrelated reprimand" to distinguish the comparators. In ¶ 6, he leaves undisturbed MPD's treatment of that same reprimand as aggravating. Award, p. 2, ¶¶ 6–7; Final Notice, p. 3, ¶ 16.
3. **The factor 12 finding fits uneasily with the factors left undisturbed.** He sustained as reasonable the findings that MPD's confidence is lost (factor 5) and that rehabilitation potential is poor (factor 10). Award, p. 2, ¶ 6; Final Notice, p. 3, ¶¶ 18, 23. He then found that a suspension would likely deter Braddock himself, without reconciling the two. Award, p. 2, ¶ 8.
4. **The Award never describes the gap between the stated and applied standards.** It states a deferential "tolerable limits of reasonableness" standard in ¶ 5. It then places on MPD a burden to prove comparability in ¶ 7, requires evidence beyond the deciding official's reasoning in ¶ 8, and weighs and selects the penalty itself in ¶ 9. Award, p. 2, ¶¶ 5, 7–9.

### E. Summary

- **Disturbed:** Douglas factors **6** and **12** only. Award, p. 2, ¶¶ 7–8.
- **Undisturbed:** Douglas factors **1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9, 10, and 11**. Award, p. 2, ¶ 6.
- **Resulting departures:** termination was vacated and replaced by a 45-day unpaid suspension, with reinstatement and back pay. Award, p. 2, ¶¶ 9–10.
- **Unchanged:** the findings of fact and the finding of cause. Award, p. 1, ¶¶ 3–4.
