## Executive recommendation

The circuit court granted the petition for first-tier certiorari, quashed the County’s rezoning approval, and concluded that the Board’s process violated due process and did not satisfy airport-compatibility requirements. The order is operative now; it does not expressly remand the application for a new hearing or address the status of separate development or environmental permits.

If the Board wants to preserve Resolution Z-23-035, I recommend promptly authorizing the County Attorney to (1) file one focused motion for rehearing and clarification in the circuit court; (2) seek a stay if construction or permitting activity depends on the rezoning; and (3) prepare a second-tier certiorari petition to the Sixth District Court of Appeal. The strongest appellate points concern the circuit court’s treatment of the Board’s oral motion and prior public County actions as the Board’s final decision, without accounting for the Board’s written resolution, and its application of FAA advisory guidance as a mandatory zoning prohibition. The airport issue carries material risk because Lee County Land Development Code (LDC) § 34-1102 refers to FAA Advisory Circular (AC) 150/5200-33B “as amended, renumbered or replaced.” The second-tier petition must show a departure from clearly established law causing a miscarriage of justice; it cannot ask the district court to reweigh the zoning evidence.

The order’s caption identifies “Resolution Z-23-022,” while the supplied resolution and the application in the order concern Resolution Z-23-035. The motion should request correction and clarification of the order’s reach. The clerk’s docket date must be confirmed immediately because it controls rehearing and certiorari deadlines.

## 1. Order and case history

### A. County zoning action

Lee County applied to rezone approximately 112.21 acres from Agricultural (AG-2) to Community Facilities Planned Development (CFPD) for the Southeast Advanced Water Reclamation Facility. Resolution Z-23-035 authorizes an essential-services wastewater treatment facility, including up to 25,000 square feet of office, maintenance, and administrative buildings and capacity of up to 10 million gallons per day. The resolution states that the future land-use categories include Public Facilities, Density Reduction/Groundwater Resource, and Wetlands. It conditions development on a master concept plan and further County, state, and federal approvals. *See* Lee Cnty. Bd. of Cnty. Comm’rs, Res. No. Z-23-035 §§ A–C (Jan. 17, 2024).

The Lee County Zoning Hearing Examiner held a public hearing on October 19, 2023, and recommended approval with conditions. The Board held its hearing on January 17, 2024, adopted the Examiner’s recommendation and findings, and unanimously approved the resolution. The written findings state, among other things, that the project as conditioned complies with the Lee Plan and LDC, is compatible with existing and planned uses, will not adversely affect environmentally critical areas and natural resources, and that the deviations satisfy the planned-development criteria. *Id.* § E. The deviations allow a 4:1 lake-bank slope, no planted littoral shelf subject to water-quality conditions, and one means of ingress and egress. *Id.* § C.

### B. Certiorari proceeding and ruling

The circuit court’s October 25, 2025 order reports that the petition was timely, finds the petitioners had standing because their homes are across the street from the site, and states that it considered the petition, County response, reply, appendices, and oral argument held August 7, 2025. *Order Granting Pet. for Writ of Certiorari* at 1–2, *Spahn v. Lee County*, No. 24-CA-1479 (Fla. 20th Cir. Ct. Oct. 25, 2025) [hereinafter *Order*]. The order identifies three grounds:

1. **Extra-record decisionmaking.** The court concluded that the Board’s motion to approve—“Move the item based on the direction of the board over the last four years”—relied on matters outside the quasi-judicial record. It emphasized that the motion came after public comment and that the Board then voted without further discussion. *Id.* at 2–3.
2. **Prejudgment or bias.** The court concluded that the County’s earlier project actions, including a prior rezoning process that ended in remand and a 2022 Kingston settlement depicting sewer infrastructure to the proposed site, prejudiced the process or created bias before the 2024 hearing. *Id.* at 3–5.
3. **Airport compatibility.** The court concluded that LDC § 34-1102 and Florida Statutes chapter 333 required consideration of current FAA airport-compatibility guidance, including the updated wildlife-attractant recommendations for wastewater facilities. It stated that the current FAA materials introduced by petitioners were “not fully considered.” *Id.* at 5–6.

The order’s opening paragraph says the rezoning is “QUASHED”; its concluding paragraph says the decision below “is REVERSED.” *Id.* at 1, 6. It does not expressly order a new hearing, direct a remand, or resolve the effect on separate permits or work already authorized under them.

## 2. Legal framework and implications

### A. Review standards

A quasi-judicial rezoning decision is reviewed in the circuit court by first-tier certiorari. The circuit court asks whether the local government afforded procedural due process, observed the essential requirements of law, and based its findings and judgment on competent substantial evidence. *City of Deerfield Beach v. Vaillant*, 419 So. 2d 624, 626 (Fla. 1982); *Bd. of Cnty. Comm’rs of Brevard Cnty. v. Snyder*, 627 So. 2d 469, 476 (Fla. 1993). The circuit court may not substitute its judgment for the Board’s or reweigh conflicting evidence. *Dusseau v. Metro. Dade Cnty. Bd. of Cnty. Comm’rs*, 794 So. 2d 1270, 1274–75 (Fla. 2001).

The next level is not an appeal as of right. The district court’s second-tier certiorari review is confined to whether the circuit court afforded due process and applied the correct law. *Miami-Dade Cnty. v. Omnipoint Holdings, Inc.*, 863 So. 2d 195, 198–99 (Fla. 2003); *Haines City Cmty. Dev. v. Heggs*, 658 So. 2d 523, 530 (Fla. 1995). Relief requires more than ordinary legal error: the circuit court must have violated a clearly established principle of law in a manner resulting in a miscarriage of justice. *Omnipoint*, 863 So. 2d at 199; *Custer Med. Ctr. v. United Auto. Ins. Co.*, 62 So. 3d 1086, 1092 (Fla. 2010). The Sixth District will review the circuit court’s order, not conduct another evidentiary review of the Board’s rezoning decision.

### B. Due process and the written resolution

A quasi-judicial zoning hearing must provide notice, an opportunity to be heard, and a decisionmaker who acts on the record. *Jennings v. Dade Cnty.*, 589 So. 2d 1337, 1340–42 (Fla. 3d DCA 1991). *Jennings* addresses proven ex parte contacts: proof of a contact may create a rebuttable presumption of prejudice. *Id.* at 1341–42. It does not make prior public planning or a County’s earlier policy decisions, without more, proof of an undisclosed communication or disqualifying bias. Attorney General Opinion 94-71 likewise concludes that a county may consider a rezoning request for county-owned property, while recommending that outside staff communications be disclosed and placed on the record to permit a response. Op. Att’y Gen. Fla. 94-71 (1994).

The County’s principal legal argument is that the resolution—not one commissioner’s statement—is the Board’s final decision. Resolution Z-23-035 expressly adopts the Hearing Examiner’s recommendation and substantive findings. A recent, closely analogous decision holds that a county commission speaks through its written resolution and that a reviewing court departs from the essential requirements of law by attributing to the commission an individual commissioner’s comment that the resolution did not adopt. *Hillsborough Cnty. v. G.L. Acquisitions Corp., Inc.*, 415 So. 3d 1205 (Fla. 2d DCA 2025) (granting second-tier certiorari and quashing circuit-court order in a rezoning matter). That decision is persuasive, not controlling, in the Sixth District, but it provides strong support for arguing that the circuit court should have analyzed the adopted resolution and the complete record rather than treat the motion alone as the Board’s final rationale.

The argument is not risk-free. The motion’s reference to “direction” over four years may support the circuit court’s concern if the full record establishes that the Board relied on undisclosed information or made its decision before the hearing. *Jennings* makes actual contacts and the opportunity to respond important. The County should obtain and review the complete record before asserting that the prior settlement, staff communications, and earlier proceedings were disclosed or immaterial.

### C. Airport compatibility and FAA guidance

As in effect January 1, 2025, Florida Statutes § 333.03(2) requires airport land-use-compatibility zoning regulations to address specified matters, including landfills, airport noise zones, and runway protection zones; subsection (6) permits more restrictive local airport zoning. Section 333.04(2) provides that the more stringent requirement prevails “[i]n the event of conflict” between airport zoning regulations adopted under chapter 333 and other regulations applicable to the same area. Fla. Stat. §§ 333.03(2), 333.03(6), 333.04(2) (2024). Those provisions do not themselves state a five-mile wastewater-treatment-facility prohibition.

The FAA’s AC 150/5200-33C recommends against new wastewater treatment facilities within specified separation distances and recommends discussion of location and mitigation where a facility falls within those distances. It also expressly states that the circular is not a regulation, is not mandatory, and is not legally binding in its own right. Fed. Aviation Admin., Advisory Circular No. 150/5200-33C, *Hazardous Wildlife Attractants on or Near Airports* § 2.3.4, at 2-7, and Application at ii (Feb. 21, 2020). LDC § 34-1102, however, identifies AC 150/5200-33B “as amended, renumbered or replaced” as part of the County’s airport-compatibility framework. Lee Cnty., Fla., Land Development Code § 34-1102 (as amended by Ordinance No. 19-03 (Apr. 2, 2019)). That language gives petitioners a substantial argument that the current circular was a relevant local zoning criterion even if the FAA circular is not independently binding federal law.

The second-tier argument should therefore be narrow: the circuit court should identify the specific mandatory provision and actual conflict it found, distinguish the state statute’s conflict rule from FAA recommendations, and account for the wording and scope of LDC § 34-1102. The order does not identify a particular statutory prohibition on this wastewater facility. The petition cites a five-mile FAA recommendation, while the applicant’s hearing presentation described the site as outside the 10,000-foot runway-based wildlife buffer after the planned runway expansion. Those distances use different reference points and should not be conflated; the full record and the circular’s criteria must be checked to determine the recommendation’s application to this site. The resolution does not expressly discuss AC 150/5200-33C or make a finding about the five-mile recommendation. Its conditions concerning airport-aware design and the absence of a littoral shelf may support the County’s position that airport compatibility was considered, but the full record is needed to assess that point.

### D. Immediate effect

Unless stayed or overturned, the order nullifies the County’s zoning approval. The pre-rezoning AG-2 zoning is therefore the operative zoning classification on the supplied record, subject to confirmation that no separate zoning action changed it. The order does not purport to amend the Lee Plan or adjudicate state or federal permits. Nor does it expressly enjoin construction. But County staff should not issue new approvals or continue work in reliance on the quashed resolution without confirming the authority for that work and obtaining counsel’s direction. The resolution itself makes clear that County permits do not substitute for required state and federal approvals. Res. No. Z-23-035 § B(7).

## 3. Options and available actions

1. **Move for rehearing and clarification in circuit court.** Florida Rule of Appellate Procedure 9.330 permits a timely, particularized motion identifying points of law or fact overlooked or misapprehended; a clarification request may identify ambiguity in the order. Fla. R. App. P. 9.330(a)(1)–(2) (2024). The motion should request correction of the resolution number, clarification whether the court quashed or reversed Z-23-035, and whether further proceedings are contemplated. On the merits, it should focus on the written resolution, the governing *Jennings* standard, and the distinction between FAA recommendations and mandatory law. It should not add new issues.
2. **Seek second-tier certiorari in the Sixth District.** The Sixth District has jurisdiction over Lee County matters from the Twentieth Judicial Circuit. A petition challenging the circuit court’s final appellate order may be filed under article V, section 4(b)(3) of the Florida Constitution and Florida Rules of Appellate Procedure 9.030(b)(2)(B) and 9.100(c)(1). This remedy is discretionary and exceptionally narrow. The petition should seek to quash the circuit court’s order and remand for application of the correct first-tier standards, or other relief the court deems appropriate.
3. **Request a stay pending review.** Neither a rehearing motion nor a certiorari petition automatically stays the order. A stay may be requested under Florida Rule of Appellate Procedure 9.310 (2024), first in the circuit court and, if denied, in the district court. Any request should identify the precise work or approvals affected and propose a workable status quo.
4. **Accept the order and pursue a new application or a different site/design.** If the County does not seek review, or review is denied, it may assess a materially revised proposal or a new quasi-judicial application. It should not attempt an administrative “reaffirmation” of the quashed resolution. Any new proceeding should build a complete record, disclose relevant contacts and prior materials, afford a meaningful opportunity to respond, and make explicit findings addressing LDC § 34-145(d), § 34-1102, airport compatibility, and the requested deviations.
5. **Further review after a Sixth District decision.** Review by the Florida Supreme Court is discretionary and available only on limited jurisdictional grounds, such as express and direct conflict or a certified question; it is not another plenary appeal. Fla. Const. art. V, § 3(b)(3)–(4); Fla. R. App. P. 9.030(a)(2) (2024).

## 4. Recommended course

Authorize the County Attorney to pursue rehearing/clarification and prepare a second-tier certiorari petition, while promptly verifying the circuit docket date and the project’s permit and construction status. The order identifies a final reversal/quash but contains a resolution-number discrepancy and no express remand instructions; clarification is warranted. On rehearing and, if necessary, in the Sixth District, lead with the strongest legal issue: the circuit court appears to have treated a commissioner’s motion and earlier County actions as the Board’s final decision without addressing the adopted written resolution. *G.L. Acquisitions* supports that argument, and *Jennings* requires a concrete due-process analysis tied to actual contacts and prejudice.

Present the airport issue as a statutory-interpretation question, not as a request for the district court to reweigh testimony. Acknowledge that the County Code incorporates updated FAA guidance and that the record may support the circuit court’s conclusion that the current guidance was not adequately addressed. The County should request a stay only if necessary to preserve a defined status quo and should not assume the quashed resolution remains an effective zoning authorization while review is pending.

The order is dated October 25, 2025, but under Rule 9.020(i) an appellate order is rendered when docketed. If docketed October 25, the 15-day rehearing period falls on Sunday, November 9, and extends to Monday, November 10, 2025; without a tolling motion, the 30-day certiorari period ends Monday, November 24, 2025. If the clerk docketed the order on October 27, the corresponding dates are November 12 and November 26 because November 11 is Veterans Day. Fla. R. App. P. 9.020(i), 9.330(a)(1), 9.420(e) (2024); Fla. R. Gen. Prac. & Jud. Admin. 2.514(a) (2024). A timely, authorized Rule 9.330 motion tolls rendition, but the County should file by the earlier dates unless the docket confirms otherwise.
