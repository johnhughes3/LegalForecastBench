## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

On October 25, 2025, Circuit Judge James Shenko granted the Petition for Writ of Certiorari filed by Peter Spahn, Marsha Ellis, and Dr. Serge Thomas. The court "QUASHED" (and, in its decretal paragraph, "REVERSED") the Board's January 17, 2024 quasi-judicial approval rezoning 112.21± acres on Alico Road from Agricultural (AG-2) to Community Facilities Planned Development (CFPD) for the Southeast Advanced Water Reclamation Facility (SEAWRF). The Order rests on three grounds: (I) the motion to approve, made "based on the direction of the board over the last four years," relied on matters outside the quasi-judicial record and showed bias, citing *Jennings v. Dade County*, 589 So. 2d 1337 (Fla. 3d DCA 1991); (II) prior County actions (the 2019 rezoning attempt, the Harper tract purchase, and the 2022 "Kingston" settlement) predetermined the outcome; and (III) the approval violated the essential requirements of law because FAA wildlife-hazard advisory circulars, allegedly incorporated through Land Development Code (LDC) § 34-1102 and § 333.04(2), Fla. Stat., were "not fully considered."

The practical effect is immediate and serious. The zoning approval for a facility on which construction began October 6, 2025 (per LCU's project page) no longer exists, and the site reverts to AG-2.

**Recommendation.** We recommend that the Board authorize the County Attorney to:

1. File a **petition for second-tier certiorari in the Sixth District Court of Appeal** within 30 days of rendition (conservatively, **no later than Monday, November 24, 2025**, measured from the October 25, 2025 signature date; we will confirm the clerk's filing date). The Order contains errors that second-tier certiorari is designed to correct:
   - **Remedy error.** The Order "reversed" the decision rather than only quashing it.
   - **Reweighing.** The court reweighed evidence instead of applying the competent-substantial-evidence standard.
   - **Wrong law.** The court treated non-binding FAA advisory guidance, and a purpose statement in the LDC, as mandatory law.
   - **Misapplied precedent.** The court relied on *Jennings*, which concerns ex parte contacts and has been modified by § 286.0115, Fla. Stat.
   - **Possible extra-record material.** The court may have relied on matters outside the quasi-judicial record.
2. **Move for a stay** of the Order pending review under Fla. R. App. P. 9.310(a). The automatic stay for public bodies likely does **not** apply to discretionary certiorari review.
3. **Pending a stay ruling, direct staff to suspend site work that depends on the quashed zoning,** and to inventory all permits, development orders, and contracts issued or let in reliance on Resolution Z-23-035.
4. **Prepare a parallel contingency:** a fresh, fully documented CFPD hearing that squarely addresses airport-compatibility and wildlife-hazard issues. The Board can use it if appellate relief is denied, or after the Sixth District acts.

### C. The Petition

The Petition invoked first-tier certiorari under Fla. Const. art. V, § 5(b) and Fla. R. App. P. 9.030(c)(3) and 9.100. It asked the court to quash or remand on two broad grounds.

1. **Due process.** The Petition alleged three things:
   - Leaving the 2019 case open until 2023 triggered the County's ex parte restrictions and "silenced" residents for four years.
   - Related actions occurred outside the Sunshine.
   - The motion relied on "the direction of the board over th[e] last 4 years."

   Pet. 14–22 (citing *Evergreen the Tree Treasurers of Charlotte Cnty., Inc. v. Charlotte Cnty. Bd. of Cnty. Comm'rs*, 810 So. 2d 526 (Fla. 2d DCA 2002)).
2. **Essential requirements of law.** The Petition alleged that the rezoning violated three bodies of rules:
   - LDC §§ 34-145(d), 34-411, 34-1101, 34-1102, and 10-473;
   - Chapters 163 and 333, Florida Statutes;
   - FAA Advisory Circulars 150/5190-4B and 150/5200-33C, which recommend against siting new wastewater treatment facilities within specified separations from airports, including a 5-mile recommendation.

   The Petition also challenged the flood modeling (which excluded Hurricanes Irma and Ian), wildlife (Florida panther), and groundwater impacts. Pet. 24–49.

Petitioners' standing was not contested. Order 2 n.1.

### D. The Order

The court found jurisdiction under Fla. Const. art. V, § 5(b) and Fla. R. App. P. 9.030(c)(2) and 9.100(c)(2), and found standing based on proximity and the nature and scale of the project. Order 1–2. It granted the petition on three grounds.

- **Ground I — Departure from the record / bias.** The motion "based on the direction of the board over the last four years" relied on facts "beyond the quasi-judicial record." With no further discussion, it "shows prejudice and bias for a pre-determined result." This was particularly so because the County was reviewing its own application. The court held the motion violated procedural due process under *Jennings*, 589 So. 2d 1337, citing also Op. Att'y Gen. Fla. 94-71 (1994). Order 2–3.
- **Ground II — Predetermination.** The court found that three prior County actions "created prejudicial presumptions" while DCI2018-10023 remained open, "thereby preventing public contact with their county commissioners":
  - the "premature[]" 2019 rezoning;
  - the purchase of the Harper conservation tract;
  - the 2022 Kingston settlement, which "depicted and presumptively chose this site."

  The court relied on the Reply Appendix and the Petitioners' Appendix (e.g., Reply App. V3 p. 335; V8 pp. 959–993). Order 3–5.
- **Ground III — Essential requirements of law.** The court found the application "did not comply with" LDC Division 12 (Airport Compatibility District), LDC § 34-1102, § 333.04(2), Fla. Stat., and the FAA wildlife-hazard advisories. Its reasoning was that the "most restrictive" provision applies, and that the current FAA advisories "introduced into the record by Petitioners were admittedly not fully considered," placing "public safety at risk." Order 5–6.

**Decretal language.** The introduction says the rezoning is "QUASHED." The decretal paragraph says the petition is "GRANTED and the decision below is REVERSED." Order 1, 6.

**Caption discrepancy.** The Order, like the Petition, identifies the decision as "Rezoning Resolution Z-23-022." The resolution actually adopted on January 17, 2024 is **Z-23-035**, and the Petition itself refers to "Zoning Action Z-23-035" in its body. Pet. 5.

## III. GOVERNING LEGAL FRAMEWORK

### A. First-Tier Certiorari

A quasi-judicial rezoning is reviewable by first-tier certiorari in circuit court. *Bd. of Cnty. Comm'rs of Brevard Cnty. v. Snyder*, 627 So. 2d 469, 474–75 (Fla. 1993). The circuit court's review is limited to three questions:
1. whether procedural due process was accorded;
2. whether the essential requirements of law were observed;
3. whether the findings and judgment are supported by competent substantial evidence.

*City of Deerfield Beach v. Vaillant*, 419 So. 2d 624, 626 (Fla. 1982); *Broward Cnty. v. G.B.V. Int'l, Ltd.*, 787 So. 2d 838, 843 (Fla. 2001).

The circuit court may not reweigh the evidence or substitute its judgment for the local government's. The question is only whether competent substantial evidence supports the decision, not whether evidence supports a contrary result. *Dusseau v. Metro. Dade Cnty. Bd. of Cnty. Comm'rs*, 794 So. 2d 1270, 1275–76 (Fla. 2001); *Fla. Power & Light Co. v. City of Dania*, 761 So. 2d 1089, 1093 (Fla. 2000).

A local government is not required to make express findings in a quasi-judicial rezoning, so long as the record supports the decision. *Snyder*, 627 So. 2d at 476.

**Remedy.** On first-tier certiorari the circuit court may only quash the order under review. It may not reverse, direct entry of a particular order, or make its own findings. *G.B.V.*, 787 So. 2d at 843–44. When an order is quashed, it "leaves the subject matter . . . pending before the tribunal . . . as if no order or judgment had been entered," with the parties free "to proceed further as they may be advised." *Id.* at 844 (quoting *Tamiami Trail Tours, Inc. v. R.R. Comm'n*, 174 So. 451, 454 (Fla. 1937)).

### B. Second-Tier Certiorari

**Jurisdiction and timing.** A district court of appeal has certiorari jurisdiction to review final orders of circuit courts acting in their review capacity. Fla. R. App. P. 9.030(b)(2)(B). The petition must be filed within 30 days of rendition. Fla. R. App. P. 9.100(c)(1). Rendition occurs when a signed, written order is filed with the clerk. Fla. R. App. P. 9.020(h).

**Forum.** Lee County (Twentieth Judicial Circuit) lies within the Sixth District Court of Appeal. § 35.044, Fla. Stat. (2024).

**Scope of review.** Review is limited to two questions:
1. whether the circuit court afforded procedural due process;
2. whether it applied the correct law.

The latter is equivalent to a departure from the essential requirements of law: a violation of a clearly established principle of law resulting in a miscarriage of justice. *Haines City Cmty. Dev. v. Heggs*, 658 So. 2d 523, 530 (Fla. 1995); *Custer Med. Ctr. v. United Auto. Ins. Co.*, 62 So. 3d 1086, 1092–93 (Fla. 2010).

**Errors recognized as correctable.** The Florida Supreme Court has held that a circuit court "applies the wrong law" when it:
- reweighs evidence rather than applying the competent-substantial-evidence test, *Dusseau*, 794 So. 2d at 1276; *Fla. Power & Light*, 761 So. 2d at 1093;
- exceeds the permissible certiorari remedy, *G.B.V.*, 787 So. 2d at 843–46.

See also *Miami-Dade Cnty. v. Omnipoint Holdings, Inc.*, 863 So. 2d 195, 199 (Fla. 2003) (circuit court departs from the essential requirements of law by reweighing the evidence and substituting its judgment for that of the local board).

**Limits.** Second-tier review is discretionary and narrow. A DCA will not grant the writ merely because it disagrees with the circuit court's reading of the record, or for a simple legal error that does not violate clearly established law. *Heggs*, 658 So. 2d at 530–31; *Custer*, 62 So. 3d at 1092–93.

### C. Ex Parte Communications and Quasi-Judicial Decision-Making

*Jennings v. Dade County*, 589 So. 2d 1337, 1340–41 (Fla. 3d DCA 1991), held three things:
- ex parte communications with a quasi-judicial decision-maker are presumptively prejudicial;
- a party may be entitled to a new hearing if prejudice is not rebutted;
- the challenger must prove the communication occurred and its substance.

The Legislature responded with § 286.0115, Fla. Stat. Under it, a local government may adopt an ordinance or resolution under which communications are not presumed prejudicial if the subject and substance are disclosed on the record before final action. § 286.0115(1)(a)–(c), Fla. Stat. (2024).

*Jennings* concerns undisclosed **communications** received outside the hearing. It does not address a commissioner's reference to adopted County policy, or to the County's own infrastructure planning, when making a motion.

### D. Airport Compatibility Law

Chapter 333 requires political subdivisions to adopt airport protection and airport land use compatibility zoning regulations. § 333.03(1)–(2), Fla. Stat. (2024). The statutory land-use-compatibility minimums do two relevant things:
- They **prohibit new landfills** within 10,000 feet of runways used by turbine aircraft and 5,000 feet of runways used only by nonturbine aircraft. § 333.03(2)(a).
- They address noise-contour incompatibilities and runway protection zones. § 333.03(2)(c)–(e).

The statute does not prohibit wastewater treatment facilities at any distance.

Section 333.04(2) provides that when "airport zoning regulations adopted under this chapter" conflict with other regulations, the more stringent governs. That is a conflict rule. It does not independently adopt FAA advisory circulars as binding law.

**The LDC provision.** LDC § 34-1102 is the purpose clause of the Airport Compatibility District. As quoted in the Petition, it states that the Division's purpose is to establish protections "in accordance with" Chapters 330 and 333, 14 C.F.R. parts 77, 150, and 151, and FAA Advisory Circulars 150/5300-13A and 150/5200-33B, "as amended, renumbered or replaced." Pet. 31–32, 39.

**The FAA circulars.** As quoted in the Petition, the circulars are framed as **recommendations**:
- "The FAA recommends against the construction of new wastewater treatment facilities . . . within the separations . . . ."
- "[T]he FAA recommends a distance of 5 miles . . . ."
- When a facility is proposed within the separations, the parties "should discuss" the location and, "[i]f possible," identify a more suitable site.

Pet. 35, 37–38 (quoting FAA Advisory Circular 150/5200-33C §§ 1.4, 2.3.4 (Feb. 21, 2020)).

## IV. ANALYSIS: IMPLICATIONS OF THE ORDER

### A. Immediate Legal Effect

1. **The zoning approval is void as of now, and the site is again AG-2.** Unless and until it is stayed or quashed by the Sixth District, the Order eliminates the CFPD approval. Under *G.B.V.*, quashal leaves the application pending "as if no order . . . had been entered." 787 So. 2d at 844. The property reverts to its prior AG-2 classification, and the CFPD conditions, deviations, and master concept plan no longer authorize anything.
2. **Development orders and permits are exposed.** Any local development order, vegetation removal permit, or other County approval that depends on the CFPD zoning is now vulnerable to challenge. Resolution Z-23-035 required development orders to be "substantially consistent" with the CFPD's master concept plan and conditions, which no longer exist. Res. Z-23-035, § B.1.
3. **Ongoing construction carries legal risk.** Site clearing began October 6, 2025, and fill work is scheduled for Winter–Summer 2026. LCU Project Detail 1–2. Continuing zoning-dependent work without a stay invites several consequences:
   - an emergency motion by Petitioners to enforce the writ;
   - code-consistency challenges;
   - injunctive relief;
   - if the Order is ultimately sustained, an argument that the County acted in disregard of a court order.

   Continued work also increases sunk costs if the site cannot be used.
4. **The automatic stay probably does not apply.** Rule 9.310(b)(2) provides an automatic stay when a public body files a "notice" seeking review. Courts have held that this automatic stay does not apply when a public body seeks discretionary review, such as certiorari. A stay must instead be sought from the lower tribunal under Rule 9.310(a). *State, Dep't of Health & Rehab. Servs. v. E.D.S. Fed. Corp.*, 622 So. 2d 90, 91 (Fla. 1st DCA 1993). The County should not assume it is automatically protected.

### B. Ambiguities and Defects in the Order

1. **"Quashed" versus "reversed."** The court lacked authority to "reverse." *G.B.V.*, 787 So. 2d at 843–44. Read with its introduction, the Order is best construed as a quashal. Even so, the "REVERSED" language invites Petitioners to argue that the approval was effectively denied on the merits and cannot be reconsidered. That reading is contrary to *G.B.V.* and *Tamiami Trail Tours*.
2. **Wrong resolution number.** The Order quashes "Rezoning Resolution Z-23-022," but the approval is Resolution Z-23-035. The Petition and the Order plainly concern DCI2023-00011 and the January 17, 2024 decision, so the error is very likely clerical. It should still be corrected, through clarification or in the appellate proceeding, to avoid confusion in title, permitting, and any future proceeding.
3. **Standard of review not applied.** The Order never states whether competent substantial evidence supports the Board's findings. It never addresses the evidence the Board adopted:
   - the Hearing Examiner's recommendation and the staff findings;
   - the Port Authority's lack of objection;
   - testimony that the site lies outside the Port Authority's 10,000-foot wildlife buffer measured from active operational areas, including the new runway (Pet. 36, quoting Tr1 pp. 226–27);
   - the bird-mitigation deviations (no littoral shelf, 4:1 slopes).

   Instead, the court makes its own environmental and safety observations, such as panther habitat, flow-ways, and that the decision "places public safety at risk." Order 2, 6. This is reweighing. *Dusseau*, 794 So. 2d at 1275–76.

### C. Assessment of Each Ground for Appellate Review

**Ground I (*Jennings* / "direction of the board").** This ground has **moderate-to-strong vulnerability** on second-tier review.
- **Wrong legal principle.** *Jennings* addresses undisclosed ex parte communications. The Order identifies no ex parte communication. It identifies only a commissioner's statement, made on the record at the public hearing, referencing prior Board direction and "the needs for the community." The Petition itself quotes the statement this way. Pet. 14.
- **Statutory modification.** To the extent any communication occurred, § 286.0115 removes the presumption of prejudice upon disclosure. That statute modified *Jennings*.
- **The Board decided on the record.** The adopted resolution recites that the Board considered "the recommendations of the staff, the Hearing Examiner, the documents on record and the testimony of all interested persons." It expressly adopts the Hearing Examiner's findings. Res. Z-23-035, recitals & § E. Under *Snyder*, the Board's decision stands if the record supports it. A commissioner's articulated rationale for a motion does not displace record findings. 627 So. 2d at 476.
- **No automatic bias.** That the County was both applicant and decision-maker does not by itself establish bias. Local governments routinely act on their own public-facility applications through the quasi-judicial process. We are not aware of clearly established law to the contrary. The Order cites none beyond *Jennings* and an Attorney General opinion. AGO opinions are persuasive only.
- **Risk.** A DCA could view the circuit court's ruling as a due-process determination within its first-tier authority, and decline relief as mere legal error. *Heggs*, 658 So. 2d at 530–31. The phrase "direction of the board over the last four years" is also not ideal, because it can be read as reliance on non-record policy decisions.

**Ground II (Predetermination: the 2019 case, the Harper purchase, the Kingston settlement).** This ground has **moderate vulnerability**, which may increase depending on the record.
- **Record limitation.** Certiorari review is confined to the record before the local tribunal. The Order relies heavily on "Reply APP" materials (Kingston settlement Exhibit K; Reply App. V3 p. 335; V8 pp. 959–993). If these materials were not presented at the October 19, 2023 or January 17, 2024 hearings, the circuit court considered matters outside the record. That is a departure from the essential requirements of law. **The record must be checked.**
- **Planning is not prejudgment.** The County's capital planning, property acquisition, and a developer settlement acknowledging that "[t]he County is designing and planning to construct" a facility (Order 4) are legislative and proprietary activities. The Kingston language, as quoted, does not purport to bind the Board's quasi-judicial zoning decision.
- **Contrary outcome.** Treating a local government's infrastructure planning as disqualifying would effectively bar any government from rezoning its own sites for public facilities.
- **The ex parte "silencing" theory.** The Order's theory that the open 2019 case prevented "public contact with their county commissioners" is in tension with § 286.0115. That statute allows communications, with disclosure. The theory also does not explain how Petitioners were prejudiced at the 2023–2024 hearings, where they participated, testified, and presented evidence (Tr1; Tr2).

**Ground III (FAA advisories / LDC § 34-1102 / § 333.04(2)).** This is **the strongest ground for appellate relief**.
- **The FAA circulars are not mandatory.** By their own terms, as quoted in the Petition, they are recommendations. Pet. 35, 37–38.
- **LDC § 34-1102 is a purpose clause.** It is not a substantive siting prohibition.
- **Section 333.04(2) is a conflict rule.** It applies only to "airport zoning regulations adopted under this chapter." It does not convert FAA guidance into binding local law.
- **The statute does not reach wastewater facilities.** Chapter 333's mandatory land-use-compatibility minimum on wildlife attractants is limited to **landfills**. § 333.03(2)(a)–(b).
- **The court never found a violation of a specific mandatory provision.** It did not identify any separation distance measured on this record. It found only that the advisories were "not fully considered." Order 6. Whether evidence was "fully considered" is a weight-of-the-evidence inquiry that the circuit court may not undertake. *Dusseau*, 794 So. 2d at 1276; *Omnipoint*, 863 So. 2d at 199.
- **Record evidence on the issue.** The record contains competent substantial evidence that:
  - the site lies outside the Port Authority's 10,000-foot buffer;
  - the Port Authority reviewed the application and had no objection;
  - the design incorporates bird-hazard mitigation.

  Pet. 36 (quoting Tr1 pp. 226–27).
- **Counterpoint.** Petitioners will argue that LDC § 34-1102's incorporation of AC 150/5200-33B "as amended, renumbered or replaced" made AC 150/5200-33C part of the LDC, and that the Board did not apply it. Even under that view, the circular is advisory ("recommends," "should," "if possible"). Before filing, we should also confirm whether the operative provisions of LDC Division 12 (beyond § 34-1102) impose any mandatory wastewater-plant separation for the site's location relative to Southwest Florida International Airport (RSW).

**Remedy ("REVERSED").** This is a **clear error** under *G.B.V.*, 787 So. 2d at 843–46. Standing alone it might yield only a correction to "quashed." Combined with the merits errors, it strengthens the petition and protects the County's ability to rehear the application.

### D. Broader Implications

- **Infrastructure and service commitments.** Per LCU, the SEAWRF responds to capacity limits at the Three Oaks facility and growth in the Corkscrew/Alico corridor. LCU Project Detail 4. The Kingston settlement, as quoted in the Order, represents that the County is planning a minimum 10 MGD facility on Alico Road to provide capacity for that development. Order 4. Delay could affect:
  - wastewater capacity for approved development;
  - County obligations under settlement and developer agreements;
  - coordination with the Alico Road expansion;
  - construction contract costs, including delay and suspension claims.
- **Precedential effect.** A circuit court certiorari order is not binding precedent, but it will be cited by challengers of other County quasi-judicial approvals. This is especially likely for its language about commissioners' motions, prior Board policy, and incorporation of FAA advisories through LDC § 34-1102. Those holdings could affect other County and Port Authority-area land use decisions.
- **Litigation exposure.** Generally, no attorney's fees are awarded in first- or second-tier certiorari absent a statutory or contractual basis. The principal exposure is delay and construction cost, not fee liability.

## V. OPTIONS AND AVAILABLE ACTIONS

### Option 1 — Second-Tier Certiorari to the Sixth DCA (with a Motion for Stay)

**Mechanics:**
- File a petition under Fla. R. App. P. 9.030(b)(2)(B) and 9.100 within 30 days of rendition. Conservatively, that is **November 24, 2025**, if the Order was filed with the clerk on October 25, 2025.
- Raise four issues:
  - (a) the circuit court applied the wrong law by reweighing evidence and failing to apply the competent-substantial-evidence standard;
  - (b) it treated FAA advisory guidance and an LDC purpose clause as mandatory law;
  - (c) it misapplied *Jennings* and ignored § 286.0115;
  - (d) it exceeded its certiorari remedy by "reversing," and, if the record confirms it, relied on matters outside the record.
- Concurrently move the circuit court for a stay under Rule 9.310(a). If the stay is denied, seek review by motion in the Sixth DCA under Rule 9.310(f).

**Advantages:**
- Preserves the existing approval and record.
- Addresses errors that are well recognized as correctable.
- Could restore the zoning without a new hearing.
- At minimum, corrects the "reversed" language and protects the right to rehear.

**Disadvantages:**
- Review is discretionary and narrow. *Heggs*, 658 So. 2d at 530–31.
- Resolution may take months.
- Without a stay, construction remains legally exposed.
- Further review in the Florida Supreme Court is very limited.

### Option 2 — Motion for Rehearing and/or Clarification in the Circuit Court

**Mechanics:** File within 15 days of rendition under Fla. R. App. P. 9.330(a). Conservatively, that is **Monday, November 10, 2025**, because the fifteenth day falls on a Sunday. The motion would target:
- the "reversed" versus "quashed" inconsistency;
- the incorrect resolution number;
- any extra-record materials.

A timely, authorized motion tolls rendition, and with it the time for second-tier certiorari. Fla. R. App. P. 9.020(h)(1).

**Advantages:**
- Inexpensive.
- Could cure the remedy and caption defects quickly.
- Clarifies that the application remains pending under *G.B.V.*

**Disadvantages:**
- Rule 9.330 prohibits reargument of the merits, so it is unlikely to change the outcome.
- It delays final appellate resolution.
- If the court corrects the remedy, it removes one argument for appellate review.

### Option 3 — Accept the Order and Rehear the Application (Remand-Type Proceeding or New Application)

**Mechanics:** Under *G.B.V.*, the application remains pending as if no decision had been entered. 787 So. 2d at 844. The County could notice a new quasi-judicial hearing before the Hearing Examiner and the Board, or file a new CFPD application. The record should be supplemented with:
- an airport-compatibility and wildlife-hazard analysis expressly addressing AC 150/5200-33C;
- written Port Authority and, if appropriate, FAA coordination;
- separation measurements from RSW's aircraft operations areas;
- updated stormwater and flood analysis (addressing the Irma/Ian criticism);
- listed-species surveys;
- a complete alternatives analysis.

At the hearing:
- Commissioners should make disclosures under § 286.0115 and the County's ex parte procedures.
- Motions and findings should be expressly grounded in the record evidence and the LDC § 34-145(d) and § 34-411 criteria.
- Motions should not reference non-record "Board direction."

**Advantages:**
- Builds a stronger record.
- Directly cures the Order's stated concerns.
- Avoids appellate uncertainty.
- Can proceed in parallel with Option 1.

**Disadvantages:**
- Significant delay: new notice, two hearings, and likely another certiorari petition.
- Construction would have to pause.
- The "REVERSED" language may prompt Petitioners to argue res judicata unless it is clarified or corrected.
- The Board would need to address the County-as-applicant optics through scrupulous procedure.

### Option 4 — Legislative Alternatives

Possible approaches include:
- amending the Lee Plan or LDC, for example to clarify that FAA advisory circulars referenced in LDC § 34-1102 are guidance rather than mandatory standards;
- adopting express, objective siting standards for essential service facilities near RSW.

**Advantages:** Prospective clarity for this and future projects.

**Disadvantages:**
- Does not reinstate Resolution Z-23-035.
- Legislative amendments cannot retroactively validate a quashed quasi-judicial approval.
- Amendments targeted at a pending dispute invite challenge.
- Requires coordination with the Port Authority and the Florida Department of Transportation (FDOT) aviation office. See § 333.03(3), Fla. Stat.

### Option 5 — Abandon or Relocate the Site

**Advantages:** Ends this litigation.

**Disadvantages:**
- Loss of the land investment and design work.
- Loss of construction already underway since October 6, 2025.
- Contract termination costs.
- A new site-selection process (with its own zoning and comprehensive plan approvals).
- Capacity shortfalls in the Corkscrew/Alico service area.
- Possible exposure under development and settlement agreements premised on an Alico Road facility.

This option is not warranted given the strength of the appellate arguments.

## VI. RECOMMENDATION

The County Attorney's Office recommends that the Board adopt a **dual-track strategy**.

1. **Authorize second-tier certiorari (Option 1).** Authorize the County Attorney to file a petition for writ of certiorari in the Sixth District Court of Appeal no later than November 24, 2025 (subject to confirmation of the rendition date). The petition would challenge the Order as applying the wrong law because it:
   - reweighed the evidence and failed to apply the competent-substantial-evidence standard, *Dusseau*, 794 So. 2d at 1275–76; *Omnipoint*, 863 So. 2d at 199;
   - treated non-mandatory FAA advisory circulars and LDC § 34-1102's purpose statement, through § 333.04(2), as binding siting prohibitions, contrary to the text of § 333.03(2) and the circulars themselves;
   - misapplied *Jennings* to an on-the-record statement, without regard to § 286.0115;
   - exceeded its certiorari authority by "reversing" the Board's decision, *G.B.V.*, 787 So. 2d at 843–46;
   - relied on matters outside the quasi-judicial record, if the record review confirms it.

   The petition should also seek correction of the resolution number.
2. **Seek a stay.** File a motion in the circuit court under Rule 9.310(a) for a stay of the Order pending review, and seek Sixth DCA review under Rule 9.310(f) if it is denied. Do not rely on the Rule 9.310(b)(2) automatic stay. *E.D.S. Fed. Corp.*, 622 So. 2d at 91.
3. **Manage construction risk now.** Pending a stay ruling, direct the County Manager and LCU to suspend zoning-dependent site work at the SEAWRF site, consistent with contract suspension provisions. Direct staff to:
   - inventory all development orders, permits, and contracts issued in reliance on Resolution Z-23-035;
   - quantify delay costs;
   - notify affected contractors and parties to developer and settlement agreements as their agreements require.

   This reduces the risk of enforcement motions and of cost escalation if appellate relief is denied.
4. **Prepare a contingency rehearing (Option 3) in parallel.** Direct staff to begin supplementing the record now for a possible new quasi-judicial hearing, with:
   - an AC 150/5200-33C wildlife-hazard and separation analysis;
   - Port Authority (and, as appropriate, FAA) coordination letters;
   - updated stormwater and flood modeling, including extreme-event data;
   - updated listed-species surveys under LDC §§ 10-473 and 10-474;
   - a documented alternatives analysis.

   If the Sixth DCA denies relief, or quashes only the remedial portion of the Order, the Board can promptly rehear the application under *G.B.V.* At any rehearing, Board members should make full § 286.0115 disclosures and base motions and findings solely on record evidence and the applicable LDC criteria.
5. **Optional limited circuit-court motion.** If, after reviewing the record, appellate counsel concludes that the "REVERSED" language or the Z-23-022 caption error threatens permitting or a future rehearing, file a narrowly drawn motion for clarification under Rule 9.330 by November 10, 2025. Absent that concern, we recommend raising these defects in the Sixth DCA, to preserve the remedy argument and avoid delay.
6. **Closed session.** The Board may discuss litigation strategy and expenditures in a closed attorney-client session under § 286.011(8), Fla. Stat. Any final decision to file must be made at a public meeting.

**Bottom line.** The Order's most significant holding is Ground III. That holding converts FAA recommendations into mandatory law and substitutes the court's view of the evidence for the Board's. It is legally vulnerable. The remedy error is clear. Grounds I and II are weaker than they appear but carry more appellate risk because of the deferential second-tier standard. Second-tier certiorari, a prompt stay motion, interim suspension of zoning-dependent work, and a parallel record-strengthening contingency together best protect the County's investment, its service obligations, and its ability to lawfully complete the SEAWRF.
