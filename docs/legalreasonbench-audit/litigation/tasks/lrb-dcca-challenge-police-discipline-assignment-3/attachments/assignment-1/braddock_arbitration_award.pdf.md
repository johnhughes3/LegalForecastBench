IN THE MATTER OF ARBITRATION BETWEEN
FRATERNAL ORDER OF POLICE / METROPOLITAN POLICE DEPARTMENT 
LABOR COMMITTEE
(on behalf of Officer Colin R. Braddock, Grievant)
and
DISTRICT OF COLUMBIA METROPOLITAN POLICE DEPARTMENT
FMCS Case No. 24-51267 | Grievance No. 24-0392
OPINION AND AWARD
Kenneth J. Larrabee, Arbitrator
Issued: March 14, 2025
I. Appearances and Procedural History
1. The Fraternal Order of Police/Metropolitan Police Department Labor Committee (the  
Union) brought this grievance on behalf of Officer Colin R. Braddock under Article 12 of the  
parties’  collective  bargaining  agreement,  challenging  the  Department’s  May  22,  2024  Final 
Notice of Adverse Action terminating the Grievant effective June 21, 2024. The parties selected  
me from the FMCS panel. I held a hearing on November 13 and 14, 2024, in Washington, D.C.,  
at which both parties presented testimony and exhibits. Anita Kowalczyk, Esq., appeared for the  
Union. Devin Ross, Esq., appeared for the Department. The parties filed post-hearing briefs on  
January 17, 2025, and the record closed on that date.
II. Issue
2. The parties stipulated the issue as follows: Did the Department have cause to discipline  
the Grievant, and if so, is the penalty of termination appropriate? If not, what shall the remedy  
be?
III. Findings on the Merits
3. The material facts are not seriously disputed and are set out in the Final Notice at  
paragraphs 5 through 12. On October 3, 2023, the off-duty Grievant armed himself, confronted  
an unarmed man named Devon Pryor beside his personal vehicle in Virginia, and fired two  
rounds  into  Mr.  Pryor’s  chest  and  shoulder  after  Mr.  Pryor,  dazed  from  striking  his  head, 
stumbled toward him with his hands in his jacket pockets. The Grievant knew he held no police  
authority in Virginia. He never saw a weapon.
4. On this record, the Department proved the charge of Conduct Unbecoming by a  
preponderance of the evidence. A reasonable officer in the Grievant’s position would not have  
perceived an imminent threat of death or serious bodily injury. The finding of cause is sustained.
IV. The Penalty

5. The remaining question is whether termination is an appropriate penalty. My review of  
the Department’s penalty selection is not de novo. Where, as here, the deciding official has  
weighed the factors identified in Douglas v. Veterans Administration, 5 M.S.P.R. 280 (1981),  
pursuant to 6-B DCMR sec. 1606.2, I ask whether the Department’s conclusions on those factors  
fall within tolerable limits of reasonableness. Cf. Douglas, 5 M.S.P.R. at 306. If a conclusion on  
a material factor falls outside those limits, the penalty determination cannot stand as issued.
6.  I  find  that  the  Department  weighed  Douglas  factors  one  through  five  and  seven 
through eleven within tolerable limits of reasonableness. The offense was grave, the Grievant is a 
sworn  officer,  his  record  contains  a  prior  sustained  reprimand,  and  the  deciding  official 
reasonably found no mitigation. Those findings are supported by the record and I do not disturb  
them.
7.  Factor  six  stands  differently.  The  Department  relied  on  four  comparator  cases  to 
conclude  that  termination  is  consistent  with  the  discipline  imposed  on  similarly  situated 
members. Having reviewed the comparator summaries, I find that the Department did not prove  
that the comparator officers were similarly situated to the Grievant. Two of the four comparators  
involved on-duty conduct. One involved a fatality. One involved an officer with two prior  
sustained force complaints. The Grievant’s misconduct occurred off duty, caused no fatality, and  
followed a single unrelated reprimand. On this record the Department failed to carry its burden to 
prove  comparability,  and  its  conclusion  on  factor  six  falls  outside  tolerable  limits  of 
reasonableness.
8. Factor twelve also fails. The deciding official concluded that no alternative sanction  
would deter the Grievant or other members from similar misconduct, but the Department offered  
no  evidence  supporting  that  conclusion  beyond  the  deciding  official’s  assertion.  A  lengthy 
suspension is a severe sanction for a sworn officer, in lost pay, lost standing, and a permanent  
disciplinary record. On this record, a suspension would likely have sufficed to deter future  
misconduct by the Grievant and by other members of the force. The Department’s contrary  
conclusion on factor twelve falls outside tolerable limits of reasonableness.
9. Because the Department’s conclusions on two material Douglas factors fall outside  
tolerable  limits  of  reasonableness,  the  penalty  of  termination  cannot  stand.  Weighing  the 
sustained findings on the remaining ten factors against the failures on factors six and twelve, I  
conclude that a 45-day suspension without pay is the appropriate penalty.
V. Award
10. The grievance is sustained in part. The finding of cause is affirmed. The penalty of  
termination is reduced to a 45-day suspension without pay. The Department shall reinstate the  
Grievant to his position of record and make him whole for back pay and benefits from June 21,  
2024, less the 45-day suspension and less interim earnings, within 45 days of this Award. I retain  
jurisdiction for 90 days solely to resolve disputes over the remedy.
