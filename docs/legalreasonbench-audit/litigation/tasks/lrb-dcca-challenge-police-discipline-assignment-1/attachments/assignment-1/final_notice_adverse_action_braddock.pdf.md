GOVERNMENT OF THE DISTRICT OF COLUMBIA
METROPOLITAN POLICE DEPARTMENT
Professional Development Bureau
FINAL NOTICE OF ADVERSE ACTION
TO: Officer Colin R. Braddock, Badge No. 4172, Sixth District
FROM: Sandra M. Delgado, Assistant Chief, Professional Development Bureau (Deciding 
Official)
RE: Adverse Action Case No. IS-23-002841; UFRB Case No. 23-UF-0117
DATE: May 22, 2024
I. Procedural History
1. On October 4, 2023, the Department opened Internal Affairs Division investigation IS-
23-002841 into an off-duty firearm discharge by Officer Colin R. Braddock that occurred on the  
evening of October 3, 2023, at Officer Braddock’s residence in Fairfax County, Virginia. The  
Commonwealth’s Attorney for Fairfax County declined criminal prosecution on December 18,  
2023.
2. The Internal Affairs Division completed its investigation on January 26, 2024, and  
referred the matter to the Use of Force Review Board. On February 21, 2024, the Board found  
the discharge outside Department policy and recommended the charge set out below. Officer  
Braddock  received  a  Notice  of  Proposed  Adverse  Action  on  March  8,  2024,  proposing 
termination. He submitted a written response through counsel on March 29, 2024, and appeared  
with counsel at a hearing before me on April 24, 2024.
3.  I  have  reviewed  the  complete  investigative  file,  the  Board’s  findings,  Officer 
Braddock’s written and oral responses, and the disciplinary records identified in Part IV below.  
This Final Notice sets out my findings, my analysis of the relevant penalty factors, and my  
decision.
II. Findings of Fact
4. At approximately 9:40 p.m. on October 3, 2023, Officer Braddock was off duty at his  
residence in Fairfax County, Virginia, when his personal vehicle’s alarm activated. From his  
porch, Officer Braddock observed a man, later identified as Devon Pryor, crouched beside the  
vehicle.
5. Officer Braddock went inside his residence, retrieved his Department-issued service  
pistol, and returned to the porch. He did not call 9-1-1 or the Fairfax County Police Department  
at any point before the discharge.

6. Mr. Pryor had been walking home from a nearby residence and had dropped his phone  
under Officer Braddock’s vehicle. While reaching for the phone, Mr. Pryor struck his head on  
the vehicle’s frame, which set off the alarm.
7.  From  the  porch,  Officer  Braddock  identified  himself  as  a  Metropolitan  Police 
Department officer and ordered Mr. Pryor to show his hands. Officer Braddock admitted to  
Internal Affairs that he knew he had no police authority in Virginia and that he identified himself  
as an officer in an attempt to scare Mr. Pryor away.
8. When Mr. Pryor did not leave, Officer Braddock left the porch and advanced to within  
approximately six feet of Mr. Pryor with his service pistol drawn. He again identified himself as  
an officer and ordered Mr. Pryor to show his hands.
9. Mr. Pryor, dazed from striking his head, stumbled slowly toward Officer Braddock  
with his hands in his jacket pockets. Without giving any further warning, Officer Braddock fired  
two rounds, striking Mr. Pryor in the chest and left shoulder.
10. Mr. Pryor was unarmed. He survived after three surgeries and approximately eight  
months of treatment.
11. At no point did Officer Braddock observe a weapon, and he told Internal Affairs that  
he never saw anything in Mr. Pryor’s hands. The vehicle sustained no damage.
III. Charge and Specification
12. Charge No. 1: Conduct Unbecoming an Officer, in violation of General Order 120.21, 
Attachment A, Part A-12, in that Officer Braddock used deadly force against an unarmed person  
without an objectively reasonable belief that he or another person faced an imminent threat of  
death or serious bodily injury. The Use of Force Review Board found, and I agree, that Officer  
Braddock acted unreasonably from the outset by arming himself and confronting Mr. Pryor in  
response to a suspected property offense, and by discharging his firearm at a man he had no  
reason to believe was armed. The charge is sustained on the preponderance of the evidence.
IV. Analysis of the Douglas Factors
13. In selecting a penalty, I have considered the twelve factors identified in Douglas v.  
Veterans Administration, 5 M.S.P.R. 280 (1981), as required by 6-B DCMR sec. 1606.2. My  
findings on each factor follow.
14. Factor One (nature and seriousness of the offense). The misconduct is of the utmost  
seriousness.  Officer  Braddock  shot  an  unarmed  man  twice  at  close  range  in  response  to  a 
suspected property crime. The offense was intentional, involved his Department-issued weapon,  
and nearly cost a man his life. This factor is aggravating.

15. Factor Two (the employee’s job level and type of employment). Officer Braddock is a 
sworn law enforcement officer entrusted with the lawful use of force. The Department holds  
sworn members to the highest standard in precisely this area. This factor is aggravating.
16.  Factor  Three  (past  disciplinary  record).  Officer  Braddock  received  an  official 
reprimand in June 2021 (Case No. IS-21-001573) for discourteous conduct toward a member of  
the public during a traffic stop. His record is not clean. This factor is aggravating.
17.  Factor  Four  (past  work  record).  Officer  Braddock  has  served  nine  years  with 
performance ratings of Meets Expectations. His tenure did not prevent this misconduct, and his  
experience makes the lapse in judgment less excusable. This factor is aggravating.
18.  Factor  Five  (effect  on  ability  to  perform  and  on  supervisors’  confidence).  The 
Department cannot deploy an officer whose judgment in the use of deadly force has failed this  
gravely. My confidence, and that of his chain of command, is lost. This factor is aggravating.
19. Factor Six (consistency of the penalty with that imposed on other employees for the  
same or similar offenses). The Disciplinary Review Division identified four comparator cases  
from 2018 through 2021, summarized in the memorandum accompanying this Notice, in which  
sworn members who used unjustified force involving a firearm or serious bodily injury were  
terminated. Termination here is consistent with the discipline imposed on those similarly situated  
members. This factor is aggravating.
20. Factor Seven (consistency of the penalty with the applicable table of penalties). The  
Table of Offenses and Penalties Guide provides a penalty range for a first sustained charge of  
this gravity that extends through removal. Termination sits within the prescribed range. This  
factor is aggravating.
21.  Factor  Eight  (notoriety  of  the  offense  and  its  impact  on  the  reputation  of  the 
Department).  The  shooting  of  an  unarmed  man  by  an  off-duty  member,  in  a  neighboring 
jurisdiction where the member held no authority, received press coverage in both Virginia and  
the District and damaged public confidence in the Department. This factor is aggravating.
22.  Factor  Nine  (clarity  with  which  the  employee  was  on  notice  of  the  rules).  The 
Department’s use-of-force policy is the most heavily trained directive in the agency. Officer  
Braddock certified use-of-force training most recently in April 2023. He was plainly on notice.  
This factor is aggravating.
23. Factor Ten (potential for rehabilitation). Officer Braddock maintained at the hearing  
that his actions were justified. He accepts no responsibility for the shooting. I find his potential  
for rehabilitation poor. This factor is aggravating.
24. Factor Eleven (mitigating circumstances surrounding the offense). Officer Braddock  
faced no provocation. Mr. Pryor committed no crime, made no threat, and possessed no weapon.

Officer Braddock created the confrontation himself. I find no mitigating circumstances. This  
factor is aggravating.
25. Factor Twelve (adequacy and effectiveness of alternative sanctions to deter such  
conduct in the future). I have considered lesser penalties, including a suspension of significant  
length. No alternative sanction would adequately deter Officer Braddock or other members from  
similar misconduct. Discipline short of removal for shooting an unarmed, non-threatening man  
would signal to the workforce that such conduct is survivable. This factor is aggravating.
V. Decision
26. Having found the charge sustained and having weighed all twelve Douglas factors,  
each of which aggravates the penalty, I find that termination is the appropriate penalty and that  
no lesser sanction serves the interests of the Department or the public. Officer Braddock is  
terminated from the Metropolitan Police Department effective June 21, 2024.
27.  Officer  Braddock  may  grieve  this  action  in  accordance  with  Article  12  of  the 
collective  bargaining  agreement  between  the  Department  and  the  Fraternal  Order  of  
Police/Metropolitan Police Department Labor Committee.
