**PLAINTIFF’S COMPLAINT AT LAW**

Plaintiff, SARA DAVIS, Individually, and as Special Administrator of the Estate of BABY DAVIS, Deceased, by and through her attorneys, complains against Defendants JOHN SMITH, M.D.; DUPAGE HEALTH, S.C.; and SAFE HOSPITAL, and states as follows:

PARTIES

1.  Plaintiff SARA DAVIS is the mother and next of kin of BABY DAVIS, Deceased.

2.  SARA DAVIS was duly appointed Special Administrator of the Estate of BABY DAVIS by order of the Circuit Court of Cook County, Illinois, in Case No.

3.  Plaintiff brings this action individually to recover medical, hospital, funeral, burial, and related family expenses for which she became liable because of the injuries and death of BABY DAVIS.

4.  Plaintiff also brings this action in her representative capacity under the Illinois Wrongful Death Act, 740 ILCS 180/1 et seq., and the Illinois Survival Act, 755 ILCS 5/27-6.

5.  BABY DAVIS was a male infant who was born alive on August 24, 2024.

6.  Defendant JOHN SMITH, M.D. (“Dr. Smith”), was at all relevant times a physician licensed to practice medicine in the State of Illinois.

7.  At all relevant times, Dr. Smith undertook to provide medical care and treatment to SARA DAVIS and BABY DAVIS during the labor and delivery of BABY DAVIS.

8.  Defendant DUPAGE HEALTH, S.C. (“DuPage Health”), was at all relevant times an Illinois professional corporation or other business entity providing medical services through physicians, employees, agents, and apparent agents, including Dr. Smith.

9.  At all relevant times, Dr. Smith was an employee, agent, servant, representative, and/or apparent agent of DuPage Health and was acting within the course and scope of that relationship.

10. Defendant SAFE HOSPITAL (“Safe Hospital”) was at all relevant times a hospital or healthcare institution providing labor, delivery, nursing, medical, and related healthcare services through its employees, agents, servants, representatives, and apparent agents.

11. Safe Hospital provided the facilities, equipment, fetal-monitoring systems, nurses, technicians, physicians, and other personnel involved in the labor and delivery of BABY DAVIS.

12. At all relevant times, Safe Hospital’s nurses, employees, agents, servants, representatives, and apparent agents were acting within the course and scope of their employment or agency.

13. On information and belief, Safe Hospital held Dr. Smith out to the public and to SARA DAVIS as a physician authorized to provide labor and delivery services at Safe Hospital.

14. SARA DAVIS reasonably relied upon Safe Hospital to provide qualified physicians, nurses, and other healthcare professionals for her labor and the delivery of BABY DAVIS.

15. SARA DAVIS did not know, and did not have reason to know, of any limitation upon the relationship between Safe Hospital and Dr. Smith.

16. On or about August 24, 2024, SARA DAVIS presented to Safe Hospital for labor and delivery.

17. Dr. Smith and the nurses and other personnel at Safe Hospital undertook responsibility for monitoring and managing the labor and delivery.

18. During labor, BABY DAVIS exhibited signs of fetal distress that were detectable through appropriate fetal monitoring and assessment.

19. The signs of fetal distress required timely recognition, communication, evaluation, escalation, and intervention.

20. Dr. Smith and the nurses, employees, agents, and apparent agents of Safe Hospital failed to properly and continuously monitor the fetal heart-rate tracings and other signs of fetal well-being.

<!-- -->

21. Dr. Smith and the nurses, employees, agents, and apparent agents of Safe Hospital failed to timely recognize and appropriately respond to signs of fetal distress.

22. Dr. Smith and the nurses, employees, agents, and apparent agents of Safe Hospital failed to timely communicate the abnormal or concerning fetal-monitoring findings to the appropriate members of the labor and delivery team.

23. Dr. Smith failed to timely order and perform an expedited delivery of BABY DAVIS.

24. The delivery of BABY DAVIS was unreasonably delayed despite signs of fetal distress.

25. As a direct and proximate result of the delayed recognition and response to fetal distress and the delayed delivery, BABY DAVIS sustained severe and permanent injuries.

26. Following his birth, BABY DAVIS required extensive medical care and treatment.

27. On August 28, 2024, BABY DAVIS was transferred from Safe Hospital to Cook County Hospital.

28. Despite medical treatment, BABY DAVIS’s condition deteriorated.

29. BABY DAVIS died at Cook County Hospital on September 16, 2024.

30. The injuries and death of BABY DAVIS were a direct and proximate result of the acts and omissions of Defendants, individually and through their employees, agents, servants, representatives, and apparent agents.

COUNT I

SURVIVAL ACTION—JOHN SMITH, M.D.

31. Dr. Smith owed BABY DAVIS a duty to possess and apply the knowledge, skill, and care ordinarily used by reasonably well-qualified physicians providing labor and delivery care under the same or similar circumstances.

32. Dr. Smith breached the applicable standard of care in one or more of the following respects:

a\. Failed to adequately monitor the condition and well-being of BABY DAVIS during labor;

b\. Failed to properly review and interpret the fetal heart-rate tracings;

c\. Failed to timely recognize signs of fetal distress;

d\. Failed to timely and appropriately respond to signs of fetal distress;

e\. Failed to order additional assessment, intervention, or monitoring in response to abnormal or concerning findings;

f\. Failed to timely communicate the condition of BABY DAVIS to appropriate members of the labor and delivery team;

g\. Failed to timely escalate the care and treatment of SARA DAVIS and BABY DAVIS;

h\. Failed to timely order an expedited operative or other medically appropriate delivery;

i\. Unreasonably delayed the delivery of BABY DAVIS;

j\. Continued labor despite signs that BABY DAVIS required an expedited delivery;

k\. Failed to formulate and implement an appropriate labor and delivery plan; and

l\. Was otherwise careless and negligent in the care and treatment of SARA DAVIS and BABY DAVIS.

33. As a direct and proximate result of one or more of Dr. Smith’s negligent acts or omissions, BABY DAVIS sustained severe injuries and ultimately died.

34. Between the time of his injury and death, BABY DAVIS experienced pain and suffering, disability, loss of a normal life, and other legally compensable damages.

WHEREFORE, Plaintiff, SARA DAVIS, as Special Administrator of the Estate of BABY DAVIS, Deceased, requests judgment against Defendant JOHN SMITH, M.D., for compensatory damages in an amount to be determined at trial, together with costs and any other relief the Court deems just.

COUNT II

WRONGFUL DEATH—JOHN SMITH, M.D.

35. BABY DAVIS died on September 16, 2024, as a direct and proximate result of the negligent acts and omissions of Dr. Smith.

36. Had BABY DAVIS survived, he would have been entitled to maintain an action against Dr. Smith for the injuries caused by Dr. Smith’s negligence.

37. BABY DAVIS left surviving next of kin, including his mother, SARA DAVIS, and father, and siblings.

38. As a direct and proximate result of the death of BABY DAVIS, his surviving next of kin have suffered grief, sorrow, mental suffering, and the loss of BABY DAVIS’s love, society, companionship, affection, guidance, and relationship.

WHEREFORE, Plaintiff, SARA DAVIS, as Special Administrator of the Estate of BABY DAVIS, Deceased, requests judgment against Defendant JOHN SMITH, M.D., for wrongful-death damages in an amount to be determined at trial, together with costs and any other relief the Court deems just.

COUNT III

SURVIVAL ACTION—DUPAGE HEALTH, S.C.

39. At all relevant times, Dr. Smith was an employee, agent, servant, representative, and/or apparent agent of DuPage Health.

40. Dr. Smith was acting within the course and scope of his employment or agency with DuPage Health when he provided medical care and treatment to SARA DAVIS and BABY DAVIS.

41. DuPage Health is vicariously liable for the negligent acts and omissions of Dr. Smith under the doctrine of respondeat superior and the applicable principles of agency.

WRONGFUL DEATH—DUPAGE HEALTH, S.C.

45. Had BABY DAVIS survived, he would have been entitled to maintain an action against DuPage Health for the conduct of its employee, agent, servant, representative, and/or apparent agent.

REVIEWING PHYSICIAN’S WRITTEN REPORT  
PURSUANT TO 735 ILCS 5/2-622  
I am a physician licensed to practice medicine in all its branches in the State of Illinois. I am board certified in OBSTETRICS AND GYNECOLOGY/MATERNAL-FETAL MEDICINE and have actively practiced and/or taught in the field of obstetrics, including the management of labor, interpretation of electronic fetal-heart-rate monitoring, recognition and management of abnormal fetal status, and determination of when an expedited operative delivery is required, within the preceding six years.

I am knowledgeable concerning the medical issues presented in this action and am qualified by education, training, experience, and demonstrated competence to evaluate the care provided during the labor and delivery of BABY DAVIS.

In preparing this report, I reviewed the following available records and materials:

1.  The labor-and-delivery records of SARA DAVIS from SAFE HOSPITAL for August 24, 2024;

2.  The electronic fetal-heart-rate-monitoring tracings and contraction-monitoring records;

3.  The physician orders, progress notes, operative or delivery records, and medication-administration records;

4.  The nursing assessments, flowsheets, progress notes, and communications;

5.  The neonatal records for BABY DAVIS from SAFE HOSPITAL;

6.  The transfer records dated August 28, 2024;

7.  The records concerning BABY DAVIS’s treatment at Cook County Hospital from August 28, 2024 through September 16, 2024;

8.  The death certificate and any available autopsy, pathology, neurological, radiological, or diagnostic reports;

9.  Plaintiff’s Complaint at Law.

My opinions are based upon the materials available to me at this time. I reserve the right to supplement or modify my opinions if additional material information becomes available.

On August 24, 2024, SARA DAVIS presented to SAFE HOSPITAL for labor and delivery. JOHN SMITH, M.D., undertook responsibility for managing the labor and delivery and for assessing the condition and well-being of SARA DAVIS and BABY DAVIS.

The fetal-monitoring records demonstrated, DECELERATIONS, BRADYCARDIA, TACHYCARDIA, and CATEGORY II FEATURES.

These findings required timely physician review, clinical assessment, communication with the labor-and-delivery team, appropriate intrauterine-resuscitative measures, and a determination whether continued labor remained medically appropriate.

The available clinical information demonstrated expedited delivery. Despite these findings, an expedited delivery was not ordered.

At birth, BABY DAVIS demonstrated low APGAR scores, need for resuscitation, and neurological intervention.

BABY DAVIS subsequently required extensive medical care. On August 28, 2024, he was transferred to Cook County Hospital. His condition deteriorated, and he died on September 16, 2024.

A reasonably careful physician, medical group, and hospital, providing obstetrical and labor-and-delivery care under the same or similar circumstances was required to:

1.  Appropriately monitor and assess maternal and fetal well-being throughout labor;

2.  Timely review and correctly interpret the fetal-heart-rate tracings and other clinical information;

3.  Recognize abnormal, concerning, or nonreassuring fetal-monitoring findings;

4.  Personally evaluate the patient and fetus when the fetal-monitoring findings indicated possible fetal compromise;

5.  Order appropriate additional assessment, intervention, monitoring, and intrauterine-resuscitative measures;

6.  Timely communicate the fetal condition and the plan of care to the nurses and other members of the labor-and-delivery team;

7.  Escalate care when the fetal condition did not improve or when continued labor created an unreasonable risk of injury;

8.  Formulate and implement a safe and appropriate labor-and-delivery plan;

9.  Timely order an expedited operative or other medically appropriate delivery when the fetal status required delivery; and

10. Avoid exposing the fetus to an unreasonable duration of hypoxia, ischemia, acidemia, or other preventable physiological stress.

Based upon my review of the presently available records and materials, it is my opinion that there is a reasonable and meritorious basis to conclude that the physician, medical group, and hospital, departed from the applicable standard of care in one or more of the following respects:

1.  Failed to adequately monitor and assess the condition and well-being of BABY DAVIS during labor;

2.  Failed to timely and properly review the fetal-heart-rate tracings;

3.  Failed to correctly interpret the fetal-heart-rate tracings and related clinical findings;

4.  Failed to recognize, or failed to timely recognize, signs of abnormal fetal status or fetal distress;

5.  Failed to personally evaluate SARA DAVIS and BABY DAVIS in a timely manner after abnormal or concerning findings developed;

6.  Failed to order appropriate additional assessment, intervention, monitoring, or intrauterine-resuscitative measures;

7.  Failed to timely communicate an appropriate plan of care to the labor-and-delivery nurses and other healthcare providers;

8.  Failed to appropriately escalate the care and treatment of SARA DAVIS and BABY DAVIS;

9.  Continued labor after the available clinical findings demonstrated that continued labor presented an unreasonable risk to BABY DAVIS;

10. Failed to timely order an expedited operative or other medically appropriate delivery;

11. Unreasonably delayed the delivery of BABY DAVIS;

12. Failed to formulate and implement an appropriate labor-and-delivery plan; and

13. Otherwise failed to exercise the knowledge, skill, and care ordinarily exercised by reasonably well-qualified physicians providing obstetrical and labor-and-delivery care under the same or similar circumstances.

It is my opinion, to a reasonable degree of medical certainty, that one or more of the departures described above caused or contributed to an unreasonable delay in the delivery of BABY DAVIS.

A timely response and delivery would have avoided or materially reduced the duration and severity of the fetal compromise. The delay permitted BABY DAVIS to experience continued hypoxia/ischemia, resulting in hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy, neurological injury, organ injury, and ultimate death.

The injuries sustained during labor and delivery caused or contributed to BABY DAVIS’s subsequent medical condition, need for extensive treatment, transfer to Cook County Hospital, deterioration, and death on September 16, 2024.

After reviewing the available medical records and other relevant materials, I have determined that there is a reasonable and meritorious cause for filing and maintaining an action against Defendants JOHN SMITH, M.D., DUPAGE HEALTH, S.C. and SAFE HOSPTIAL.
