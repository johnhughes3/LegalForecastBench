PRESIDING JUSTICE HOFFMAN delivered the opinion of the court:  
The plainƟﬀ, KrisƟna Bradbury, ﬁled the instant wrongful death acƟon in the circuit court of Cook County.

On September 30, 1991, the plainƟﬀ was admiƩed to St. Mary's Hospital and treated by Bernard Ruder, 
now deceased, and defendants Roger Taylor and Patricia Podach, who were all employees of defendant 
Westwood Obstetrics and Gynecology, Ltd. On October 1, 1991, aŌer the plainƟﬀ was diagnosed as

At the Ɵme of his birth, Joseph was in respiratory distress and suﬀering from intraventricular

On September 27, 1993, the plainƟﬀ ﬁled the instant wrongful death acƟon in the circuit court of Cook

First, we address the denial of the defendants' moƟons to transfer by reason of improper venue. SecƟon 
2-101 of the Code provides in perƟnent part as follows:  
"Except as otherwise provided in this Act, every acƟon must be commenced (1) in the county of 
residence of any defendant who is joined in good faith and with probable cause for the purpose of 
obtaining a judgment against him or her and not solely for the purpose of ﬁxing venue in that county, or 
(2) in the county in which the transacƟon or some part thereof occurred out of which the cause of acƟon

In this case, the plainƟﬀ does not contend that any of the defendants are residents of Cook County. The 
plainƟﬀ argues, however, that because Joseph died in Cook County, venue is proper here because some 
part of the transacƟon out of which this acƟon arose occurred in Cook County.  
The defendants assert that because none of their alleged negligent acts or omissions occurred in Cook 
County, for venue purposes, the "transacƟon" out of which this cause of acƟon arose took place solely in 
Kankakee County. St. Mary's Hospital even goes so far as to contend that death is not an element of a 
cause of acƟon for wrongful death. We disagree.  
The Wrongful Death Act ( 740 ILCS 180/0.01 et seq. (West 1992)) provides a remedy to the surviving 
spouse and next of kin for their pecuniary injuries suﬀered as a consequence of a decedent's death. 
While it is true that the acƟon is premised upon the wrongful conduct of the defendant in breach of a 
duty owed to the decedent resulƟng in death (see Vaughn v. Granite City Steel Division of NaƟonal Steel 
Corp. (1991), 217 Ill. App.3d 46, 576 N.E.2d 874), the Wrongful Death Act does not provide for the 
survival of a right of acƟon the decedent had for damages he incurred. The Wrongful Death Act governs 
the right of the persons designated by the statute to recover for their own pecuniary injuries suﬀered as 
a consequence of the death of the decedent. ( Wyness v. Armstrong World Industries, Inc. (1989), 131 
Ill.2d 403, 546 N.E.2d 568.) Consequently, the acƟon accrues upon the decedent's death and provides a 
remedy for damages incurred as a result of the death itself. ( DeƩman-Brunsfeld v. Szanto (1994), 267 Ill. 
App.3d 1050, 642 N.E.2d 809.) Not only is death an element of such an acƟon, it is the last element 
necessary to render an actor liable for the pecuniary injuries suﬀered by the surviving spouse and next of 
kin.  
Albeit in the context of a jurisdicƟonal discussion, our supreme court has held that the place of a wrong 
is where the last event takes place which is necessary to render the actor liable. ( Gray v. American 
Radiator Standard Sanitary Corp. (1961), 22 Ill.2d 432, 176 N.E.2d 761.) In a wrongful death acƟon, that 
place is the place of the decedent's death. Because secƟon 2-101 provides that venue is proper in the 
county where some part of the transacƟon occurred out of which a cause arises, we believe that venue 
in this case could properly be ﬁxed in Cook County, the county in which plainƟﬀ's decedent died. For 
these reasons, we aﬃrm the trial court's denial of the defendants' moƟons brought pursuant to secƟon 
2-104 of the Code alleging improper venue of this acƟon in Cook County.  
We now address the propriety of the trial court's denial of the defendants' moƟon premised on the 
doctrine of forum non conveniens.  
The intrastate applicaƟon of the doctrine of forum non conveniens presupposes the existence of more 
than one proper venue within the State and focuses the inquiry upon the relaƟve convenience of the 
plainƟﬀ's chosen venue. ( Moore v. Chicago North Western TransportaƟon Co. (1983), 99 Ill.2d 73, 457 
N.E.2d 417.) The resoluƟon of a forum non conveniens moƟon requires the court to apply an uneven 
balancing test to various private interest factors aﬀecƟng the convenience of the liƟgants and public 
interest factors aﬀecƟng the administraƟon of the courts. ( Boner v. Peabody Coal Co. (1991), 142 Ill.2d 
523, 568 N.E.2d 883; Griﬃth v. Mitsubishi AircraŌ InternaƟonal, Inc. (1990), 136 Ill.2d 101, 554 N.E.2d 
209; McClain v. Illinois Central Gulf R.R. Co. (1988), 121 Ill.2d 278, 520 N.E.2d 368; Bland v. Norfolk 
Western Ry. Co. (1987), 116 Ill.2d 217, 506 N.E.2d 1291.) To jusƟfy the granƟng of a forum non 
conveniens moƟon, the defendant must meet its burden of establishing that the relevant factors for 
consideraƟon, viewed in their totality, strongly favor the suggested forum. ( Boner, 142 Ill.2d 523, 568 
N.E.2d 883.) Whether to grant or deny such a moƟon is a maƩer commiƩed to the sound discreƟon of

the trial court, the exercise of which will not be disturbed on appeal absent a clear abuse. Washington v. 
Illinois Power Co. (1991), 144 Ill.2d 395, 581 N.E.2d 644.  
Our supreme court has idenƟﬁed the relevant private and public interest factors that must be 
considered. The private interest factors include the relaƟve ease of access to sources of proof, the 
availability of compulsory process for aƩendance of unwilling witnesses, the cost of obtaining the 
aƩendance of willing witnesses, the possibility of the jury viewing the premises, pracƟcal problems that 
make the trial of a case easy, expediƟous, and inexpensive, and the advantages and obstacles to a fair 
trial. ( Boner, 142 Ill.2d 523, 568 N.E.2d 883; Griﬃth, 136 Ill.2d 101, 554 N.E.2d 209; Bland, 116 Ill.2d 217, 
506 N.E.2d 1291.) The public interest factors include the administraƟve diﬃculƟes ﬂowing from court 
congesƟon, the local interest in having localized controversies decided at home which requires a 
consideraƟon of the situs of the injury, the protecƟon of ﬁnite judicial resources, and the unfairness of 
burdening ciƟzens in an unrelated forum with jury duty. Boner, 142 Ill.2d 523, 568 N.E.2d 883; Griﬃth, 
136 Ill.2d 101, 554 N.E.2d 209; Bland, 116 Ill.2d 217, 506 N.E.2d 1291.  
AŌer balancing the private and public interests at issue, a court must take into consideraƟon the 
substanƟal right of a plainƟﬀ to select the forum in which she brings her acƟon. That choice must be 
given deference. The degree of deference to be given is determined by whether the plainƟﬀ is a resident 
of the selected forum. When a plainƟﬀ chooses her home forum, that choice is to be accorded 
substanƟal deference. ( McClain, 121 Ill.2d 278, 520 N.E.2d 368.) If the plainƟﬀ is foreign to the selected 
forum, her choice is given less deference. ( Griﬃth, 136 Ill.2d 101, 554 N.E.2d 209.) In a wrongful death 
acƟon, it is the decedent's residence at the Ɵme of death that is of signiﬁcance. Moore, 99 Ill.2d 73, 457 
N.E.2d 417.  
The record in this case is quite remarkable, not for what it contains, but rather, for what it does not 
contain. The defendants' forum non conveniens moƟon was not veriﬁed and it was not supported by any 
aﬃdavits. In their moƟon, the defendants asserted that: (1) Kankakee County is the situs of all of the 
negligent acts alleged to have been commiƩed by them, a fact which is not contested by the plainƟﬀ; (2) 
Kankakee County is the residence of all of the individual and corporate defendants, another fact which 
the plainƟﬀ does not contest; (3) the records of treatment of the plainƟﬀ's decedent relevant to the 
defendants' alleged acts of negligence are located in Kankakee County; (4) certain unnamed nurses 
employed by St. Mary's Hospital menƟoned in the plainƟﬀ's complaint are located in Kankakee County; 
(5) witnesses likely to be called at trial include unidenƟﬁed medical personnel who treated the plainƟﬀ's 
decedent in Kankakee County and these witnesses will ﬁnd it easier to travel to court in Kankakee 
County; and (6) the court docket in Cook County is crowded. The defendants conceded in their moƟon 
that the plainƟﬀ's decedent was transferred to a hospital in Chicago, that he died there, and that there 
may be medical witnesses from that hospital who reside in Cook County, but conclude that these 
"speculaƟve factors should not be given undue weight."  
For her part, the plainƟﬀ ﬁled an unveriﬁed response supported by a single aﬃdavit. In her response, the 
plainƟﬀ stated that aŌer the decedent was transferred to the University of Chicago Hospital he received 
three days of intensive care and treatment before his death. The aﬃdavit aƩached to the response is 
from a registered nurse employed by the law ﬁrm represenƟng the plainƟﬀ. The aﬃant stated that she 
reviewed the decedent's medical records from the University of Chicago Hospital and concluded that 13 
named doctors and nurses played signiﬁcant roles in the decedent's care and should be considered likely 
trial witnesses.

The transcript of the hearing on February 15, 1994, reveals that the enƟre argument before the trial 
court was devoted to the defendants' secƟon 2-104 moƟons. As to the forum non conveniens moƟon, 
the trial judge merely stated that he found the factors for consideraƟon to be relaƟvely equal between 
the plainƟﬀ's chosen venue in Cook County and the defendants' suggested venue in Kankakee County, 
and denied the defendants' moƟon in deference to the plainƟﬀ's choice of forum. Other than a 
concession by one of the defendants' aƩorneys that his clients made arrangements for the decedent to 
be transferred from St. Mary's Hospital to the University of Chicago Hospital, there is nothing further in 
this record either in support of or in opposiƟon to the forum non conveniens moƟon.  
As the scant facts in this record relate to the relevant private interest factors for consideraƟon, we can 
say only that it appears medical records relaƟve to the decedent are located in both Cook and Kankakee 
CounƟes and that process would be available in either county to compel the aƩendance of any unwilling 
Illinois resident witnesses pursuant to Supreme Court Rule 237 (134 Ill. 2d R. 237). Other than the 
obvious convenience to the defendants if they were to defend this acƟon in their home forum, they have 
failed to idenƟfy any nonparty witnesses from Kankakee County. Neither this court nor the trial court 
should be forced to reach a decision by speculaƟng as to the idenƟty, number, and locaƟon of witnesses 
that a party might call to tesƟfy. See Boner, 142 Ill. 2d at 533.  
The defendants argue that liƩle weight should be accorded to the plainƟﬀ's idenƟﬁcaƟon of 13 health 
care professionals from the University of Chicago Hospital as potenƟal trial witnesses. They support this 
argument by ciƟng to Bland (116 Ill. 2d at 227), which held that plainƟﬀs should not be allowed to 
frustrate the forum non conveniens principal by selecƟng a treaƟng physician in an inconvenient forum. 
We, however, fail to see how the holding in Bland is applicable to the facts of this case because it was the 
defendants that arranged for the plainƟﬀ's decedent to be transferred from St. Mary's Hospital in 
Kankakee to the University of Chicago Hospital in Cook County. Nothing in this record suggests that the 
decedent's treaƟng physicians in Cook County were chosen to establish a nexus to venue.  
Aside from the facts already noted, the parƟes have not idenƟﬁed any other pracƟcal problems that 
would make the trial of this case any easier, more expediƟous, or inexpensive in one venue over the 
other. Further, the parƟes have not idenƟﬁed any advantages or obstacles to a fair trial in either locaƟon.  
Turning to the public interest factors for consideraƟon, it appears that both Cook and Kankakee CounƟes 
have an interest in this liƟgaƟon. Kankakee County has an interest because the acƟon involves alleged 
acts of medical negligence occurring within its borders, and Cook County has an interest because the 
decedent died here. Because neither county can be said to be totally unrelated to this acƟon, we are 
unprepared to say that it would be unfair to burden the ciƟzens of either county with the obligaƟon of 
jury service in this maƩer. Also, while the defendants are correct that judicial noƟce may be taken of the 
staƟsƟcs issued by the Director of the AdministraƟve Oﬃce of the Illinois Courts in assessing court 
congesƟon ( Washington, 144 Ill.2d 395, 581 N.E.2d 644), we note that the defendants never requested 
that the trial court take noƟce of those staƟsƟcs and never quoted any such staƟsƟcs to either the trial 
court or to this court. (See Golden Rule Insurance Co. v. Manasherov (1990), 200 Ill. App.3d 961, 558 
N.E.2d 543.) Absent the parƟes' reliance upon the staƟsƟcs issued by the AdministraƟve Oﬃce, the trial 
court is in a much beƩer posiƟon than we are to assess the burden on its own docket that the trial of this 
case might pose. Hoﬀmeister v. K mart Corp. (1989), 181 Ill. App.3d 739, 537 N.E.2d 460.  
Lastly, and most importantly, we do not know the residence of the decedent at the Ɵme of his death to 
determine the level of deference that should be accorded to the plainƟﬀ's choice of forum. The
