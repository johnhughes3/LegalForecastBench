## O2: The 30-point separation-of-powers item cites the wrong constitutional article and rests on the same legislative-function error

Status: **problematic**; category: `legal_error`; criteria: states_in_the_second_assignment_that_the_circuit_court_s_erred_b.

The heaviest item in assignment 2 (+30 of 160) requires the brief to argue that the court violated 'the separation of powers under Fla. Const. art. III, § 3' by substituting its judgment 'in exercising its legislative functions.' Article III, § 3 governs sessions of the legislature. Florida's separation-of-powers clause is art. II, § 3, which is the text the rubric quotes and the article its URL anchor points to (A2S03). A brief that cites the correct article risks failing a literal check, and the criterion rewards a brief that cites the wrong one. The premise is also wrong: the Board's rezoning decision was quasi-judicial (Snyder), not a legislative function. The correct framing is that a court on first-tier certiorari cannot reweigh evidence or act as a 'super agency' (Dusseau; G.B.V.), which is a scope-of-review error under Heggs rather than a constitutional separation-of-powers violation between branches of state government. A careful appellate lawyer would present the Dusseau scope argument and would not cite art. III, § 3 or call the Board's rezoning legislative.
