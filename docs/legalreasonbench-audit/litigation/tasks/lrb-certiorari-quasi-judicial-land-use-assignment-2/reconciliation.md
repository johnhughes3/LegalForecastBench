## R1: The fairly-debatable criteria treat a quasi-judicial rezoning, and acts the court never reviewed, as legislative decisions

This consolidates Sol F2 and Opus O1. Two criteria (+10 each) require the brief to argue that the circuit court erred by not applying the 'fairly debatable' standard to 'the county's legislative decision to construct a water treatment plant on its own property' and to 'the county's legislative decision to enter into the Kingston Settlement.' The Order reviewed the January 17, 2024 decision 'to rezone property from Agriculture (AG-2) to Community Facilities Planned Development' for one 112-acre parcel, after hearings before the Hearing Examiner and the Board. Snyder holds that a board's legislative actions 'will be sustained as long as they are fairly debatable,' while its quasi-judicial rulings 'are subject to review by certiorari and will be upheld only if they are supported by substantial competent evidence.' Snyder treats parcel-specific rezonings as quasi-judicial. The circuit court did not adjudicate a decision 'to construct' or the 2022 Kingston Settlement. It cited them as evidence of predetermination. A careful appellate lawyer would argue that the Board's prior lawful acts cannot establish disqualifying bias in the quasi-judicial proceeding. That lawyer would not tell the Sixth DCA that the fairly-debatable standard governed, because doing so concedes the wrong framework and contradicts the brief's competent-substantial-evidence arguments (criteria 2-5). The criteria reward the legally mistaken framing and fail the correct one.
