**ORDER GRANTING PETITION FOR WRIT OF CERTIORARI**

THIS CAUSE having come for consideration upon the filing of a Petition for Writ of Certiorari. Pursuant to Article V, Section 5(b) of the Florida Constitution and Fla. R. App. P. 9.030(c)2 and 9.100(c)(2), Petitioners timely invoked this Court’s “first-tier” certiorari jurisdiction. Petitioners ask this Court to review the January 17, 2024, decision of the Lee County Board of County Commissioners (“Board”) to rezone property from Agriculture (AG-2) to Community Facilities Planned Development (“CFPD”) to construct a wastewater treatment plant on 112 acres to serve new private, large scale developments located along Alico and Corkscrew Road.

Having considered the Petition, Response, Reply, the Appendixes, having heard oral arguments of the parties at a hearing held on August 7, 2025, the applicable law, being otherwise duly advised, the Petition for Writ of Certiorari is hereby **GRANTED** and the rezoning is hereby **QUASHED** for the reasons discussed below.

## Page 2 (image2.jpg; printed page 2)

The court finds that petitioners have standing to bring this petition for writ of certiorari based on their proximity of Petitioners’ property (located across the street from the subject rezoning) and the nature and scale of the subject rezoning requested in relation to petitioners, petitioners’ property and petitioners’ homes and interests.¹ The subject parcel to be rezoned to public facilities for a wastewater treatment plant was part of the Wild Turkey Preserve, which was purchased as Conservation land under Lee County’s Conservation 20/20 program and (1) contains land that is habitat for the endangered Florida panther that exists on or near the subject parcel; (2) is within 5 miles of the Lee County Regional Southwest Florida International Airport subject to various restrictions on uses that could attract birds creating flight hazards; and (3) is also located on a strip of land located between two existing natural flow-ways that accommodate natural surface water flow during storm events. All of these matters were discussed in the quasi-judicial hearings held below by the Lee County Hearing Examiner and then the Lee County Board of County Commissioners (“BOCC”).

I. After taking testimony at the quasi-judicial hearing held by the Lee County Hearing Examiner, the Lee County Board of County Commissioners (“BOCC”) improperly departed from the record created at the quasi-judicial hearings held below and instead went outside the record basing the motion, and second to “*Move the item based on the direction of the board over the last four years*” the substance of which was not disclosed in the quasi-judicial hearing on this rezoning. R. App. Vol. 1, Bates 314-315. Approval of the rezoning based on the “last four years of board direction” exceeded the record created in the quasi-judicial proceeding, shows prejudice and bias for a pre-determined result.
