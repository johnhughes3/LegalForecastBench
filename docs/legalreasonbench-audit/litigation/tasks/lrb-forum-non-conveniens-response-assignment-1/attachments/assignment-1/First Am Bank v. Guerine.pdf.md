ANALYSIS 
 SecƟon 2-101 of the Code of Civil Procedure provides:  “every acƟon must be commenced (1) in the 
county of residence of any defendant who is joined in good faith and with probable cause for the 
purpose of obtaining a judgment against him or her and not solely for the purpose of ﬁxing venue in that 
county, or (2) in the county in which the transacƟon or some part thereof occurred out of which the 
cause of acƟon arose.”   735 ILCS 5/2-101 (West 2000).   Here, the plainƟﬀs ﬁled their complaint in Cook 
County, where Guerine resides.   Before the trial court, J.Q. Tex did not dispute that venue is proper in 
Cook County.   Rather, J.Q. Tex asserted, another venue is more appropriate than Cook County.   In 
ruling on a forum non conveniens moƟon, a trial court enjoys considerable discreƟon.  Peile, 163 Ill.2d 
at 336, 206 Ill.Dec. 179, 645 N.E.2d 184;  see Fender v. St. Louis Southwestern Ry. Co., 49 Ill.2d 1, 4, 273 
N.E.2d 353 (1971).   Accordingly, the sole issue before us is whether the trial court abused its discreƟon 
in granƟng J.Q. Tex's moƟon to transfer venue. 
 Forum non conveniens is an equitable doctrine “founded in consideraƟons of fundamental fairness 
and sensible and eﬀecƟve judicial administraƟon” (Adkins v. Chicago, Rock Island & Paciﬁc R.R. Co., 54 
Ill.2d 511, 514, 301 N.E.2d 729 (1973)), which allows a trial court to decline jurisdicƟon in the 
excepƟonal case where trial in another forum with proper jurisdicƟon and venue “would beƩer serve 
the ends of jusƟce” (Vinson v. Allstate, 144 Ill.2d 306, 310, 162 Ill.Dec. 43, 579 N.E.2d 857 (1991)).   
Although the forum non conveniens doctrine has a long history at common law, its general applicaƟon 
crystalized following the United State Supreme Court's landmark decision in Gulf Oil Corp. v. Gilbert, 330 
U.S. 501, 67 S.Ct. 839, 91 L.Ed. 1055 (1947).   The Gulf Oil Court stated: 
“The principle of forum non conveniens is simply that a court may resist imposiƟon upon its jurisdicƟon 
even when jurisdicƟon is authorized by the leƩer of a general venue statute.  * * * 
* * * 
Wisely, it has not been aƩempted to catalogue the circumstances which will jusƟfy or require either 
grant or denial of remedy.   The doctrine leaves much to the discreƟon of the court to which plainƟﬀ

resorts, and experience has not shown a judicial tendency to renounce one's own jurisdicƟon so strong 
as to result in many abuses. 
If the combinaƟon and weight of factors requisite to given results are diﬃcult to forecast or state, those 
to be considered are not diﬃcult to name.   An interest to be considered, and the one likely to be most 
pressed, is the private interest of the liƟgant.   Important consideraƟons are the relaƟve ease of access 
to sources of proof;  availability of compulsory process for aƩendance of unwilling, and the cost of 
obtaining aƩendance of willing, witnesses;  possibility of view of premises, if view would be appropriate 
to the acƟon;  and all other pracƟcal problems that make trial of a case easy, expediƟous and 
inexpensive.   There may also be quesƟons as to the enforcibility of a judgment if one is obtained.   The 
court will weigh relaƟve advantages and obstacles to fair trial.   It is oŌen said that the plainƟﬀ may not, 
by choice of an inconvenient forum, ‘vex,’ ‘harass,’ or ‘oppress' the defendant by inﬂicƟng upon him 
expense or trouble not necessary to his own right to pursue his remedy.   But unless the balance is 
strongly in favor of the defendant, the plainƟﬀ's choice of forum should rarely be disturbed. 
Factors of public interest also have place in applying the doctrine.   AdministraƟve diﬃculƟes follow for 
courts when liƟgaƟon is piled up in congested centers instead of being handled at its origin.   Jury duty is 
a burden that ought not to be imposed upon the people of a community which has no relaƟon to the 
ligaƟon.”  (Emphasis added.)  Gulf Oil, 330 U.S. at 507-08, 67 S.Ct. at 842-43, 91 L.Ed. at 1062. 
 Our cases subsequently have recast these factors.   In Illinois, the private interest factors include (1) 
the convenience of the parƟes;  (2) the relaƟve ease of access to sources of tesƟmonial, documentary, 
and real evidence;  and (3) all other pracƟcal problems that make trial of a case easy, expediƟous, and 
inexpensive-for example, the availability of compulsory process to secure aƩendance of unwilling 
witnesses, the cost to obtain aƩendance of willing witnesses, and the ability to view the premises (if 
appropriate).   See Griﬃth v. Mitsubishi AircraŌ InternaƟonal, Inc., 136 Ill.2d 101, 105-06, 143 Ill.Dec. 
274, 554 N.E.2d 209 (1990);  Bland v. Norfolk & Western Ry. Co., 116 Ill.2d 217, 224, 107 Ill.Dec. 236, 506 
N.E.2d 1291 (1987);  see also Adkins, 54 Ill.2d at 514, 301 N.E.2d 729 (these factors also may include the 
relaƟve capaciƟes of the two forums to provide a fair trial).   The public interest factors include (1) the 
interest in deciding localized controversies locally;  (2) the unfairness of imposing the expense of a trial 
and the burden of jury duty on residents of a county with liƩle connecƟon to the liƟgaƟon;  and (3) the 
administraƟve diﬃculƟes presented by adding further liƟgaƟon to court dockets in already congested 
fora.   See Griﬃth, 136 Ill.2d at 106, 143 Ill.Dec. 274, 554 N.E.2d 209.   Court congesƟon is a relaƟvely 
insigniﬁcant factor, especially where the record does not show the other forum would resolve the case 
more quickly.   See BrummeƩ v. Wepfer Marine, Inc., 111 Ill.2d 495, 503, 95 Ill.Dec. 841, 490 N.E.2d 694 
(1986) (“Courts should be extremely reluctant to dismiss a case from the forum rei gestae merely 
because that forum's docket has a backlog”).   These factors govern both interstate and intrastate 
transfers. 
 A further consideraƟon is the forum which the plainƟﬀ has chosen to ﬁle the complaint.   The plainƟﬀ 
has a substanƟal interest in choosing the forum where his rights will be vindicated, and the plainƟﬀ's 
forum choice should rarely be disturbed unless the other factors strongly favor transfer.  Griﬃth, 136 
Ill.2d at 106, 143 Ill.Dec. 274, 554 N.E.2d 209;  Jones v. Searle Laboratories, 93 Ill.2d 366, 372-73, 67 
Ill.Dec. 118, 444 N.E.2d 157 (1982), quoƟng Gulf Oil, 330 U.S. at 508-09, 67 S.Ct. at 843, 91 L.Ed. at 1062-
63;  see Restatement (Second) of Conﬂict of Laws § 84, Comment c (1971) (“since it is for the plainƟﬀ to 
choose the place of suit, his choice of a forum should not be disturbed except for weighty reasons”).

We acknowledge, however, that the plainƟﬀ's interest in choosing the forum receives somewhat less 
deference when neither the plainƟﬀ's residence nor the site of the accident or injury is located in the 
chosen forum.  Griﬃth, 136 Ill.2d at 106, 143 Ill.Dec. 274, 554 N.E.2d 209.  “ When the home forum is 
chosen, it is reasonable to assume that the choice is convenient.   When the plainƟﬀ is foreign to the 
forum chosen, however, this assumpƟon is much less reasonable and the plainƟﬀ's choice deserves less 
deference.”  Wieser v. Missouri Paciﬁc R.R. Co., 98 Ill.2d 359, 367, 74 Ill.Dec. 596, 456 N.E.2d 98 (1983); 
 accord Bland v. Norfolk & Western Ry. Co., 116 Ill.2d 217, 228, 107 Ill.Dec. 236, 506 N.E.2d 1291 (1987), 
quoƟng Piper AircraŌ Co. v. Reyno, 454 U.S. 235, 255-56, 102 S.Ct. 252, 266, 70 L.Ed.2d 419, 436 (1981); 
 but see Elling v. State Farm Mutual Automobile Insurance Co., 291 Ill.App.3d 311, 318, 225 Ill.Dec. 426, 
683 N.E.2d 929 (1997) (“[W]hile the deference to be accorded to a plainƟﬀ regarding his choice of forum 
is less when the plainƟﬀ chooses a forum other than where he resides * * * nonetheless the deference 
to be accorded is only less, as opposed to none” (emphases in original)).   Similarly, when the site of the 
accident or injury is chosen, the choice is convenient because the liƟgaƟon has the aspect of being 
“decided at home.”   See BrummeƩ, 111 Ill.2d at 500, 95 Ill.Dec. 841, 490 N.E.2d 694. 
 Contrary to our suggesƟon in Washington v. Illinois Power Co., 144 Ill.2d 395, 399, 163 Ill.Dec. 490, 581 
N.E.2d 644 (1991), the trial court, however, does not weigh the private factors against the public factors.   
Instead, the trial court must evaluate the total circumstances of the case in deciding whether the 
defendant has proven that the balance of factors strongly favors transfer.  Peile, 163 Ill.2d at 336-37, 206 
Ill.Dec. 179, 645 N.E.2d 184;  see Bland, 116 Ill.2d at 227, 107 Ill.Dec. 236, 506 N.E.2d 1291 (“ ‘If central 
emphasis were placed on any one factor, the forum non conveniens doctrine would lose much of the 
very ﬂexibility that makes it so valuable’ ”), quoƟng Piper AircraŌ, 454 U.S. at 249-50, 102 S.Ct. at 263, 70 
L.Ed.2d at 432.   The defendant must show that the plainƟﬀ's chosen forum is inconvenient to the 
defendant and another forum is more convenient to all parƟes.   See Hall v. CBI Industries, Inc., 264 
Ill.App.3d 299, 303, 201 Ill.Dec. 605, 636 N.E.2d 1037 (1994), ciƟng Kwasniewski v. Schaid, 153 Ill.2d 550, 
555, 180 Ill.Dec. 320, 607 N.E.2d 214 (1992).   The defendant cannot assert that the plainƟﬀ's chosen 
forum is inconvenient to the plainƟﬀ.   See Whirlpool Corp. v. Certain Underwriters at Lloyd's London, 
295 Ill.App.3d 828, 837, 230 Ill.Dec. 63, 692 N.E.2d 1229 (1998). 
Our last full discussion of the forum non conveniens doctrine came seven years ago.   In Peile, the 
plainƟﬀ was severely injured in an explosion at his Pike County home as he aƩempted to light his 
propane gas furnace.   Hours before the explosion, Skelgas, Inc., had delivered to the plainƟﬀ 500 
gallons of propane gas from its Pike County facility.   The plainƟﬀ ﬁled suit in Madison County against 
Skelgas, Inc., York InternaƟonal CorporaƟon, manufacturer of his furnace, and Honeywell, Inc., 
manufacturer of the control valve on his furnace, alleging design defects in the furnace.   The 
defendants ﬁled a forum non conveniens moƟon to transfer venue to Pike County, and the trial court 
granted the moƟon.   While the plainƟﬀ's lawsuit was pending in Pike County, he ﬁled an amended 
complaint adding addiƟonal negligence claims and joining ﬁve defendants, all propane gas suppliers or 
sellers. 
Two months before the scheduled trial date, the plainƟﬀ voluntarily dismissed his complaint and, eight 
months later, reﬁled his complaint in St. Clair County, approximately 100 miles from Pike County.   The 
reﬁled complaint named the same defendants as the amended complaint, but did not include York or 
Honeywell.   The defendants ﬁled another forum non conveniens moƟon to transfer venue, asking the 
trial court to send the case back to Pike County, but the trial court denied the moƟon.   The appellate

“While we are not insensiƟve to such concerns, we are not persuaded that abandonment of the

Ɵme consumed in its resoluƟon.   The doctrine that was acƟvated in Torres was designed to give the 
courts ‘discreƟonary power which should be exercised only in excepƟonal circumstances when it has

(Torres, 98 Ill.2d at 346, 74 Ill.Dec. 880, 456 N.E.2d 601, ciƟng Gulf Oil, 330 U.S. 501, 67 S.Ct. 839, 91 
L.Ed. 1055.)   In most instances, the plainƟﬀ's iniƟal choice of forum will prevail, provided venue is 
proper and the inconvenience factors aƩached to such forum do not greatly outweigh the plainƟﬀ's 
substanƟal right to try the case in the chosen forum.   If, however, the liƟgaƟon has no pracƟcal 
connecƟon to the forum, and a defendant establishes the necessary showing under the doctrine, the 
court should grant the moƟon for transfer.   We conclude that the forum non conveniens doctrine 
conƟnues to serve a valuable policy that the courts of this State are suﬃciently equipped to eﬀectuate.   
Accordingly, we decline the invitaƟon to abandon intrastate transfer of cases when appropriate, under 
the principles enunciated in Torres v. Walsh.”   Peile, 163 Ill.2d at 335-36, 206 Ill.Dec. 179, 645 N.E.2d 
184. 
 Since Peile, we have exercised our supervisory authority more than 30 Ɵmes to transfer cases away 
from plainƟﬀs' chosen fora.  (See appendix.)   Obviously, one of the purposes of the forum non 
conveniens doctrine-sensible and eﬀecƟve judicial administraƟon-is not being served by this protracted 
interlocutory liƟgaƟon over plainƟﬀs' forum choices.   The resources of this court are more proﬁtably 
spent deciding fully developed controversies than micromanaging errant forum rulings with 
nonprecedenƟal supervisory orders.   We urge our trial courts to give more careful consideraƟon to 
forum non conveniens moƟons and to leave a beƩer record of their analyses.   In turn, this will allow our 
appellate court to make informed decisions on Rule 306(a)(2) peƟƟons and to develop further a body of 
law to guide the trial courts.

See Torres, 98 Ill.2d at 351, 74 Ill.Dec. 880, 456 N.E.2d 601 (discouraging “the incessant jockeying for a

“The truth of the maƩer is that both plainƟﬀs' counsel and defendants' counsel are jockeying for

where he can recover the most money and the defendant is seeking a forum where it will have to pay

In Torres, the plainƟﬀs ﬁled a personal injury complaint in Cook County following a motor vehicle 
accident in Sangamon County.   The plainƟﬀs alleged that defendant Dale Palmer negligently operated 
his automobile and that the staﬀ at Springﬁeld Community Hospital negligently treated one of the 
plainƟﬀs.   The plainƟﬀs were residents of San Antonio, Texas;  Palmer was a resident of Sangamon 
County;  and the individual doctors and nurses named as defendants all pracƟced in Sangamon County.   
Humana of Illinois, Inc., which owed and operated the hospital, had a registered agent in Cook County.   
The defendants requested a venue transfer, and the trial court sent the case to Sangamon County.   The 
plainƟﬀs peƟƟoned this court for mandamus relief. 
We reviewed Gulf Oil and noted that courts in 18 other states have statutory authority to order 
intrastate venue transfers.  Torres, 98 Ill.2d at 345-47, 74 Ill.Dec. 880, 456 N.E.2d 601.   We further 
noted that at common law, English courts had the authority to remove a case to another county in order 
to obtain an imparƟal trial or to minimize the inconvenience of witnesses residing a great distance away 
from the chosen forum.  Torres, 98 Ill.2d at 347-48, 74 Ill.Dec. 880, 456 N.E.2d 601.   AŌer discussing 
our interstate forum non conveniens precedent, we concluded:  “When the doctrine of forum non 
conveniens is available in transferring a case from Springﬁeld, Illinois, to St. Louis, Missouri, we feel that 
a case should be able to be transferred from Chicago to Springﬁeld-two ciƟes in the same State-under 
the same theory.”  Torres, 98 Ill.2d at 350, 74 Ill.Dec. 880, 456 N.E.2d 601.   We reiterated the relevant 
factors for trial courts to consider, but we warned that if suﬃcient factors favor the plainƟﬀ's forum 
choice, “the defendant's inconvenience should not be considered, provided venue is proper.”  Torres, 98 
Ill.2d at 351, 74 Ill.Dec. 880, 456 N.E.2d 601.   Because we determined that the trial court had discreƟon 
to order an intrastate transfer, mandamus relief was inappropriate.  Torres, 98 Ill.2d at 353, 74 Ill.Dec. 
880, 456 N.E.2d 601.

Private Interest Factors

The accident here occurred in De Kalb County, and the plainƟﬀs' negligence claim against Guerine has a

 We live in a smaller world than that contemplated by the Gulf Oil Court, or even this court in Torres.

 In this case, a conscienƟous and experienced trial judge thought forum non conveniens decisions from

For the reasons we have discussed, we reverse the decision of the trial court transferring this cause to 
Kane County and remand for further proceedings.
