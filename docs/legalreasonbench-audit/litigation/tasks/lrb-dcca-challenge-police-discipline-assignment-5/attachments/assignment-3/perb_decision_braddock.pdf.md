GOVERNMENT OF THE DISTRICT OF COLUMBIA
PUBLIC EMPLOYEE RELATIONS BOARD
In the Matter of:
District of Columbia Metropolitan Police Department, Petitioner,
v.
Fraternal Order of Police/Metropolitan Police Department Labor Committee (on behalf of Colin 
R. Braddock), Respondent.
PERB Case No. 25-A-09 | Opinion No. 1904
DECISION AND ORDER
Issued: November 6, 2025
I. Statement of the Case
1. On April 21, 2025, the District of Columbia Metropolitan Police Department (MPD)  
filed an Arbitration Review Request pursuant to D.C. Official Code sec. 1-605.02(6), asking the  
Board to set aside the March 14, 2025 Opinion and Award of Arbitrator Kenneth J. Larrabee.  
The Award sustained MPD’s finding of cause against Officer Colin R. Braddock but reduced the  
penalty  of  termination  to  a  40-day  suspension  without  pay.  The  Fraternal  Order  of  
Police/Metropolitan Police Department Labor Committee (FOP) filed an Opposition on May 9,  
2025.
2. MPD contends that the Award on its face is contrary to law and public policy. In  
support,  MPD  argues  that  the  Arbitrator  misapplied  Douglas  v.  Veterans  Administration,  5 
M.S.P.R. 280 (1981), by requiring MPD to prove the comparability of its comparator cases under 
the sixth factor; that the Arbitrator substituted his own judgment for the deciding official’s  
conclusion on the twelfth factor; and that reinstating an officer who shot an unarmed, non-
threatening man contravenes a dominant public policy against the unjustified use of deadly force  
by police officers.
II. Discussion
3. The Board’s review of an arbitration award is narrow. Under D.C. Official Code sec.  
1-605.02(6), the Board may modify or set aside an award only where the arbitrator was without  
or exceeded his jurisdiction, the award on its face is contrary to law and public policy, or the  
award was procured by fraud, collusion, or similar unlawful means.
4. The parties bargained for the Arbitrator’s interpretation. Mere disagreement with the  
Arbitrator’s interpretation of Douglas or with his evaluation of the evidence does not make an  
award contrary to law and public policy, and the Board will not substitute its judgment for that of 
the duly designated arbitrator.

5. The public policy exception is extremely narrow. MPD has not identified a clear  
violation of public policy on the face of the Award.
6. The Award draws its essence from the parties’ agreement. No statutory basis exists for  
setting it aside.
III. Order
7. IT IS HEREBY ORDERED THAT: (a) the Arbitration Review Request is denied, and  
the Opinion and Award of Arbitrator Kenneth J. Larrabee is affirmed in all respects; and (b)  
pursuant to Board Rule 559.1, this Decision and Order is final upon issuance.
By direction of the Board.
Members Osei, Calloway, Herrera, and Lindqvist concurring; Member Pang not participating.
Washington, D.C.
November 6, 2025
