## Genuine inconsistencies

**Item 16’s environment field is a clerical inconsistency.** The [protocol](../../../../../../docs/legalreasonbench-audit/litigation/human-review/README.md#L56) defines the environment verdict for the *assignment*, even when its problem does not touch the sampled criterion. Item 16 marks forum non conveniens assignment 2 **Correct**; items 14, 15 and 17–20 mark that same assignment **Defective**. If the latter judgment is retained, item 16 must say Defective, while its own defect origin remains **No**. This is the clearest mechanical correction identified by both [consistency passes](../../../../../../docs/legalreasonbench-audit/litigation/human-review/stress-test/consistency/fable.md#L191). It changes environment Defective **9→10**, and the Defective-rubric/Defective-environment table cell **9→10**. It changes none of the headline, objective-error, or origin figures. **Classification: clerical.**

**Item 23 treats a rubric/record mismatch as an environment error, unlike item 10.** The supplied *Facts.pdf*, PDF p. 7, says Nelson opened fire as the Trailblazer approached ([extraction](../../../../../../docs/legalreasonbench-audit/litigation/tasks/lrb-vicar-trial-jury-instructions-assignment-2/attachments/assignment-2/Facts.pdf.md#L159)). Item 23’s criterion rewards saying there was a lack of evidence about who fired first. The Defective verdict and objective basis hold: a careful AUSA can acknowledge the supplied account and still argue retreat and preplanning. The [Eighth Circuit’s real-case opinion](https://ecf.ca8.uscourts.gov/opndir/24/06/222777P.pdf), *United States v. Mallory*, 104 F.4th 15 (8th Cir. 2024), slip op. pp. 2, 4, describes the trial evidence as unclear and also acknowledges physical evidence consistent with someone else firing first. That does **not** establish that the supplied government statement differs from the real government brief or trial record. The worksheet’s “differs from the real record” explanation is unverified. As with item 10, the error is in applying a proposition to the supplied hypothetical facts. Item 23’s **origin Yes→No** is a **substantive field disagreement**, reducing the environment-origin count **9→8** alone; it leaves Defective **21**, estimate **53**, lower bound **45**, and objective errors **7** intact. I agree with [item-23’s stress test](../../../../../../docs/legalreasonbench-audit/litigation/human-review/stress-test/items/item-23.md) on that distinction.

I would go further than either consistency pass on **the VICAR assignment-2 environment verdict**. The alternative ground recorded in items 24–25 is an arguable instruction about the scope of `response2.md`, not an objective error in the supplied record or a direction to put improper work before a court—the two grounds specified for an environment verdict. The [shared preamble](../../../../../../docs/legalreasonbench-audit/litigation/tasks/lrb-vicar-trial-jury-instructions-assignment-2/upstream-instruction.md#L30) asks for a “final complete legal deliverable”; the [supervisor’s assignment](../../../../../../docs/legalreasonbench-audit/litigation/tasks/lrb-vicar-trial-jury-instructions-assignment-2/instruction.md#L50) asks to “supplement your brief.” The resulting scope dispute belongs in the criterion verdicts. ## Factual and legal corrections

**Item 10 is the most consequential basis dispute.** *Douglas v. Veterans Administration*, 5 M.S.P.B. 313, printed pp. 332–34 (also reported at 5 M.S.P.R. 280), places the ultimate burden of showing an appropriate penalty on the agency and includes consistency with penalties for similar offenses among its factors ([supplied extraction](../../../../../../docs/legalreasonbench-audit/litigation/tasks/lrb-dcca-challenge-police-discipline-assignment-2/attachments/assignment-2/douglas_v_veterans_admin.pdf.md)). MPD affirmatively used four comparators as an aggravating factor in the Final Notice, ¶¶ 19, 26 ([extraction](../../../../../../docs/legalreasonbench-audit/litigation/tasks/lrb-dcca-challenge-police-discipline-assignment-2/attachments/assignment-1/final_notice_adverse_action_braddock.pdf.md)); the Award, ¶ 7, faulted the showing of similarity. A careful memo has reason to resist the rubric’s categorical “no uploaded authority requires” formulation.

But *Douglas* does not state an invariably separate burden to prove **comparability**, says not every factor matters in every case, and the assignment stipulates that reasonable minds may disagree about these comparators. The worksheet itself concedes the rubric may be “narrowly correct” ([item 10](../../../../../../docs/legalreasonbench-audit/litigation/human-review/worksheet.md#L1195)). I would retain **Defective** as a defensible professional judgment, but treat **Unambiguous objective error→Professional judgment** as a disclosed substantive basis disagreement. Objective errors would be **7→6 of 25**; their estimated number among 63 would be **18→15**, and their one-sided lower bound **11→9**. The headline remains **21/25, 53, 45**. If item 10 were instead Arguable, the headline would be **20/25, 50, 42**, as well as the objective-error change. I agree with [Opus’s consistency pass](../../../../../../docs/legalreasonbench-audit/litigation/human-review/stress-test/consistency/opus.md#L50) that “unambiguous” is too strong; I place more weight than it does on MPD’s affirmative reliance on the four cases. The note should also correct “handled in the same way”: all four comparators were terminated; **similar situation**, rather than identical outcome, was disputed. **Classification: substantive basis disagreement; note-only factual correction.**

**Item 8’s exact predicate has not been established.** The criterion calls for a “staff report **recommending approval**” ([worksheet item 8](../../../../../../docs/legalreasonbench-audit/litigation/human-review/worksheet.md#L855)). The Petition quotes the 2023 Staff Report’s site discussion (SR23 pp. 3–4), and a planner mentions staff conditions ([Petition extraction](../../../../../../docs/legalreasonbench-audit/litigation/tasks/lrb-certiorari-quasi-judicial-land-use-assignment-2/attachments/assignment-1/1774361815-petition.docx.md#L323)). Neither quoted passage says which way staff recommended. *City of Hialeah Gardens v. Miami-Dade Charter Foundation, Inc.*, 857 So. 2d 202, 204–05 (Fla. 3d DCA 2003) ([public opinion](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/fl-district-court-of-appeal/1254782.html)), supports the legal use of staff evidence on second-tier review; it cannot supply that missing fact about this report. I disagree with [item-08’s stress test](../../../../../../docs/legalreasonbench-audit/litigation/human-review/stress-test/items/item-08.md#L15) and the consistency passes insofar as they treat “a report exists” as proof that it recommended approval. The best defense is a plausible inference from staff conditions and the County’s application, plus the open research universe. Because I could not inspect the report itself, **Arguable**, rather than a confident Not defective or Defective, is my view. That would change Not defective **4→3**, Arguable **0→1**, and AI reasoning Wrong **4→3**/Correct **21→22**, but **not** the Defective count, estimate, lower bound, objective-error count, or environment counts. **Classification: substantive field disagreement.**

## Hardest verdicts to defend

**Items 3–4 (appeal and remand while appeal pending).** I would place both in Arguable. Preserving a 30-day second-tier route is a reasonable recommendation, and the real County pursued it; the rubric may prefer that strategy. John’s contrary judgment—that careful counsel might advise against an appeal rendered moot by a remand—is also credible. His note calls it a judgment call. The protocol says choosing one permissible strategy is not, by itself, a defect; here reasonable litigators can dispute whether the reward unfairly rejects the other. Item 4 inherits item 3’s appeal requirement even though remand itself is sound. The [item-03](../../../../../../docs/legalreasonbench-audit/litigation/human-review/stress-test/items/item-03.md) and [item-04](../../../../../../docs/legalreasonbench-audit/litigation/human-review/stress-test/items/item-04.md) stress tests give the best defense of Defective: an internal advice memo ought not lose credit for recommending a prudent alternative. I still regard the two as boundary judgments. **Substantive sensitivity:** together Defective **21→19**, estimate **53→48**, lower bound **45→39**; environment and objective counts stay put. The citation-check’s observation that item 4’s source points to the wrong Lee County LDC subsection is a separate **note-only** source correction, not the reason to change its verdict ([citation-check problems](../../../../../../docs/legalreasonbench-audit/litigation/citation-check/problems.md#L12)).

**Item 15 (Sara Davis’s affidavit).** I would call it Arguable, distinguishing it from the five nonparty-physician items. The partner supplies Sara’s Kane County residence, the defendants’ motion puts residence in issue (PDF p. 2, ¶ 9), and preparing a client affidavit for review and signature is ordinary practice. The counterweight is decisive enough to prevent Not defective: no executed affidavit is supplied, the closed-universe instruction says an unuploaded document does not exist, and the saved Opus brief presents an unsigned, partly invented affidavit as sworn. A competent drafter can instead flag it for Sara’s approval. John’s concern about filing unsupported sworn facts is well founded; it is less categorical for this criterion than for the nonparty witnesses. I agree with [item-15’s stress test](../../../../../../docs/legalreasonbench-audit/litigation/human-review/stress-test/items/item-15.md) on this distinction. **Substantive sensitivity:** Defective **21→20**, estimate **53→50**, lower bound **45→42**; professional-judgment defects **14→13** and environment-origin defects **9→8**. The assignment-level environment verdict can remain Defective.

**Items 24–25 (the *Kuhse* supplement).** An insert for a supervisor who already has the brief is competent practice; five carried-over criteria consume **65 of 135** assignment-2 positive points. That is John’s strongest defense. The other reading is substantial: the solver is told that `response2.md` is the final complete deliverable and the only file read by the verifier. Neither saved solver took an insert-only approach. Item 25 is particularly weak as a Defective call because *State v. Kuhse*, No. 18-0765, PDF p. 5, expressly discusses an available alternative course of action, and Opus’s [supplemented answer](../../../../../../docs/legalreasonbench-audit/litigation/model-runs/lrb-vicar-trial-jury-instructions-assignment-2/opus55-low/response.md#L118) applied that discussion to stopping the chase. Luna omitted the drive-away point in both assignments, so its item-25 failure does not demonstrate an insert penalty. Item 24’s stationary-car fact is less directly tied to *Kuhse*, making John’s call stronger there; I would still mark both **Arguable** under the protocol’s reasonable-disagreement clause. **Substantive sensitivity:** the pair changes Defective **21→19**, estimate **53→48**, lower bound **45→39**; objective errors stay **7**. If only item 25 moves, the figures are **20, 50, 42**. I agree with [item-25’s stress test](../../../../../../docs/legalreasonbench-audit/litigation/human-review/stress-test/items/item-25.md) more than the consistency passes here.

## Stronger arguments left on the table, and calls that hold

The **five nonparty affidavit calls**—items 14 and 17–20—are defensible as Defective, though less automatic than their notes suggest. Assignment 1 reports the doctors’ collective willingness and inconvenience; assignment 2 asks to include affidavits. That supports preparing **proposed** forms. It does not establish that each named nonparty approved a particular statement or executed it. The item-14 note should rest on the criterion’s demand to say an opposition *is supported* by an affidavit, and the saved Opus brief’s claim that five blank forms were separately signed ([brief and forms](../../../../../../docs/legalreasonbench-audit/litigation/model-runs/lrb-forum-non-conveniens-response-assignment-2/opus55-low/response.md#L174)), rather than assert that the prompt never contemplated drafting. That is a **note-only** strengthening, no figure change. Their shared premise also means six sampled criteria in this family—including item 15—are **six units, not six independent underlying mistakes**. A sensitivity moving the five nonparty items to Arguable would put Defective at **16/25**, estimate **40/63**, lower bound **32/63**; I do **not** recommend that as my preferred coding.

The penalty calls at **13 and 16** survive a literal reading. Item 13’s assignment asks for the strengths and weaknesses of *each* defense argument, while its −30 criterion penalizes merely “mentions” the Cook-interest position; *Bradbury v. St. Mary’s Hospital of Kankakee*, 273 Ill. App. 3d 555 (1st Dist. 1995), PDF p. 4, supplies the rebuttal. The saved judges did not impose that penalty, a limitation the worksheet candidly states. Item 16’s −30 criterion did fire on answers that identified and then answered the defendants’ **true 60.5% all-case** statistic: *2023 Annual Report Statistical Summary.pdf*, printed pp. 21–22, gives 60.5% for all cases and different civil rates; the defendants’ motion, PDF p. 6, relies principally on its comparison. Those are distinct from a mere preference to omit unfavorable facts. Item 22 is closer because the −5 penalty fired on a drafting note saying counsel would **not** rely on an unrelated April 13 plea; its lower impact makes Arguable a fair sensitivity, but the literal “mentions” wording supports John’s Defective judgment. **Note-only** qualification for 13 and 22; no preferred tally change.

I also checked and would retain the verdicts on **1, 2, 5–7, 9, 11–12, 21 and 23**. Among the clearest: item 9 asks for a comparator-date conflict the comparator memo, ¶¶ 1–5, does not contain; 2015/2017 concern prior discipline, not the four 2018–2021 comparators ([memo extraction](../../../../../../docs/legalreasonbench-audit/litigation/tasks/lrb-dcca-challenge-police-discipline-assignment-1/attachments/assignment-1/comparator_discipline_cases.pdf.md#L16)). Item 11 reasonably rewards spotting PERB’s **40-day** description against the Award’s operative **45-day** suspension; the task asks for a record-anchored memo, a stronger reason than calling it a “desirable training signal.” Item 12 tracks *Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern Ry. Co.*, 219 Ill. 2d 430, 444 (2006): a defendant must show the chosen forum is inconvenient to it, not just that DuPage is more convenient. Item 21 should lead with the narrow statutory point: Iowa Code [§ 702.11 (2020)](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/2020/702.11.pdf) does not enumerate conspiracy as a forcible felony, while [§ 704.6(1) (2020)](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/2020/704.6.pdf) speaks of participating in a forcible felony. John’s broader claim that the felony must *always* be independent of the act asserted to be justified is not established by an Iowa holding I could verify; the Florida analogy is support, not an Iowa rule. These are **note-only** refinements with no tally effect. For items 1 and 6, the cited AC-2-6 provision does not establish automatic February 2020 withdrawal, but I could not verify the historical **2019** text; the [citation check](../../../../../../docs/legalreasonbench-audit/litigation/citation-check/problems.md#L7) acknowledges that limit.
