# Item 20: lrb-forum-non-conveniens-response-assignment-2 — criterion_9

**Bottom line.** John's Defective call is defensible, but it is closer than his note admits, and on the protocol's own definitions I think it fits Arguable better (medium confidence). The strongest objection is that the task does ask for physician affidavits. Assignment 1's rubric rewards advising the client to "obtain affidavits from the 5 Cook County Hospital Doctors" (A1 criterion 13). The assignment 2 prompt then says "Please include all affidavits and information you referenced from Assignment #1" and immediately lists the five physicians' names. A careful brief can satisfy criterion 9 honestly by attaching a proposed affidavit for Dr. Linwood, marked for her review and signature. John's strongest point is still a real one: the criterion assumes an existing affidavit the closed record does not contain, and it cannot tell that candid draft apart from Opus's false claim that each affidavit "is separately signed." Any change should be reported as a disclosed post-tally sensitivity line covering the physician-affidavit cluster (items 14 and 17–20), not as a silent edit.

## Strongest objections

**1. The prompt chain does direct the physician affidavits.** The environment explanation says "the prompt does not instruct the model to create one." The record is against that reading.
- The assignment 1 email: "We have spoken with 5 of the Cook County Hospital Doctors … They have advised me that they are all Cook County residents and are willing to testify, however, it would be inconvenient for them to take time away from their practice/at the hospital to testify in DuPage rather than Cook County" (`tasks/lrb-forum-non-conveniens-response-assignment-2/input.json`, `prior_prompts[0]`).
- The assignment 1 rubric expects the memo to recommend exactly those affidavits: criterion 13 (+1), "Plaintiff should obtain affidavits from the 5 Cook County Hospital Doctors … that it would be inconvenient for them to testify in DuPage County" (`tasks/lrb-forum-non-conveniens-response-assignment-1/input.json`).
- The assignment 2 prompt: "Please include all affidavits and information you referenced from Assignment #1. The names of the physicians from Cook County we have spoken to are: Liam Vance … Maya Linwood …" The names have no purpose except to go on affidavits.
- Item 14's note reads "include" to mean documents already "referenced in the environment." The only affidavits in the environment are the defendants' two (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, Exs. A–B). A plaintiff's response would not "include" those, so John's reading leaves the instruction with almost nothing to do.

The reconciliation overturned GPT-6 Sol's flag on this ground ("Drafting those five affidavits from facts the supervising partner relayed, for the witnesses to review and sign, is ordinary litigation practice and is what the prompt requests"). *Answered by the note?* Partly. Item 14 addresses the "include" sentence, but not the A1 criterion 13 link or the names list, and its reading of "include" is strained.

**2. A careful response satisfies the criterion, so it does not misgrade one.** Criterion 9 asks only that the response identify "that the affidavit by Maya Linwood indicates that DuPage County would be an inconvenient forum for her." Consider a brief that attaches a proposed affidavit (for example, "Ex. 5, Affidavit of Maya Linwood, M.D. [draft, to be executed]") and describes what it says. That brief meets the criterion text, and the native judges passed an unexecuted form here. John's own preferred approach, a placeholder citation to a draft affidavit, would very likely earn these points. The protocol treats requiring one permissible approach as no defect unless the criterion "rejects an approach a careful litigator would adopt on this record." The approach it fails is a brief that prepares no affidavits at all, after the partner asked for them. *Answered by the note?* No. The note treats the hazard as one of drafting "without placeholders," but the criterion does not require dropping placeholders. That is how Opus chose to write it.

**3. Luna is not a clean example of a competent response that was misgraded.** Luna's brief says "The supplied record does not set out each physician's role, expected testimony, precise residence, willingness to appear, or scheduling burden" (`model-runs/lrb-forum-non-conveniens-response-assignment-2/gpt6luna-xhigh/response.md`, line 52). On the task as the protocol defines it, which includes earlier prompts, that is wrong. The A1 email says the five are Cook County residents, willing to testify, and burdened by a DuPage trial. Luna's A1 memo took the same over-literal view ("The Complaint does not state Sara Davis's … residence"; `model-runs/lrb-forum-non-conveniens-response-assignment-1/gpt6luna-xhigh/response.md`, line 14), even though the partner's email states it. Some of Luna's 0/3 therefore reflects a real error, not just caution. *Answered by the note?* No. Item 20 offers Luna as the counterexample, and item 14 calls Luna's approach "more correct" only "if forced to choose." That phrasing itself describes a judgment on which reasonable litigators could differ, which is the protocol's definition of Arguable.

**4. Protocol fit of "Defect arises from environment: Yes."** "Yes" requires "an error in the supplied task materials." As John's environment explanation is worded (no affidavit supplied, and no instruction to create one), it describes a criterion that asks for something the instructions did not ask for (basis (c)). That would be a defect in the criterion alone, so origin No, and arguably environment Correct. The fields are best supported on a different ground, an internal conflict in the materials. The prompt asks the associate to "include all affidavits" but also says "Do not rely on, reference, or infer any … factual information … beyond what is contained in these uploaded files. If a document is not uploaded, it does not exist for purposes of this analysis." Even the physicians' reported inconvenience comes from the email, not an uploaded file. *Answered by the note?* Partly. The explanation quotes the "does not exist" sentence but not the conflicting "include" instruction.

## Stronger arguments for the call

- **The criterion assumes a record fact that does not exist.** "The affidavit by Maya Linwood" refers to an existing document. Unlike every record-based criterion in this rubric, its source note cites no record document, only Langenhorst. The record supports only that counsel "spoke with" five unnamed doctors who said a DuPage trial would be inconvenient (A1 email). That fits basis (a), "unsupported by the supplied record," better than the note's wording does.
- **The judge cannot tell a candid draft from a fabricated filing.** The judge sees only the criterion text. Opus's brief says "Plaintiff submits sworn affidavits from five named Cook County treating physicians" and "the treating physicians have sworn to these facts themselves." It says "Each … has executed an affidavit in the same form … Each affidavit is separately signed and certified under Section 1-109," and the form attached has "[Name]" blanks (`model-runs/.../opus55-low/response.md`, lines 174, 181, 376, 386–410). All three judges passed it. Filed as written, that brief would make a false statement of fact to the tribunal. Ill. R. Prof'l Conduct 3.3(a)(1) forbids knowingly making "a false statement of fact or law to a tribunal," and 3.3(a)(3) forbids offering "evidence that the lawyer knows to be false." The criterion therefore passes work that is wrong on the very point it covers, which the protocol counts as misgrading. The note would be stronger if it said this directly, with the rule.
- **"As required under Langenhorst" misstates the law.** The cited page faults the *movants*: "no affidavits have been filed stating that St. Clair County [the plaintiff's chosen forum] would be an inconvenient forum for any of the witnesses … We reiterate that the defendant must show that the plaintiff's chosen forum is inconvenient to the defendant" (Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, PDF p. 10). Langenhorst requires nothing from a plaintiff's witnesses. Plaintiff-side affidavits help (Guerine relied on witness affidavits "stating that Cook County is not an inconvenient forum," First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, PDF p. 8), but they are not "required." The reconciliation recognized this misstatement for criterion 12 (Sara Davis) but not for criteria 6–10. On its own this is minor. A judge would likely pass a response that invokes Langenhorst correctly. But it adds to the point that the criterion was written as if the affidavits were a required, existing part of the record.
- **Under the benchmark's own design, the assignment 2 solver may never see the doctors' facts.** The native assignment 2 instruction contains only the assignment 2 email. Prior context reaches the solver only through its own `response1.md` (`tasks/.../upstream-instruction.md`: "your prior deliverable `response1.md` is also on disk"; `model-runs/experiment.md`: "Each assignment starts a fresh agent conversation"). A solver whose A1 memo did not repeat the physicians' statements, as Luna's did not, has no basis in its own context for Linwood's inconvenience. This is context, not a protocol ground, because the protocol defines the task as given to include earlier prompts. It does help explain why the environment verdict is Defective.

## Errors in the note

- **"the prompt does not instruct the model to create one"** (note and environment explanation). This is contestable. See objection 1: A1 criterion 13, "include all affidavits … referenced from Assignment #1," and the names list. A more accurate statement would be that the prompt asks for affidavits while also forbidding reliance on anything not uploaded.
- **"affidavits the record shows have not been signed."** The record does not show that the affidavits are unsigned. It shows that none was supplied and that counsel had only "spoken with" the doctors. "Affidavits that the record does not contain and that nothing shows have been executed" is accurate.
- **The Opus description is accurate.** Opus represented one unsigned "[Name]" form as five executed, separately signed affidavits (response lines 376–410). The Luna description, that it "cited no physician affidavit" and scored 0/3, is also accurate.
- **The source note's pinpoint is accurate.** Langenhorst PDF p. 10 contains the "no affidavits have been filed" passage. I confirmed this in the pinned PDF. It does not support the "as required" framing (see above), but the note does not rely on it.
- No miscaptioned authorities. Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern Ry. Co., 219 Ill. 2d 430, 848 N.E.2d 927 (2006), was decided March 2, 2006 (confirmed on CourtListener).

## Defect basis and environment origin

If Defective stands, **Professional judgment** is the right basis. No authority or arithmetic contradicts the criterion outright. The "as required under Langenhorst" misstatement is real but not what drives the grading. **Origin Yes** is correct only on this ground: the supplied materials pair "include all affidavits" with a closed-universe rule under which a document that was not uploaded "does not exist," and they never supply or clearly authorize the affidavits the criterion treats as existing. If the reason is only that the prompt did not ask for affidavits, the defect is basis (c), in the criterion alone, and origin should be No. **Environment Defective** holds on the same instruction-conflict ground. It is also consistent with items 14, 15 and 17–19. A secondary record inconsistency, Lurie Children's Hospital in the A1 email versus "Cook County Hospital" in the complaint (¶¶ 27, 29), is immaterial and need not be added.

## Suggested revision of the note

> Same issue as item 14. The criterion rewards a brief that "[i]dentifies … that the affidavit by Maya Linwood indicates that DuPage County would be an inconvenient forum for her … as required under Langenhorst." No Linwood affidavit is in the record. The only basis for her views is the assignment 1 email, which says counsel "spoke with" five unnamed Cook County doctors who are willing to testify and find DuPage inconvenient. The prompt pulls both ways. It asks the associate to "include all affidavits and information you referenced from Assignment #1" and lists the physicians' names, and assignment 1's criterion 13 expected a recommendation to obtain these affidavits. It also says "If a document is not uploaded, it does not exist for purposes of this analysis." A careful associate can draft a proposed affidavit for Dr. Linwood's review and cite it as a draft, and that response would earn these points. But the judge sees only the criterion. It passes a brief that falsely represents unsigned affidavits as sworn just as readily. Opus 5.5 (low) met this criterion 3/3 with a brief saying "Each affidavit is separately signed" over a single form with "[Name]" blanks, which would be a false statement to the tribunal if filed (Ill. R. Prof'l Conduct 3.3(a)(1), (3)). And the "as required under Langenhorst" framing is wrong: Langenhorst faults movants who filed no affidavits that the plaintiff's chosen forum was inconvenient (Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, PDF p. 10). It requires nothing from a plaintiff's witnesses. GPT-6 Luna (xhigh) cited no physician affidavit and scored 0/3, although its brief also wrongly said the record does not show the physicians' willingness or burden. This is one of the physician-affidavit items (14 and 17–20).

If John keeps Defective, the same text works with a closing sentence: "On balance I treat this as Defective because the criterion assumes an existing sworn statement the closed record does not contain and rewards misrepresenting it." In that case, change the environment explanation to the instruction-conflict ground.
