## Strongest objections

**1. The supplied record does not contain the expert testimony, so a ready-to-file brief cannot responsibly assert it (Sol F1).** The four attachments are the Resolution, the first-tier Petition, the Order and the LCU project page. No hearing transcript, staff report or expert report is supplied. Sol argues that a careful brief limited to the face of the Order would lose these 5 points.
*Answer.* The objection fails on the record, and John answers it, though he concedes more than he needs to. The Petition (the residents' own filing) quotes the County's witnesses verbatim with transcript pin cites:
- Professional engineer Erik Howard of Johnson Engineering: the project "is situated between two flow-ways, and we're going to develop within the upland portion" and the flow-ways "will be undisturbed" (Tr1 pp. 40–41); the plant "will be regularly inspected from FDEP" (Tr1 p. 36); stormwater sizing for "the 25-year, 3-day storm event, which is in accordance with the rules of the State of Florida" (Tr1 p. 241).
- RVI planner Alexis Crespo, presenting the Johnson Engineering buffer exhibit built from Port Authority line work: "we are outside of that buffer zone upon completion of that runway," and "[t]he Port Authority reviews all zoning applications that interface with their property, and they had no objection to this request" (Tr1 pp. 226–27).

The Petition also describes Johnson Engineering technical work in the record: the July 21, 2023 Surface Water and Groundwater Impacts/Benefits Analysis and the "Documented Occurrences of Listed Species" map. Appellate practice requires the record to go in an appendix (Fla. R. App. P. 9.220), so a County brief would cite Tr1 directly. The criterion does not require transcript details beyond what the Petition supplies. The note's concession that the testimony "is not actually in the environment" therefore overstates the gap. Only the full transcripts are missing.

**2. *Dusseau* bars the district court from assessing record evidence, so a second-tier brief should not ask it to find that expert testimony "is competent substantial evidence."** Sol cites *Dusseau*'s statement that "the district court thus was precluded from assessing the record evidence." The Supreme Court did quash the Third District's decision to the extent that court reviewed the record itself.
*Answer.* The note does not address this objection, but it fails. *Dusseau* approved the Third District's ruling that the circuit court applied the wrong law when it "substituted its judgment for that of the Commission as to the relative weight of the conflicting evidence" ("This ruling was proper"). A second-tier petitioner shows that error by pointing to the supporting evidence the circuit court disregarded or reweighed. *City of Hialeah Gardens*, the rubric's own source, is a second-tier certiorari decision. There the Third District quashed a circuit court that had discounted staff testimony and held that the testimony was competent substantial evidence. 857 So. 2d at 204–05. The criterion asks the brief to "state" the point, which is advocacy, not a request that the court reweigh evidence. A brief that frames it as wrong-law error (the circuit court ignored competent substantial evidence and reweighed) satisfies the criterion and complies with *Dusseau*. The companion 20-point criterion (`states_in_the_second_assignment_that_the_circuit_court_erred_by`) takes exactly that framing.

**3. The Order did not rest on lack of competent substantial evidence, so the point is surplus, and rewarding it imposes an approach the task did not ask for (basis (c)).** The Order's three grounds are the *Jennings* due-process ground (pp. 2–3), prejudgment and bias (pp. 3–5), and the airport-code and FAA-advisory ground (pp. 5–6).
*Answer.* The note partly answers this ("any competent lawyer would consider adding that point"). The record makes the answer stronger. Ground III faults the Board because the FAA advisories "were admittedly not fully considered" and concludes the decision "unnecessarily places public safety at risk" (Order p. 6). Page 2 recites panther habitat, the airport radius and the flow-ways as matters "discussed in the quasi-judicial hearings." That is a weight-of-the-evidence ruling, and the natural response is that the record holds unrebutted professional testimony on exactly those subjects: Crespo on the airport buffer, Howard on the flow-ways and stormwater. The criterion is worth 5 of 150 points. At most it prefers one permissible line of argument, and it rejects none. Under the protocol, that is not a defect.

**4. Is Crespo's testimony "expert" testimony?** She is a planner, not an engineer, and the Petition offers her statements as a recess report, not qualified expert opinion.
*Answer.* The note does not address this objection, and it carries little weight. *Hialeah Gardens* treats professional staff's fact-based testimony, "whether expert or not," as competent substantial evidence. Howard is a licensed professional engineer and fits the criterion squarely. Citing him would make John's locator stronger.

**5. A point not considered: the native judges misread "second assignment."** All six native judge votes (three judges on each of two runs) read "in the second assignment" as "the second assignment of error" (Argument II) and scored 0. In the task, "2nd ASSIGNMENT" is the prompt's heading for this step, and every criterion in both assignments says "in the first/second assignment." Claude Opus 5.5 (low) made the expert-testimony argument in its Statement of Facts and in Argument I.C (`model-runs/.../opus55-low/response.md`). The Sonnet judge saw that passage and still failed it because it was "not in a second assignment of error." The same misreading pulled that run's native correctness down to 0.033. Someone could argue that the wording is ambiguous enough to make the criterion Arguable. Under the protocol, though, the test is the criterion applied as written to the task as given. A careful reader of this task reads "second assignment" as the task step, so the error lies in how the judges applied the criterion, not in the criterion. The note should still record this, because it is the main reason this criterion misgrades in practice.

## Stronger arguments for the call

- **Licensed engineer testimony quoted in the record:** the Howard passages above (Petition, Tr1 pp. 36, 40–41, 241). The criterion's premise, that expert testimony is on the record, therefore rests on direct quotation, not inference.
- **The Board adopted the findings that this evidence supports.** The Board "adopts the recommendation of the Hearing Examiner, including the following findings," among them that the project "[c]omplies with the LDC and other County regulations or qualifies for deviations" and "[w]ill not adversely affect environmentally critical areas and natural resources" (Resolution § E). It considered "the testimony of all interested persons" (Resolution recitals).
- **The opponents' filing concedes the evidence exists.** The Petition argues against Johnson Engineering's modeling and maps (it says Hurricanes Irma and Ian data "were deliberately omitted"). That confirms the County's expert evidence was before the Board, even though the Petition disputes its weight. Under *Dusseau*, contrary evidence is "outside the scope of the inquiry" on first-tier review.
- **A model made the argument without being told to.** The Opus answer argued that the Board heard "contrary engineering testimony" and that the circuit court reweighed it (Argument I.C). That is evidence that a competent response reaches this point unprompted.
- **The authority is accurate.** The *Hialeah Gardens* quotation in the source note appears at 857 So. 2d at 205, within the cited 204–05. The opinion is a second-tier case, which fits this posture (matter citation check, entry 42).
