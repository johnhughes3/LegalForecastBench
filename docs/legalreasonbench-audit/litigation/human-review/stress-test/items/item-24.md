# Item 24: lrb-vicar-trial-jury-instructions-assignment-2 — criterion_7

Bottom line: John's Defective verdict is defensible, and his mechanism is right. Assignment 2 asks the associate to "supplement your brief with relevant legal analysis of State v. Kuhse." The rubric then re-grades five assignment 1 fact points, criteria 5–9, worth 65 of 135 points. This criterion is one of them, and it fails a competent Kuhse insert that relies on the brief already written. The case is closer to the Arguable line than the note admits. Both native runs read the task as calling for a complete revised section, and Luna's response is not the shorter insert the note implies. The most significant problem is the environment coding. Prompt underspecification is not an "objective error in the supplied record," and it does not "direct work product a competent litigator should not put before a court." So "Defect arises from environment: Yes" and the Environment explanation recorded for this item do not fit the protocol's definitions. I recommend a disclosed post-tally change of origin to No. The environment verdict should be harmonized with item 23 at the assignment level. The rubric verdict and AI reasoning should stay as recorded, along with note corrections.

## Strongest objections

1. **The native preamble points toward a self-contained, complete deliverable.** The pinned assignment 2 `instruction.md` says: "Write the final complete legal deliverable for this assignment to `/app/response2.md`. The verifier reads only that file," and "Build on that analysis as the prompt directs." A defender would say that a solver told the grader sees only response2.md should not leave the core retreat facts in a file the grader never reads. The reconciliation adopted this view and overturned both blind findings, saying the preamble "decides the question." Even Claude Opus 5.5's blind finding (O3) conceded that the preamble "cuts the other way."
   - *Does the note answer it?* Partly. John quotes both preamble sentences and argues that "supplement" frames the deliverable. But he does not engage with the best counter-reading: "complete legal deliverable *for this assignment*" can mean a complete supplement, because the assignment is the supplement. "Build on that analysis *as the prompt directs*" sends the solver back to the prompt, and the prompt says "supplement." That is the strongest textual answer, and the note should make it.

2. **Both native runs chose the full-brief reading, and neither failed this criterion because it was written as an insert.** `model-runs/lrb-vicar-trial-jury-instructions-assignment-2/gpt6luna-xhigh/response.md` is headed "Supplemental response," but it reproduces Luna's entire assignment 1 argument nearly word for word. Its opening section, standard-of-review paragraph and § 704 paragraphs match `.../assignment-1/gpt6luna-xhigh/response.md`, and it adds two Kuhse sections at the end (assignment 1: 1,090 words; assignment 2: 1,501). Opus (6,417 words) also rewrote the whole section. Both responses missed criterion 7 on substance, as both missed criterion 12 in assignment 1. Opus says Nelson "was hiding in the bushes" (line 108) and that "the OTB group could simply have stopped following Nelson" (line 80), but never that Nelson's car was parked. A defender would say the only observed behavior is the full-brief reading, so an insert-only response is the "unusual response[]" that the protocol assigns to Arguable.
   - *Does the note answer it?* No. The note reports the 0/3 results accurately, but it presents Luna as the shorter-supplement example, which it is not (see Errors). It does not admit that the runs show no instance of the defect operating.

3. **The supervisor re-uploaded the Facts, which signals that the supplement should apply Kuhse to the record.** The assignment 2 prompt says "State v. Kuhse ... (Kuhse.pdf) and Facts (Facts.pdf) have been uploaded for your use." Kuhse's holding as quoted in the rubric is a production burden: "The defendant bears the initial burden of producing sufficient evidence to support the instruction" (Kuhse.pdf, PDF p. 9). A defender would argue that applying that burden requires marshaling why the record lacks substantial evidence. The parked car is the premise of the escape argument the real court used to reach that conclusion ("a golden opportunity to escape, given that Nelson's car was already stopped," *Mallory*, slip op. at 4).
   - *Does the note answer it?* No. The answer is that applying Kuhse to the record calls for *some* record application, which criteria 3, 4, 9 and 10 already test. It does not call for this particular retreat fact, which a Kuhse section can cover by cross-referencing the brief's existing duty-to-retreat discussion. The note should say so.

4. **Environment coding.** The protocol makes an environment Defective for "objective errors in the supplied record, or task materials that direct work product a competent litigator should not put before a court" (README § 3). Its "Defects in the environment" paragraph gives examples of "unplanted error[s] in the supplied record (conflicting figures, arithmetic that does not add up, fabricated or misdescribed authority)." Origin Yes requires that the defect "stems from an error in the supplied task materials rather than in the criterion alone." An underspecified deliverable is not an objective error in the record, and it directs nothing improper. The defect is the mismatch between what the prompt asks for and what the criterion grades. That mismatch is defect basis (c), "requires something the instructions did not ask for." If basis (c) counted as an environment error whenever the instructions could have been clearer, nearly every basis (c) defect would count as environmental. Both blind models put the fix on the rubric side ("Score the new Kuhse analysis..." for GPT-6 Sol; "limit assignment-2 criteria to the Kuhse burden analysis" as one of Opus's two options).
   - *Does the note answer it?* No. The environment explanation describes the mismatch but does not identify a record error.

5. **No objection on the record.** The expected fact is supported. Facts.pdf, PDF p. 7, reads: "Nelson had his car parked near 30th and Clark Streets, and had hidden in the bushes. ... He opened fire as the Trailblazer approached." I confirmed this against the pinned PDF. So the car was stationary before Patton fired, and there is no basis (a) problem. John says as much.

## Stronger arguments for the call

- **The protocol's permissible-approaches rule fits better than a "token inefficiency" framing.** The README says a criterion "is Defective when it rejects an approach a careful litigator would adopt on this record." Asked to "supplement your brief with relevant legal analysis of" a newly found case, a careful associate commonly returns a new or revised subsection for insertion and cross-references facts the brief already states. Redlining the whole brief is also common. The criterion rejects the first approach unless the insert happens to repeat this retreat fact. That is a stronger, protocol-anchored framing than "reward[ing] token inefficiency."
- **The rubric's own justification overreaches.** The upstream justification for criterion 7 (and for criteria 5–9) says it "is requested directly in ... 'Please supplement your brief with relevant legal analysis of State v. Kuhse.'" But Kuhse is a burden-allocation and ineffective-assistance case about a marshaling instruction (Kuhse.pdf, PDF pp. 2, 8–9). It says nothing about retreat or a stopped car. The stopped-car fact belongs to the duty-to-retreat argument already graded in assignment 1, criterion 12 (+15, source "Facts pp. 7–8"), and it comes from the Eighth Circuit's retreat analysis (*Mallory*, slip op. at 4). So the "requested directly" claim is wrong on its face. That supports basis (c).
- **The point totals show the scale.** Criteria 5–9 are 65 of assignment 2's 135 positive points (48%). Only criteria 1, 2 and 10 (20 points) rest on Kuhse itself. An insert that gets every Kuhse point and cross-references the brief for facts could lose about half the available score. This shows materiality, which matters for keeping the item out of Arguable.
- **The sandbox design makes the insert reading natural.** `model-runs/experiment.md` (line 25) confirms that each assignment starts a fresh conversation, with response1.md on disk. A solver that reads its own prior brief and is told to "supplement" it has every reason to write only the supplement.

## Defect basis and environment origin

**Basis: Professional judgment** is right. Nothing in the record, the law or arithmetic contradicts the criterion. The parked car is in the Facts, and the criterion states it correctly. The defect is a judgment that the criterion grades content the assignment did not ask for, so it is basis (c).

**Origin: No.** The defect comes from the criterion's reuse of assignment 1 fact points on a supplement assignment. The supplied record contains no error that produces it. The prompt's silence on whether response2.md must restate the whole brief is the baseline against which basis (c) is measured, not an environment error. Fixing the criterion by scoring only Kuhse analysis would remove the defect without touching any task material.
