## Strongest objections

1. **This is a judgment call where the rubric takes a reasonable side, so it is Not defective or at most Arguable.** The rubric's preferred course is a second-tier petition plus a remand (criteria `..._should_file_a_tim` and `..._should_remand_the`, +20 each). Proceeding with construction while seeking review and a stay is a defensible recommendation for a county with a facility planned since 2010 to serve growth (LCU Project Detail, PDF p. 4). Claude Opus 5.5's blind report called it a two-sided judgment call (O6, "arguable").
   *Answer.* Under the protocol, preferring one permissible approach is not a defect, but rejecting a correct one is ("It is Defective when it rejects an approach a careful litigator would adopt on this record"). A −30 penalty does not express a preference. It deducts the rubric's largest single weight (−30 against 170 positive points) from the other permissible answer. The note answers this in substance ("this rubric fails a response many careful lawyers would prefer") but does not tie it to the protocol's rule. This is the closest question in the item. A second reviewer could reasonably call it Arguable if they thought only an unusual memo would recommend a pause, so the note should state why the pause is mainstream on this record (points 2–4 below and "Stronger arguments").

2. **The Supreme Court's prospective statement is dicta, and the operative law is the Third DCA's immunity holding.** The discharge left *Metropolitan Dade County v. Parkway Towers Condominium Ass'n*, 281 So. 2d 68, 69–70 (Fla. 3d DCA 1973), standing. That decision holds that a county "has the right to erect a governmental facility . . . notwithstanding the use classification of any particular site," absent an affirmative waiver in the zoning ordinance. The companion criterion `..._could_take_the_po` (+5) credits that position, and its source note calls the Supreme Court's language "dicta and not binding."
   *Answer.* The note does not address this. It has good answers. (a) Whether or not it is binding, the Supreme Court's stated prospective view is guidance a careful county lawyer would weigh heavily. The rubric's own article reads it as the Court's "future view . . . that unless expressly exempted, a county is subject to its own zoning regulations" (Sandra G. Smith, *Immunity of State and State Related Activities from Local Municipal Zoning Regulations: Florida Focus*, 28 Fla. L. Rev. 800 (1976), PDF p. 17), and it reports that "the pendulum in Florida has swung in a new direction." (b) The Third DCA itself recognized waiver where the ordinance provides for the use (its police-station example, 281 So. 2d at 69). Lee County did not rely on immunity here. It applied for a CFPD rezoning and a comprehensive-plan amendment for this very facility (Order p. 1; Resolution Z-23-035). (c) Immunity is at most a position the County "could take" (+5). It is not a reason to penalize a lawyer who declines to stake a public works project on it. (d) The rubric's own penalty rests on the *Supreme Court's* disposition, the same non-merits discharge it calls non-binding in the companion criterion.

3. **Parkway Towers' practical reasoning fits this record.** The Court declined to interrupt construction because doing so "would cause needless expense and damage with no prospect or likelihood that the Commission would reach a different final result" (295 So. 2d at 295). The Lee County Board is the applicant's own governing body, it has pursued this site since 2018 (LCU Project Detail, PDF p. 4), and it predictably would re-approve. In the real litigation it did so in February 2026 (see item 3's note).
   *Answer.* The note does not address this. (a) In *Parkway Towers* the commission had twice resolved at public hearings that construction should proceed without a zoning waiver. Here there is no such resolution, and the only approval has been quashed (Order p. 1). (b) The quashal rests on predetermination and bias: the Board voted on "the direction of the board over the last four years," and the County took prior steps, including the Kingston settlement, that "predetermined, or at very least created prejudice and bias" (Order pp. 2–5). A county lawyer advising the Board on October 27, 2025 could reasonably conclude that continuing to build before the remand hearing would look like the same predetermination the court condemned, and would hand the petitioners their next argument. The assumption that "the Board will re-approve anyway" is the reasoning the Order faulted. (c) The February 2026 re-approval is hindsight and was not available to the memo writer.

4. **Construction is under way, so stopping is costly.** The penalty's source note says construction has started, "which means the county has spent time and money," and the companion criterion's source says stopping is possible "though the costs would be extremely high."
   *Answer.* The note answers this ("nothing in the environment establishes that it would be costly, impossible, or imprudent"). It could cite the record more pointedly. The only supplied evidence is LCU Project Detail: construction "started on October 6, 2025" (PDF p. 1), with "Site Clearing — Fall 2025 to Winter 2026," "Site Fill Work — Winter 2026 to Summer 2026," and "Facility Construction — Fall 2026 to Summer 2028" (PDF p. 2). On the memo date the project was three weeks into site clearing, and vertical construction was about a year away. No document gives a cost, a contract or a suspension term.

5. **The penalty reaches only an unqualified "cease construction" recommendation, which a careful lawyer would not make.** On that reading, a careful memo would recommend at most a conditional, temporary pause, and the penalty would not fire.
   *Answer.* The note does not mention the native runs, which answer this directly. Claude Opus 5.5 (low) recommended, "Pending a stay ruling, direct staff to suspend site work that depends on the quashed zoning" (response, Executive Summary item 3; repeated in the Recommendation). Two of the three judges treated that as triggering the penalty, so the answer lost 30 points. The criterion as applied reaches the careful, qualified version. Even the unqualified version ("do not build until the zoning is cured") is what the Supreme Court said prospectively.

6. **The order does not enjoin construction, and injunctive relief is unavailable in certiorari.** The companion penalties (`..._must_stop_constru`, −20; `..._petitioners_may_file_a_s`, −15) treat continued construction as legally permitted.
   *Answer.* The note does not address this, but it does not need to. Those penalties target a claim that the County *must* stop, which is a different proposition. The sampled penalty fires on a recommendation that the County *should* stop, which is prudential advice. "Not compelled" does not mean "imprudent." A memo can say both that the Order does not enjoin construction and that the Board should pause zoning-dependent work.

7. **Open universe, limited research.** The prompt limits research to what is "necessary to identify applicable rules and case law relevant to these documents" (Prompt). A defender might say a solver should not reach for *Parkway Towers*.
   *Answer.* This is not material. The penalty fires regardless of the authority a memo cites, and *Parkway Towers* is the rubric's own authority.

## Stronger arguments for the call

- **The rubric's own law-review source supports a pause.** Smith, 28 Fla. L. Rev. 800 (cited in companion criterion `..._could_take_the_po`), concludes that Florida had moved away from categorical immunity. It reads *Parkway Towers* as the Supreme Court's "future view in such cases would be that unless expressly exempted, a county is subject to its own zoning regulations" (PDF p. 17). The matter citation check reached the same reading (`citation-check/results/lrb-certiorari-quasi-judicial-land-use.md`, entry for Smith, second check).
- **The record shows an early-stage project.** Construction had run three weeks of a four-year schedule, and the current phase was site clearing (LCU Project Detail, PDF pp. 1–2). No cost or contract evidence appears anywhere in the supplied record.
- **The Order's grounds make continued construction risky.** The court quashed for predetermination and for a decision based on matters outside the record (Order pp. 2–5). Building before a fresh, fair hearing invites the same finding on remand and supports a renewed challenge.
- **After quashal the site reverts to AG-2.** The Order describes the decision under review as a rezoning "from Agriculture (AG-2) to Community Facilities Planned Development" (Order p. 1). Quashal leaves the matter "as if no order had been entered" (*Broward County v. G.B.V. Int'l, Ltd.*, 787 So. 2d 838, 844–45 (Fla. 2001), cited in the companion criterion `..._effect_of_the_order_gran`). Opus 5.5's answer drew this consequence and explained why zoning-dependent work becomes exposed (response § on implications, items 1–3).
- **The native run shows a real misgrade.** The Opus 5.5 answer above is a competent, conditional recommendation paired with a stay motion, and the penalty took 30 points from it. That is the failure the protocol describes, shown on an actual response.
- **A caution on authority the note rightly did not use.** Opus's blind report also cited Fla. Stat. § 163.3194(1)(a). That subsection requires government development to be consistent with the adopted *comprehensive plan*, and Ordinance 23-28's Public Facilities plan amendment was not quashed. It is weak support for a pause, and the note is better without it.

## Errors in the note

- **"Municipality" (three times).** Lee County is a county. *Parkway Towers* also involved a county (Metropolitan Dade), and its prospective statement covers "county or municipal facility purposes." Use "county."
- **"merely exercises discretion not to hear an appeal on certiorari."** This is imprecise. The Court had granted conflict certiorari and heard oral argument. It then found "no direct conflict justifying our taking certiorari conflict jurisdiction" and discharged the writ as "improvident" (295 So. 2d at 295). That is a jurisdictional discharge, not a discretionary refusal, and the proceeding was not an appeal.
- **"essentially says the opposite of what it claims."** This is an overstatement. The disposition did leave the construction undisturbed, and part of its stated reason was avoiding "needless expense." The accurate point is that the result rested on facts absent here (two commission resolutions to proceed; no prospect of a different result), while the Court's forward-looking statement supports a pause.
- **Quotation and pin cite verified.** The quoted passage is verbatim, the opinion is per curiam, and the passage spans the page break, so "at 295–96" is correct (Caselaw Access Project text of 295 So. 2d 295, with the `*296` marker before "or, if this has not been done").
- **The CourtListener link is correct** but currently sits behind a bot challenge. A Caselaw Access Project link would be a stable alternative.

## Suggested revision of the note

> Confirmed. The rubric deducts 30 points, its largest single weight, if the memo to the Board of County Commissioners recommends that the County stop construction. Its basis is *Parkway Towers Condominium Ass'n v. Metropolitan Dade County*, 295 So. 2d 295 (Fla. 1974) (per curiam), which discharged a writ of conflict certiorari as improvidently granted because there was no direct conflict. The construction there went forward, but on facts absent here: the county commission had twice resolved at public hearings to proceed without a zoning change, so interruption would have caused expense "with no prospect or likelihood that the Commission would reach a different final result." *Id.* at 295. Here the only zoning approval has been quashed for predetermination and bias (Orderpdf.docx pp. 2–5), and the Court's reasoning, if anything, supports a pause: "it is nevertheless our view prospectively that zoning variations to accommodate county or municipal facility purposes should either have been anticipated in zoning ordinances before construction or operation of such facilities is commenced or, if this has not been done, construction should not be undertaken thereof until after due modification or change therefor is made in existing zoning ordinances." *Id.* at 295–96. The law-review article the rubric cites in a companion criterion reads the case the same way, as the Court's view that "unless expressly exempted, a county is subject to its own zoning regulations." Sandra G. Smith, 28 Fla. L. Rev. 800 (1976) (PDF p. 17). The Third DCA's immunity holding, 281 So. 2d 68 (Fla. 3d DCA 1973), gives the County an argument for continuing, which the rubric credits at +5, but it is no reason to penalize a lawyer who declines to stake the project on it, especially since Lee County itself sought this rezoning. Nor does the record show that stopping would be costly: LCU Project.pdf shows construction began October 6, 2025, three weeks before the memo, with site clearing through Winter 2026 and facility construction not starting until Fall 2026 (pp. 1–2). No cost or contract figures appear. A careful county lawyer could recommend pausing zoning-dependent work until a stay or a cured rezoning, partly so that continuing to build does not repeat the predetermination the court condemned. The penalty takes 30 points from that answer. In the native runs it did so to Claude Opus 5.5's memo, which recommended only suspending zoning-dependent work "[p]ending a stay ruling" (2 of 3 judges).
