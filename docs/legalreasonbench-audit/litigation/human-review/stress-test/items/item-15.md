# Item 15: lrb-forum-non-conveniens-response-assignment-2 — criterion_11

Bottom line: John's concern is real, but on this criterion it is weaker than he records. The criterion asks only that the brief say it is supported by Sara Davis's affidavit. A careful plaintiff's lawyer would prepare that affidavit: the supervising partner supplied her Kane County residence, the defendants' motion put her residence at issue (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, PDF p. 2 ¶ 9), the uploaded *Guerine* opinion shows plaintiffs answering a transfer motion with their own affidavits (First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, PDF p. 7), and the prompt says to "include all affidavits ... you referenced from Assignment #1." A draft that cites "Affidavit of Sara Davis (Ex. 1)" and marks it as awaiting her review and signature meets the criterion and creates no hazard. What remains is the closed-universe instruction ("If a document is not uploaded, it does not exist"), which gives a competent solver a reasonable basis for doing what GPT-6 Luna did. That is a ground on which reasonable litigators could disagree. I recommend **Arguable** in place of Defective. Because this is a post-tally substantive change, it should be disclosed as a reviewer-disagreement sensitivity figure, not entered silently. I also found a few smaller errors in the note: the Opus grade was full credit, not partial, and the judge-label scan omits criterion 5.

## Strongest objections

1. **The criterion does not require the hazardous practice. It is satisfiable by careful drafting.** The criterion text is: "States in Assignment #2 that the response is supported by the affidavit of the plaintiff, Sara Davis." It says nothing about placeholders, execution, or which facts the affidavit must contain. The recorded ground is the risk that an unexecuted affidavit gets presented as sworn, which is a risk in how a response satisfies the criterion. A brief citing "Affidavit of Sara Davis (Ex. 1) [draft for client review and execution]" with a residence-only affidavit satisfies the criterion. The deliverable also goes to the supervising partner (the prompt is an internal email from James Rogers to Quinn Vivero), not to the clerk. The supplied record follows the same convention: the defendants' own exhibits carry bracketed placeholders ("Affidavit of [Records Custodian]," Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf PDF p. 7; Dr. Smith's "[ZIP]," PDF p. 8 ¶ 2). Under the protocol, the question is whether the criterion "would misgrade a permissible competent response," and a judge's acceptance of Opus's unmarked exhibit is not a defect in the criterion.
   - *Does the note answer it?* Partly. John says "apparently without placeholders," but that is an inference about the rubric author's intent, not something the criterion says. His argument that a client "may tell the lawyer to change" her facts is answered by a draft affidavit, which exists so that she can do exactly that. The note does not explain why a placeholder-marked draft would fail.

2. **A plaintiff's affidavit is the ordinary, authority-endorsed response on this record.** The motion says that "[t]he Complaint does not allege that Sara Davis ... resided in Cook County, or anywhere else," and that the defendants "will seek to confirm this fact through written discovery" (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, PDF p. 2 ¶ 9; confirmed in the pinned PDF). In *Guerine*, plaintiffs who lived in Kane County and sued in Cook County put in "affidavits from Patrick, Christopher, and Samuel Malone stating that they will suffer no inconvenience by traveling to Cook County," and the Supreme Court relied on them in reversing the transfer (First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, PDF p. 7, "Private Interest Factors," ¶ 1; confirmed in the pinned PDF). The residence fact comes from the client through the supervising partner ("our client, Sara Davis, resides in Kane County, Illinois," assignment 1 prompt). Unlike the nonparty physicians in item 14, Sara is the firm's own client. Getting her signature on a two-paragraph residence affidavit is routine, and her lawyers can confirm the facts directly with her. Opus's saved assignment 1 memo reached this advice without the rubric. It noted that client-file facts are "not yet in the record and will have to be proven by affidavit," and recommended: "We should not hide this. Ms. Davis lives in Kane County. We should disclose it by affidavit" (`model-runs/lrb-forum-non-conveniens-response-assignment-1/opus55-low/response.md`, lines 14, 54).
   - *Does the note answer it?* Partly. John acknowledges the client distinction and calls this "a closer call," but he still records Defective without saying why the client distinction does not move the item to Arguable. In the protocol, a "closer call" on which "[r]easonable litigators could disagree" is the definition of Arguable.

3. **The prompt does tell the solver to prepare the affidavits.** John's environment explanation says the prompt "does not instruct the model to create one." But the assignment 2 prompt says "Please include all affidavits and information you referenced from Assignment #1," and the affidavits "you referenced" in Assignment #1 could only have been recommendations to obtain them. Assignment 1's rubric expects those recommendations (criterion 10, +10: Sara "should file an affidavit establishing her residence outside of DuPage County"; criterion 11, +15; criterion 13, +1). Read in sequence, "include" most naturally means "prepare and attach." John's item 14 note reads "include" as limited to documents already in the environment, but no affidavit from the plaintiff side is in the environment, so on that reading the sentence would have nothing to refer to.
   - *Does the note answer it?* Not for this item. The item 14 reading is incorporated by reference ("the same problem as item 14") but not defended against this point.

4. **The reconciliation ruled the other way on exactly this criterion.** It found criterion 11 not defective because "[a]n affidavit of her Kane County residence is supported by the record, and assignment-1 criterion 10 recommends it," and retained only criterion 12, the inconvenience affidavit (`tasks/lrb-forum-non-conveniens-response-assignment-2/reconciliation.md`, R2). Both blind models built their findings mainly on the missing *inconvenience* facts, which belong to criterion 12. The reconciliation is an argument, not a source, but its distinction between 11 and 12 is sound.
   - *Does the note answer it?* No. The note mentions criterion 12 only to say it "rests on the same missing affidavit." That treats the two criteria as one. Criterion 12's requirement has no record basis, but criterion 11's does.

**What survives (why I recommend Arguable rather than Not defective).** The closed-universe instruction is real and bears on this point: "Do not rely on, reference, or infer any ... factual information ... beyond what is contained in these uploaded files. If a document is not uploaded, it does not exist for purposes of this analysis" (assignment 2 prompt). A competent solver could reasonably read that to bar citing an affidavit that does not exist. GPT-6 Luna did so and wrote a careful brief that argues deference without claiming a residence the record does not establish (`model-runs/.../gpt6luna-xhigh/response.md`, line 26). The criterion fails that response. There is also a defensible strategic reason not to volunteer residence: *Bradbury* affirmed the denial of transfer partly because "we do not know the residence of the decedent," and placed that gap on the defendants (Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, PDF pp. 4–5). The sequence also depends on what the solver's own assignment 1 memo said. If that memo did not recommend a Sara affidavit, then "include all affidavits ... you referenced" asks for none. That was Opus's blind dependency point, which the reconciliation rejected by relying on a rubric the solver never sees. Luna's saved assignment 1 memo recommended no Sara affidavit (`model-runs/lrb-forum-non-conveniens-response-assignment-1/gpt6luna-xhigh/response.md`, line 38), so its assignment 2 brief followed its own earlier analysis. Each of these is a minority approach on this record, but each is reasonable. Together they make the criterion arguable, not defective.

I found no misreading of the record. The ¶ 9 locator, the Kane County quotation, and the description of Opus's Exhibit 1 ¶¶ 7 and 9 are all accurate.

## Stronger arguments for the call

To the extent the Defective call is kept, these points support it better than the note does:

- **Opus's Exhibit 1 swears to facts on the contested *Bland* issue.** The prompt says "[t]he family ... had him transferred" (assignment 1 prompt), and the complaint says only that he "was transferred" (Davis Complaint.docx ¶ 27). Opus's affidavit has Sara swear that the transfer was "so that he could receive a higher level of specialized neonatal care" and "solely for my son's medical care," and that "I had not consulted a lawyer" (Ex. 1 ¶¶ 6–7). That is the precise point on which *Bradbury* distinguished *Bland*: there, "it was the defendants that arranged for the plaintiff's decedent to be transferred," so nothing suggested the Cook physicians "were chosen to establish a nexus to venue" (Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, PDF p. 4 ¶ 3). The brief then relies on those paragraphs as sworn fact (`opus55-low/response.md`, lines 57 and 185). This is a much more concrete example of the hazard than ¶ 9, because the invented sworn facts go to a disputed legal question.
- **The brief represents the affidavit as executed.** "Plaintiff discloses her residence by sworn affidavit" and "Plaintiff has sworn that Cook County is convenient for her" (`opus55-low/response.md`, lines 128 and 147). Exhibit 1, meanwhile, has a blank signature line and "Dated: ______" (lines 369–370). That is the specific misrepresentation risk, and it is a stronger locator than "without placeholders."
- **The rubric author's own source does not support a plaintiff's affidavit.** The source note cites Bradbury p. 3. There, the plaintiff's single affidavit came "from a registered nurse employed by the law firm representing the plaintiff" (PDF p. 3, last paragraph; confirmed in the pinned PDF), not from the plaintiff. The authority for a plaintiff's own affidavit is *Guerine*, which the criterion does not cite.
- **The companion criterion shows how the sequence was built.** Criterion 12 requires that Sara's affidavit say DuPage is inconvenient "as required under Langenhorst." *Langenhorst* says only that "no affidavits have been filed stating that St. Clair County would be an inconvenient forum for any of the witnesses," and that the defendant "cannot assert that the plaintiff's chosen forum is inconvenient to the plaintiff" (Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, PDF pp. 7, 10). The rubric thus treats Sara's affidavit as a vehicle for facts the record does not supply. This shows that criterion 11 is not just asking for routine residence support. It still does not make criterion 11 itself unsatisfiable.

## Defect basis and environment origin

The verdict I recommend is Arguable, so neither field applies. If the Defective verdict stands, **Professional judgment** is the correct basis. Nothing in the record, the law or the cited authority objectively contradicts a requirement that the brief be supported by the client's affidavit. On origin, the defect is better attributed to the task design, an instruction conflict plus a rubric that assumes the affidavit exists, than to "an error in the supplied task materials." "Yes" is defensible only on the view that conflicting instructions count as a materials error. John's items 14 and 17–20 use the same theory, so the fields should be consistent across them.

**Environment.** I agree it is Defective, on firmer grounds than the recorded explanation:
- *Instruction conflict.* The assignment 2 prompt says to "include all affidavits ... you referenced from Assignment #1," and the same prompt says "If a document is not uploaded, it does not exist."
- *Record inconsistencies.* The assignment 1 prompt says the baby was transferred "to the Lurie's Children's Hospital in Cook County," but refers to "the Cook County Hospital Doctors." The complaint says "Cook County Hospital" (Davis Complaint.docx ¶¶ 27, 29). The prompt says he was treated "for the next 23 days until he almost passed away on September 16, 2024," which conflicts with the complaint's allegation of death that day (¶ 29) and with the prompt's own wrongful-death framing. It also says the baby died "last year" in a memo dated 7/20/2026, though the death was in September 2024.
- *Cross-item inconsistency.* Item 16 is the same assignment and records Environment **Correct**, while items 14, 15 and 17–20 record **Defective**. The protocol's environment verdict concerns the assignment's materials "whether or not they touch the sampled criterion," so item 16 should match the others. That is a matter for the consistency pass.

## Suggested revision of the note

> Arguable. The criterion asks only that the brief say it is supported by Sara Davis's affidavit. Unlike the nonparty physicians in item 14, Sara is our client, and her lawyers would ordinarily prepare that affidavit: the partner told us she "resides in Kane County, Illinois" (assignment 1 prompt), the motion says the complaint "does not allege" her residence (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, p. 2 ¶ 9), and in *Guerine* the Kane County plaintiffs answered a transfer motion with their own affidavits (First Am Bank v. Guerine.pdf, p. 7). The prompt also says to "include all affidavits ... you referenced from Assignment #1." A draft that cites "Affidavit of Sara Davis (Ex. 1)" and flags it for her review and signature meets the criterion and is good practice. The problem is that the same prompt says "[i]f a document is not uploaded, it does not exist," and a competent drafter could reasonably decline to cite an affidavit that does not exist, as Luna did. The criterion fails that response. *Bradbury* also gives a strategic reason not to volunteer residence (pp. 4–5).
>
> The Opus run shows the risk this criterion invites. Its brief says "Plaintiff discloses her residence by sworn affidavit" and "Plaintiff has sworn that Cook County is convenient for her," but Exhibit 1 is unsigned and undated. It also has Sara swear to facts found nowhere in the record, including that the transfer was "solely for my son's medical care" and that she "had not consulted a lawyer" (¶¶ 6–7). That goes to the *Bland* point *Bradbury* turned on (p. 4 ¶ 3). Opus met the criterion 2–1 and received the full 5 points. Criterion 12, which requires that her affidavit say DuPage is inconvenient "as required under Langenhorst," has no record basis and misstates *Langenhorst*, but it was not drawn into the sample.
>
> Separately, Sonnet's dissent here rests only on the missing "Assignment #2" label. It did the same on criteria 4, 5 and 22 of this run. Each time the other two judges passed the brief, so the majority result did not change. I am not treating that as a rubric defect: every criterion in the land-use and forum non conveniens matters (90 of 179) names an assignment number the judge cannot see, and that is a benchmark-wide setup issue.

Suggested environment explanation: "The assignment 2 prompt tells the solver to 'include all affidavits ... you referenced from Assignment #1,' none of which exists, while also saying that a document not uploaded 'does not exist.' The record is also internally inconsistent about the transfer hospital (assignment 1 prompt: 'Lurie's Children's Hospital'; Davis Complaint.docx ¶¶ 27, 29: 'Cook County Hospital') and says the baby 'almost passed away' on the date the complaint alleges he died."
