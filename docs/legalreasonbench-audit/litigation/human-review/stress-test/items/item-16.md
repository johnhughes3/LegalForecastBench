# Item 16: lrb-forum-non-conveniens-response-assignment-2 — criterion_18

John's Defective call holds, and the record supports it more strongly than his note says. The -30 penalty fires on any statement that the supplied 2023 Statistical Summary reports a 60.5% Cook County clearance rate. The report does report that figure, the defendants' motion rests its congestion argument "principally" on it, and the rubric's own assignment-1 twin (criterion 25) shows the author meant to penalize naming the figure at all, even with an "overall" qualifier. The one field that needs attention is Environment. John recorded it as Correct, but he recorded the same assignment's environment as Defective in items 14, 15 and 17–20, and the protocol makes that verdict a property of the assignment. That change affects only the environment table, not the estimate.

## Strongest objections

1. **The criterion targets only an unqualified statement, not a rebuttal.** A defender could read "States ... that the 2023 Illinois Annual Report Statistics report a 60.5% clearance rate in Cook County" as aimed at a response that presents 60.5% as *the* Cook County clearance rate, implicitly the relevant one, and not at a response that labels it as the all-case figure and rebuts it. On that reading the native judges misapplied a sound criterion, and the protocol keeps a judge's misapplication separate from a criterion defect. *Evidence against:* the criterion contains no "relies on," "concedes" or "without qualification" language. The native instruction tells the judge to "Answer Pass only if the response DOES commit this failure" ([pinned `criterion_18.toml`](https://huggingface.co/datasets/micro1-inc/LegalReasonBench/blob/1bfa904c8f255925baf2610c0f86aa7c05d94ed9/tasks/lrb-forum-non-conveniens-response/steps/assignment-2/tests/raw_correctness/criterion_18.toml)), and the judge sees nothing that limits the failure to unqualified reliance. Most decisively, the assignment-1 companion, criterion 25, penalizes a memo that "Mentions ... that Cook County's overall clearance rate is 60.5%". That wording already includes the qualifier, and its justification says the figure "is not applicable to Civil Cases." So the author meant to penalize naming the number, even when it is labeled as the overall rate. Six of six judge calls in this matter's assignment-2 runs read the criterion that way. *Answered by the note?* Partly. John notes the parallel with criterion 25 but does not use its "overall" wording to rule out the narrow reading.
2. **A careful brief need not repeat the opponent's bad number, so a competent response can avoid the penalty.** A brief can write "Defendants' all-case statistic" without printing 60.5%. *Evidence against:* the protocol makes a criterion Defective when it "rejects an approach a careful litigator would adopt on this record." Quoting a figure the movant has put before the court, with a citation to Exhibit C and a request for judicial notice (motion PDF p. 5–6, §V.C), and then showing why it is the wrong measure is ordinary advocacy. It may well be the most effective way to answer the point. The opposition also has to address the movant's claim that the figure "does not depend on that reporting change" (motion PDF p. 6), and that is hard to do without identifying the figure. *Answered by the note?* Yes. John addresses the "don't raise bad facts" view directly.
3. **Luna's response conceded the figure, which is what the penalty is meant to catch.** Luna wrote that the report "gives Defendants some support: the all-case clearance rate was 60.5%" and that "Plaintiff does not contend these statistics show Cook is less congested" ([Luna response](../../../model-runs/lrb-forum-non-conveniens-response-assignment-2/gpt6luna-xhigh/response.md), "Court congestion"). It never gave the civil-category rates (criteria 19 and 20, 0/3 each). A defender could say Luna is a fair example of the failure. *Response:* even granting that, the Opus response shows the misgrade. It names the figure only to attack it, under the heading "Defendants rely on the wrong statistic, and it is affected by the reporting caveat," and it then gives the civil rates of 97.9% and 96.3% (criteria 19 and 20, 3/3 each). It still loses 30 points ([Opus response](../../../model-runs/lrb-forum-non-conveniens-response-assignment-2/opus55-low/response.md), §IV.C.3.c–d; [run record](../../../model-runs/lrb-forum-non-conveniens-response-assignment-2/opus55-low/README.md)). *Answered by the note?* Partly. The source line describes Luna accurately, but the note's main sentence says both models explained why the figure was "less relevant than civil-case data." That fits Opus and does not quite fit Luna.
4. **Basis should be Unambiguous objective error.** The criterion penalizes an accurate statement of a supplied document, and the rubric's own source note cites the page where the figure appears. *Response:* a rubric can legitimately penalize an accurate statement for strategic or relevance reasons. The defect is that this penalty also reaches a permissible rebuttal, which is a professional-judgment point about advocacy. Professional judgment is the better basis. *Answered by the note?* Implicitly. The note frames the issue as what a competent opposition would do.

## Stronger arguments for the call

- **The rubric's assignment-1 twin proves the author's intent.** Assignment-1 criterion 25 penalizes mentioning Cook County's "overall clearance rate is 60.5%". The author penalized the figure even with the qualifier that the narrow reading would rely on. The justification ("not applicable to Civil Cases") shows the author thought the right response was to use civil data. Using civil data persuasively means contrasting it with the figure the movant used.
- **The prompt carries the figure forward.** Assignment 2 says "Please include all affidavits and information you referenced from Assignment #1." The assignment-1 prompt asked the solver to note, for each of the motion's arguments, "what's strong about it, what's weak," so a good assignment-1 memo addresses the 60.5% argument.
- **The record gives a second rebuttal that requires naming the figure.** The motion says the clearance-rate comparison "does not depend on that reporting change" (motion PDF p. 6). The report's Cook County note goes on to say "There will also be noticeable differences in the number of cases filed and closed in 2023 when compared to prior years" (2023 Annual Report Statistical Summary.pdf, PDF p. 19, note under the caseload table). The clearance rate is "the number of closed cases divided by the sum of new filed and reinstated cases" (id., PDF p. 21, note). So the 60.5% figure depends directly on the inputs the caveat flags. Opus made exactly this point and was penalized for it.
- **The supplied authority treats congestion as a weak factor that requires case-specific data.** Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, PDF p. 11 (majority, quoting *Guerine*, 198 Ill. 2d at 517): "Court congestion is a relatively insignificant factor, especially where the record does not show the other forum would resolve the case more quickly." The Garman dissent's account of *Dawdy* (same file, PDF pp. 13–14) shows the court comparing AOIC figures for "jury actions for damages in excess of $50,000" and time from filing to verdict, which are category-specific measures. A response that contrasts the movant's all-case number with those measures is correct under the supplied law.
- **Weight.** At -30 against 73 positive points (41%), the penalty is outsized. It can wipe out the entire congestion rebuttal and much more, so the misgrade matters.

## Defect basis and environment origin

- **Basis: Professional judgment.** The supplied report is accurate, and the criterion's premise that the all-case figure is the wrong comparator is correct. The defect is that the penalty, as written, also reaches the standard and likely best way of making that very point: name the movant's figure and show why it is the wrong one. Whether a brief should repeat the number is a question of advocacy judgment, not one the record or the law settles.
- **Origin: No.** The figure is correctly transcribed from p. 21, and the motion quotes it accurately. Nothing in the supplied materials causes this defect. It lies in the criterion's wording alone.
- **Environment (assignment-level): Defective** on the missing-affidavit ground already recorded for this assignment in items 14, 15 and 17–20. That ground does not touch criterion 18. Minor record inconsistencies also exist: the complaint and motion use case No. 2025 L 5841 although the prompt says the case was filed in June 2026; the prompt says the baby died "last year" when the death date is September 2024 and the memo is dated July 2026; and the prompt says "until he almost passed away." None of these changes what a response must do, and none is needed for the environment verdict.

## Suggested revision of the note

> The rubric penalizes (-30, against 73 positive points) any statement that the 2023 Illinois Annual Report Statistics report a 60.5% clearance rate in Cook County. That document is in the environment and does report 60.5% as Cook County's clearance rate for all case types (2023 Annual Report Statistical Summary.pdf, PDF p. 21); the civil-category rates are 97.9% for Cook and 96.3% for the 18th Circuit (id., PDF p. 22). The defendants' motion asks the court to take judicial notice of the 60.5% figure and "rel[ies] principally on the clearance-rate comparison" (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, PDF p. 6), so any competent opposition has to answer it, and the clearest answer names the figure and shows it is the wrong measure. It also helps to show that, contrary to the motion, the figure is affected by Cook County's reporting change (the report warns of "noticeable differences in the number of cases filed and closed in 2023," PDF p. 19, and the clearance rate is closed cases divided by filed and reinstated cases, PDF p. 21). Criteria 19 and 20 reward the civil figures that make this rebuttal, and the assignment-1 twin (criterion 25) penalizes even a mention of the "overall" 60.5% rate, so the author meant to bar naming the number, not just relying on it. In our runs both models were penalized 3/3. [Opus 5.5 (low)](../model-runs/lrb-forum-non-conveniens-response-assignment-2/opus55-low/response.md), under "Defendants rely on the wrong statistic," names the figure only to attack it and then gives the 97.9% and 96.3% civil rates, earning criteria 19 and 20. [GPT-6 Luna (xhigh)](../model-runs/lrb-forum-non-conveniens-response-assignment-2/gpt6luna-xhigh/response.md) concedes the figure gives Defendants "some support" but says its weight is "limited" because it "expressly covers all case types" and the report has no comparable Cook time-to-verdict data. Some lawyers prefer not to repeat an adverse number, but here the other side put it squarely before the court, and a penalty that fires on a correct rebuttal misgrades a competent response.
