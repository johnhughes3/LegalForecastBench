# Item 19: lrb-forum-non-conveniens-response-assignment-2 — criterion_8

Bottom line: John's call (Defective, professional judgment, arising from the environment) is defensible and I would not change any field, but the note leans on a premise that is weaker than it looks. "The prompt does not instruct the model to create one" is hard to sustain: the assignment 2 prompt says "Please include all affidavits and information you referenced from Assignment #1," the assignment 1 rubric (criterion 13) rewards recommending affidavits from the five Cook County doctors, and the prompt then lists their names. The stronger ground is different: criterion 8 presupposes that an executed affidavit from Dr. Montgomery exists, so it passes a brief that represents an unsigned form as sworn testimony and fails a truthful response that relies on what counsel actually has, and it attributes to *Langenhorst* a requirement the opinion does not impose. I recommend a note-only revision.

Item 14's note, which this one adopts, adds the reasoning: "include" means include documents referenced in the environment, not generate new ones; a nonparty physician decides what he will swear to; the normal approach is a draft with clearly marked placeholders; and Opus's brief creates a real risk that a lawyer files a brief claiming support from affidavits that have not been sworn.

## Strongest objections

1. **The prompt does ask for the affidavits.** The assignment 2 prompt says: "Please include all affidavits and information you referenced from Assignment #1," and immediately follows with "The names of the physicians from Cook County we have spoken to are:" and the five names (`tasks/lrb-forum-non-conveniens-response-assignment-2/instruction.md`, current assignment 2). The assignment 1 rubric shows what "affidavits ... you referenced" was meant to capture: criterion 13 rewards a memo that "States ... that Plaintiff should obtain affidavits from the 5 Cook County Hospital Doctors ... that it would be inconvenient for them to testify in DuPage County," and criteria 10 and 11 (10 and 15 points) reward recommending affidavits from Sara Davis (pinned assignment 1 `rubric.json`). The assignment 1 email supplies the substance: the doctors "are all Cook County residents and are willing to testify, however, it would be inconvenient for them to take time away from their practice/at the hospital to testify in DuPage rather than Cook County." Read together, the partner is asking the associate to prepare the affidavits the memo recommended. The reconciliation makes this argument and calls drafting them for signature "ordinary litigation practice." The "does not exist" sentence sits in the paragraph that limits sources ("Do not rely on, reference, or infer any legal authority, factual information, or data beyond what is contained in these uploaded files"). It most naturally governs what the solver may rely on as authority or fact, not whether it may draft a new document for a witness to sign.
   - *Does the note answer it?* Partly. Item 14's note reads "include" as "include documents that were referenced in the environment." That reading has some textual support: the only affidavits in the record are Defendants' Exhibits A and B (Smith and the hospital administrator, `Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf`, PDF pp. 8–11), and the assignment 1 rubric rewards discussing them (criteria 1–3). But a plaintiff's response would not "include" the defendants' affidavits, and the list of names that follows points the other way. The note does not mention assignment 1 criterion 13. I would drop the claim that the prompt does not ask for affidavits and rest the defect on what the criterion presupposes (objection 2 and "Stronger arguments").

2. **The criterion credits the careful response John describes.** Criterion 8 asks only that the response "Identifies ... that the affidavit by Caleb Montgomery indicates that DuPage County would be an inconvenient forum." It does not require the brief to say the affidavit is executed. A response that drafts a proposed Montgomery affidavit, labels it for the witness's review and signature, and cites it in the brief with a bracketed note that execution is pending would probably satisfy the judges. All three judges passed Opus's brief because its Exhibit 4 form contains the inconvenience statement (¶ 8), not because of the "executed" claim. On this view, the criterion does not misgrade the placeholder response John endorses. Opus's false statement that "Each affidavit is separately signed" is a flaw in Opus's response that no criterion penalizes, not a defect in criterion 8. GPT-6 Luna's response, which John prefers in item 14, does not follow the partner's direction to include affidavits. It also says the record "does not set out each physician's ... willingness to appear" (`model-runs/.../gpt6luna-xhigh/response.md`, line 52), which the assignment 1 email contradicts ("They are all willing to testify"). A defender would say Luna is not the benchmark of a competent response and that the item is at most Arguable.
   - *Does the note answer it?* Not directly. Item 14's note argues that the right approach is placeholders but does not explain why criterion 8 would fail a placeholder response. The best answers are in "Stronger arguments" below. The criterion's wording ("the affidavit by Caleb Montgomery") treats a completed affidavit as given, and so does its sibling criterion 3 ("States ... that the response is supported by the affidavit of Caleb Montgomery," item 17). The criterion therefore passes the wrong answer on the very point it covers, which the protocol counts ("pass one that is wrong"). It also fails a truthful, filing-ready alternative that the uploaded *Bradbury* endorses. These answers keep Defective defensible. The objection is strong enough that a second reviewer could reasonably record Arguable (see "Recommended fields").

3. **Is this an environment defect at all?** The record is not objectively wrong because it lacks plaintiff affidavits. The rubric expects the solver to draft them. On that reading the defect, if there is one, lies in the criterion's wording, so origin would be No.
   - *Does the note answer it?* Partly. Origin Yes is still defensible on a better ground than the one recorded. The task materials contradict themselves: they instruct the brief to "include all affidavits" that do not exist while declaring that a document not uploaded "does not exist." The protocol's environment test also covers "task materials that direct work product a competent litigator should not put before a court," and a prompt that directs a filing-ready brief "includ[ing]" witness affidavits nobody has signed fits that. The recorded environment explanation should say this rather than "the prompt does not instruct the model to create one."

4. **"The record shows have not been signed."** The record does not show that the affidavits are unsigned. It is silent apart from the partner's "we have spoken to." The accurate statement is that no plaintiff-side affidavit is in the record and the prompt says a document not uploaded "does not exist." This is a wording point and does not change the call.

I found no misreading of who bears the burden, no overlooked stipulation, and no closed-universe problem with the authority the criterion names: *Langenhorst* is an uploaded attachment.

## Stronger arguments for the call

- **The criterion misstates *Langenhorst*.** The criterion says the Montgomery affidavit shows DuPage would be inconvenient "as required under Langenhorst," citing PDF p. 10. That page says: "In this case, no affidavits have been filed stating that St. Clair County would be an inconvenient forum for any of the witnesses," and then: "We reiterate that the defendant must show that the plaintiff's chosen forum is inconvenient to the defendant and that another forum is more convenient to all parties" (`Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf`, PDF p. 10, confirmed in the pinned PDF). The missing affidavits in *Langenhorst* were the movants' failure to show that the plaintiff's chosen forum was inconvenient. The opinion places the burden on the defendant (PDF p. 7: "The burden is on the defendant to show that relevant private and public interest factors 'strongly favor' the defendant's choice of forum"). It imposes no requirement that a plaintiff's witnesses swear the transferee forum is inconvenient. The matter's citation check marks this citation MISCHARACTERIZED on both checks (`citation-check/results/lrb-forum-non-conveniens-response.md`, FNC-0053). The rubric author applied the correct reading in assignment 1, criteria 2–3, which ask whether Dr. Smith's affidavit says Cook County is inconvenient. Here the author turned it around. Illinois Supreme Court Rule 187(b) confirms that opposing affidavits are optional: FNC motions "may be supported and opposed by affidavit." The criterion therefore rewards repeating a misstatement of law, and it can fail a response that correctly declines to call the affidavit "required." This supports John's verdict on an independent basis (b). I would not change the recorded basis on that ground, because judges seem unlikely to insist on the "required" wording, so its effect on grading is uncertain.
- **The uploaded authority supports a truthful alternative that criterion 8 fails.** In *Bradbury*, the plaintiff's opposition "was supported by a single affidavit ... from a registered nurse employed by the law firm representing the plaintiff," identifying 13 treating doctors and nurses as likely witnesses, and denial of transfer was affirmed (`Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf`, PDF p. 3; confirmed in the pinned PDF). A careful associate could file the brief now on an affidavit from counsel recounting the five physicians' stated residence, willingness and inconvenience, while sending the physicians draft affidavits to review, adjust and sign. That brief is truthful and can be filed. Criterion 8 fails it, because it rewards only "the affidavit by Caleb Montgomery." Under the protocol, a criterion that rejects an approach a careful litigator would adopt on this record is Defective.
- **The criterion passes the wrong answer on its own point.** Opus's brief tells the court: "Plaintiff submits sworn affidavits from five named Cook County treating physicians" and "the treating physicians have sworn to these facts themselves" (`model-runs/.../opus55-low/response.md`, lines 174, 181). Its exhibit says: "Each of the five treating physicians has executed an affidavit in the same form ... Each affidavit is separately signed" (line 376), over one form with "[Name]" and "[specialty]" blanks and no execution (lines 386–410). All three judges passed criterion 8 on that basis. Telling a court that nonparty witnesses have sworn to statements they have never seen is a false statement of fact to a tribunal (Ill. R. Prof'l Conduct 3.3(a)(1)). It is not a stylistic lapse.
- **The form overstates the record.** Opus's form says each physician "practice[s] medicine at Cook County Hospital" and has "not been retained by any party as an expert," and it sets out a testimony list. The assignment 1 email says only that the five reviewed the Safe Hospital records, believe Dr. Smith deviated from the standard of care, live in Cook County, are willing to testify, and would find DuPage inconvenient. This illustrates John's point that a nonparty decides what he will swear to.

## Errors in the note

- **"the prompt does not instruct the model to create one"** (note and environment explanation) is contestable at best, given "Please include all affidavits and information you referenced from Assignment #1," the list of names that follows, and assignment 1 criterion 13. It should be reframed (see objection 1).
- **"the record shows have not been signed"** overstates the record. The record contains no plaintiff-side affidavit, and the prompt says non-uploaded documents do not exist. Nothing affirmatively shows that the affidavits are unsigned.
- **Native-run description:** accurate. Opus's exhibit is one unsigned form, and its brief calls the affidavits executed and sworn. Luna names the five as physicians "with whom counsel has spoken" and cites no physician affidavit (line 52).
- **Cross-reference:** "see item 14" carries the reasoning, but item 14's preference for Luna should be qualified, because Luna's response misstates the record on the doctors' willingness to testify (objection 2). That is an item 14 wording point.
- No citation errors. The note cites no authority, and the criterion's *Langenhorst* locator (PDF p. 10) is the right page for the passage the rubric author had in mind.

## Defect basis and environment origin

- **Basis: Professional judgment.** The core defect is a judgment that the criterion credits representing unexecuted nonparty testimony as sworn and fails a truthful alternative. The *Langenhorst* misattribution is an objective legal error on the criterion's face (basis (b)). The matter citation check (FNC-0053) independently classifies it as mischaracterized. It would justify "Unambiguous objective error" only if it changed grading, and on the native runs it did not visibly do so.
- **Origin: Yes.** The defect stems from the task materials. The assignment 2 prompt asks the brief to "include all affidavits" while the same prompt says a document not uploaded "does not exist," and no plaintiff-side affidavit is supplied. The rubric then treats the affidavits as existing.
- **Environment, other objective inconsistencies (supporting the Defective environment verdict).** The assignment 1 email says the baby was transferred to "Lurie's Children's Hospital in Cook County" and "almost passed away on September 16, 2024." The complaint says he was transferred to Cook County Hospital on August 28, 2024 and died there on September 16, 2024 (`Davis Complaint.docx`, ¶¶ 27, 29), as do Defendants' affidavits (`Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf`, PDF p. 9 ¶ 8; PDF p. 10 ¶ 5). The email says the case was filed in June 2026, but the caption number is 2025 L 5841. The motion calls Exhibit B an "Affidavit of [Records Custodian]" (PDF p. 7), but the attached affidavit is the hospital administrator's (PDF p. 10). None of these changes criterion 8.

## Suggested revision of the note

> Same issue as item 14. The criterion rewards a brief that says Dr. Caleb Montgomery's affidavit shows DuPage County would be an inconvenient forum for him. No affidavit from Dr. Montgomery is in the record. The partner asks the associate to "include all affidavits and information you referenced from Assignment #1," and the assignment 1 rubric (criterion 13) rewards recommending affidavits from the five doctors, so drafting one for him to review and sign is fair game. But the prompt also says a document that is not uploaded "does not exist," and the criterion treats a finished affidavit as a given ("the affidavit by Caleb Montgomery"). It therefore pays for a brief that tells the court a nonparty physician has sworn to something he has never seen. Opus 5.5 (low) met it 3/3 with a brief saying "the treating physicians have sworn to these facts themselves" and "Each affidavit is separately signed," over a single unsigned form with "[Name]" blanks (opus55-low/response.md, lines 174–181, 376–410). It also fails a truthful brief that relies on what counsel actually has, such as the law-firm nurse's affidavit identifying the treating witnesses that sufficed in Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, p. 3, with the physicians' own affidavits to follow once they sign. Separately, Langenhorst does not "require" such an affidavit. The passage cited (Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, p. 10) faults the defendants for filing no affidavits that the plaintiff's chosen forum was inconvenient, and the burden is on the defendant (id., p. 7). The matter citation check (FNC-0053) flags the citation as mischaracterized. GPT-6 Luna (xhigh) cited no physician affidavit and scored 0/3. This is one of the missing-affidavit items (14, 15 and 17–20).

Suggested environment explanation:

> The prompt asks the brief to "include all affidavits and information you referenced from Assignment #1" but also says "If a document is not uploaded, it does not exist for purposes of this analysis," and no affidavit from any of the five physicians is supplied. The task thus directs a filing-ready brief resting on witness affidavits no one has signed. (The record is also internally inconsistent about the hospital and transfer date: the assignment 1 email says Lurie Children's Hospital, while Davis Complaint.docx ¶¶ 27, 29 and Defendants' affidavits say Cook County Hospital on August 28, 2024.)
