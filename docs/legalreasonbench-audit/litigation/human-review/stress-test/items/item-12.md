# Item 12: lrb-forum-non-conveniens-response, assignment 1 — criterion_2

John's call holds. Criterion 2 is Not defective, and AI reasoning is Wrong. The criterion rests on a correct statement of Illinois law: the defendant must show that the plaintiff's chosen forum is inconvenient *to the defendant* (Langenhorst, 219 Ill. 2d at 444, 450, reaffirmed in *Fennell*, 2012 IL 113812, ¶ 20). Dr. Smith's affidavit says only that DuPage would be "substantially more convenient" than Cook, which is a comparative claim. The task asks the associate to flag points to target, and this gap is an obvious one. The strongest objection is that Smith's affidavit is comparative and names Cook, unlike the *Langenhorst* affidavits, which spoke only to the transferee county. That objection goes to how much the point is worth, not whether a competent memo should flag it. The note could be tightened and given stronger record cites, but no field should change.

## Strongest objections

**1. Smith's affidavit is comparative and names Cook, which distinguishes it from the *Langenhorst* affidavits (Sol's ground, in its strongest form).** The *Langenhorst* individual defendants swore only that "It would not be inconvenient for me to appear in Clinton County," and the court noted that "[t]he affidavits did not indicate these defendants would be inconvenienced by appearing at a trial in St. Clair County" (Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, PDF p. 3; 219 Ill. 2d at 437). Those affidavits said nothing about the chosen forum. Smith's ¶ 7 does: DuPage "would be substantially more convenient for me, both as a party and as a fact witness, than trial in the Circuit Court of Cook County" (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, PDF p. 9, Ex. A ¶ 7). The *Langenhorst* plaintiff's counsel even suggested that a comparative affidavit was what the defendants had been unwilling to sign: "Presumably, they were unwilling to sign an affidavit which suggested that a trip from Indiana to Belleville versus a trip from Indiana to Carlyle would in some fashion be more inconvenient" (PDF p. 3; 219 Ill. 2d at 437–38). A defender could argue that, by *Langenhorst*'s own lights, Smith's affidavit supplies relative inconvenience and the remaining weakness is only its lack of concrete burden facts.

*Answer.* That passage is counsel's argument, recited in the background, not the court's holding. The court's own test is stricter. Defendants must show "the plaintiff's chosen forum is inconvenient to the defendant," and the court faulted defendants who "have not claimed any inconvenience whatsoever in trying this case in St. Clair County" (PDF p. 10; 219 Ill. 2d at 450). "Substantially more convenient than Cook" does not say that litigating in Cook is inconvenient. Cook may be perfectly convenient and DuPage more so. The criterion also requires only that the memo *identify* this gap as a point to target, which the prompt asks for. It does not require the memo to say the point is dispositive. A response can flag the gap and acknowledge that the defense will call ¶ 7 relative inconvenience. *Does the note answer it?* Partly. The note answers the comparative-versus-inconvenient point in substance. It does not address the 437–38 distinction, which is the best version of Sol's argument.

**2. Real-world practice in the closest analog treats relative convenience as enough.** In *Adamian v. Balash*, 2024 IL App (1st) 231876, the closest real case (identified in `real-cases.json`), the First District reversed the denial of a Cook-to-DuPage transfer in an obstetric case. Its analysis is framed as relative convenience. It relied in part on the defendant doctor's affidavit that "it would be far more convenient for [her] to testify in Du Page County" (¶ 19), and it made no separate finding that Cook was inconvenient to the movants. An experienced Cook County litigator might therefore expect the point to carry little weight in adjacent counties.

*Answer.* The assignment is closed-universe, and *Adamian* is not supplied. Under the protocol, outside authority can show that supplied law is wrong, but it does not enlarge what the solver may use. *Adamian* shows no such error. The Illinois Supreme Court restated the *Langenhorst* rule in 2012 (*Fennell*, ¶ 20, citing 219 Ill. 2d at 444), and an appellate decision cannot displace it. *Langenhorst* itself shows the adjacent-county point cutting the plaintiff's way: "We discern no inconvenience for the defendants to try this case in St. Clair County when Clinton and St. Clair Counties are adjacent" (PDF p. 10; 219 Ill. 2d at 450). Even if the point is weak in practice, it is still a point to flag. *Does the note answer it?* No. The note does not consider *Adamian*, but it would not change the verdict.

## Stronger arguments for the call

- **The criterion tracks *Langenhorst*'s own words.** "The affidavits did not indicate these defendants would be inconvenienced by appearing at a trial in St. Clair County" (PDF p. 3; 219 Ill. 2d at 437). Criterion 2 asks for exactly that observation about Smith: his affidavit "does not indicate that Cook County would be an inconvenient forum for him." John cites only the rule statement at 444. The 437 passage and the application at 450 are better support.
- ***Langenhorst* applies the rule against affidavits like this one.** "[N]o affidavits have been filed stating that St. Clair County would be an inconvenient forum … Not only have defendants not claimed any inconvenience whatsoever in trying this case in St. Clair County … We reiterate that the defendant must show that the plaintiff's chosen forum is inconvenient to the defendant" (PDF p. 10; 219 Ill. 2d at 450). The same page notes that the counties are adjacent, as Cook and DuPage are.
- **The motion states the standard its own evidence does not meet.** Defendants quote the rule that a defendant "must show that the plaintiff's chosen forum is inconvenient to the defendant" (Motion PDF p. 3). The only assertion that "Cook County is convenient to no Defendant" (Motion PDF p. 4, § IV.A) is unsworn argument of counsel. Neither affidavit says it. The motion calls itself verified, but the supplied copy has no verification page; only counsel's signature appears (PDF p. 7). For a memo that is supposed to "flag any points we should target," this is close to a required observation.
- **The law is current.** *Fennell v. Illinois Central R.R. Co.*, 2012 IL 113812, ¶ 20, restates the rule and cites *Langenhorst* at 444. The criterion therefore does not rest on an incorrect legal proposition (basis (b)).
- **The companion criterion is consistent.** Criterion 3 makes the same point about the Safe Hospital affidavit (Ex. B), which says nothing comparative at all. The rubric consistently treats chosen-forum inconvenience as a gap in both affidavits.
- **The native grades support the call.** The Claude Opus 5.5 memo made the point, citing both *Langenhorst* passages (PDF pp. 3 and 10), and all three judges passed it. The Luna memo, which treated ¶ 7 as a defense strength, failed 2 to 1. The criterion separates a memo that sees the gap from one that does not.

## Errors in the note

- **Slight mischaracterization of Sol.** The note says Sol "complained that the doctor's affidavit clearly does indicate that DuPage County would be more convenient." Sol's flag was Arguable, not a claim that anything was clear. Its argument was that the comparative statement "can reasonably convey comparative inconvenience" of Cook, so a critique should focus on missing burden facts. The point everyone agrees on is that the affidavit says DuPage is more convenient; the dispute is whether that conveys Cook inconvenience.
- **Mild overstatement about *Langenhorst*.** The note says the opinion "expressly teases out this distinction between an argument that another location would be 'more' convenient … versus proof that the plaintiff's chosen location is actually inconvenient." The opinion's two-part rule does draw that line (chosen forum inconvenient to the defendant; other forum more convenient to all parties). But the *Langenhorst* affidavits said the transferee county was "not inconvenient." They were not "more convenient than" statements, so the court did not apply the rule to a comparative affidavit. "Draws" is more accurate than "expressly teases out … 'more' convenient."
- **Extra-record geography.** The O'Hare remark is accurate: most of the airport is in Cook County, and a southwest portion extends into DuPage. It is outside the supplied record and does no work in the analysis. Harmless, but it could be cut.
- **Citations check out.** Exhibit A ¶ 7 is quoted correctly; it appears on PDF p. 9 of the motion. The rubric's "p. 8" is where the affidavit begins. *Langenhorst*, 219 Ill. 2d 430, 848 N.E.2d 927 (2006), is correctly captioned and dated (decided March 2, 2006). The quotation at PDF p. 7 is verbatim, and the pin to 444 is confirmed against the Caselaw Access Project page markers and *Fennell* ¶ 20. The statement that the reconciliation overturned Sol's flag on this ground is correct.

## Suggested revision of the note

> Rubric is correct as written. Defendants seek to move the case from Cook County to DuPage County, which borders it to the west. Dr. Smith's affidavit says trial in DuPage "would be substantially more convenient for me, both as a party and as a fact witness, than trial in the Circuit Court of Cook County" (Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, PDF p. 9, Ex. A ¶ 7). That is a comparative statement. It never says that litigating in Cook would be inconvenient for him, and neither does the Safe Hospital affidavit (Ex. B). The rubric wants the memo to flag that gap, and it is right to. Under the uploaded *Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern Railway Co.*, 219 Ill. 2d 430, 848 N.E.2d 927 (2006), "[t]he defendant must show that the plaintiff's chosen forum is inconvenient to the defendant and that another forum is more convenient to all parties" (PDF p. 7, 219 Ill. 2d at 444). The court applied that rule to defendants whose affidavits "did not indicate these defendants would be inconvenienced by appearing at a trial in" the plaintiff's chosen county (PDF p. 3, at 437) and who had "not claimed any inconvenience whatsoever in trying this case" there (PDF p. 10, at 450). The motion even quotes the same rule (PDF p. 3); its assertion that "Cook County is convenient to no Defendant" (PDF p. 4) is unsworn argument of counsel. Sol argued that a comparative statement can reasonably convey that Cook is inconvenient, and the defense will say so. But "more convenient elsewhere" is not "inconvenient here," and the criterion asks only that the memo identify the gap as a point to target, not that the memo call it dispositive. The later reconciliation overturned Sol's flag on this ground.
>
> Sources: Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf, PDF pp. 3–4, 9 (Ex. A ¶ 7); Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, PDF pp. 3, 7, 10 (219 Ill. 2d at 437, 444, 450); see also *Fennell v. Illinois Central R.R. Co.*, 2012 IL 113812, ¶ 20 (restating the rule).
