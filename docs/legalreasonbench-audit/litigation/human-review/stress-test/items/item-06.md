## Strongest objections

1. **The record arguably supports applicant "action or inaction".** The applicant in DCI2018-10023 was Lee County, acting through its utilities and solid-waste departments. The staff report quoted in the Petition says: "Applicant submitted a zoning request in 2018 … Staff withdrew the request to address public concerns and evaluate design alternatives" (Petition.docx, passage quoting SR23 pp. 3–4). The remand read into the record was a *joint* applicant/staff request: "Following the coordination between the applicant and staff we jointly request that DCI2018-10023 be remanded back to the staff" (Petition.docx, passage quoting Tr5 pp. 2–3). Nothing then happened on that application until 2022–2023, and the County's own presentation says the project was "re-initiated by LCU" in 2022 (LPA presentation, "Project Need & History"). A defender could argue that the applicant's own request, followed by years of inaction, is exactly the "action or inaction of the Applicant" that § 4.1.B(1) contemplates. **Does the note answer it?** No. The note says "There's nothing in the environment to support that claim," which overstates the point: the record does not *establish* applicant fault, but it does not rule it out either. This objection does not save the criterion (see objection 2 and the stronger arguments), but it does weaken the note's sole stated rationale.

2. **A county advocate could take the position that the case was dormant or withdrawn by operation of law.** The memo is written for the County. The Order's predetermination ground rests partly on events occurring "all while DCI2018-10023 remained an active case" (Order p. 4), so a county lawyer might want to argue the 2019 case was effectively dead long before 2023. On this reading the criterion merely "takes a reasonable side" and would be Not defective or at most Arguable under the protocol. **Does the note answer it?** Not expressly. The answer is that the criterion requires a flat statement that the case "was considered withdrawn on February 13, 2020" *under § 4.1.B*. That is not an advocacy option; it is a legal conclusion the cited provision cannot produce, because § 4.1 does not reach a Hearing Examiner remand to staff. An "it was dormant" argument is available, but a "considered withdrawn under § 4.1.B on February 13, 2020" statement is not. A memo that outlines the Order accurately, as item 1 of the prompt requires, must report the court's "active case" finding, and the criterion fails every such memo. Both native runs scored 0/3 on this criterion: GPT-6 Luna did not address the point, and Claude Opus 5.5 reported the record's September 13, 2023 date.

3. **Version of the code.** The criterion cites the "(2025)" code for a 2019–2020 event. A critic of John's call might say he relied on a version not in force. **Answered**, in item 1: the § 4.1 text as amended through 11/17/15, the version in force in 2019–20, is identical to the 8/2/22 text. The prompt also directs analysis under "the versions of statutes, regulations, and ordinances in effect as of January 1, 2025" (upstream-instruction.md, Prompt), which is the 8/2/22 version. So the "(2025)" cite is not itself an error, and the version question makes no difference to the outcome. I re-read both archived versions: § 4.1, § 4.1.A, § 4.1.B and § 4.1.B(1) match word for word.

4. **Another withdrawal rule could apply.** Lee County's LDC sufficiency-review procedure deems an incomplete application "considered withdrawn" if requested supplements are not supplied within 30 days (as quoted in County sufficiency letters). A defender could say *some* rule withdrew the case early. **Not answered, and immaterial.** That rule governs pre-hearing sufficiency review, not a post-hearing remand. Nothing in the record ties it to February 13, 2020. And the criterion names § 4.1.B and that date specifically. I could not read the current LDC text; see "What could not be verified".

5. **Protocol application: is it truly "unambiguous objective error"?** If the only ground were missing applicant fault, a reviewer might call the basis professional judgment, because fault is a factual inference on which reasonable lawyers could differ (objection 1). **Not answered in the note**, but the scope limitation in § 4.1's heading and first sentence is a textual, objective bar, and the Order's own finding contradicts the expected answer. With those grounds stated, the objective-error basis is secure.

## Stronger arguments for the call

- **Threshold inapplicability (objective, textual).** AC-2-6 § 4.1 is headed "Applications or Appeals Remanded by the Board to the Hearing Examiner" and provides that "[t]he following procedures apply to applications or appeals remanded to the Hearing Examiner by the Board." Its deadlines run from "the remand vote" (§ 4.1.A(1)–(2)), and "Board directed remands" are handled as Department-requested (§ 4.1.A(3)). The DCI2018-10023 remand was the reverse. At the Hearing Examiner stage, before any Board action, the case was "remanded back to the staff" on a joint request (Petition.docx, Tr5 pp. 2–3 passage). Hearing Examiner Collins then described what staff would do (Tr5 p. 4). The County's own LPA presentation describes it the same way: "Staff requested the CFPD remanded back to Staff for additional analysis." There was no Board remand, so § 4.1.B's six-month deadline never started to run, and the applicant-fault question never arises. Both blind reports (Sol F2; Opus O1), the reconciliation (R1), and the matter citation check (refs 9–11) all identified this ground.
- **The expected answer contradicts the Order the memo must outline.** The Order finds the County's predetermination conduct occurred "all while DCI2018-10023 remained an active case, thereby preventing public contact with their county commissioners" (Order p. 4; confirmed against the page image in the pinned DOCX). The prompt asks the memo first to "outline[] the Order and case history". A memo that reports the case as withdrawn in February 2020 misstates the Order's premise without flagging that it is contesting it.
- **The record gives only two dates, and neither is February 2020.** The Petition says "Procedurally, the 2019 zoning case was left open until September 13, 2023," and "The HEX Withdrawal for the 2019 case was issued September 13, 2023." It also quotes Ellis correcting County testimony that the case "was withdrawn in 2022." The County's own September 2023 presentation lists "March 2022 – Previous CFPD rezoning petition withdrawn." No source, record or extra-record, puts withdrawal in February 2020.
- **The trigger date is unsupported too.** § 4.1.B counts six months from "the date the remand order was rendered," not from the hearing at which the remand was requested. The record gives August 13, 2019 as the hearing date, but no rendition date for any written remand order. The source note's arithmetic ("August 13, 2029 … six months after that is February 13, 2020") assumes the two are the same.
- **The criterion passes wrong work.** A memo that adopts the February 13, 2020 date earns 5 points for an incorrect legal conclusion, and the companion −15 penalty (item 1) punishes the accurate alternative. The pair compounds the misgrade.

## Errors in the note

- **Overstatement: "There's nothing in the environment to support that claim."** The record shows the applicant (the County) joining the remand request and the case lying idle for years (Petition.docx, Tr5 and SR23 passages). That does not establish fault under § 4.1.B(1), but it is something, and a defender will point to it. Better: "the record does not establish."
- **Unsupported as phrased: "Neither party in the actual litigation contends …".** Item 1's evidence for the County's position is a September 25, 2023 Local Planning Agency presentation, not a filing in No. 24-CA-1479 or No. 6D2025-2926, and I did not locate either side's briefs. Nothing I found shows either side contending applicant fault or a February 2020 withdrawal. Still, the statement should be tied to the sources actually read ("neither the petitioners' record nor the County's own 2023 presentation …").
- **Omission of the dispositive ground.** The note states only the applicant-fault condition and treats it as the reason § 4.1.B(1) does not "apply." Inapplicability follows independently and more firmly from § 4.1's limitation to Board-to-Hearing-Examiner remands.
- **Cross-reference.** "Wrong for the same reasons as item 1" is accurate as far as it goes, but item 1 concerns the companion penalty. This item's note should stand on its own so that a reader of the reward criterion sees its grounds.
- No pin-cite, quotation or link errors were found in the note itself. The quoted § 4.1.B(1) language is accurate in both versions, and the "(2025)" year matches the prompt's January 1, 2025 instruction.
