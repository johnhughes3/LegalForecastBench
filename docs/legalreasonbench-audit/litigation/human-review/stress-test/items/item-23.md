## Strongest objections

1. **"The criterion means only that the record does not *conclusively* establish who fired first, which is consistent with Patton's burden of production."** A defender could read "lack of evidence proving" as "nothing proves it beyond dispute." On that reading the government argues that Patton, who bears "the initial burden of producing sufficient evidence to support the instruction" (Kuhse.pdf, slip op. at 9), cannot show he was fired on first. The objection fails on the record and on the standard. The record is the government's own Facts, and they state as fact that Nelson fired first: "Nelson had his car parked near 30th and Clark Streets, and had hidden in the bushes. ... He opened fire as the Trailblazer approached." (Facts.pdf, PDF p. 7.) "He" can only be Nelson. The threshold question is also not proof but whether there is "an 'underlying evidentiary foundation' ... such that a reasonable person could conclude that the evidence supported the defendant's position" (Poe.pdf, slip op. at 4), or "substantial evidence" (Kuhse.pdf at 9; Ellison.pdf at 9). The government's own statement that Nelson opened fire is that foundation on this element. An AUSA could not candidly tell the court that no evidence shows who fired first when the government's brief says otherwise. *Answered by the note?* Yes, in substance ("the supplied documents state that Nelson did so"). The note does not address the burden framing.

2. **"Other passages make the sequence uncertain."** Facts p. 7 says Patton and Shafer rolled the windows down before Nelson fired, and p. 8 says "Shots were also fired at the Trailblazer. (R. Doc. 389-90, 389-91.)" Neither passage creates doubt about who fired first. Rolling the windows down is preparation, not a shot. The p. 8 sentence is consistent with Nelson's opening fire. Nothing else in the accumulated attachments speaks to the order of shots. Patton State Plea.pdf, PDF p. 5, admits a conspiracy "by planning with others to engage in a shooting," which says nothing about order, and Kuhse is a domestic-assault case. *Answered by the note?* Not expressly, but the note's reading is correct.

3. **"The harness let the solver use the web, so it could find the real Mallory opinion."** The shared upstream preamble says "You also have internet access via OpenCode's `websearch` tool; use it to research any statutes, regulations, cases ... that are not supplied" (`upstream-instruction.md`). The assignment prompt overrides this: "Do not rely on, reference, or infer any legal authority, factual information, or data beyond what is contained in these uploaded files." `input.json` records the research universe as `closed-universe`, and the protocol says outside authority "does not enlarge what the solver was permitted to use." The task also moves the trial to April 2025 (Facts.pdf, PDF p. 11), so the real June 2024 opinion could not describe this fictional record. *Answered by the note?* Partly ("closed book"). The note does not mention the preamble conflict, and it does not need to.

4. **"The defect arises from the environment" (against John's Yes).** This is the objection that carries weight. The protocol records Yes only "if the defect stems from an error in the supplied task materials rather than in the criterion alone." Facts p. 7 is not an error. It is clear, internally consistent, and the rest of the rubric relies on it. Criterion 9's own source note says the group rolled the windows down "before being fired upon," and criterion 3 credits saying "Nelson also fired a gun." What is wrong is the criterion, which imports a premise from outside the supplied record. That premise appears in the assignment 1 penalty justifications (criteria 25 and 27) and in the Eighth Circuit's opinion. John's own note puts it this way: "a rubric that would be correct for the real-world case is incorrectly applied to this task environment." In item 10, a criterion that imported a real-case proposition onto changed task facts was coded origin **No** and environment **Correct**. John's item 23 note cites item 10 as the parallel. The two items should be coded alike. *Answered by the note?* No. The note does not explain why a divergence from the real case is an "error in the supplied task materials."

5. **"The Facts may not differ from the real record at all."** John's environment explanation says the supplied Facts differ "from the real record." That is not established. Facts.pdf reads as the government's actual Eighth Circuit statement of facts with the dates moved. It cites trial-transcript volumes and "R. Doc." entries, and its abbreviations footnote lists "M. Br.," "P. Br.," and "G. Br." (PDF p. 1, n.1). The Eighth Circuit itself said the shell casings, bullet fragments and bullet holes "are consistent with someone else shooting before Patton did" (slip op. at 4). So the real government brief may well have said that Nelson opened fire, as the supplied Facts do. The court's "it is unclear who shot first" describes the trial evidence. It does not show that the government's brief said something different. That brief is not public (as John's item 21 note records), so the comparison cannot be made. Either way, the environment origin is No. A fictional change to the facts is not an error, and a faithful copy is not one either. *Answered by the note?* No.

6. **Environment field consistency.** Items 21 and 22 (assignment 1) use the identical Facts.pdf (the assignment 1 and assignment 2 extractions differ only in the source URL) and record the environment as Correct. If the Facts' account of who fired first were an environment defect, assignment 1's environment would be Defective too. Assignment 2's environment is nonetheless Defective for a different, assignment-specific reason that John recorded in items 24 and 25: the prompt asks only to "supplement your brief" while the verifier reads only response2.md and re-grades five assignment 1 criteria. The protocol makes the environment an assignment-level verdict "whether or not [the errors] touch the sampled criterion." So the verdict for item 23 should remain Defective, with the items 24–25 explanation. *Answered by the note?* No.

## Stronger arguments for the call

- **The rubric's own author concedes the evidence.** The assignment 1 justifications for penalty criteria 25 and 27 say: "Because some evidence supports that Nelson fired first, Patton might have reasonably believed deadly force was necessary." At the instruction stage the question is whether *some* evidence supports the defense (Poe.pdf at 4; Kuhse.pdf at 9). The author therefore concedes the very point that makes "lack of evidence proving who fired first" both inaccurate and beside the point.
- **Companion criteria in the same rubric assume Nelson fired.** Criterion 9's source note: the group "rolled the windows of the Trailblazer down before being fired upon." Criterion 3: "the only evidence of self-defense is that Nelson also fired a gun." The rubric's account of the facts contradicts criterion 4.
- **The opinion the criterion tracks does not need the point.** *Mallory* affirmed while accepting that the physical evidence was "consistent with someone else shooting before Patton did" (slip op. at 4). It rested on the duty to retreat and the "preplanned drive-by shooting." The better government argument, which criteria 5–9 reward, does not depend on doubt about who fired first. A response that concedes Nelson fired first and argues duty to retreat and preplanning is the stronger brief.
- **The native runs show the misgrading.** GPT-6 Luna (xhigh) acknowledged that "Nelson opened fire as the Trailblazer approached" (`response.md`, line 15). Opus 5.5 (low) treated the first shot as legally immaterial ("who fired the first shot does not answer the question under Iowa law," line 110). Each lost the full 25 points, 0/3 from all three judges, for following the supplied record.
- **Weight.** At 25 of 135 positive points (18.5%), this is tied for the second-largest reward in the assignment.

## Errors in the note

- **Environment explanation:** "differs from the real record" overstates what can be shown. The real trial record and the government's Eighth Circuit brief are not public. What the supplied Facts differ from is the Eighth Circuit's description. The opinion itself reports physical evidence "consistent with someone else shooting before Patton did" (slip op. at 4), so the real record may agree with the supplied Facts.
- **Note:** "there was a dispute concerning who fired first" is a slight paraphrase. The opinion says "it is unclear who shot first" (slip op. at 2). Patton argued that "the other side's decision to shoot first justified his decision to return fire" (slip op. at 3). "Unclear" is the more accurate word.
- **Quotations checked and accurate:** Facts.pdf PDF p. 7 ("He opened fire as the Trailblazer approached," "A rolling gun battle ensued"), confirmed against the pinned PDF; the assignment 2 prompt language; *Mallory*, slip op. at 2 ("though it is unclear who shot first"); assignment 1 criterion 25 justification ("at a minimum, it is unclear which side fired first"). The citation 104 F.4th 15 (8th Cir. June 12, 2024) is confirmed. The note puts "opened fire as the Trailblazer approached" in quotation marks after supplying the name "Raysean Nelson," while the source says "He." That is acceptable.
- **Cross-reference:** The note calls item 10 "the other sampled criterion that carries a real-case rule onto changed task facts," but item 10 was coded origin No and environment Correct. The cross-reference supports No here.
