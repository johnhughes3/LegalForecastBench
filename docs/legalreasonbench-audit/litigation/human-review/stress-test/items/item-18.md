# Item 18: lrb-forum-non-conveniens-response-assignment-2 — criterion_6

Bottom line: John's call (Defective, professional judgment, arising from the environment; AI reasoning Correct; environment Defective) is defensible, and I would not change it, but it is closer than the short note suggests. The strongest objection is that the assignment 2 prompt does tell the solver to "include all affidavits ... you referenced from Assignment #1," and the assignment 1 email and rubric show those were affidavits the solver was expected to recommend obtaining. On that reading, drafting a proposed Vance affidavit is the requested work, and a careful draft marked "to be executed" would meet this criterion. The call is better supported than the note shows: the matter's citation check found the criterion's "as required under Langenhorst" MISCHARACTERIZED on both passes, and the supplied *Bradbury* opinion shows a permissible counsel-side affidavit that this criterion would fail. I recommend a note-only revision. It should drop "the prompt does not instruct the model to create one" and "the record shows have not been signed," which overstate the record, answer the "include all affidavits" objection directly, and add the Langenhorst and Bradbury grounds. Arguable is the most plausible alternative reading. Any shift to it should be reported as a sensitivity analysis together with items 14, 15, 17, 19 and 20, not as a silent edit.

## Strongest objections

1. **The prompt does ask for the affidavits.** Assignment 2 says: "Please include all affidavits and information you referenced from Assignment #1" (`tasks/lrb-forum-non-conveniens-response-assignment-2/instruction.md`, current assignment 2). In assignment 1 the partner wrote that the five Cook County doctors "are all Cook County residents and are willing to testify, however, it would be inconvenient for them to take time away from their practice/at the hospital to testify in DuPage rather than Cook County" (same file, prior assignment 1). The assignment 1 rubric shows what "affidavits ... you referenced" was meant to capture. Criterion 13 rewards stating "that Plaintiff should obtain affidavits from the 5 Cook County Hospital Doctors ... that it would be inconvenient for them to testify in DuPage County," and criteria 10–11 reward recommending that Sara Davis file affidavits (`tasks/lrb-forum-non-conveniens-response-assignment-1/input.json`). The only plaintiff-side affidavits a solver could have "referenced" in assignment 1 are affidavits to be obtained. Drafting proposed affidavits from facts the supervising partner relayed, for the witnesses to review and sign, is ordinary practice. The reconciliation overturned GPT-6 Sol's flag on exactly this ground (`tasks/lrb-forum-non-conveniens-response-assignment-2/reconciliation.md`).
   - *Does the note answer it?* Partly. Item 14's note addresses the sentence and reads "include" as referring to documents already in the environment, not new ones. That reading is hard to sustain. The only affidavits in the environment are the defendants' (Smith and the hospital administrator, `Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf`, PDF pp. 8–11), and a plaintiff's response does not "include" the movant's exhibits. Neither note mentions the assignment 1 rubric. Item 18's own statement that "the prompt does not instruct the model to create one" is too strong as written. The better answer is that, even on the drafting reading, the prompt never says the affidavits have been or will be signed. The criterion's wording ("the affidavit by Liam Vance indicates") presupposes an existing sworn statement, and the rubric gives no credit for marking the affidavit as unexecuted.

2. **Criterion 6 does not by itself require the hazardous behavior.** This criterion asks the response to identify that Vance's affidavit "indicates that DuPage County would be an inconvenient forum." A careful brief that cites a draft "Vance Aff. ¶ 8 [to be executed; conform to final]" and attaches a proposed affidavit for signature would very likely satisfy it. That is close to the placeholder approach item 14 calls normal. The false statement in the Opus run is its brief's claim that "Each of the five treating physicians has executed an affidavit ... Each affidavit is separately signed" (`model-runs/lrb-forum-non-conveniens-response-assignment-2/opus55-low/response.md`, Exhibits 2–6 preface). The criterion does not require that statement. It merely fails to penalize it. On this view, the responses criterion 6 fails are those that offer no Vance affidavit at all, despite the partner's instruction to include affidavits. A defender would call that a demanding but defensible standard, or at worst Arguable.
   - *Does the note answer it?* Only implicitly. John's "passes wrong work" theory is a real ground under the protocol ("or pass one that is wrong"): Opus's statement that Vance's affidavit says DuPage is inconvenient is false on this record, and the criterion passes it 3/3. But neither note confronts the point that the careful draft also passes. The answer should be stated: the criterion cannot tell a response that asserts sworn testimony from one that proposes it, and it fails a supplied-authority approach (objection 4 below and "Stronger arguments").

3. **The Luna run is not a clean example of the careful approach.** Item 14 says Luna's approach is "more correct," and item 18 cites Luna's 0/3. But Luna did not decline to cite affidavits out of caution about unsworn testimony. It said "The supplied record does not set out each physician's role, expected testimony, precise residence, willingness to appear, or scheduling burden" (`model-runs/lrb-forum-non-conveniens-response-assignment-2/gpt6luna-xhigh/response.md`, § III.C). Against the task as given, that is wrong: the assignment 1 email states residence, willingness and inconvenience. The explanation lies in the native environment. The pinned assignment 2 instruction does not restate the assignment 1 email; it says only that "your prior deliverable `/app/response1.md` is also on disk" (`tasks/lrb-forum-non-conveniens-response-assignment-2/upstream-instruction.md`). Luna's assignment 1 memo had treated the email facts as outside the uploaded files ("do not represent those witnesses as already identified or located," `model-runs/lrb-forum-non-conveniens-response-assignment-1/gpt6luna-xhigh/response.md`, § on witnesses), so they never reached its assignment 2 run. Luna also failed assignment 1 criterion 13, 0/3.
   - *Does the note answer it?* No. This does not undercut the verdict, which turns on the criterion and not on Luna, but the note should not hold up Luna as the better exemplar. The native setup is itself worth recording: the inconvenience facts that criterion 6's content depends on reach the assignment 2 solver only through its own prior answer.

4. **Defect origin: is the absence of an affidavit an "error in the supplied task materials"?** A defender could say the materials omit the affidavits by design and that the defect, if any, lies in the criterion's presupposition (origin No). John's own item 14 note says the problem could be fixed either way: supply a signed affidavit, or ask for clearly labeled placeholders.
   - *Does the note answer it?* Adequately for a professional-judgment call. The prompt asks the solver to "include" affidavits that do not exist in a closed universe where "If a document is not uploaded, it does not exist." That gap in the task materials is what forces the criterion's presupposition, so Yes is reasonable.

I found no misreading of the criterion's direction: it is a reward criterion, +3, and John treats it as one. The research universe (closed) is applied correctly.

## Stronger arguments for the call

- **The criterion's legal premise is wrong, and the matter's citation check says so.** Criterion 6 says the Vance affidavit is "required under Langenhorst." *Langenhorst*, at the page the rubric cites, says the opposite allocation: "In this case, no affidavits have been filed stating that St. Clair County [the plaintiff's chosen forum] would be an inconvenient forum for any of the witnesses. ... We reiterate that the defendant must show that the plaintiff's chosen forum is inconvenient to the defendant and that another forum is more convenient to all parties" (`Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf`, PDF p. 10, confirmed in the pinned PDF). The case imposes no requirement that a plaintiff's witnesses swear the transferee forum is inconvenient. The citation check marked this occurrence MISCHARACTERIZED on both independent passes (`citation-check/results/lrb-forum-non-conveniens-response.md`, FNC-0049). The criterion also sits uneasily with the partner's stated theory ("the burden is on the defendants") and with penalty criterion 23, which docks 30 points for saying Plaintiff "has met its burden." A response that tells the court the plaintiff need not prove DuPage inconvenient, which is correct, is not what this criterion describes. This adds a basis (b) element that the note does not use.
- **The supplied authority shows a permissible alternative that the criterion rejects.** In *Bradbury*, the rubric's own source for criteria 1–5, the plaintiff's response was supported by "a single affidavit" from "a registered nurse employed by the law firm representing the plaintiff," who identified 13 Cook County doctors and nurses as likely trial witnesses, and the court affirmed denial of transfer (`Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf`, PDF p. 3). A careful litigator facing a deadline, and unsure that five nonparty physicians would sign in time, could support the response with an affidavit from a member of the case team identifying the five physicians, their Cook County practice and residence, and their reported willingness to testify. Treating physicians' own affidavits would follow when they sign. That response is grounded in the record and the supplied law, and it misrepresents nothing. Criterion 6 fails it, because there is no "affidavit by Liam Vance." Under the protocol, rejecting an approach a careful litigator would adopt on this record is a defect.
- **The partner's information is thin and secondhand.** The email reports that "they have advised me" the doctors find DuPage inconvenient "to take time away from their practice/at the hospital." It says nothing about any doctor's willingness to sign an affidavit. Opus's single form, which states each doctor's specialty, residence, dates of care, subjects of testimony and records custody, goes well beyond what the record supports, and its brief calls the form "sworn" and "separately signed." The criterion rewarded that 3/3, and the judges relied specifically on form ¶ 8 (`model-runs/lrb-forum-non-conveniens-response-assignment-2/opus55-low/README.md`, criterion_6).
- **GPT-6 Sol's blind reason matches.** Sol: "A careful litigator could prepare proposed declaration forms for each doctor ... but could not truthfully represent the brief as already supported by five affidavits. The criteria fail that careful response and reward fabricated filed evidence" (`tasks/lrb-forum-non-conveniens-response-assignment-2/gpt-6-sol-audit.md`, G1). That supports AI reasoning Correct.

## Errors in the note

- **"the prompt does not instruct the model to create one"** (note and environment explanation). This is overstated. The prompt says "Please include all affidavits and information you referenced from Assignment #1," and the assignment 1 materials make plaintiff-side affidavits the natural referent. The accurate statement is that the environment supplies no affidavit, and nothing says one has been signed or will be.
- **"affidavits the record shows have not been signed."** The record does not show this. It is silent: the partner says only that counsel has "spoken with" the physicians, and the closed-universe instruction treats unsupplied documents as nonexistent. "Affidavits the record does not contain" is accurate.
- **Paraphrase omits the legal premise.** The note paraphrases the criterion as rewarding a statement "that his affidavit says DuPage County would be an inconvenient forum." The criterion adds "for him to litigate this case as required under Langenhorst." That clause is the mischaracterization noted above. "To litigate" is also misapplied to a nonparty witness; the wording appears to be carried over from assignment 1 criteria 2–3, which concern the defendants' own affidavits. Omitting the clause is not an error, but the note loses a ground by not quoting it.
- **Native-run descriptions are accurate.** Opus's brief says "Plaintiff submits sworn affidavits from five named Cook County treating physicians" (§ IV.B.3.c), and its exhibit preface says each "has executed an affidavit in the same form ... separately signed," over one form with "[Name]" and "[specialty]" blanks and an unsigned signature line. Luna names the five as physicians "with whom counsel has spoken" and cites no affidavit. The sources line is correct.
- **Environment consistency across the assignment.** Item 16, a different criterion in the same assignment, records this assignment's environment as Correct, while items 14, 15 and 17–20 record it as Defective. The protocol makes the environment verdict a property of the assignment ("whether its assignment's task environment is Defective or Correct"). That is outside item 18's fields, but the consistency pass should reconcile it.

## Defect basis and environment origin

**Basis: Professional judgment.** The question is whether a careful Illinois litigator would treat a brief that cites a nonparty physician's affidavit, with no indication that the physician has signed it or agreed to its wording, as correct work on this record. That is a practice judgment, not a matter the record or law settles. The *Langenhorst* misattribution ("as required") is objectively wrong per the supplied opinion at PDF p. 10 and the citation check. It reinforces the defect, but a grader would likely award the criterion to a response that cites the affidavit without calling it "required," so it is not the operative cause of misgrading.

**Origin: Yes.** The task materials ask the solver to "include" affidavits that exist nowhere in the closed universe and say nothing about their execution status. That gap is what forces the criterion's presupposition.

**Environment: Defective.** John's ground fits the protocol's second prong (task materials that direct work product a competent litigator should not put before a court) only on his reading of the prompt. The verdict is safer resting on objective record errors in the same assignment's materials, which the protocol counts "whether or not they touch the sampled criterion":

- The assignment 1 email says the family "had him transferred to the Lurie's Children's Hospital" and that he received care "for the next 23 days." The Davis Complaint says he was transferred "On August 28, 2024 ... to Cook County Hospital" (¶ 27) and "died at Cook County Hospital" (¶ 29), which is 19 days. The 2-622 report says the same (`Davis Complaint.docx`, report items 6–7). Opus's own assignment 1 memo flagged this conflict.
- The email says "We filed our case in June, 2026," but the Complaint caption reads "No. 2025 L 5841."
- The email says Baby Davis "almost passed away on September 16, 2024," the date he died.
- The defendants' motion cites an "Affidavit of [Records Custodian]" (`Defendants Motion to Transfer.pdf`, PDF pp. 3–4, 7), and the Smith affidavit leaves "[street address], [city] ... [ZIP]" blanks (PDF p. 8). These may be intended weaknesses for the solver to spot, but no criterion rewards catching them.

## Suggested revision of the note

> Same issue as item 14. The criterion rewards a brief that "identifies ... that the affidavit by Liam Vance indicates that DuPage County would be an inconvenient forum for him ... as required under Langenhorst." No affidavit from Dr. Vance exists in the environment, and nothing says one has been signed or that he has agreed to any wording. The partner reports only that the five doctors "have advised me" that testifying in DuPage would be inconvenient (assignment 1 email). The prompt's instruction to "include all affidavits and information you referenced from Assignment #1" at most calls for proposed affidavits to send the doctors for review, and the criterion gives no credit for marking them as drafts. As written, it passed Opus 3/3 for a brief calling a single unsigned "[Name], M.D." form "sworn" and "separately signed." It would also fail a response supported, as in *Bradbury*, by a case-team affidavit identifying the treating physicians (Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, PDF p. 3). Its legal premise is also wrong: *Langenhorst* puts the burden on the movant to show the plaintiff's chosen forum is inconvenient and requires no plaintiff-side affidavit about the transferee forum (Langenhorst v. Norfolk Southern.pdf, PDF p. 10; the citation check marks this criterion MISCHARACTERIZED, FNC-0049). This is one of the missing-affidavit items (14, 15 and 17–20), which all reward citing affidavits the record does not contain.

> Sources: see item 14. Native runs: Opus 5.5 (low) met this criterion 3/3 by citing its single unsigned physician form as an executed affidavit (judges relied on form ¶ 8). GPT-6 Luna (xhigh) cited no physician affidavit and scored 0/3. Luna's run is not a clean example of the careful approach: it says the record does not show the doctors' willingness or scheduling burden, because the native assignment 2 instruction does not restate the assignment 1 email and Luna's own assignment 1 memo had not carried those facts forward.

Suggested environment explanation: "No affidavit from any of the five physicians is in the closed universe, though the prompt asks the solver to 'include' them. The record also conflicts with itself: the assignment 1 email has Baby Davis transferred to Lurie Children's Hospital for 23 days, while Davis Complaint ¶¶ 27, 29 have him transferred on August 28, 2024 to Cook County Hospital and dying there; the email says suit was filed in June 2026, but the caption is No. 2025 L 5841."
