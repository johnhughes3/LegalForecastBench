# Item 17: lrb-forum-non-conveniens-response-assignment-2 — criterion_3

Bottom line: John's call (Defective, AI reasoning Correct, professional judgment, arising from the environment; environment Defective) holds up, and I recommend no field change. The strongest objection is that the prompt does ask the brief to "include all affidavits ... you referenced from Assignment #1," and that the careful approach John himself prefers (affidavits drafted for signature, with clearly labeled placeholder citations) would probably *pass* this criterion. That narrows the defect, but it does not remove it. The criterion pays full marks for an unqualified statement that the brief "is supported by" an affidavit that does not exist in the record. The native run shows exactly that: 3/3 for a brief that says each physician "has executed an affidavit." The criterion also fails a response whose permissible assignment-1 memo referenced no physician affidavits. The note is a cross-reference to item 14 and should be made self-contained. It should meet the "include all affidavits" objection directly and correct one overstatement: the record does not show the affidavits "have not been signed"; it shows that none exists.

## Strongest objections

1. **The prompt does direct affidavits, so drafting and citing them follows a reasonable instruction in a hypothetical record.** The assignment 2 prompt says: "Please include all affidavits and information you referenced from Assignment #1" (pinned `steps/assignment-2/instruction.md`, line 51). The assignment 1 rubric expects the assignment 1 memo to recommend these very affidavits: criterion_13 of assignment 1 rewards stating "that Plaintiff should obtain affidavits from the 5 Cook County Hospital Doctors who provided care to Baby Davis that it would be inconvenient for them to testify in DuPage County" (`tasks/lrb-forum-non-conveniens-response-assignment-1/input.json`, criterion_13). The assignment 1 email supplies their substance: the doctors "are all Cook County residents and are willing to testify, however, it would be inconvenient for them to take time away from their practice/at the hospital to testify in DuPage rather than Cook County." Under the protocol, a hypothetical-record instruction that a response can reasonably follow is not a defect, and the brief is an internal draft to a supervising partner, not a filing. This is the reconciliation's ruling, and Claude Opus 5.5's blind pass checked criteria 1–10 and found no defect (`claude-opus-5-5-audit.md`, coverage paragraph).
   - *Does the note answer it?* Partly, through item 14. Item 14 reads "include" as meaning "include documents that were referenced in the environment, not generate new ones." That is one available reading. The phrase could also refer to the defendants' two affidavits, which any assignment 1 memo would reference. But the note does not engage the assignment 1 rubric, which shows that the task author meant the recommended physician affidavits. The environment explanation's flat statement that "the prompt does not instruct the model to create one" is therefore contestable. The better answer concedes that the prompt can be read to ask for drafted affidavits. It then explains why that does not save the criterion (objection 2).

2. **The criterion would pass John's own preferred approach, so it does not fail the careful response.** Item 14 says the "normal approach" is "placeholder citations to an affidavit that make clear the language must be adjusted to conform to the witness's final testimony." A brief citing "Affidavit of Caleb Montgomery, M.D. (Ex. 4) [draft; to be conformed and executed]" plainly "states ... that the response is supported by the affidavit of Caleb Montgomery," and a judge would very likely pass it. GPT-6 Sol's blind claim that the criteria "fail that careful response" is to that extent overstated. If the only misgrade is failing a response that cites no affidavit at all, a defender would call it Arguable: that response arguably disregards the "include all affidavits" instruction.
   - *Does the note answer it?* No. This is the most material gap. There are two answers. (i) *Passing wrong work.* The protocol makes a criterion defective if it would "pass one that is wrong." The criterion asks for the statement unqualified and cannot tell a placeholder from a representation of executed, sworn evidence. The rubric contains no penalty for misrepresenting the evidentiary record; its penalties, criteria 18, 23 and 24, concern a clearance-rate figure, the burden and deference. In the saved run, the judges gave 3/3 to a brief stating "Plaintiff submits sworn affidavits from five named Cook County treating physicians" (`model-runs/.../opus55-low/response.md`, line 174) and "Each of the five treating physicians has executed an affidavit in the same form ... Each affidavit is separately signed and certified under Section 1-109" (line 376), over a single form headed "AFFIDAVIT OF [NAME], M.D." (lines 386–410). A false statement to the court that a nonparty has sworn to facts is wrong on the very point the criterion covers. (ii) *Failing a permissible response.* The prompt conditions the affidavits on what the solver "referenced from Assignment #1." An assignment 1 memo that did not recommend physician affidavits is a permissible, if imperfect, answer: assignment 1 criterion_13 is worth 1 point. GPT-6 Luna's actual assignment 1 memo is an example, since it discusses only the defendants' affidavits (`model-runs/lrb-forum-non-conveniens-response-assignment-1/gpt6luna-xhigh/response.md`). On that memo, the assignment 2 instruction calls for no physician affidavit, yet criterion 3 still requires one. Together these defeat the Arguable fallback: the criterion grades wrongly in both directions on this record.

3. **Environment-origin "Yes" may be wrong because nothing in the record is erroneous.** The doctors' statements are relayed orally and consistently, so the record is incomplete rather than erroneous, and the defect lies in a criterion that presupposes evidence.
   - *Does the note answer it?* Implicitly, and I would keep "Yes." The task materials themselves create the mismatch. The prompt asks the brief to "include all affidavits," but the materials supply none and never say any was obtained, and they also declare that "If a document is not uploaded, it does not exist." John's suggested fixes in item 14 both lie in the environment: supply executed affidavits, or change the prompt to request labeled placeholders. That fits "stems from an error in the supplied task materials." The explanation should say so in those terms rather than "the prompt does not instruct the model to create one."

4. **"Cook County Hospital" conflicts with the assignment 1 email.** The criterion describes Montgomery as caring for Baby Davis "at Cook County Hospital." The assignment 1 email says the baby went to "Lurie's Children's Hospital in Cook County" (pinned `steps/assignment-1/instruction.md`, line 54). The Complaint (¶¶ 27, 29) and the defendants' motion (¶ 7; Hospital Administrator Aff. ¶ 5) say "Cook County Hospital." The same email also calls them "Cook County Hospital Doctors."
   - *Does the note answer it?* No, but it is immaterial to this criterion. A judge would not fail a response for naming the facility either way, as Claude Opus 5.5's blind report also concluded. It is, however, an objective inconsistency in the supplied record that the environment explanation could add. Opus's own assignment 1 memo flagged it ("Which Cook County hospital?").

I found no misreading of the native grades and no closed-universe problem: every document the criterion's source cites is supplied. The criterion is a reward, correctly treated as such.

## Stronger arguments for the call

- **The rubric's own source undercuts the criterion.** The source note cites Bradbury p. 3. That passage describes a plaintiff's response "supported by a single affidavit ... from a registered nurse employed by the law firm," who "reviewed the decedent's medical records" and identified 13 likely witnesses (Bradbury v. St. Mary's Hospital.pdf, p. 3, last ¶). Bradbury shows that the plaintiff's FNC record is built from affidavits that actually exist, from people with knowledge, and it lets a court weigh them. It does not support presuming treating-physician affidavits. It also illustrates a competent alternative this criterion would not credit: an affidavit from someone on the case team about the records and the witnesses' locations.
- **Illinois practice treats affidavits as evidence, not argument.** Ill. S. Ct. R. 187(b): FNC motions "may be supported and opposed by affidavit," and "[i]n determining issues of fact raised by affidavits, any competent evidence adduced by the parties shall also be considered." A brief's statement that it "is supported by the affidavit of" a witness is an evidentiary representation. Under 735 ILCS 5/1-109, which the Opus brief invokes, a certification has the force of an oath, and a knowingly false material statement in a certified document is a Class 3 felony. That is why a nonparty's control over the content matters, as item 14 argues.
- **The task's own sequential design treats the affidavits as yet to be obtained.** Assignment 1 criterion_13 rewards saying the plaintiff "should obtain" them. Nothing in assignment 2 says they were obtained; it says only "The names of the physicians from Cook County we have spoken to are." Opus's own assignment 1 memo said the physician facts "are **not yet in the record** and will have to be proven by affidavit" and "We must close these gaps with affidavits **before** the response is filed" (`model-runs/lrb-forum-non-conveniens-response-assignment-1/opus55-low/response.md`, lines 14, 40). Its assignment 2 brief then claimed they were executed, and the criterion paid for that.
- **The native harness narrows what the assignment 2 solver knows.** The pinned assignment 2 instruction does not reproduce the assignment 1 email; the solver gets only the names plus its own `response1.md` (pinned instruction, line 28). A solver whose memo did not carry forward the doctors' statements has no record basis at all for the affidavit's content.

## Errors in the note

- **"which all reward citing affidavits the record shows have not been signed"** overstates the record. The record does not show that any affidavit was unsigned. It contains no physician affidavit, describes the physicians' views only as relayed in conversation, and under the prompt's rule a document not uploaded "does not exist." Suggested wording: "affidavits that are not in the record and that, under the prompt's own rule, do not exist."
- **"the prompt does not instruct the model to create one"** (note and environment explanation) is contestable for the reasons in objection 1. It should be reframed, not dropped.
- **Native-run description:** accurate. Opus's brief lists "Affidavit of Caleb Montgomery, M.D." as Exhibit 4 (line 341) and uses one "[Name]" form for all five (lines 376–410); Luna's brief names Montgomery among physicians "with whom counsel has spoken" and cites no affidavit (line 52). "Single unsigned physician form" is fair: the form's signature line is "[Name], M.D." with no signature.
- No wrong pin cites. The note gives none of its own; it relies on item 14's, which check out against the pinned files.

## Defect basis and environment origin

Basis: **professional judgment.** The defect turns on how to read "include all affidavits ... you referenced" and on what a careful litigator would represent about unexecuted nonparty testimony. A defender can reasonably read the instruction as asking for drafted affidavits, so this is not an unambiguous objective error. Origin: **Yes.** The prompt and record together create the defect. The prompt asks for affidavits that the materials never supply, and its closed-universe rule says those affidavits do not exist. Either fix John identifies, supplying executed affidavits or asking for labeled placeholders, is a fix to the task materials. The same analysis applies to the companion items 14, 15, 18, 19 and 20, and to the unsampled criteria 2, 4, 5, 7, 10 and 12.

## Suggested revision of the note

**Environment explanation:** The assignment 2 prompt asks the brief to "include all affidavits and information you referenced from Assignment #1," but no affidavit from any of the five physicians is supplied, nothing says any was obtained (the prompt says only that these are physicians "we have spoken to"), and the prompt also says, "If a document is not uploaded, it does not exist for purposes of this analysis." Separately, the assignment 1 email places Baby Davis at "Lurie's Children's Hospital," while the Complaint (¶¶ 27, 29) and the motion (¶ 7) say "Cook County Hospital."

**Note and source locator:** Same issue as item 14. The criterion rewards a brief stating that it "is supported by the affidavit of Caleb Montgomery," but no affidavit from Dr. Montgomery exists in the record. The physicians' views are known only from the assignment 1 email's account of a conversation, and assignment 1's own rubric (criterion_13) treats the affidavits as something the plaintiff "should obtain." The prompt's "include all affidavits ... you referenced from Assignment #1" can fairly be read to ask for drafts, and a brief citing a clearly labeled, to-be-executed draft would likely pass. But the criterion cannot tell that draft from a representation that a nonparty has already sworn, and it paid full marks for the latter. Opus 5.5 (low) scored 3/3 for "Each of the five treating physicians has executed an affidavit in the same form ... Each affidavit is separately signed," over a single unsigned "[Name], M.D." form. The rubric has no penalty for that misstatement. The criterion also fails a response whose permissible assignment 1 memo recommended no physician affidavits, as Luna's did, because the instruction then calls for none. Under Ill. S. Ct. R. 187(b), affidavits are the evidence on an FNC motion, so stating that the brief rests on an affidavit nobody has signed is wrong on the point this criterion covers. This is one of the missing-affidavit items (14, 15 and 17–20).
