## Arguable mandatory-advocacy concern

- **[C-022](https://github.com/harveyai/harvey-labs/blob/1dd81403b2fbb60596f7aea3fcecafad7bf73143/tasks/litigation-dispute-resolution/draft-motion-in-limine-to-exclude-expert-testimony-and-prejudicial-evidence/task.json#L188) makes first federal appearance a basis for “heightened scrutiny.”** The fact is supported (Marchetti deposition pp12–14, extraction P50–79), and defense memo P63 recommends exploiting it. But Rule 702 prescribes a qualification and reliability inquiry applicable to all experts; it has no heightened standard based on whether an expert previously testified in federal court. A careful motion can focus on methodological failures and omit this weak credential attack. Because the criterion could mean rhetorical emphasis rather than a formally different legal burden, this is arguable rather than a definite statement-of-law defect. [Rule 702 and committee note](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_702). Repair: do not require the heightened-scrutiny inference; credit factual mention without implying a different legal burden.
