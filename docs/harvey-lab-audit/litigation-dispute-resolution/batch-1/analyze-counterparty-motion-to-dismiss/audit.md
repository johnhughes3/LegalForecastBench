## Definite defects

- **C-012 and consequential C-024: mandatory incorrect categorical merger-clause proposition.** The rubric requires an argument that an integration clause cannot bar fraudulent inducement under Georgia and/or Texas law, and assigns that issue high severity. The actual MSA P158, §14.7, expressly says each party has not relied on representations outside the agreement; it is not merely a standard merger clause. The complaint P161, ¶97, makes the rubric's broad claim, but that advocacy is not controlling law. Georgia's Supreme Court holds that affirming a contract with a merger/disclaimer clause can defeat reliance; rescission and other exceptions require analysis. [Novare Group v. Sarif, 290 Ga. 186 (2011), full opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/georgia/supreme-court/2011/s11g0478.html). Texas also distinguishes integration from disclaimer of reliance; the Texas Supreme Court's Italian Cowboy opinion explains the distinction. [Texas Supreme Court FY2011 opinions, Italian Cowboy](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/819896/OpinionsFY2011.pdf). A sound memo may distinguish or challenge the disclaimer, but should not have to declare a categorical immunity-from-merger rule. Repair: credit jurisdiction-specific analysis of this actual disclaimer, including adverse authority and rescission/negotiation issues.

[Iqbal, 556 U.S. 662, discussion of Rule 9(b)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/07-1015.ZS.html).
