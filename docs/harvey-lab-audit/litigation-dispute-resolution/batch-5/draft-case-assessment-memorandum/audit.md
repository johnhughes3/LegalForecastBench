**F1 — confirmed — C-053**

Requires an unsupported assertion about an absent separation agreement.

Evidence: No separation agreement is supplied. Complaint paragraph 55 only addresses nonrelease of the corporate Section 11.3 obligation; forensic summary section 5 says confidentiality was not released. Neither says the separation agreement is silent on personal noncompete/non-solicitation covenants. Employment agreement sections 2.2 and 11.5 expressly preserve those duties.

Repair: Require obtaining and analyzing the missing separation agreement; remove mandatory assertion of silence.

**F2 — arguable — C-024, C-025**

Compels dismissal recommendation despite an expressly alternative unjust-enrichment pleading.

Evidence: Complaint paragraph 127 expressly pleads alternatively if contract does not govern full dispute or is unenforceable. Oregon JV LLC v Advanced Investment Corp., D. Or. June 7, 2023, pp.17–18 permits alternative unjust-enrichment pleading pending contract determination. A contract-based defense is plausible, but a compulsory motion recommendation is too rigid without examining scope/admissions.

Repair: Accept a reasoned decision to preserve the defense while deferring a motion.

**F3 — arguable — C-027, C-028**

Treats low accounting book value as demonstrating damages overstatement.

Evidence: Bridger report section VI.B expressly explains why economic value differs from straight-line book depreciation. A valuation challenge is appropriate, but difference from book value alone does not establish inflated legal damages.

Repair: Require evaluation of depreciation, remaining useful life, mitigation and methodology, allowing supported disagreement.

**F4 — arguable — C-029**

Potential overlap is sound issue spotting; exclusive either/or framing is too broad.

Evidence: Distribution agreement section 9.2 requires both 12 months continuing performance and a fee; Bridger section V.B says lost margin during omitted notice period and fee compensate distinct obligations. Lost profits for overlapping post-termination periods may duplicate, but notice-period profits plus termination fee need not.

Repair: Distinguish time periods and recoverable interests before requiring exclusivity.

**F5 — arguable — C-034**

Mandatory Oregon noncompete discussion omits contractual Washington law.

Evidence: Jantzen employment agreement section 12.1 chooses Washington law and section 12.2 King County forum; distribution agreement chooses Oregon for its own claims. ORS 653.295 may be relevant via choice-of-law/public-policy analysis, but cannot be assumed controlling.

Repair: Permit analysis under chosen Washington law with appropriate conflicts discussion.

**F6 — arguable — C-015**

Likely waiver is defensible but criterion categorical general rule omits antiwaiver term and unequivocal conduct standard.

Evidence: Distribution agreement section 17.4 requires signed written waiver and says failure to enforce is not waiver. Oregon law permits waiver by unequivocal conduct notwithstanding such clauses, so this is a counterargument, not a confirmed false conclusion.

Repair: Accept reasoned alternative analysis.

**F7 — arguable — C-036, C-037, C-042, C-046, C-047**

Formatting and repeated recommendation requirements exceed broad prompt.

Evidence: Prompt asks only comprehensive case assessment memo. Functional summary and risk assessment are sensible but forced graduated ratings and recommendation in both executive summary and final section can fail an otherwise complete memo.

Repair: Grade substantive risk/recommendation wherever clearly expressed.
