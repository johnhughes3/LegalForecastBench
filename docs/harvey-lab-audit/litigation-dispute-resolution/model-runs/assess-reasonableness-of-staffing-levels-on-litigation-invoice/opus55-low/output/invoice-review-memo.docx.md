Methodology: Entries rejected in full receive no other reduction. Where an entry is both re-rated and block-billed, the 25% block-billing reduction is applied to the re-rated amount, not the original amount. This avoids stacking penalties, and I recommend it because it makes the reductions easier to defend on appeal. The Guidelines would allow the 25% to be applied to the original fees (§5.4 "in addition to"). Doing so would increase the reduction by only a small amount. Amounts are rounded to the cent. Compared with the \$193,372.50 in fees actually billed, the net fee reduction is \$55,030.61 (28.5%).

## 1. Nina Barros – Unapproved Mid-Level Associate (§4.2) — Reject in Full

Karen Cho asked on April 28, 2025 to add Ms. Barros. On May 1, Ms. Dominguez replied only that she would review the request with Ms. Whitmore and "circle back"; she gave no approval. Guidelines §4.2 states that "the absence of a response shall not constitute approval," and that fees are subject to "reduction or rejection of all fees" where a timekeeper "has already commenced work prior to receiving written approval." Ms. Barros billed on May 1, the same day as the non-committal reply. H&L's own Rate Schedule lists her as "Pending Approval — client response pending." The Staffing Plan states that Pettersson "is the only mid-level associate assigned to this matter." H&L claimed no exigency, and even if it had, §4.2 requires notice within 24 hours explaining the exigency. The status report's statement that her work was "consistent with the request submitted" does not substitute for approval. The request also did not include the §4.2(d) budget-impact analysis.

Calculation: summary billing 45.0 hrs × \$475 = \$21,375.00. The detail shows 49.0 hrs × \$475 = \$23,275.00. Reduction applied: 100%. Much of her work also duplicated Pettersson's work (for example, Entries 2/4 on the same interrogatory responses and GTSA preemption research, and Entry 80 reviewing Pettersson's drafts). That duplication independently supports rejection under §6.2.

The \$210 rate also exceeds the \$200/hr cap in §5.1, and §5.1 provides that rates above the cap are "automatically reduced." Because the approved rate is lower than the cap, it controls.
