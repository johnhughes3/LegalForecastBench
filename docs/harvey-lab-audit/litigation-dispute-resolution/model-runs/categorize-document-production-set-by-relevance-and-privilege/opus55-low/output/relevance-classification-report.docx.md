# 2. Review Methodology and Criteria

Each document was read in full and coded for (a) responsiveness/relevance to the claims and defenses; (b) privilege and work product; (c) confidentiality designation; and (d) hot/key status. Relevance was assessed against the issues framed by the complaint and the Oct. 15, 2023 demand letter:

- Issue A – Whistleblower retaliation / wrongful termination (R.C. 4113.52): protected activity, employer knowledge, causation, pretext, temporal proximity.

- Issue B – Trade secret misappropriation (R.C. 1333.61 et seq.) and inventorship: MPC-7X, U.S. Pat. 11,234,567, continuation App. 18/456,789, invention assignment.

- Issue C – Damages: compensation, benefits, emotional distress (\$8.7M demand).

- Issue D – Preservation / discovery conduct.

# 4. Hot Documents

- **vasquez-performance-summary.docx –** Created 8/14/2023, four days after Simmons's 'Vasquez situation' email and seven months after the protected report. Metadata: Revision 3, last modified 9/18/2023 3:47 PM, three days after termination. Plaintiff already describes this document as 'transparently' pretextual. Produce natively with metadata. Also names 'Crestview Patent Services LLC' as the firm handling the filing, then refers to Aldersgate at the same address. Confirm which firm was involved.

- **chandrasekaran-meeting-notes-aug28.docx –** The unredacted business portions show the termination discussion began on 8/28, with Simmons pushing back on the 'minimum 60-day PIP window per our policy'. Termination came 7 days into the PIP. The redacted legal portions (C. Burrell's advice to 'build contemporaneous performance record to insulate against retaliation claim') are highly damaging if privilege is lost (see §6).

- **vasquez-pip.docx / vasquez-termination-letter.docx –** 60-day PIP issued 9/8; termination 9/15, with 53 days left. The termination letter does not mention the PIP. The PIP says Vasquez joined as 'Director of R&D', while every other document says she was hired as VP. This internal inconsistency could hurt the document's credibility.

- **simmons-vasquez-situation-email.eml / bellworth-reply-adding-gc.eml –** Bellworth's reference to 'the action we discussed' (redacted portion) and Simmons's 8/10 email show adverse action was being planned before the documentation existed.

- **mpc7x-continuation-app.docx –** Para. \[0012\] says the cure-accelerator screening was done 'primarily during Q3 and Q4 of 2022 and Q1 of 2023.' Vasquez was still VP of R&D then. This contradicts Burrell's position that the improvements were developed 'after Vasquez's departure on September 15, 2023' (burrell-stanton email) and Mehta's statement that they came from 'recent work over the last several months.' It creates a real risk of an incorrect inventorship designation (35 U.S.C. §§ 115-116, 256) and a candor issue before the USPTO.

- **alderwood-draft-audit-report.docx –** The report is double-edged. It supports the defense because only 4 of 14 instances were confirmed. It supports plaintiff because it confirms real violations, including a significant deficiency, which supports her good-faith, reasonable belief. The engagement letter calls the audit 'proactive' and never mentions the Jan. 15 memo.

# 6. Privilege Analysis and Risk Assessment

- **Withheld in full (4):** The litigation-risk email (3/3/2023), the litigation hold (10/5/2023), the GC summary of the Burrell memo (10/10/2023), and the Burrell–Greer emails (10/20/2023). The 3/3 email is a classic request for legal advice. Privilege attaches even though Kirkfield's engagement letters are dated 3/6/2023 (M&A) and 10/3/2023 (litigation), because consultations by a prospective client are protected. Sharing Burrell's analysis with Simmons stays within the privilege: he is a senior manager whose input was needed to render legal advice.

- **Partially privileged (2):** The Bellworth/GC chain: Simmons's underlying 8/22 email is a pre-existing business communication and cannot be shielded by later forwarding it to counsel, so produce it. The 8/28 notes: the GC was acting in both a business and a legal role. Redact only the portions whose primary purpose was legal advice. Business discussion is not privileged just because counsel was in the room.

- **Common-interest risk (burrell-stanton):** No written common-interest or joint-defense agreement is referenced. Mehta has been dismissed, although re-joinder is a realistic possibility. Greer's proposal that the two sides keep 'a consistent position', and Burrell's statement that Mehta's testimony 'must be consistent,' could prompt a crime-fraud or witness-coaching challenge and in camera review. Recommendation: execute a written common-interest agreement now, effective 10/20/2023. Keep future exchanges focused on legal strategy, not on aligning testimony.

- **At-issue / advice-of-counsel waiver:** If Meridian defends by relying on its good-faith process or on having consulted counsel before terminating Vasquez, it risks an at-issue waiver over the 3/3 email, the 8/28 legal notes, and the Burrell advice. Burrell and the GC are also fact witnesses to the decision timeline. Decide the defense theory before relying on counsel's involvement in any filing.

- **Work-product timing vs. preservation duty:** Asserting work product over the 3/3/2023 email means Meridian anticipated litigation, including whistleblower claims, by March 2023. The litigation hold did not issue until 10/5/2023, six days after suit was filed. Plaintiff can use that gap to argue the preservation duty arose in March 2023. Audit whether anything relevant (texts, drafts, earlier versions of the Performance Summary, Alderwood work papers) was lost between March and October 2023 under routine retention (IT-POL-2019-04).

- **Litigation hold:** Hold notices are generally privileged. They can become discoverable if spoliation is put at issue, and this one contains candid assessments by counsel, e.g. the PIP timing is 'our single greatest vulnerability'. Offer the date, recipients and categories instead of the document.

- **Alderwood audit not privileged:** In March the GC asked Burrell whether the audit should be run under attorney direction. In the end it was engaged by Operations as a 'proactive' business review, with an express no-attorney-client disclaimer. No Kovel-type or work-product argument is viable. Expect a subpoena to Alderwood for the final report and work papers.

- **Mislabeled draft separation agreement:** The 'Attorney Work Product' footer does not protect a document sent to the opposing party. Produce it. Admissibility objections under Evid.R. 408 are a separate question.

# 7. Confidentiality and Special Handling

- **vasquez-benefits-enrollment.docx –** Contains plaintiff's STD claim, psychiatric diagnoses (ICD-10 F32.1, F41.1), prescriptions and physician certification from April–May 2022. By seeking \$500,000 for emotional distress, plaintiff likely waived physician-patient privilege for causally or historically related records (R.C. 2317.02(B)(1)(a)(iii)). The records are relevant to causation because the condition predates the protected activity. Produce only under a protective order, designated Highly Confidential – AEO, and limit internal access (HIPAA/ADA confidentiality of medical files).

- **Other –** Designate Confidential: Alderwood materials, the unpublished continuation application, and the PIP and HR records. Before production, redact personal identifiers such as employee IDs where the protective order allows.
