**Relevance Classification Report**

Document-by-document review of the 25-document production set

*Review scope: 25 supplied native documents • Matter: Vasquez v. Meridian Polymers, Inc.*

# Executive summary

This is a preliminary relevance assessment based on the face of the supplied documents and the claims identified in the set: alleged Ohio whistleblower retaliation/wrongful termination and Ohio trade-secret misappropriation/inventorship. It is not a finding that an allegation is true, nor a final admissibility determination. No discovery requests, pleadings beyond the references within the production, protective order, or native Bates numbering were supplied.

| **Code**      | **Meaning**                                                                                                                                                                    |
|---------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| R1 — Core     | Directly bears on protected activity/knowledge, decision-making, stated reason/pretext/causation, environmental response, ownership/inventorship, or alleged misappropriation. |
| R2 — Material | Supports chronology, notice, damages, defenses, preservation, or litigation position but is not itself central proof.                                                          |
| R3 — Limited  | Narrow potential value or conditional relevance; use only if the corresponding issue is in discovery.                                                                          |

Classification totals: 18 R1 (core), 3 R2 (material), 3 R3 (limited), and 1 R4 (low). Privilege and privacy are assessed separately below; relevance does not waive privilege, and privilege does not make a document irrelevant.

# Cross-document issues requiring follow-up

- Performance evidence is inconsistent across the set. The August 10 email, August 22 standalone metrics, August 23 embedded metrics, August 14 HR summary, August 28 notes, and September 8 PIP use different project counts/names, completion percentages, budget overruns, transfer counts, and survey dates. Reconcile against contemporaneous project-management, finance, survey, and HR source records; retain each version and metadata.

- The PIP states a 60-day remediation window and no adverse action based solely on its listed deficiencies before completion, yet termination occurred seven days later and the termination letter does not mention the PIP. The PIP’s own timeline and the August 28 notes are key sources for the termination decision and pretext analysis.

- The June 5 Alderwood engagement was signed by Operations as a standard consultant engagement and disclaims an attorney-client relationship; the July 22 report calls itself a standard environmental audit. The March 3 request to outside counsel asked whether to structure an audit under attorney supervision, but the produced engagement/report do not establish that it was so structured. The report should not be withheld as privileged on this record.

- The audit’s “four findings” should not be paraphrased as four confirmed violations. F-001 says the weight variance was below the regulatory threshold and not a violation; the report identifies other discrete deficiencies and characterizes only F-004 as significant. Obtain source manifests, the final report, and regulatory records before stating what was established.

- The supplied patent texts appear technically inconsistent: the original patent describes a fluoropolymer/modified-diamine formulation, whereas the continuation text describes an epoxy-acrylate matrix and gives a different parent title. Verify official USPTO filings, priority chain, claims, inventorship declarations, lab notebooks, and drafting history before relying on either narrative.

- RC-15 is a non-lawyer business email directly discussing a request to omit Vasquez from the continuation inventor list. It should be preserved and assessed alongside the patent prosecution file and the counsel-to-counsel coordination at RC-14; do not treat the existence of legal discussions as converting the underlying business communications into privileged material.
