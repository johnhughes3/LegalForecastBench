# 1. Executive Summary

This report provides document-by-document privilege recommendations for the 18 files supplied in the workspace. Five documents are recommended for full withholding; five warrant a limited or conditional claim with segregation or redaction; two require further review before any final designation; and six are not privileged on the supplied record. The designations are recommendations for counsel’s review, not final production instructions.

The most significant issues are (1) the apparent disclosure of Priya Nandakumar’s legal-risk email in full to independent consultant Dr. Kenji Moritani, which likely waives protection for that communication; (2) the March 2021 Rennick–Tsao chain, which is facially a confidential attorney-client communication but must be escalated for crime-fraud review; (3) the potential common-interest protection for the Bridger–Cascade counsel email; and (4) separating privileged litigation/legal material from otherwise nonprivileged business content in the Board deck, invoice, mixed-purpose reformulation email, and claw-back file.

# 2. Scope and Review Method

The review applies the supplied Harwell Bridger & Koss LLP Privilege Review Protocol and the facts stated in each document. It distinguishes attorney-client communications made for legal advice from ordinary business material, and work product prepared in anticipation of litigation from pre-litigation regulatory advice. Privilege labels and confidentiality legends were treated as relevant context, not as dispositive.

No Bates numbers were supplied for these files. The companion workbook therefore uses filenames as provisional document identifiers; replace them with assigned Bates numbers before service. This review addresses privilege only. Responsiveness, relevance, confidentiality, other objections, and production format remain separate questions.

The supplied draft-privilege-log.xlsx contains a pending-batch tab describing documents that are not included as separate source files in the 18-document set. This report does not make determinations or create log entries for those absent documents based solely on the draft log’s descriptions.

# 4. Key Issues and Required Follow-Up

- **Third-party disclosure / waiver — Moritani.** The Emmerich forward appears to reproduce the complete Nandakumar legal analysis for an independent consultant retained directly by Regulatory Affairs, not through counsel, with no confidentiality or common-interest arrangement identified. Treat both the forwarded copy and the original communication as unprotected on the supplied facts; escalate to the lead partner to define the scope of waiver before production.

- **Crime-fraud escalation — Rennick–Tsao.** The March 2021 email chain is a confidential GC–CEO communication, but the CEO’s response raises a potential crime-fraud issue involving the handling of routine QA documentation. This report does not conclude that fraud occurred or that the exception applies. The protocol requires partner-level review; hold the document and do not serve a final log entry pending that review.

- **Mixed business/legal communications.** For the Emmerich reformulation email, do not claim the technical and operational discussion wholesale. Consider withholding only the severable legal request and necessary context if predominant legal purpose and need-to-know circulation are confirmed. For the Board deck, limit any claim to slides 7–8 and produce the business, financial, marketing, and audit material.

- **Invoice and claw-back file.** The Pemberton invoice is not categorically privileged; objective billing fields should not be withheld. In the claw-back compilation, the internal memorandum may be protected work product, but the letter sent to opposing counsel and its nonprivileged exhibit should be separated and not claimed.

- **Cascade counsel communication.** The Bridger email contains litigation counsel’s work product and may qualify for common-interest protection, but the supplied record does not establish the necessary shared legal interest, confidentiality, or absence of adversity. A written agreement is a strong safeguard but not necessarily an absolute prerequisite under the supplied protocol. Obtain partner confirmation before withholding.

- **Undated handwritten note.** Establish authorship, date, purpose, and source of any legal content. Keep it out of production pending that review. If protection remains genuinely uncertain, counsel may consider in camera review; do not represent the candidate log description as a final claim.

# 5. Draft Privilege Log and Service Controls

The companion workbook contains 12 draft candidate entries for documents or portions recommended for withholding. It includes conditional entries for the two documents requiring further review. Conditional entries are not service-ready: remove them if the final review determines the document is not protected, and revise Bates identifiers once assigned. Before any production, counsel should verify the exact portions withheld, the final privilege basis, recipient lists and distribution context, any relevant non-waiver/common-interest agreements, and the status of any prior disclosure. The review protocol requires second-level review and partner sign-off for waiver, crime-fraud, and in-camera issues.
