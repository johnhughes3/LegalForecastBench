# 1. Executive Summary — What the Record Means for Summary Judgment

**Bottom line.** On the current record, Greenleaf is unlikely to win summary judgment on liability for exclusivity breach, wrongful termination, or its own counterclaim. The documents create — at a minimum — genuine disputes of material fact on each, and on several issues (the Cascade diversion; whether the termination notice was premature under the text of § 9.2) Harborview has a credible cross-motion for partial summary judgment. Greenleaf's best Rule 56 terrain is **damages and the claims that go beyond contract**: the five-year lost-profits theory, the contractual limitation-of-liability provisions, the attorney-fee demand, tortious interference, and parts of the fraud claim. A Daubert / Rule 37(c) motion against Dr. Chakrabarti, due by the **February 1, 2025** Daubert deadline (a Saturday — file by Friday, January 31), is the key companion motion.

**The ten findings that matter most:**

1.  **The EDA's actual § 9.2 text is worse for Greenleaf than any pleading acknowledges.** It provides that a termination notice "issued prior to the occurrence of an actual material breach … shall be of no force or effect," and § 5.3 measures the minimum "during the full applicable Purchase Year." Year 3 ended March 14, 2023; the termination notice issued February 15, 2023. This is Harborview's cleanest cross-motion argument. (All pleadings, both deposition summaries and the breach notice also describe a "60 days' notice" requirement that does not appear in § 9.2.)

2.  **Stanton's August 23, 2022 email links the Cascade diversion to the QA campaign.** It directs aggressive scaling of Cascade, separate invoicing "not \[through\] the Harborview account codes," building "leverage," and keeping Fong out of the Cascade routing because "I'll handle the QA side separately." Three weeks later she directed QA tightening for "the Harborview batches" only. The Complaint was drafted without this email; expect it to anchor the opposition brief.

3.  **Section 3.1 defeats the Answer's "third-party distribution only" construction as to Cascade.** Section 3.1 bars Greenleaf from appointing, "directly or through any third party," any "other distributor, dealer, reseller." Cascade is a distributor, and Holcomb conceded that even his narrow reading covers third-party distributors. Holcomb's August 22 email ("The EDA has specific terms around the territory") shows he knew about the restriction when it was happening.

4.  **Causation defeats the counterclaim even on conservative numbers.** Diversion alone (\$1.9M) plus actual purchases (\$5.8M) = \$7.7M, which is above the \$7.0M minimum without counting any QA rejection. Holcomb's "the minimum is the minimum regardless" is a problematic admission under the prevention doctrine.

5.  **Greenleaf's own QA expert (Buckley) found 11 of 14 rejections inconsistent with Greenleaf's own written manual and industry standards** (\$1,098,700), with a disparity significant at p \< 0.005. If that report has been served, Harborview can use it. Decide immediately whether Buckley remains a testifying expert.

6.  **Serious data-integrity conflicts exist across the QA evidence.** The produced QA log, Fong's October 3, 2022 email, Fong's testimony and Buckley's lot table give incompatible lot numbers, dates, products, values, reasons and denominators (see Section 5). Examples: the log lists 15 numbered rejections while its own summary, the Answer and every witness say 14; the October 3 email reports results for lots the log shows were inspected after October 3 or passed. These conflicts must be resolved before any QA fact goes into a Rule 56 statement.

7.  **The non-renewal notice was consciously not sent.** Holcomb's December 1, 2022 email raised the deadline expressly ("belt-and-suspenders"). No notice was sent by the deadline (correctly December 14, 2022, not December 15 as the Complaint, Chakrabarti and Holcomb say), so the EDA arguably auto-renewed on March 15, 2023. The flip side favors Greenleaf: lost-profit exposure should be capped at the one-year Renewal Term, because Greenleaf could have non-renewed for Year 5.

8.  **Limitation-of-liability clauses (§§ 12.1, 12.2) are an unpleaded but potentially powerful damages defense.** Consequential damages are excluded, and liability is capped at 12 months' commissions, for everything except Article 3 (exclusivity) breaches. That could confine wrongful-termination and QA damages to roughly \$1.0M (18% × about \$5.8M). The same § 12.1 bars Greenleaf's own \$792,000 consequential counterclaim component.

9.  **The EDA contains no attorney-fee clause.** Section 14.8, which the Complaint cites for fees, is the governing-law/venue clause. Both sides' fee demands lack a contractual basis. This is a clean partial summary judgment issue (and Greenleaf should drop its own fee request).

10. **Privilege and preservation exposure.** The Stanton→Ivers January 3, 2023 email ("let's use that as the basis") was produced on September 30, 2024 (GL-PROD-007834). Privilege was first asserted 36 days later, and no clawback is documented. Separately, the litigation hold (about March 2024) came roughly 13 months after Harborview's February 22, 2023 preservation demand.
