# II. Background and Framework

## A. Claims and defenses that define relevance

Relevance under Rule 26(b)(1) is measured against the claims and defenses: Caspian's claims for (1) breach of the JV Agreement, (2) DTSA and (3) NCTSPA misappropriation, (4) unjust enrichment, and (5) UDTPA; and Greenleaf's counterclaims for (1) a declaratory judgment that PureLine 400 does not infringe Caspian IP and (2) breach of confidentiality through disclosure to Helix Waterworks. Key contractual anchors:

- JV formed Jan. 15, 2019; dissolved June 30, 2023; wind-down July 1 – Sept. 30, 2023 (Diss. Agr. §§ 2.1, 5.1).

- JV Purpose limited to membrane-based filtration products for industrial water treatment (JV Agr. §§ 3.1–3.3); exclusivity during the Term (§ 3.3) expressly excludes Greenleaf's existing product lines and non-membrane products.

- NanoSieve license terminated at dissolution (JV Agr. § 7.3(c)); post-dissolution use prohibited (Diss. Agr. § 4.1). Non-compete on Joint IP runs through June 30, 2025 (Diss. Agr. §§ 6.1, 6.4); Joint IP royalties begin July 1, 2025 (Diss. Agr. Ex. B).

- PureLine 400 R&D was initiated **April 3, 2023** — during the JV Term and before the Dissolution Agreement was signed — and launched November 15, 2023 (hold memo § 2). Expect Caspian to focus heavily on this timeline under the § 3.3 exclusivity covenant; requests aimed at PureLine 400's origin (Nos. 9, 24, 25, 35, 36, 39) are core and difficult to resist wholesale.

- Seconded engineers remained employees of their home party (JV Agr. §§ 6.1, 6.3); each was required to sign confidentiality and invention-assignment agreements (§ 6.5); returning personnel were subject to exit interviews and device audits (Diss. Agr. § 5.6).

- Damages cap of \$26M under the Dissolution Agreement does not apply to misappropriation or willful misconduct (Diss. Agr. § 9.2) — which increases Caspian's incentive to pursue financial discovery.

## B. Governing discovery standards

- **Rule 26(b)(1):** relevance plus proportionality (importance of issues, amount in controversy, relative access, resources, importance of discovery, burden vs. benefit).

- **Rule 26(b)(2)(B)–(C):** ESI not reasonably accessible (e.g., backup tapes); court must limit cumulative, duplicative, or disproportionate discovery.

- **Rule 26(b)(3), (b)(5)(A):** work product; privilege log requirement. **Rule 26(c)(1)(G):** protection for trade secrets and confidential commercial information.

- **Rule 34(b)(2)(B)–(C):** objections must be stated "with specificity," and each response must state whether responsive materials are being withheld on the basis of the objection. Generalized/boilerplate objections are routinely disregarded by district courts in the Fourth Circuit; each objection below should be request-specific. Where we agree to produce a narrowed scope, the response should say so expressly and state a reasonable completion date (Rule 34(b)(2)(B)).

- **Rule 26(g):** signing counsel certifies reasonable inquiry — relevant to how we describe pre-2018 and third-party materials.

- **Rule 45:** proper vehicle for documents held by non-parties (Ridgepoint, Sterling Ark, Ostrowski personally).

I have also not independently verified the content of "Local Rule 26.2" referenced in Plaintiff's Instruction No. 3.*
