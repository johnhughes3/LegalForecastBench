## Governing framework and response discipline

Rule 26(b)(1) limits discovery to nonprivileged matter relevant to a claim or defense and proportional to the needs of the case, considering the importance of the issues, amount in controversy, relative access to information, resources, importance of the discovery, and whether burden or expense outweighs likely benefit. Rule 34 requires a request to describe items with reasonable particularity and reaches materials in the responding party’s possession, custody, or control. Under Rule 34(b)(2)(B)–(C), state specific grounds for each objection and say whether responsive material is being withheld. Rule 34(b)(2)(E) permits production as kept in the usual course of business or organized and labeled by request. Rules 26(b)(5)(A) and 26(b)(3) govern privilege/work-product claims. Rule 26(c)(1)(G) permits protection for trade secrets and confidential commercial information; confidentiality alone is not a basis to withhold otherwise discoverable material. The protective order (“PO”) governs handling, not discoverability or privilege (PO §§1.3–1.4).

For ESI, use a phased, custodian- and subject-focused collection, with search terms and/or sampling where appropriate. Rule 26(b)(2)(B) applies if identified ESI is not reasonably accessible because of undue burden or cost; support any such contention with actual system, volume, restoration, and cost facts rather than assuming inaccessibility. Preservation is not production: the litigation hold covers January 1, 2018 forward and specific custodians/systems, but does not make every preserved record responsive or proportional. Conversely, the hold’s instruction to preserve records does not authorize deletion of potentially responsive material while objections are negotiated.

Use the PO’s actual tiers—**CONFIDENTIAL** and **ATTORNEYS’ EYES ONLY (AEO)**. The RFPs’ phrase “Highly Confidential — Attorneys’ Eyes Only” is not the PO’s defined label; clarify that productions will be designated under the entered order. The existing AEO tier is useful for technical and financial material (PO §§2.3, 5), but neither tier replaces a relevance objection. It also does not automatically entitle Caspian personnel to see AEO material. For difficult technical material, consider an agreed protocol limiting access to outside counsel and an independent, noncompetitive expert, with secure storage, no competitive use, and appropriate redaction or segregation. Seek further Rule 26(c) relief if the specific material and risk warrant it.

Do not rely on omnibus boilerplate. The definitions of “Greenleaf,” “relating to,” and “Document” should not expand collection beyond responsive material within Greenleaf’s possession, custody, or control; the “broadest permissible sense” phrase cannot override relevance, proportionality, or reasonable particularity. Identify responsive material withheld under each objection. For the instructions: (1) supplement as Rule 26(e) requires, not under an unlimited “promptly” obligation; (2) follow Rule 34(b)(2)(E)—produce as kept in the usual course or organize/label by request, without an extra source-by-source identification obligation if produced as kept; (3) provide a privilege log under Rule 26(b)(5)(A) and Local Rule 26.2; (4) do not create a narrative for every unknown/lost item, but report known material loss as required and preserve relevant evidence; (5) distinguish preservation from production, including backup/archival sources; (6) use the PO’s defined confidentiality tiers; (7) apply the date in each request, not the undefined “broadest” default; (8) do not allow the instruction to override proper privilege or other legally justified treatment, and seek agreement or Court direction before redacting responsive nonprivileged content; (9) produce responsive attachments as document families where reasonably practicable; and (10) no objection to the ordinary singular/plural rule. CMO ¶9 requires counsel to attempt resolution in a substantive telephone or in-person discussion; correspondence alone is insufficient. If a discovery dispute remains, the CMO provides for a letter brief of no more than five pages, with a five-page response due within three business days.

Begin the privilege review now. Preserve native material and family relationships; deduplicate without losing distinct annotations/versions; identify counsel and legal-advice threads; review mixed legal/business communications individually; and separate factual business records from legal advice. Use privilege terms/domains to prioritize, not as a substitute for review. The litigation-hold estimate of approximately 950,000 post-deduplication documents describes a broad preservation universe, not the number responsive to RFP 31 or the number that must be individually logged.

Provide a log that satisfies Rule 26(b)(5)(A) and W.D.N.C. Local Rule 26.2. A **categorical log should be proposed only by agreement or Court approval**, with categories narrow and descriptive enough for Caspian and the Court to assess the claim; do not impose a categorical log unilaterally. Separate counsel’s mental impressions, legal analysis, and litigation strategy. Apply the same logging approach as RFP 31.
