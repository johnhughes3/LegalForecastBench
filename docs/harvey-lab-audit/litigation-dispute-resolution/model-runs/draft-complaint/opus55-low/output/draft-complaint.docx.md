Plaintiff Verdant Biotech Solutions, Inc. (“Verdant”), by and through its undersigned counsel, alleges as follows against Defendants Dr. Marcus Ellison Tate (“Tate”) and AgriNova Crop Sciences, LLC (“AgriNova”) (together, “Defendants”). Allegations concerning Verdant and its own acts are made on personal knowledge; all other allegations are made on information and belief formed after reasonable inquiry, including a forensic examination of Verdant’s systems.

# **NATURE OF THE ACTION**

1\. This is an action to stop, and to remedy, the theft of the core technology of Verdant’s business. In a nineteen-day window in the autumn of 2024, while he was still Verdant’s Vice President of Research & Development, Tate downloaded 3,814 files (24.6 GB) comprising substantially all of Verdant’s proprietary TerraPrime soil-microbiome platform — including the complete source code for Verdant’s MicroMap 3.0 strain-synergy model, genomic and characterization data for Verdant’s library of 4,217 microbial strains, fourteen formulation dossiers, and Verdant’s confidential strategic roadmap — and copied that data to a personal USB drive.

2\. Tate then sent a 1.2 GB encrypted attachment from a personal ProtonMail account to an undisclosed recipient, deleted the downloaded files from his Company laptop and purged the recycle bin, downloaded Verdant’s most closely held executive strategy document, and only then tendered his resignation. On the night before his final day, he reset his Company laptop to factory settings.

3\. Within weeks of his departure, AgriNova — Verdant’s principal competitor — announced Tate as its Chief Science Officer and simultaneously unveiled “BioYield,” a “soil-microbiome enhancement platform” built on a “proprietary library of characterized microbial strains” and “AI-driven bioinformatic models for predicting strain synergies,” which AgriNova says will reach market on an “accelerated timeline” by Q4 2025. Since then, Tate has personally solicited Verdant scientists to join him at AgriNova, and AgriNova’s recruiter has solicited another Verdant scientist expressly on Tate’s “specific\[ \] recommend\[ation\].”

4\. Verdant brings claims under the Defend Trade Secrets Act of 2016, 18 U.S.C. § 1836 *et seq.* (“DTSA”); the North Carolina Trade Secrets Protection Act, N.C. Gen. Stat. § 66-152 *et seq.* (“NCTSPA”); the North Carolina Unfair and Deceptive Trade Practices Act, N.C. Gen. Stat. § 75-1.1 (“UDTPA”); and North Carolina common law for breach of contract, tortious interference with contract, unjust enrichment, and civil conspiracy. Verdant seeks a temporary restraining order, preliminary and permanent injunctive relief, the return and forensic sequestration of its property, damages, and all other appropriate relief.

# **FACTUAL ALLEGATIONS**

15\. ## **B. The TerraPrime Platform and Verdant’s Trade Secrets**

16\. TerraPrime is Verdant’s flagship research and development platform for next-generation soil-microbiome enhancement products. From fiscal year 2018 through fiscal year 2024, Verdant invested approximately \$62.3 million in TerraPrime, including approximately \$28.1 million in personnel costs, \$14.7 million in laboratory and equipment costs, \$11.2 million in computational genomics infrastructure, and \$8.3 million in field-trial costs.

17\. Without disclosing their substance, Verdant identifies the following trade secrets at issue (collectively, the “Trade Secrets”). Verdant will provide a further particularized identification subject to an appropriate protective order.

18\. **The Strain Library.** Verdant’s proprietary library of 4,217 characterized microbial strains, including for each strain its taxonomic classification, genomic sequence data (maintained in .fasta/.fastq files), phenotypic characterization records and databases, soil-compatibility profiles, synergy indices with other strains, field-trial performance data, and proprietary annotations, maintained on Verdant’s VaultSci system in the directory /TerraPrime/StrainLibrary/ (the “Strain Library”). The Strain Library is the product of seven years of bioprospecting, isolation, characterization, and screening and has never been published or disclosed outside Verdant.

19\. **MicroMap 3.0.** The source code, algorithms, model architecture and parameters, training datasets, configuration files, and algorithm documentation for MicroMap 3.0, Verdant’s proprietary machine-learning model for predicting microbial strain synergies in target soil environments and optimizing multi-organism formulations, maintained in /TerraPrime/MicroMap3.0/src/ (“MicroMap 3.0”). Only twelve Verdant employees had full access to MicroMap 3.0. Its code and algorithms have never been published, presented, or disclosed to any third party.

20\. **The Formulation Dossiers.** Verdant’s development dossiers for fourteen TerraPrime formulations identified by internal codename, including for each the specific strain combination, concentration ratios, carrier-medium composition, stabilizer formulation, application protocols, stability data, greenhouse and field-trial results, and regulatory pre-submission drafts, maintained in /TerraPrime/Formulations/ (the “Formulation Dossiers”). The Trade Secrets include all information in the Formulation Dossiers that has not been disclosed in a published patent application or issued patent or in a commercially available product, including experimental data, negative results, manufacturing know-how, and development-stage formulations.

21\. **The Strategic Roadmap and Business Documents.** Verdant’s strategic planning, market analysis, competitive intelligence, pricing, and financial projection documents maintained in /TerraPrime/Strategy/, including the 47-page “TerraPrime Strategic Pipeline & Launch Roadmap 2025–2029” (the “Strategic Roadmap”), which contains projected launch dates, target crops and geographies, pricing, projected revenue by product line, regulatory timelines, partnership and licensing strategy, and Verdant’s assessment of competitive threats, including AgriNova. Access to the Strategic Roadmap was limited to seven Verdant executives.

22\. Each of the Trade Secrets derives independent economic value, actual and potential, from not being generally known to, and not being readily ascertainable through proper means by, others who could obtain economic value from its disclosure or use. A competitor possessing the Trade Secrets could bypass years of research and tens of millions of dollars of investment, target its development toward formulations Verdant has already validated, and anticipate and counter Verdant’s product launches, pricing, and partnerships.

## **C. Verdant’s Reasonable Measures to Protect the Trade Secrets**

23\. Verdant has taken reasonable measures to keep the Trade Secrets secret, including:

- **Physical controls:** badge-restricted access to Verdant’s R&D facility; biometric authentication for the cryogenic strain-storage area; escorted visitor access conditioned on execution of a nondisclosure agreement; and camera monitoring of entry and exit points.
- **Electronic controls:** storage of TerraPrime files exclusively on VaultSci, Verdant’s internal document-management system, with role-based permissions; restriction of full MicroMap 3.0 access to twelve employees and of the Strategic Roadmap to seven executives; logging of file access and downloads; encryption of Company laptops; and email-gateway monitoring.
- **Policies:** an Acceptable Use Policy that prohibits connecting external storage devices to Company equipment without prior IT authorization (§ 4.3), and an IT Asset Return Policy that requires equipment to be returned “in its existing operational state, without alteration, deletion, or reformatting.”
- **Contracts:** confidentiality and invention-assignment agreements for all employees; restrictive-covenant agreements for senior employees; and nondisclosure agreements with contractors, distributors, and licensing partners.
- **Classification and training:** a tiered document-classification system (including “HIGHLY CONFIDENTIAL — EXECUTIVE DISTRIBUTION ONLY” and “CONFIDENTIAL — R&D RESTRICTED”) and mandatory annual trade-secret training, which Tate most recently completed on September 12, 2024.

## **D. Tate’s Employment and Contractual Obligations**

24\. Verdant hired Tate on March 15, 2018 as Vice President, Research & Development. Tate oversaw the entire TerraPrime development pipeline and had access to each category of the Trade Secrets. At the time of his departure, his base salary was \$385,000 per year.

25\. As a condition of his employment, and in exchange for his employment, compensation, and access to Confidential Information, Tate executed two written agreements on March 15, 2018: an Employment Agreement (the “Employment Agreement”) and a Confidentiality and Invention Assignment Agreement (the “CIAA”) (together, the “Agreements”). True and correct copies are attached as **Exhibits A and B**.

26\. **Confidentiality and non-use.** In the CIAA, Tate agreed not to disclose Confidential Information — defined to include trade secrets, formulations, genomic data, strain libraries, bioinformatic models, source code, product roadmaps, and business strategies — during or after his employment (§ 2.1); not to use Confidential Information for any purpose other than his Verdant duties, including for the benefit of any competitor (§ 2.2); and not to “copy, download, transfer, transmit, or remove Confidential Information from Company Systems” without written authorization, or to “connect any personal storage device . . . to Company Systems or equipment without prior written authorization” (§ 2.5). These obligations survive in perpetuity as to trade secrets and for five years as to other Confidential Information (§ 8.1).

27\. **Return of materials and preservation of equipment.** Tate agreed upon termination to return all Company Materials, expressly including “USB devices” containing Confidential Information (CIAA § 4.1(d)); to delete Confidential Information from personal devices and accounts and certify that deletion in writing (§ 4.2); to retain no copies (§ 4.3); and to return Company equipment “in \[its\] existing state, with all data, software, configurations, and files intact” and not to “delete, alter, destroy, or modify any data or files” on it (§ 4.4; *see also* Employment Agreement § 4.3).

28\. **Invention assignment.** Tate assigned to Verdant all right, title, and interest in inventions that he conceived or developed during his employment that relate to Verdant’s business or that were developed using Verdant’s Confidential Information (CIAA § 3.2).

29\. **Non-competition.** Tate agreed that for eighteen months after his employment ended he would not “be employed by, serve as a consultant or advisor to, or have any ownership interest in” any business that competes with Verdant “in the research, development, manufacture, marketing, distribution, or sale of microbial or genetically engineered agricultural products, soil-enhancement products, or bioinformatic agricultural technology platforms” within the United States (Employment Agreement § 5.2).

30\. **Non-solicitation of employees.** Tate agreed that for twenty-four months after his employment ended he would not, “directly or indirectly,” “solicit, recruit, encourage, induce, or attempt to hire, or assist any other person or entity in soliciting, recruiting, encouraging, inducing, or attempting to hire,” any Verdant employee. The covenant expressly “prohibits Executive from providing names, contact information, or recommendations of current or recent Company employees to recruiters, hiring managers, or any other third parties for purposes of soliciting, recruiting, or hiring such employees away from the Company” (Employment Agreement § 5.3).

31\. **Non-solicitation of customers and distributors.** Tate agreed that for eighteen months after his employment ended he would not, directly or indirectly, solicit, divert, or contact any customer, distributor, or research partner of Verdant with whom he had material contact during his last twenty-four months of employment, for the purpose of marketing products competitive with Verdant’s (Employment Agreement § 5.4).

32\. **Notice of resignation.** Tate agreed to provide “not less than sixty (60) calendar days’ prior written notice” of resignation and acknowledged that the notice period is “reasonable and necessary to enable the Company to protect its Confidential Information and trade secrets,” including by placing him on garden leave and restricting his systems access, and that providing shorter notice “constitutes a material breach of this Agreement” (Employment Agreement § 7.3).

33\. Tate acknowledged that these covenants are reasonable and necessary to protect Verdant’s legitimate business interests (Employment Agreement §§ 5.1, 5.6), and that any breach would cause Verdant irreparable harm entitling it to injunctive relief (Employment Agreement § 6.1; CIAA § 7.1). The Agreements are governed by North Carolina law (Employment Agreement § 9.1; CIAA § 9.1).

## **E. Tate Exfiltrates the TerraPrime Platform**

34\. After Tate’s departure, Verdant retained an independent digital-forensics firm to examine Tate’s Company laptop (Asset Tag VB-L-0447), Tate’s VaultSci access logs, and Verdant’s network records. That examination revealed the following.

35\. **October 27, 2024 — mass download.** Between approximately 10:17 p.m. and 11:48 p.m. on Sunday, October 27, 2024, Tate, using his account “mtate_vp” from the Verdant R&D facility, downloaded 3,814 files totaling 24.6 GB from the TerraPrime directory on VaultSci to his Company laptop. The download included 847 files of MicroMap 3.0 source code, training data, and algorithm documentation; 1,206 files of Strain Library genomic and characterization data; 387 files from the Formulation Dossiers; 198 strategic planning and business documents; and 1,176 other R&D working files.

36\. This was the largest single download by any user in at least the preceding twenty-four months of VaultSci logs. The next-largest single-day download by any R&D employee in that period was approximately 2.1 GB — less than one-tenth the size. Tate had no legitimate business need to download substantially the entire TerraPrime platform to his laptop.

37\. **November 2, 2024 — transfer to a personal USB device.** At approximately 8:32 p.m. on November 2, 2024, Tate connected a personal SanDisk Extreme Pro 256 GB USB flash drive (serial number SDP-82741-EXT) — a device never before connected to his laptop and not in Verdant’s asset inventory — to his Company laptop, and over the next seventy-five minutes transferred approximately 24.6 GB of data to it. Hash-value comparison confirms that at least 3,641 of the 3,814 files downloaded on October 27 (95.5%) were transferred to the USB device, and the total volume is consistent with the entire set. Verdant never authorized Tate to connect any external storage device to Company equipment. Tate has never returned that device or its contents to Verdant.

38\. **November 8, 2024 — encrypted transmission to an unknown recipient.** At approximately 7:14 p.m. on November 8, 2024, while connected to Verdant’s R&D network, Tate sent an email from his personal account, m.tate.phd@protonmail.com, to an external recipient, bearing an encrypted attachment of approximately 1.2 GB. On information and belief, the attachment contained Trade Secrets and was sent to AgriNova or a person acting on its behalf.

39\. **November 14, 2024 — concealment.** Between approximately 6:22 p.m. and 6:43 p.m. on November 14, 2024, Tate deleted the 3,814 downloaded files from his laptop and then purged the recycle bin.

40\. **November 15, 2024 — the Strategic Roadmap.** At approximately 2:17 p.m. on November 15, 2024, Tate downloaded the Strategic Roadmap, marked “HIGHLY CONFIDENTIAL — EXECUTIVE DISTRIBUTION ONLY,” which had not been included in the October 27 download.

41\. **November 18, 2024 — resignation.** Three days later, Tate delivered a letter resigning his employment “effective January 10, 2025” to “pursue a new professional opportunity.” The letter did not identify his new employer. Tate’s letter provided fifty-three days’ notice, not the sixty days required by Section 7.3 of the Employment Agreement.

42\. **December 22, 2024 — AgriNova role disclosed.** While still employed by Verdant, Tate updated his LinkedIn profile to identify himself as “Chief Science Officer, AgriNova Crop Sciences,” with a start date of February 2025. On information and belief, Tate and AgriNova had agreed on Tate’s employment, or were negotiating it, during or before the period in which Tate took the Trade Secrets.

43\. **January 9–10, 2025 — destruction of evidence.** At approximately 11:18 p.m. on January 9, 2025 — the night before his final day — Tate executed a “Reset this PC — Remove everything” operation on his Company laptop, erasing all user data. He returned the wiped laptop on January 10, 2025, in violation of CIAA § 4.4, Employment Agreement § 4.3, and Verdant’s IT Asset Return Policy. Forensic artifacts documenting the events above were recovered only because the reset did not securely overwrite unallocated space.

44\. Tate did not disclose to Verdant that he had downloaded the TerraPrime platform, copied it to a personal device, or transmitted data from his personal email account. He did not return the USB device or any copy of the Trade Secrets, and he did not certify the deletion of Confidential Information from his personal devices and accounts, as CIAA § 4.2 requires.

## **F. AgriNova Announces BioYield on an “Accelerated Timeline”**

45\. On February 3, 2025, AgriNova issued a press release announcing Tate’s appointment as Chief Science Officer “effective immediately” and “\[c\]oncurrent\[ly\]” unveiling BioYield™, described as “a revolutionary soil-microbiome enhancement platform that leverages proprietary AI-driven strain selection and synergy modeling.”

46\. According to AgriNova, BioYield “utilizes a proprietary library of characterized microbial strains, advanced AI-driven bioinformatic models for predicting strain synergies and optimizing formulation performance, and a robust pipeline of next-generation formulations targeting key commercial crop applications,” with initial formulations for “soybeans, corn, and wheat.” Each element of that description corresponds to a category of the Trade Secrets — the Strain Library, MicroMap 3.0, and the Formulation Dossiers — and the initial target crops correspond to TerraPrime formulations that Verdant sells or is developing for soybean, corn, and wheat.

47\. AgriNova stated that Tate “will lead AgriNova’s expanded research division and oversee the full BioYield product development pipeline,” that his arrival “positions us to accelerate our entry into the soil-microbiome enhancement market at a pace we could not have previously imagined,” and that AgriNova “expect\[s\] an accelerated timeline” with first products reaching market by Q4 2025. AgriNova also stated that it “anticipates filing additional intellectual property protections in connection with the BioYield platform in the coming months.” Tate was quoted describing BioYield as “grounded in leveraging proven approaches to strain characterization and synergy modeling.”

48\. Developing a characterized strain library, a validated strain-synergy model, and a pipeline of field-ready formulations requires years of work and tens of millions of dollars; Verdant’s own effort took seven years and \$62.3 million. On information and belief, AgriNova did not possess a comparable platform before Tate’s arrival, and its ability to announce a platform and a market-entry date of Q4 2025 within weeks of hiring Tate reflects its acquisition and use of the Trade Secrets.

49\. On March 12, 2025, Heartland — Verdant’s largest distributor, and a distributor with which Tate had material contact during his last twenty-four months at Verdant — informed Verdant that AgriNova had presented BioYield at a distributor meeting, and that BioYield’s described mechanism of action, strain-combination approach, and target crop applications were “remarkably similar” to Verdant’s TerraPrime formulations. On information and belief, Tate participated in, directed, or supplied information for AgriNova’s solicitation of Heartland.

## **G. Tate and AgriNova Solicit Verdant’s Senior Scientists**

50\. On February 20, 2025, Tate sent a text message to Dr. Anya Kowalski, a Verdant Senior Research Scientist with full access to MicroMap 3.0: “Hey Anya, are you happy at Verdant? Things are moving fast here at AgriNova. We’re building something incredible. I’d love to chat about what we’re putting together — looking for top talent. Coffee sometime?” Dr. Kowalski did not respond and reported the message to Verdant the next day.

51\. On February 28, 2025, Dr. James Okonkwo, a Verdant Principal Scientist in Microbial Genomics who also has full access to MicroMap 3.0 and worked under Tate’s supervision, received a LinkedIn message from a person identifying as a “Recruitment Manager, AgriNova Crop Sciences”: “Dr. Okonkwo, Dr. Marcus Tate has specifically recommended you for a senior genomics role on our new BioYield team. Would you be open to a confidential conversation?” Dr. Okonkwo did not respond and reported the message to Verdant.

52\. On information and belief, Tate identified Dr. Okonkwo to AgriNova’s recruiting personnel by name, described his expertise, and recommended that AgriNova recruit him, for the purpose of hiring him away from Verdant — conduct that Section 5.3 of the Employment Agreement expressly prohibits. On information and belief, AgriNova, acting through its recruiting personnel, has contacted or attempted to contact other Verdant R&D employees in connection with recruiting efforts linked to Tate.

53\. Tate and AgriNova targeted scientists who are among the small group with full access to MicroMap 3.0 and who work at the core of the TerraPrime platform. On information and belief, Defendants seek to recruit those scientists to further accelerate BioYield using their knowledge of Verdant’s Trade Secrets.
