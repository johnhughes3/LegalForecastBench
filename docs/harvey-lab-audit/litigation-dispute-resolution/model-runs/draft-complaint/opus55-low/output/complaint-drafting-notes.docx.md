# **1. Bottom Line**

1.  **The trade secret case against Tate is strong.** The forensic record is unusually good. It shows a 24.6 GB off-hours download, a transfer to an unauthorized USB drive (95.5% hash-matched), an encrypted ProtonMail transmission, deletion of the files, and a factory reset the night before he left.
2.  **The case against AgriNova is much thinner.** Today it rests almost entirely on timing, marketing language in the press release, and a secondhand report from Heartland. AgriNova is the most likely defendant to win a Rule 12(b)(6) motion, and the TRO against AgriNova is the most exposed part of our position.
3.  **The most important legal problem is that neither of Tate’s agreements contains the DTSA whistleblower-immunity notice** (18 U.S.C. § 1833(b)). Both were signed after May 11, 2016. Without the notice, Verdant **cannot recover DTSA exemplary damages or attorneys’ fees from Tate** (§ 1833(b)(3)(C)). The Simmons workbook’s “up to \$170.04M” DTSA exemplary figure should not be used against Tate. The draft asks for DTSA exemplary damages and fees **only against AgriNova**, which is not Tate’s “employer” for this purpose. It seeks punitive damages and fees against Tate under NCTSPA § 66-154(c)–(d), Chapter 1D, and UDTPA instead. Verdant should also update its template CIAA for all current employees.
4.  **We should file sooner than April 7.** Verdant has had the Sentinel report since February 15. It learned of the solicitations February 21 and March 5, and of the Heartland presentation March 12. A seven-week-plus gap weakens the “irreparable harm” and “emergency” story for a TRO. I recommend filing the complaint and TRO/expedited-discovery motion as soon as the verification and open items below are done.

## **2.2 Considered and omitted**

- **Tortious interference with prospective economic advantage (requested by Showalter).** North Carolina requires that a specific contract would have been formed *but for* the interference. Heartland is an existing distributor, and no lost sale or terminated order has been identified. Pleading it now invites dismissal and dilutes credibility. **Recommendation:** add by amendment if Heartland (or another distributor) reduces orders or moves business to BioYield.
- **Unjust enrichment against Tate (requested)** is barred by the express contract (above).
- **Computer Fraud and Abuse Act.** Tate had authorized VaultSci access. *Van Buren v. United States*, 593 U.S. 374 (2021), and *WEC Carolina Energy Solutions v. Miller*, 687 F.3d 199 (4th Cir. 2012), foreclose “misuse of authorized access” theories. The laptop wipe might support a “damage” theory under § 1030(a)(5), but it adds little and invites a sideshow.
- **Breach of fiduciary duty / duty of loyalty.** Under *Dalton*, ordinary employees are not fiduciaries in North Carolina, and there is no freestanding duty-of-loyalty tort. **Open item:** if Tate was a board-appointed *corporate officer* (check bylaws and board minutes), a fiduciary-duty count becomes viable and should be added.
- **Conversion / trespass to chattels.** North Carolina generally does not allow conversion of intangible information, and the laptop was returned. The wipe is better used as spoliation evidence (adverse inference) than as a separate claim.
- **Individual claims against AgriNova’s CEO (Delacroix) or the unnamed recruiter.** There are no facts yet. Revisit after expedited discovery. Consider a Doe defendant for the recipient of the 11/8 email.

# **3. Strategic Concerns**

## **3.1 The garden-leave “consequential damages” theory should not be pursued**

Showalter asks us to plead that the 7-day notice shortfall deprived Verdant of time to detect or prevent the exfiltration. **The chronology defeats this.** Every exfiltration event (Oct. 27, Nov. 2, Nov. 8, Nov. 14, Nov. 15) happened *before* the Nov. 18 notice. A full 60-day notice would have started the same day and changed nothing about those events. Also, Verdant had 53 days in which it could have invoked § 7.3(c)–(d) garden leave and cut off access, and it apparently did not. The only post-notice harmful act is the Jan. 9 laptop wipe, and Verdant could have prevented that within the 53 days it had. Pleading consequential damages here would:

- invite an easy rebuttal;
- spotlight Verdant’s own failure to restrict a departing head of R&D who would not name his new employer; and
- feed the “reasonable measures” defense.

The draft pleads Count VII as a distinct material breach, as Showalter requested, with nominal damages and the contractual § 7.3(ii) remedy (withholding discretionary compensation). **Do not elect the § 7.3(iii) remedy.** “Invoking” the covenants from Nov. 18, 2024 would start the post-employment clocks earlier and could *shorten* protection (for example, the non-compete would end around May 2026 instead of July 10, 2026). Measured from termination on Jan. 10, 2025:

- non-compete runs to **July 10, 2026**;
- customer non-solicit runs to **July 10, 2026**; and
- employee non-solicit runs to **Jan. 10, 2027**,

all subject to § 5.7 tolling.

## **3.2 Reasonable-measures exposure**

Defendants will argue Verdant’s controls failed in practice:

- The largest download in VaultSci’s logged history (about 12× the next largest) produced no alert.
- The USB ban was policy-only, not technically enforced.
- ProtonMail traffic with a 1.2 GB attachment went through the gateway without being flagged.
- Tate kept full access for 53 days after resigning to an undisclosed “new opportunity.”
- No exit certification was obtained.
- The remote-wipe and monitoring capabilities described in the Trade Secret Summary were not used.

Courts judge reasonableness, not perfection, and the multi-layer program is well documented. But we should prepare a declarant (the IT Director, Kevin Marsh) to explain what monitoring existed and why it did not trigger.

## **3.3 Heavy reliance on “information and belief” against AgriNova (Twombly / Rule 11)**

We have **no direct evidence** that AgriNova received or used any Verdant file. The press-release language (“strain library,” “AI-driven synergy modeling”) describes concepts that are common in the ag-biologicals industry. AgriNova was founded in 2016 and may have had a microbial program before Tate. Before filing:

1.  Investigate AgriNova’s pre-2025 R&D footprint: patents and applications, publications, EPA/state registrations, job postings, and investor materials. If AgriNova had no meaningful microbial platform, the “accelerated timeline” inference becomes much stronger. If it did, reframe the allegations.
2.  Get a detailed statement or declaration from the Heartland contact: who presented, what was said, and whether any materials were handed out (obtain copies).
3.  Use the TRO motion to seek **expedited discovery and a neutral forensic protocol**: the USB drive, the ProtonMail account, and AgriNova’s BioYield repositories. That is where proof of use will come from.

## **3.4 Knowledge element for AgriNova: send a notice letter**

Nothing in the file shows Verdant ever put AgriNova on notice of Tate’s covenants. For tortious interference, and to make every later AgriNova act knowing and willful (which supports DTSA exemplary damages and UDTPA fees), **serve AgriNova with a notice and preservation letter enclosing the Agreements**. Serve it at the latest at the moment of filing; earlier if we decide the risk that Tate destroys evidence is manageable. Tate’s documented anti-forensic conduct argues for serving at filing, with a preservation demand, not before.

## **3.5 Relief: TRO scope and the DTSA employment limitation**

- Under 18 U.S.C. § 1836(b)(3)(A)(i)(I), a DTSA injunction may not “prevent a person from entering into an employment relationship.” Conditions must rest on *evidence of threatened misappropriation*, not merely on what the person knows. Ask for the employment restriction under the **contract (Count V)**, not the DTSA. Emphasize return and sequestration of the USB drive, no-use orders, and forensic imaging. Those are the most defensible, highest-value items.
- **DTSA ex parte civil seizure (§ 1836(b)(2))** is available for the USB drive in “extraordinary circumstances.” The Jan. 9 wipe and the Nov. 14 deletion give a real basis for arguing Tate would destroy evidence if given notice. Seizure is rarely granted, however. A TRO with an immediate turnover order is the usual route. We need a client decision on this.
- **Bond.** Contractual bond waivers (EA § 6.1; CIAA § 7.1) do not bind the court under Rule 65(c). Budget for a meaningful bond, especially for any restraint on BioYield development.
- **Sealing.** File a sealed version, or a redacted one, under Local Civil Rule 79.2. Do not attach the Trade Secret Summary.

## **3.6 Privilege**

The Sentinel report, the Showalter memo, and the Trade Secret Summary are marked privileged or work product. The complaint pleads *facts* from the forensic work but does **not** attach or cite the report. Once Sentinel (Driscoll) is designated as a testifying expert or submits a TRO declaration, expect disclosure of his materials under Rule 26(a)(2)/(b)(4). Plan for a stand-alone Driscoll declaration for the TRO rather than filing the report.

## **3.7 Damages: do not plead the \$85.02M figure**

The complaint pleads damages “in an amount to be proven at trial” and uses no dollar totals, for these reasons:

- **Lost competitive advantage (\$64.5M):** the 30% erosion rate is an unsupported assumption. The DCF also includes cash flows from products already on the market (for example, TP-101 SoyBoost, commercialized in FY2022). That will draw a causation attack.
- **Distributor diversion (\$16.92M):** 15% of top-10 distributor revenue has no tie to any observed loss. The workbook’s note that Heartland “has already reported AgriNova solicitation” overstates what Heartland said, which was a report of a presentation.
- **Employee losses (\$3.6M):** **no scientist has left.** Both solicited employees refused. This is a training-cost figure for hypothetical departures, not damages.
- **Overlap:** R&D impairment (\$62.3M), lost advantage, and unjust enrichment are overlapping or alternative measures. NCTSPA § 66-154(b) awards the *greater* of loss or unjust enrichment, not both.
- Retain a damages expert early. Unjust enrichment (AgriNova’s avoided R&D costs and head start) and a reasonable royalty are likely the most defensible measures.

# **4. Anticipated Defenses and Responses**

## **4.1 Non-compete is unenforceable (Tate; AgriNova derivatively)**

- **Defense:** Under North Carolina’s *United Laboratories v. Kuykendall*, 322 N.C. 643 (1988) framework:
  - the covenant is **nationwide**, while Verdant sells in 38 states;
  - it bars employment by a competitor **in any capacity**, which North Carolina courts have repeatedly found overbroad (*e.g.*, *VisionAIR, Inc. v. James*, 167 N.C. App. 504 (2004));
  - it covers “bioinformatic agricultural technology platforms” generally; and
  - North Carolina applies a **strict blue-pencil rule**: courts may strike separable terms but not rewrite them. The Supreme Court of North Carolina has indicated that a contractual “reformation” clause like EA § 5.6 does not expand that power (*Beverage Systems of the Carolinas, LLC v. Associated Beverage Repair, LLC*, 368 N.C. 693 (2016)).
- **Response:**
  - Tate was the head of R&D with comprehensive access, and North Carolina courts give executive-level covenants more room.
  - AgriNova markets nationally, so the national restriction matches the actual competition.
  - The role Tate took (CSO leading a directly competing platform) falls at the core of any reasonable covenant.
  - Hedge: separate counts, narrowed relief, and a case that stands on the trade secret and non-solicit claims even if § 5.2 falls.
  - Cite-check all North Carolina authority before briefing.

## **4.2 Customer non-solicit overbreadth**

Clause (b) of § 5.4 reaches any customer about whom Tate “had access to Confidential Information.” Given his role, that is effectively every customer, and it is vulnerable. Clause (a) (material contact) is separable and likely enforceable. Rely on clause (a) for Heartland.

## **4.3 Employee solicitation**

- **Kowalski:** Tate will say it was a friendly “coffee” text, not a solicitation. The response is the text of § 5.3 (“encourage,” “induce”; “regardless of whether” a competitive role) and the message’s own words.
- **Okonkwo:** The recruiter’s message is admissible against AgriNova as an agent’s statement (FRE 801(d)(2)(D)). **Against Tate, it is hearsay within hearsay** (the recruiter reporting what Tate said) unless the co-conspirator exception (801(d)(2)(E)) applies. Next steps:
  - identify the recruiter;
  - preserve the LinkedIn metadata; and
  - seek AgriNova recruiting communications in expedited discovery.
- Okonkwo ¶ 10 (“other colleagues received similar outreach”) is unsourced hearsay. **Interview R&D staff and obtain declarations** before relying on it. The complaint pleads it only on information and belief.

## **4.4 Tate’s innocent-explanation defenses to the trade secret claims**

Expected arguments, with our responses:

- **Legitimate work purpose:** “As VP I routinely worked with this data.” Response: the volume, the Sunday-night timing, and the lack of any comparable prior event.
- **“Backup” USB; deletion equals giving the data back:** Response: the USB was never returned, and he never certified deletion.
- **The 1.2 GB email was personal:** Response: content unknown. This is our weakest forensic point. Do not overstate it (the draft says “on information and belief”).
- **Wipe was routine:** Response: it violated express contract terms and policy, and should support an adverse-inference or spoliation argument (Rule 37(e) may not apply pre-suit to non-ESI-preservation duties; the common-law inherent-power route is the fallback).
- **General skill and knowledge:** Tate will argue he is free to use the expertise in his head, and that “strain selection” and “synergy modeling” are industry concepts. Response: we claim specific code, data, and dossiers, not general expertise.

## **4.5 Secrecy and “readily ascertainable” challenges (these need client verification)**

- **Patent publication:** U.S. applications generally publish about 18 months after the earliest priority date. The workbook shows the first applications were filed in FY2021, so those are very likely **already published**. That conflicts with the Trade Secret Summary’s statement that the applications “have not yet published.” Published content is not a trade secret. **Confirm with Ashford Cromwell & Pratt the publication status of every application** and limit the claimed secrets to unpublished material. The draft already carves out published and commercially available content (Formulation Dossier paragraph).
- **Commercialized products:** At least TP-101 is on the market, and the workbook lists TP-207 and TP-310 as distributed through Heartland and others. Defendants will argue that strains in sold products can be isolated and sequenced (reverse engineering is a defense under both the DTSA and NCTSPA § 66-152(3)).
- **Internal inconsistencies to reconcile before filing:**
  - Sentinel describes the dossiers as “TP-101 through TP-214.” The Trade Secret Summary’s 14 codenames run TP-101 to TP-900.
  - The Summary lists TP-207 and TP-310 as Phase 3 / Phase 2 development-stage. The workbook shows both being sold through distributors.

- Defense counsel will use these discrepancies to attack the particularity and credibility of the trade secret identification. The draft therefore avoids listing codenames and describes the dossiers by directory.

## **4.6 AgriNova-specific defenses**

- Failure to plausibly allege acquisition or use (Twombly): see § 3.3.
- Independent development, supported by pre-2025 AgriNova R&D records.
- No knowledge of the covenants: see § 3.4.
- Competitor’s privilege to hire (defeats interference absent wrongful means).
- Intracorporate conspiracy doctrine.
- Unjust enrichment requires a benefit conferred by the plaintiff.
- Possible counter-messaging or a Lanham Act / defamation counterclaim if Verdant tells distributors that BioYield is “stolen.” **Advise the client to route all distributor communications through counsel** and to keep the Heartland business-development team from making accusations.

## **4.7 Potential counterclaims by Tate**

- **N.C. Wage and Hour Act:** if Verdant withholds accrued pay or PTO under § 6.3 or § 7.3, Tate could bring a claim with liquidated damages and fees. Accrued base salary and PTO are not “discretionary compensation.” Confirm what was paid at separation.
- Declaratory judgment that § 5.2 is unenforceable.
- Non-disparagement claim (EA § 5.5 contains a limited Company obligation). Keep executives’ statements disciplined.

## **4.8 Limitations and discovery**

This is not a real risk. The DTSA and NCTSPA periods are 3 years; contract is 3 years (N.C. Gen. Stat. § 1-52(1)); UDTPA is 4 years (§ 75-16.2). The earliest act is Oct. 27, 2024. Note: Showalter’s memo says Verdant had “no knowledge” until Feb. 15. Sentinel was actually engaged Jan. 15 at outside counsel’s direction, after IT saw the wiped laptop on Jan. 10. This does not matter for limitations, but it matters for the TRO **delay** narrative (§ 1, item 4). Witnesses should describe the chronology consistently.
