# I. Executive Summary and Bottom Line

**Recommendation: Do not execute the engagement letter as drafted.** The proposed engagement letter from Hawthorne, Bellamy & Cross LLP ("HB&C") conflicts with the Outside Counsel Guidelines ("OCG") in nearly every substantive area. We identified 40+ deviations, including several that are threshold barriers to engagement, not just negotiable terms. Under OCG §§ 1.1 and 2.1, conflicting terms are void unless the General Counsel waives them in a separate writing. Even so, signing a letter with these terms would invite disputes over which document controls. The letter's own integration clause (§ 19) purports to supersede everything else, and it never mentions the OCG.

Three issues must be resolved before HB&C can be engaged at all:

- **Undisclosed conflict / defective conflicts clearance (OCG § 2.2).** HB&C currently represents MedBridge Therapeutics, which holds about 8% of the equity in NovaTech, the adverse party. That exceeds the OCG's 5% "material financial interest" threshold. The concurrent representation is therefore prohibited unless HB&C makes a detailed written disclosure and the General Counsel gives informed written consent. Ms. Morrow raised the issue informally with Mr. Ridley and called it a "non-issue." The firm's "formal" clearance email from Mr. Alcott (Oct. 4) says the check was run only against NovaTech, its principals and Prescott Lane, and does not mention MedBridge at all. Engagement letter § 11 states there are "no current conflicts." The letter also contains a blanket advance waiver, which the OCG prohibits.

- **Insurance below mandatory minimums (OCG § 2.3).** HB&C carries \$10M per occurrence / \$20M aggregate. The OCG requires \$25M / \$50M and states that Pinnacle "will not engage" a firm below these minimums. The \$10M per-occurrence limit is also less than NovaTech's \$14.2M damages claim.

- **Liability-shifting terms that are unacceptable and ethically questionable.** Section 13 caps HB&C's malpractice liability at the lesser of \$1M or fees paid, which is \$0 at signing. Section 14 requires Pinnacle to indemnify the firm for everything except "solely" willful misconduct, which would cover the firm's own negligence. Section 15 claims a retaining lien over Pinnacle's files, and § 9 imposes a 15% termination fee.

**Financial impact.** If we accepted the terms as written, Pinnacle's realistic exposure would be well above the \$2.4M "budget":

# IV. Detailed Analysis

## A. Threshold Issues

### 1. Conflicts of Interest — MedBridge Therapeutics (Critical)

**Facts.** In her October 3 email to Mr. Ridley, Ms. Morrow disclosed that HB&C currently represents MedBridge Therapeutics in a reimbursement dispute against BlueLine Insurance, and that MedBridge holds about 8% of the equity in NovaTech. She described this as a "passive" investment and a "non-issue," and said Mr. Alcott would send "a confirmation from our conflicts team." Mr. Alcott's October 4 email says the check covered "NovaTech Solutions, LLC, its known principals, and opposing counsel Prescott Lane LLP" and found "no issues." It does not mention MedBridge. Engagement letter § 11 states the firm "has no current conflicts of interest."

**OCG analysis.**

- OCG § 2.2(b) requires disclosure of any current or former representation of an entity with a "material financial interest" in an adverse party, defined as a 5% or greater equity stake. MedBridge's 8% stake qualifies. Whether the stake is passive, or the subject matter unrelated, does not matter under the OCG definition.

- OCG § 2.2 bars HB&C from accepting the engagement while it concurrently represents such an entity, "unless a detailed written disclosure is provided and informed written consent is obtained from the General Counsel." An informal email to the Associate General Counsel is not a detailed written disclosure. No consent from the General Counsel has been requested or given.

- The clearance does not meet § 2.2(a)–(d). It is an informal email, not a clearance letter. It was run against only three names (it did not search NovaTech's equity holders or affiliates). It omits the known MedBridge relationship. It does not address personal relationships with the judge, witnesses or others. It was not delivered to the General Counsel with the insurance certification (§§ 2.1(f)–(g), 2.3).

- Engagement letter § 11's statement that there are "no current conflicts" contradicts what the firm itself disclosed in writing the day before. This is a candor concern as well as a compliance defect.

**Substantive risk.** A lawyer for an 8% owner of NovaTech would be working to defeat a \$14.2M claim that directly affects the value of that owner's investment. This raises a material-limitation question under N.C. RPC 1.7(a)(2), and possible disqualification or relationship risk if MedBridge objects. We also need to know whether any HB&C timekeeper on this team works on the MedBridge matter, whether HB&C has advised MedBridge about its NovaTech investment, and whether MedBridge has information rights or influence at NovaTech. Ms. Morrow's statement that MedBridge has "no involvement" is "from what we understand" and has not been verified.

**Advance waiver.** Section 11 asks Pinnacle to consent in advance to any unrelated adverse representation, specifically including "other healthcare companies, healthcare litigation, and related regulatory matters," with no notice. OCG § 2.2 states: "Blanket or advance waivers of future conflicts are not acceptable." Morrow's email describes this as the firm's "customary provision." It must be deleted.

**Required action:** (i) Require a formal conflicts clearance letter that addresses every item in § 2.2(a)–(d), including a search of NovaTech's equity holders, lenders, affiliates and key revenue relationships. (ii) Require a detailed written disclosure of the MedBridge relationship: team overlap, ethical screens, MedBridge's rights in NovaTech, and whether HB&C has told MedBridge about the proposed engagement or obtained its consent. (iii) The General Counsel should then decide whether to consent in a separate signed writing or decline the engagement. (iv) Delete the advance waiver. (v) Correct § 11.

### 2. Professional Liability Insurance (Critical)

Engagement letter § 12 discloses \$10M per occurrence / \$20M aggregate. That is 40% of each OCG minimum (\$25M / \$50M). OCG § 2.3 makes these "firm requirements … regardless of firm size" and states Pinnacle "will not engage" a firm below them. The firm's promise to maintain "substantially equivalent" coverage would also allow reductions. No certificate of insurance, carrier A.M. Best rating, or exclusion disclosure has been provided. With \$14.2M at stake, the per-occurrence limit would not cover a malpractice loss equal to the full claim.

**Required action:** HB&C must provide proof that it has bound coverage meeting the minimums (for example, through excess coverage), with an A- or better carrier, before the engagement begins. Otherwise the engagement cannot proceed unless the General Counsel signs a separate written waiver, which we do not recommend.

### 3. Limitation of Liability and Indemnification (Critical)

- **§ 13 Liability Cap.** The cap is the lesser of \$1M or fees actually paid. At signing, that is effectively \$0, and it covers negligence and malpractice. N.C. RPC 1.8(h)(1) and D.C. RPC 1.8(g)(1) prohibit agreements that prospectively limit a lawyer's malpractice liability unless the client is independently represented in making the agreement. The acknowledgment in § 20(b) that Pinnacle had the "opportunity to consult with independent counsel" appears designed to address this, but it is not enough. Separately, the claim that fees "would be materially higher" without the cap is hard to credit when every rate already exceeds our caps. Delete in full.

- **§ 14 Indemnification.** Pinnacle would indemnify and defend the firm against all claims "arising out of or relating to the engagement," except those arising "solely" from willful misconduct. That would cover the firm's own negligence and malpractice claims. Together with § 13, it would shift essentially all professional risk to Pinnacle. Delete in full.

### 5. Automatic 5% Annual Rate Escalation (High)

Engagement letter § 3.3, Exhibit A and Staffing Plan § 5 impose a 5% increase every January 1, starting in 2026, "automatically" and "without prior notice to or approval by the Client." OCG § 3.2 fixes rates for the calendar year the engagement starts, requires written proposals by November 1, and gives automatic escalation clauses "no force or effect" even if a Pinnacle representative signs the letter. Nearly all of the work (November 2025–March 2027) would fall in 2026–2027, when rates would be 5% and 10.25% higher. The \$2.4M budget is priced at 2025 rates and does not account for this. Our rough estimate is about \$65,000 or more in added core-team fees. Delete the clause.

### 6. Blended Rate Alternative (High)

The \$685/hour blended rate exceeds the \$625 cap in OCG §§ 3.3 and 9.3. It is also a worse deal than the firm's own standard rates. Using the staffing plan's hours, the effective blended rate at standard rates is \$607/hour (\$1,141,500 ÷ 1,880). At OCG caps it is about \$530/hour (\$996,500 ÷ 1,880). Billing 1,880 hours at \$685 would cost \$1,287,800: \$146,300 more than standard rates and \$291,300 more than capped rates. The \$685 rate would also apply to paralegal time (350 hours at \$685 instead of \$225) and, apparently, to contract reviewers. The proposal also lacks the per-timekeeper hours breakdown required by § 3.3, and it would lock in the election at signing even though § 3.3 lets Pinnacle switch to hourly billing at any time. Reject it. If HB&C wants a blended rate, it should propose one at or below the effective capped rate, excluding paralegals and contract reviewers.

### 7. Success Fee (High)

Engagement letter § 10 puts a success bonus into the letter itself. OCG § 9.2 prohibits this: success fees must be in a separate written agreement approved by the General Counsel before execution. It also says a success-fee provision in an engagement letter does not bind Pinnacle. The proposal also lacks several required elements:

- **No dollar cap (§ 9.2(c)).** The fee is 10% × (\$14.2M − resolution amount), so the maximum is \$1,420,000 if Pinnacle pays nothing.

- **Cliff and perverse incentives (§ 9.2(a), (e)).** Nothing is payable at \$7.0M or more, but \$720,000 is payable at \$6.99M. A \$10,000 difference in outcome swings the fee by \$720,000, and the firm has a strong incentive around that settlement threshold.

- **Measured against NovaTech's demand.** The fee is benchmarked to the plaintiff's \$14.2M claim, not to a realistic case valuation. Any ordinary defense result would earn a large fee. For example, the \$4M illustration pays \$1,020,000.

- **No offsetting hourly discount (§ 9.2(d)).** The fee is "in addition to all hourly fees," which are already above the caps.

- **Termination and scope (§ 9.2(e)).** The letter does not say how the fee is treated on termination, on a change of counsel, or if the case settles after HB&C withdraws. "Resolution amount" includes court-awarded costs, which is ambiguous.

**Required action:** Delete § 10. HB&C may propose a separate AFA that complies with § 9.2, for example: an objective success trigger tied to an agreed case valuation, a hard dollar cap, a hourly discount or holdback during the case, and no cliff.

### 8. Non-Refundable Engagement Retainer (High)

Engagement letter § 4 requires a \$250,000 retainer that is "non-refundable" and "earned upon receipt," deposited into the firm's operating account and forfeited on early termination. OCG § 3.6 states that Pinnacle does not normally pay retainers, and any retainer must be held in trust, refundable, and applied only to final invoices. Non-refundable retainers require extraordinary circumstances and a detailed justification. None is offered. Under N.C. RPC 1.5 and 1.16(d), and the parallel D.C. rules, a fee labeled "non-refundable" must still be reasonable and any unearned part refunded. Combined with the termination fee, this provision would impose a heavy penalty on Pinnacle for changing counsel. It is also unclear how it relates to the budget: Exhibit B lists the retainer as "not included," yet § 4 says it will be credited against final invoices. Delete it, or at most replace it with a refundable trust-account deposit if the General Counsel agrees.

## D. Staffing

- **Sole discretion over staffing (EL § 2; Staffing Plan §§ 3, 6).** This contradicts OCG § 6.2, which states outside counsel "does not have unilateral discretion." Changes at the senior associate level or above require 14 days' written notice and Pinnacle's approval, and Pinnacle's approval right covers all partners. The plan's reservation to bring in associates from Atlanta, New York and D.C. "as workload requires" at standard rates must be subject to this process.

- **Contract attorneys (EL § 2; Ex. A; Staffing Plan § 3).** They need prior written approval with qualifications, scope and duration (§ 6.3). Rates are capped at \$225 for document review and \$425 for substantive work, and may not exceed comparable firm associates. The estimated 750–1,000 hours at \$350 (\$262,500–\$350,000) would be \$168,750–\$225,000 at the cap. Also require a technology-assisted review (TAR) workflow, since CrossPoint is pricing TAR, to reduce contract review hours.

- **Two equity partners.** Mr. Alcott is budgeted for 120 hours (\$117,000) for "strategic oversight, budget reviews, … billing review, and engagement compliance." Billing review and invoice preparation are firm administrative costs and are not billable. OCG § 6.1 also bars paying for more than one partner at routine depositions, hearings or strategy meetings without approval. The staffing plan budgets both partners in every phase, including 25 Alcott hours at trial. We should require a justification, or limit Mr. Alcott's billable time to specific pre-approved tasks.

- **Diversity (§ 6.4).** The letter and staffing plan do not address diverse staffing or the quarterly diversity reports. Add the commitment.

## E. E-Discovery Vendor — CrossPoint Analytics

- **Firm relationship / possible financial interest (§ 1.2).** The letter calls CrossPoint "a strategic partner of the Firm." CrossPoint says it has handled more than 40 HB&C engagements in five years, with "preferred rates available exclusively to" HB&C. OCG § 1.2 prohibits using vendors in which the firm or its attorneys hold a financial interest unless disclosed and approved. HB&C must certify in writing whether it or any attorney has any ownership, revenue-share, referral, rebate or other financial arrangement with CrossPoint.

- **Approval and competitive bidding (§ 1.2).** At \$380,000, the engagement is far above the \$25,000 prior-approval threshold and the \$50,000 threshold at which Pinnacle may require competitive bids. The OCG also lets Pinnacle direct the use of a Pinnacle-preferred vendor. We recommend competitive bids, including any Pinnacle-preferred e-discovery vendor. The claimed 15–20% discount should be tested against the market.

- **Contracting structure.** CrossPoint's contract would be with HB&C only. It would be HB&C that authorizes the start of work, approves contingency spending over \$5,000, and signs the NDA. CrossPoint's confidentiality notice even forbids disclosing the proposal to "third parties," which would include Pinnacle. Pinnacle would have no direct privity, audit rights or approval rights, yet would pay a 1.5%/month late charge passed through by HB&C, under Virginia law. CrossPoint's liability is capped at 12 months of fees, with consequential and data-loss damages excluded, which is a concern given that PHI is involved. Under § 1.2, HB&C must ensure CrossPoint complies with the OCG, including § X. Pinnacle must be a party to, or a named third-party beneficiary of, the vendor terms, and all approvals must come from Pinnacle.

- **HIPAA / PHI (§ 10.2).** This case concerns EHR data-integrity failures that "compromised patient record accuracy." Collections from EHR integration platforms and system logs will almost certainly contain PHI. A Business Associate Agreement (BAA) is required, not optional "if required," both with CrossPoint and (as a downstream arrangement) with HB&C. The proposal does not include the 24-hour breach notice to the General Counsel, encryption of communications, need-to-know access limits, or Pinnacle's right to conduct security assessments.

- **Budget and pricing.** E-discovery is excluded from the litigation budget, contrary to § 4.1(c), which specifically requires e-discovery processing and hosting to be budgeted. The estimate checks arithmetically (\$380,000 total; for example, 1,500 GB × \$35 = \$52,500 and 12 × \$8,500 = \$102,000), but: (i) CrossPoint charges both an \$8,500/month project management flat fee and a \$275/hour Senior Project Manager rate, which risks double billing; (ii) rates are "subject to annual review and adjustment" on notice to HB&C only; (iii) hosting assumes 12 months, while the schedule runs through trial in March 2027 (about 17 months), so hosting will likely be understated; (iv) collections of 45 custodians and three productions are assumptions only. - **Pre-engagement disclosure.** CrossPoint refers to "preliminary discussions" with HB&C about Pinnacle's custodians and systems before any engagement or vendor approval. Confirm what Pinnacle information has been shared with CrossPoint.

## F. Budget and Cost Management

- **Structure (§ 4.1).** The budget has four phases instead of the OCG phases. It omits separate phases for case assessment, ADR/mediation, e-discovery, and appeals or post-judgment work. For a \$14.2M commercial case, the absence of any mediation or early settlement phase is notable.

- **Exclusions and "not a cap" (§§ 4.1, 4.3).** The OCG requires all cost categories to be included and makes the firm bear unapproved overruns. The letter excludes e-discovery, the success fee and undefined "extraordinary" expenses, and states the budget "is not a cap." Exhibit B also adds "interlocutory appeals" to the exclusions, which § 8 of the letter does not list. The 10% variance notice and approval requirement (§ 4.2) is missing.

- **Presentation (§ 4.1).** Total spend is about \$2.78M including e-discovery, which is above the \$500,000 threshold. HB&C must present and defend the budget to the General Counsel in person or by video.

- **Unexplained amounts.** See Section VI: roughly \$685,000–\$772,000 of the \$2.4M is not supported by the staffing detail.

## G. Termination and Withdrawal

- **Termination fee (EL § 9).** A 15% fee on all fees billed, payable within 30 days, is void under OCG § 8.1. The OCG allows no termination fee, penalty, wind-down surcharge or minimum fee, and Pinnacle's only obligation is to pay for services properly rendered. Calling it "not intended as a penalty" does not change that. Delete.

- **Mutual 30-day termination.** Pinnacle may terminate at any time (§ 8.1). The firm must give 60 days' notice and obtain Pinnacle's consent (§ 8.2), with a transition plan and full staffing during the notice period. Revise.

- **File transfer on firm termination is "subject to Section 15"**, meaning the lien. This conflicts with OCG §§ 7.2 and 8.1.

## H. Work Product, Confidentiality and Marketing

- **Ownership and lien (EL § 15).** OCG § 7.1 provides that Pinnacle owns all work product absolutely, "not contingent upon payment." OCG § 7.2 prohibits any lien, and asserting one is a material breach. Replace § 15 with the OCG language and an express lien waiver.

- **Marketing (EL § 16).** The standing consent to name Pinnacle in marketing materials, the website, client lists and pitches, continuing after the engagement "unless revoked," violates OCG § 10.1. That section requires the General Counsel's prior written consent for each use and bars even disclosing the existence of the engagement. The confidentiality clause is also "mutual" and does not include § 10.2 data-security obligations. Delete the marketing consent. Replace the confidentiality clause with OCG § X.

## I. Dispute Resolution and Governing Law

Engagement letter § 17 requires binding AAA arbitration in Washington, D.C., including malpractice claims, with a jury waiver. Section 18 selects D.C. law and D.C. courts. OCG § XIII provides for negotiation, then optional mediation in Charlotte, then litigation in the state or federal courts in Mecklenburg County, with North Carolina law (§§ 13.2, 14.1). The engagement is Charlotte-centered: Pinnacle, the court, and most of the HB&C team are all in Charlotte. Mandatory arbitration of malpractice claims also raises informed-consent issues (see ABA Formal Op. 02-425). Replace with the OCG provisions.
