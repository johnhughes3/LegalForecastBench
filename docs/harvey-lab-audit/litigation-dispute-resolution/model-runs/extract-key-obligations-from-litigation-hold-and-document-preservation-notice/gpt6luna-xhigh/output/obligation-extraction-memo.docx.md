# 1. Executive summary

The notice is effective immediately and requires broad, continuing preservation for Ridgeline and covered affiliates, including RPAF to the extent it possesses, controls, or has custody of responsive information. It overrides RDG-LGL-007 and every routine destruction, deletion, backup-recycling, and purge practice for information within scope. Preservation is not limited to the 23 named custodians or the enumerated categories: the notice expressly reaches all HCP communications, potentially relevant information in any location, related compounds, and later-created responsive information.

- Deadline sequence in the notice/cover letter: (1) written notices to relevant third-party vendors by March 13, 2025; (2) written certification to AUSA Faulkner and Trial Attorney Gutierrez by March 17, 2025; and (3) forensic imaging of all 23 named custodians’ qualifying mobile devices by March 24, 2025. The notice states these dates by reference to receipt; the cover letter expressly identifies them as the deadlines. If actual receipt differs, counsel should document it and seek written clarification rather than assume an extension.

- Highest immediate preservation risk: the IT email reports approximately 340,000 pre-September 2021 emails for about 2,100 employees were not migrated to Microsoft 365. Approximately 47,000–52,000 may relate to eight custodians, six of whom match the notice roster; the other two identities do not match Exhibit C. The only known surviving source may be six powered-down Exchange servers at Sentinel Records Management. Integrity has not been checked; legacy backup-tape status is unknown.

- Separate significant issue: an internal email reports that approximately 4.2 million records, including KOL event files, Velcara field records, RPAF correspondence, emails, and 47 boxes of paper, were destroyed on January 15, 2025. This predates the notice, but the described KOL and RPAF categories appear to have six-year retention periods under the supplied policy, not the shorter periods cited in the destruction email. Establish what was destroyed, recoverability, notice/knowledge timeline, and whether any responsive copies remain. Do not make a conclusion about spoliation from the present record.

- Roster reconciliation is essential: the notice’s total of 23 comprises 9 named employees, 7 Exhibit C sales managers, 2 unnamed role-based employees, and 5 external HCPs. The 2 role-based employees are not named. Two migration-affected names (Jennifer Calloway and David Yun) do not appear in Exhibit C; the precise employee/custodian identities must be verified against HR and IT records.

- Preserve originals and metadata; native-format preservation is required. A PDF, printout, screenshot, or export to CSV alone does not satisfy the ESI requirement. Collect and secure legacy sources before remediation or system access could alter evidence.

# 5. Internal records reveal immediate preservation issues

## 5.1 Exchange-to-Microsoft 365 migration gap

Dennis Wardlow’s March 5 email reports a September 2021 migration from on-premises Exchange Server 2016 to M365, conducted in waves from August 23 to October 1, 2021. Ashford Data Solutions’ tool reportedly skipped archive mailboxes and certain PST stores for about 2,100 employees. The missing data is estimated at approximately 340,000 emails from January 2019 through September 2021 and exists only on six decommissioned Dell PowerEdge servers at Sentinel Records Management (Raleigh; Account SM-2247891). The servers have reportedly remained powered off in climate-controlled storage since December 2021, but have not been integrity-checked; hardware degradation is possible. The affected emails are commingled with other employees’ data.

- IT estimates 47,000–52,000 missing emails for eight migration-affected individuals it says match the 23-custodian list: Franklin Osei, Greg Hsu, Amanda Terrell, Richard Blaine, Katerina Novak, Luis Delgado, Jennifer Calloway, and David Yun. The first six match ¶ 13. Calloway and Yun do not appear in Exhibit C; verify whether they are alternate names, former employees, notice omissions, or a mismatch in the IT crosswalk. Do not silently substitute them for listed custodians.

- M365 holds can reportedly be applied to all 23 named custodians within 24–48 hours after legal authorization and capture current/deleted M365 data from October 2021 forward. This does not close the pre-migration gap. Place M365/Teams/SharePoint/OneDrive holds immediately; separately preserve, assess, image and recover legacy servers and identify other affected custodians/systems.

- IT is uncertain whether pre-December 2021 legacy backup tapes survived or were recycled. Investigate promptly with Sentinel, IT inventories, invoices, destruction logs and backup catalogs; preserve any surviving tapes. Treat server disks as potentially the only surviving source unless proved otherwise.

- Preserve Ashford contract, mapping/validation records, incident report, remediation plan/estimate, internal escalation and budget decisions, affected-employee crosswalk, Sentinel storage/inventory records, and all migration / system documentation. These are expressly within Notice ¶¶ 30, 34, 42 and 43 and may illuminate source completeness.

## 5.2 January 15, 2025 destruction

Patricia Albright’s January 16 email reports a January 15 destruction cycle, after a January 13 Hold Register check found no active hold, and gives approximate totals of 4.2 million records dated 2019–2021 (with the sales tranche through 2022). It reports permanent deletion from primary systems, destruction of on-site paper, and destruction by Sentinel of 47 boxes of 2019–2020 paper (Certificate SM-CERT-2025-00412). The represented categories are: 2.1 million routine emails/attachments; 1.4 million CRM/field activity records; 380,000 KOL event files; and 320,000 RPAF routine correspondence records. The email says compensation records, HCP due diligence, patient enrollments, grant disbursements, and eligibility files were not destroyed.

- Potential policy mismatch: the supplied RDG-LGL-007 schedule sets speaker program records (Category L) and Ridgeline-system patient assistance records (Category M) at six years; compliance records (K) at six years; general correspondence (E) at three years; sales field records (F) at two years. The destruction email assigns KOL event files a three-year period and RPAF routine correspondence a three-year period and cites policy sections/categories (e.g., §§ 4.8 and 5.3) that do not correspond to the supplied policy text. Reconcile against the controlling policy version and schedules, classification, destruction batch and authorizations; do not assume the reported categories were eligible under the provided policy.

- Potential responsiveness: KOL event planning, attendance, venue, and post-event material and RPAF operational correspondence are squarely within the notice examples/categories. Velcara commercial field activity and relevant emails may also be responsive. The records were destroyed before the March 3 notice, but investigate any earlier actual or reasonably anticipated preservation trigger, what versions/copies remain, and whether deleted items, backups, archives, vendor copies, users’ devices, exports or paper duplicates are recoverable.

- The destruction email says the destruction log spreadsheet was attached, but no spreadsheet was among the documents provided for review. Obtain the original attachment, complete batch-level destruction log, certificates, vendor work orders, categories/date ranges, list of system paths, deleted-object/recovery status, and pre-destruction approval and notice records. Preserve all documentation about the event and report it promptly to DOJ if counsel concludes it is a circumstance covered by ¶ 46.

- No finding is made here about intent, knowledge, legal duty at the time, or whether every item in the described destruction was relevant. Preserve what remains, stop any further destruction, and document recovery efforts and decisions contemporaneously.

## 5.3 RPAF governance and control

The notice expressly includes RPAF to the extent it possesses, controls, or has custody of responsive information (¶ 4). The March 5 organizational chart describes RPAF as a separate 501(c)(3) with an independent board, separate systems/records and retention practices; three of five directors are Ridgeline officers or designees, two are independent; Richard Blaine is a Ridgeline liaison but has no authority over RPAF records or operations. The internal chart says board approval may be needed for a direct RPAF preservation directive. Thus, preserve all Ridgeline copies and Blaine’s/other Ridgeline employees’ records immediately and, through counsel, promptly assess control and arrange a separate written hold directed to RPAF’s board/executive director if appropriate. Preserve records needed to evidence funding, board interactions, and Ridgeline–RPAF communications; do not treat legal separation as a reason to omit RPAF from the investigation response.

## 5.4 Privileged and former-counsel records

The notice requires preservation regardless of privilege and does not itself require production of privileged material. If items are later withheld under compulsory process, the notice calls for a sufficiently detailed privilege log. The organizational chart identifies Hargrove, Tillett & Mays LLP as current defense counsel, Greenwell Analytics Group as counsel-retained e-discovery/forensic vendor, and Linden & Pruitt, P.A. as former regulatory counsel (approximately 2020–mid-2024) now conflicted out, with Dr. Osei as primary business contact. Preserve all potentially privileged files and metadata; segregate access and use an independent counsel-led privilege protocol. The chart’s “prepared at direction of counsel” label is a handling signal, not by itself a privilege determination. Do not ask conflicted former counsel to conduct the privilege review.
