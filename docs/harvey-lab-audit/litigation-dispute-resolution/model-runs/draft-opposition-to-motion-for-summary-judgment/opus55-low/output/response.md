## How the defense's arguments are answered

- **The Bank says it didn't know about the whistleblower report (BSA).** Hargrove's August 1 email names Vasquez as the "obvious candidate" behind the regulator's inquiry and says the source must be "identif[ied]" and "contain[ed]." Caldwell replied "Let me handle this" and admits he knew what Hargrove believed. Supporting authority: *Heffernan* (the employer's belief is what matters), *Staub* (a biased official's influence on the decision is attributed to the employer), and Rule 801(d)(2)(D) (the emails are the Bank's own admissions).
- **The Bank says the performance reasons were real, not pretext.** Each PIP ground is contradicted by the Bank's own records:
  - The signed business plan sets the Q2 target at $10M, not $15M, and she produced $11.3M.
  - Internal Audit rated the three cited loan files "Satisfactory."
  - Thornburg denies ever complaining, and the other complaint came from the principal of Orion Gateway, the entity she reported.
  - The Handbook's full text reads "in no event less than 60 days"; the defense quoted only the "generally" part.
  - Whitfield and Lomax were rated worse and got no PIP.
  - Medina was fired 22 days later.
  - Caldwell took back his own deposition testimony.
- **The Bank says it isn't covered by SOX.** The defense's quotation of § 1514A(a) uses an ellipsis to drop the Dodd-Frank language covering "any subsidiary or affiliate whose financial information is included in the consolidated financial statements" of a public company. *Lawson* supports the broader reading.
- **The Bank says the state claim is preempted.** SOX expressly preserves state-law remedies (§ 1514A(d)). *English v. GE* and *Silkwood* reject similar preemption arguments. The Bank also can't argue that federal remedies are both adequate and unavailable to her.
