## 2.1 Slack: hold omitted the primary internal platform

The September 16, 2024 hold covered Exchange email, shared drives, and local drives, but did not identify Slack or Teams. No Slack retention suspension occurred at that time. On November 1, after custodian interviews, a supplemental hold covered Slack and Teams. Kessler, Morell, and Reeves reportedly used Slack as a primary internal channel for sales, account, territory, and Northpoint discussions; relevant matters may not have been duplicated in email.

Slack applies a rolling 90-day auto-delete policy. By the November 1 collection, messages dated before approximately August 3, 2024 were no longer available through ordinary export or support, and no independent backup or prior export was identified. The lost interval overlaps the entire Agreement term (January 15, 2021–January 14, 2024), the Northpoint relationship, the Q3 2022/Q1 2023 testing, the August 2023 advisory, and the pre-suit period. Pinnacle reports that standard recovery avenues were exhausted, but recommends local-cache/device forensics; that limited forensic step has not been done.

Duty-timing concern: on September 16, messages older than approximately June 18, 2024 were already outside a 90-day window, but messages created from approximately June 18 through August 2 would have been deleted on rolling dates approximately September 16–October 31, after the September 16 hold and before the November 1 supplement. Some of that deletion window may postdate the Complaint’s filing (the source documents conflict on whether filing was October 1 or October 7). Exact message-level deletion dates and content are unavailable. Do not state categorically that all lost Slack data predates the preservation duty. The pre-suit demand and outside-counsel retention on September 15 strongly warrant treating the preservation duty as active by September 16. Have IT/Pinnacle document the retention configuration, when it was changed (if it was), available audit logs, affected channels/custodians, and forensic recovery efforts. Preserve all surviving Slack data immediately; consider an independent forensic review of the three heavy users’ laptops/mobile devices and browser caches.

## 4.2 Year 3 minimum volume and breach/waiver defense

The Answer alleges a \$4.17 million Year 3 shortfall and calls it a material breach excusing Redfield’s performance. The Agreement’s Section 3.3 is materially more specific: a shortfall alone is not a material breach unless Redfield timely elects in writing to waive, reduce territory, or terminate for cause; failure to exercise a remedy within 60 days after the Contract Year ends is deemed a waiver of that year’s shortfall. Contract Year 3 ended January 14, 2024; identify whether Redfield made a written election by approximately March 14, 2024 and whether any 90-day Section 9.1 notice/cure process was followed. If no timely election exists, the stand-alone Year 3 defense is substantially weakened.

Also validate the \$7.83 million calculation against Section 3.2, which measures aggregate List Price of Orders accepted during the Contract Year, net of returns/credits—not discounted invoice revenue or shipments alone. Obtain quarterly reports and reconciliations; test acceptance date, credits, returns, cancellations, backorders, and the accounting treatment. The Answer’s second affirmative defense references marketing, territory coverage, and promotional expenditure duties under Sections 4 and 6; those sections in the Agreement govern orders and product quality/warranty, while the express promotion commitment appears in Section 2.1 and no promotional-spend covenant is apparent in the supplied agreement. Verify before repeating the contention.

## 4.3 V-Series quality and advisory

The Answer admits the Q3 2022 test’s 3.2% failure rate was within Redfield’s 5% tolerance and the Q1 2023 result was 7.8%, above the contractual threshold. Section 6.2 requires manufacturing to published Quality Standards, maintenance of complete QA records, and five-year retention. Section 6.4 allocates advisory/recall implementation costs to Redfield. The defense that no automatic disclosure duty existed does not remove the relevance of the elevated result, the four-month interval to the August 15 advisory, or communications about the proper response. Obtain raw Elkhorn data, methods, sample/lot identification, repeat testing, engineering change orders, CAPA/root-cause records, decision chronology, advisory scope/distribution records, warranty/return counts, and records at Decatur. Determine what V-Series specs/testing data Kessler shared or offered to Foss and whether any quality materials were disclosed to Northpoint.

## 4.4 Damages, customers, and mitigation

The current defenses assert failure to mitigate and challenge the \$6.2 million demand, including \$281,600 attributed to \$1.28 million in Northpoint V-Series sales, \$3.9 million in customer attrition, and \$2.02 million in warranty-related claims. Preserve and produce the account-level sales, margin, customer, credit, return, and warranty records relevant to Lakeshore. Determine whether the alleged \$1.28 million Northpoint V-Series figure and geography are supported by Redfield’s own records. Avoid equating Lakeshore’s purchase-volume shortfall with causation or damages without analyzing the contract’s remedy and the market/customer evidence.
