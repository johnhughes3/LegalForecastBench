   - Aldersgate inspected Tri-Basin on April 12, 2023. It found the indoor space climate-controlled and ruled out storage as a cause.
   - Trevino's July 18, 2023 email says Pinnacle "can no longer credibly assert" storage caused the failures.
   - Nothing in the file supports the Answer's statement about "inadequate climate control and improper stacking."
   - The responses give only facts the record supports (outdoor overflow storage under an open-sided roof, which Exhibit E does not allow) and they disclose the Aldersgate conclusion.
   - Given Rule 11 and Rule 26(g), we should consider narrowing or withdrawing this defense.

3. **Work product claim over the Aldersgate report (Interrogatory 6(f), marked in brackets).** The claim looks weak:
   - The retention was business-driven. Trevino's March 3, 2023 email cites product liability and warranty exposure.
   - The report was addressed straight to Trevino, and it was used to run the recall.
   - The Answer already admits the key findings.

   The draft discloses the underlying facts and claims privilege only over communications with counsel. Please pick a strategy.

4. **Too many interrogatories.** Counting discrete subparts, the 25-interrogatory limit is reached at No. 17(a). The draft objects to everything after that and answers anyway, preserving the objection. The guidelines treat this as the partner's call; the alternatives are to refuse to answer or seek a protective order.

5. **Waiver of legal advice already produced.** The September 22, 2022 Hauck email (PINNACLE_004223–26) passes along Trevino's advice ("thin because we never flagged it"). It is already produced, so we should decide whether to claw it back under FRE 502 or treat it as waived. The responses cite it only by Bates number.

6. **Section 11.2 cap (Interrogatories 17(d) and 24).**
