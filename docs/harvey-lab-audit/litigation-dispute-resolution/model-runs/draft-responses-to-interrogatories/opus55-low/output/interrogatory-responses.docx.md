**<u>GENERAL OBJECTIONS</u>**

Each of the following General Objections is incorporated by reference into the Response to each Interrogatory to which it applies, as stated in that Response. Specific objections are stated in each individual Response.

**General Objection No. 1 — Scope and Relevance.** Pinnacle objects to the Interrogatories to the extent they seek information that is not relevant to any party's claim or defense or proportional to the needs of the case under Fed. R. Civ. P. 26(b)(1). In particular, Pinnacle objects to Instruction No. 8, which applies a default time period of January 1, 2015 to the present, to the extent it extends Interrogatories concerning the 2022–2023 events at issue (the Series 7200 complaints, the Meridian relationship, and the termination) across more than nine years without a corresponding need. Specific relevance objections are stated in the individual Responses.

**General Objection No. 2 — Overbreadth and Undue Burden.** Pinnacle objects to the Interrogatories to the extent they request "all" communications, documents, or facts without reasonable limitation, or request narrative compilation of voluminous transactional data (e.g., shipment-level sales data, complaint logs, and routine order communications) maintained in Pinnacle's business records. Where appropriate, Pinnacle responds pursuant to Fed. R. Civ. P. 33(d) with specifically identified records.

**General Objection No. 3 — Attorney-Client Privilege and Work Product.** Pinnacle objects to the Interrogatories to the extent they seek information protected by the attorney-client privilege, the work product doctrine under Fed. R. Civ. P. 26(b)(3), or any other applicable privilege, including communications with Pinnacle's General Counsel, Sandra M. Trevino, and with outside counsel. Pinnacle will provide a privilege log identifying documents and communications withheld on these grounds in accordance with Fed. R. Civ. P. 26(b)(5)(A) and any applicable scheduling order. Any inadvertent disclosure of privileged or protected information shall not constitute a waiver, in accordance with Fed. R. Evid. 502(b).

**General Objection No. 4 — Vagueness and Ambiguity.** Pinnacle objects to the Interrogatories to the extent they use undefined or argumentative terms, or embed factual characterizations that Pinnacle disputes (for example, that Tri-Basin was "excluded" from the recall, or that Meridian was "engaged" as a distributor during the term of the MDA). Pinnacle does not adopt any such characterization by responding.

**General Objection No. 5 — Ongoing Investigation.** Pinnacle's Responses are based on information reasonably available as of the date hereof. Pinnacle's investigation, including its review of documents and electronically stored information, is ongoing. Pinnacle expressly reserves the right to amend or supplement these Responses in accordance with Fed. R. Civ. P. 26(e) as additional information is identified through continuing investigation and the discovery process. Nothing in these Responses should be construed as a representation that Pinnacle's investigation is complete or that all responsive information has been identified.

**General Objection No. 6 — Definitions and Instructions.** Pinnacle objects to Plaintiff's Definitions and Instructions to the extent they purport to impose obligations beyond those imposed by the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure, the Local Rules of this Court, or the Case Management Order. Without limitation: (a) Pinnacle objects to Definition Nos. 8 and 14 ("Pinnacle," "You") to the extent they include Pinnacle's attorneys and require Pinnacle to answer on behalf of independent third parties; (b) Pinnacle objects to Definition No. 5 to the extent it seeks residential addresses and telephone numbers of Pinnacle employees, who may be contacted only through counsel; (c) Pinnacle objects to Definition No. 9 insofar as it describes the Territory by reference to "Exhibit A" to the MDA, and construes "Territory" in accordance with Section 4.1 and Exhibit C of the MDA; (d) Pinnacle objects to Definition No. 12 to the extent it refers to "Series 7200-A, 7200-B, and 7200-C models," designations that Pinnacle does not use, and construes the term to mean Pinnacle's Series 7200 butterfly valve product line; (e) Pinnacle objects to Definition No. 13 to the extent it defines the "Aldersgate Report" to include drafts and supporting materials and to the extent the materials so defined include privileged communications; and (f) Pinnacle objects to Instruction No. 4 to the extent it requires a privilege log on a schedule other than that provided by Fed. **Response:**

Subject to and without waiving the foregoing General Objections and the specific objections stated herein, Defendant responds as follows:

Based on information currently available, and without limitation, the facts on which Pinnacle relies include the following:

\(a\) Alleged breaches. The Termination Letter dated June 2, 2023 identified two grounds for termination under Section 9.1: (i) Tri-Basin's failure to satisfy the Minimum Purchase Obligation under Section 6.1 of the MDA for the 2020 contract year, and (ii) persistent quality complaints that Pinnacle attributed at that time to Tri-Basin's storage and handling of Pinnacle products. As to the first ground, Pinnacle's business records reflect that Tri-Basin's purchases for calendar year 2020 were approximately \$9,700,000, against the \$11,000,000 Minimum Purchase Obligation applicable to Contract Years 4 through 7, a shortfall of approximately \$1,300,000. Section 9.1(d)(i) of the MDA identifies a failure to achieve the Minimum Purchase Obligation for any Contract Year as a material breach, and Section 6.2(c) provides for termination for cause in that circumstance. As to the second ground, see Pinnacle's Responses to Interrogatory Nos. 5, 18, and 23.

\(b\) Date of awareness. Pinnacle's sales records reflecting Tri-Basin's 2020 purchase volume were maintained contemporaneously in Pinnacle's SAP ERP system, and Pinnacle was aware of Tri-Basin's reduced 2020 purchase volume at or shortly after the close of 2020. Pinnacle first received Series 7200 complaints forwarded by Tri-Basin in or about January 2022. See Response to Interrogatory No. 5.

\(c\) Written notice. Pinnacle did not send Tri-Basin a written notice regarding the 2020 shortfall before the Termination Letter. The Termination Letter, authored and signed by Gerald R. Hauck and addressed to Dale W. Keeler, sent June 2, 2023 by certified mail, return receipt requested, and by electronic mail, with copies to Sandra M. Trevino and Thomas J. Crandall, states that it "constitutes formal written notice of the material breaches described" therein pursuant to Section 9.1. See also Pinnacle's Response to Interrogatory No. 19.

\(d\) Cure period. The Termination Letter provided Tri-Basin ninety (90) days, through August 31, 2023, to cure the breaches identified, and stated that the MDA would terminate effective August 31, 2023 absent cure. Tri-Basin responded by letter dated June 16, 2023 disputing the grounds stated in the Termination Letter. To Pinnacle's knowledge, Tri-Basin did not purchase additional product to make up the 2020 shortfall or otherwise propose a cure of the stated breaches during the ninety-day period. The MDA terminated effective August 31, 2023. Pinnacle's position regarding the continuing nature of the 2020 shortfall is set forth in Paragraph 21 of its Answer.

Pinnacle's investigation, including its review of approximately 48,000 potentially responsive documents, is ongoing. Pinnacle reserves the right to supplement or amend this response in accordance with Fed. R. Civ. P. 26(e).
