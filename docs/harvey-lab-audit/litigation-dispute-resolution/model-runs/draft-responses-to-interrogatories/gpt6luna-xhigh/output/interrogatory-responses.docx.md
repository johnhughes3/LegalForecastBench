# GENERAL OBJECTIONS

The following objections are asserted only to the extent applicable to a particular interrogatory. A specific response below controls over any general objection to the extent of an inconsistency.

**1. Numerical limit; discrete subparts.** Pinnacle objects under Fed. R. Civ. P. 33(a)(1) and paragraph 4(a) of the Case Management Order to the extent the set, when discrete subparts seeking independent categories of information are counted, exceeds 25 interrogatories. This objection is preserved; Pinnacle nevertheless provides substantive responses to all numbered interrogatories voluntarily, subject to and without waiving the objection. This voluntary response is not a concession that the set complies with the numerical limit.

**2. Privilege and work product.** Pinnacle objects to disclosure of attorney-client communications, attorney mental impressions, and material protected from discovery under Fed. R. Civ. P. 26(b)(3). The objection does not withhold underlying nonprivileged facts. If responsive material is withheld, Pinnacle will identify it in a privilege log compliant with Rule 26(b)(5)(A), to be served after completion of the privilege review. No privileged advice is disclosed by these responses.

**3. Definitions and instructions.** Pinnacle objects to definitions or instructions to the extent they purport to impose requirements beyond the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure or applicable Court orders, including an obligation to provide personal/residential contact details not reasonably available or to make each response exhaustive before discovery is complete. Pinnacle answers based on information reasonably available after reasonable inquiry and provides known business contact information where available.

**4. Continuing investigation and supplementation.** The document and factual investigations are ongoing. Responses reflect information reasonably available as of April 25, 2024. Pinnacle expressly reserves its obligation and right to supplement or correct responses in accordance with Rule 26(e). Nothing represents that the investigation is complete or that every responsive person, document, communication, transaction, or fact has been identified.

**5. No blanket relevance or burden objection.** Pinnacle does not assert a generic relevance or burden objection to the set. Where a specific objection is stated, it identifies the challenged portion and the basis for that objection.

## INTERROGATORY NO. 7

*With respect to the Series 7200 butterfly valves: (a) state the total number of Series 7200 valve units shipped to Tri-Basin and/or to Tri-Basin’s customers between November 2021 and March 2023; (b) state the total dollar value of such shipments; (c) identify the foundry or manufacturing source for the valve disc castings used in Series 7200 valves during this period, including the name, location, and country of each such source; (d) describe all metallurgical or quality testing performed on Series 7200 valve disc castings prior to shipment, including the applicable ASTM standards or other industry specifications used as benchmarks; and (e) state whether any Series 7200 valve units were found to fail to conform to ASTM A351 Grade CF8M specifications, and if so, describe the nature and extent of the non-conformity, the number of affected units, and the date(s) on which the non-conformity was discovered.*

**Response.** Subject to and without waiving the foregoing General Objections and any specific objection stated above, Pinnacle responds as follows: The June 14, 2023 Aldersgate report identifies approximately 3,200 potentially affected Series 7200 units shipped through Pinnacle’s distribution channels between November 2021 and March 2023, at an average value of \$1,300 per unit and total shipped value of approximately \$4,160,000. The report says distribution was primarily through Tri-Basin and that some affected units were shipped to Meridian’s Odessa address; a channel-by-channel unit and value allocation is not stated in the materials. The disc castings were supplied by Gansu Precision Metals Co., Lanzhou, Gansu Province, China. The report reviewed 14 heat lots. Pinnacle’s incoming inspection consisted of visual inspection and dimensional verification; no Pinnacle incoming radiography, ultrasonic testing, or porosity measurement was performed. Gansu’s records reported void fractions of 0.8%–1.3% and referenced Chinese standard GB/T 5677 for radiography, rather than ASTM E446 in Pinnacle’s purchase order requirements. The report’s post-failure evaluation used metallurgical testing including ASTM A351 Grade CF8M comparison, ASTM E446 radiography, ASTM E2109 porosity measurement, and ASTM A370 mechanical testing; these were investigation tests, not evidence of routine pre-shipment testing by Pinnacle. The report states that finished valves were machined, assembled, hydrostatically and functionally tested at Pinnacle’s Verona facility, but the materials do not specify the test protocol/acceptance criteria for those finished-unit tests. Aldersgate tested 18 returned discs and measured average void fraction 4.7% (range 3.9%–5.6%), compared with a 1.5% maximum identified in the report; tensile strength, yield strength and elongation were also reported below specification. The report concluded the 14 lots/approximately 3,200 units were potentially affected by excessive porosity, but does not establish that every finished unit had failed in service. Pinnacle received the report on June 14, 2023. No earlier date of discovery of nonconformity is established. The specific report, including the June 14 Executive Summary and underlying test data, is the source for these reported findings; further channel-specific shipment data is being reviewed.
