# Executive assessment

Dalton has substantial evidence of a serious product failure and strong potential claims against HydraCore Components, LLC (a defective accumulator bladder) and Ironclad Systems Corp. (a press design/integration deficiency and a potentially consequential failure to respond to pre-incident complaints). The strongest present evidence is the preliminary laboratory testing showing the recovered bladder materially outside HydraCore’s stated specifications, normal operating data immediately before failure, and Ironclad’s February 2025 emails discussing multiple HX-9000 accumulator complaints and delaying a field bulletin.

The case is not a simple recovery of the full numbers in the damages workbook. Ironclad has a signed purchase-order/standard-terms package that purports to require Michigan-law AAA arbitration, cap liability at the \$1,287,500 price of the press, exclude consequential damages, and impose a 12-month claim deadline. Those provisions present material recovery and forum risks, although enforceability—particularly as to personal injury, gross negligence/willful misconduct, non-signatory injured workers, and HydraCore—should be contested rather than assumed. Dalton also faces meaningful OSHA and comparative-fault/indemnity exposure over guarding and accumulator energy isolation.

Immediate priorities are (1) determine and meet the Ohio OSHA contest deadline measured from actual receipt, (2) verify and preserve all insurance and evidence, (3) serve the contract dispute notice and start the required executive negotiation with Ironclad, and (4) prepare to commence arbitration well before March 14, 2026. The employee and wrongful-death claims are not Dalton’s corporate claims; the engagement letter limits counsel’s client to Dalton, and separate representation/conflicts must be addressed.

# 1. Record and incident snapshot

The press (Ironclad HX-9000, S/N HX9-2024-03417) was installed by Ironclad in December 2024 and accepted December 16, 2024. On March 14, 2025, during a routine cycle and at normal logged operating conditions, the HydraCore HC-ABL-440 accumulator bladder ruptured; a pressure surge ejected the approximately 1,200-pound piston rod. Marco Reyes was killed; Kevin Trask and Priya Anand suffered serious injuries. The press, an adjacent Haas mill, facility infrastructure, dies/tooling, and Line 3 production were affected.

- Meridian’s April 21 preliminary report attributes the rupture to a bladder compound with mean tensile strength 1,450 psi versus a 2,800-psi minimum and mean plasticizer content 18.7% versus a 12.0% maximum. The report identifies the absence of dedicated redundant relief on the accumulator circuit as an amplifying design defect. No operator error or incorrect/contaminated hydraulic fluid was identified.

- The report relies on retained physical evidence and tests, but Ironclad’s engineering and validation files, HydraCore batch/quality records, and broader complaint history remain outstanding. Destructive tests and all further inspection should be jointly noticed and documented.

- Potential inconsistencies need prompt reconciliation: the OSHA citation says the accumulator isolation valve had no lockout hasp; Dr. Cordero reports observing a manual ball valve with a lockout hasp. The citation also describes LOTO deficiencies even though the press was in production and no LOTO was in progress. Reyes’s distance from the cylinder is reported differently (about four feet in Dalton’s incident report; about eight feet in the citation).

The supplied copy of the March 22, 2025 engagement letter has blank execution lines. Confirm that the corporate engagement was fully executed and that the client remains Dalton Precision Manufacturing, Inc. only; the letter expressly excludes individual officers, employees, and agents.

## Dalton’s own exposure; defenses likely asserted

The forensic report distinguishes cause of rupture from severity: it says Dalton’s guarding and energy-isolation conditions did not cause the bladder to fail, but could bear on injury consequences and safe maintenance. The Ohio citation alleges serious LOTO and machine-guarding violations. It expressly states it is not a finding of fault or negligence for the incident, but the underlying findings, evidence, and any final order may still be damaging in civil litigation and should be managed carefully. In third-party litigation, the defendants are likely to argue comparative fault, negligent acceptance/installation-site review, inadequate guarding, deficient LOTO procedures, failure to detect or report anomalies, and contractual indemnity. Dalton’s September 2024 audit, the absence of an accumulator-specific procedure for the new press, and acceptance in the OEM configuration create litigation risk; completed tag replacement/retraining and the below-industry TRIR are favorable but do not answer the HX-9000-specific allegations.

- LOTO causation is particularly contestable on the present facts: the incident occurred during normal production, not servicing or setup under LOTO. The citation nevertheless alleges program/device inadequacy. The physical hasp discrepancy and precise cited standard/subsection should be tested against scene photographs, the machine, written procedures, and interview records.

- Guarding is a real severity issue. Reyes was at the operating station, while the absence of a blast/retention barrier is alleged to have left employees in the piston-rod trajectory. Determine whether a feasible barrier would have contained or deflected a 1,200-pound rod without creating other hazards. Do not overstate that this condition caused the bladder rupture.

- Ironclad’s standard terms contain a broad Buyer indemnity for claims by Buyer’s employees and claims arising from use/operation, subject to an exception only for claims “directly and solely” caused by a covered defect and not contributed to by Buyer. Ironclad may invoke this in light of alleged concurrent Dalton conduct. Challenge its scope/enforceability, especially for Ironclad’s own defect/negligence and non-signatory employee claims; do not concede indemnity or make coverage admissions. Section 11.2 provides only narrow seller indemnity (installation gross negligence/willful misconduct or IP claims).

The employees’ bodily-injury and Reyes wrongful-death matters belong to the individuals/estate, not Dalton. The damages workbook identifies a required personal representative for the wrongful-death action and notes separate counsel/conflict concerns for Trask and Anand. The supplied engagement is corporate-only. Workers’ compensation exclusivity may limit direct employee claims against Dalton, subject to confirmation of the governing law and any statutory exception; the current record does not establish an employer-intentional-tort claim. Any such allegation, indemnity/contribution claim, or conflict must be separately evaluated.

## Venue recommendation

Keep the corporate recovery track distinct from the employee tort track. For Dalton’s affirmative claims against Ironclad, comply with the contract’s negotiation and AAA path in Grand Rapids while seeking a tolling agreement and coordinated discovery. Analyze HydraCore’s forum, personal-jurisdiction, governing-law, and nonsignatory/beneficiary positions separately; do not assume Ironclad’s arbitration clause binds HydraCore. Ohio is the natural factual forum for the incident and injured Ohio workers, and any estate/individual claims should be assessed in consultation with their independent counsel. Those claimants did not sign the PO; the attempted extension of arbitration to them is debatable. Avoid filing overlapping merits actions before forum analysis, but seek Ohio provisional relief if needed to preserve evidence or prevent irreparable harm.

- OSHA civil penalties are imposed on Dalton and should be tracked separately from damages claimed against defendants; do not assume they are recoverable or insurable. Keep them out of the core compensatory valuation absent a specific legal basis. Contract penalties/delay damages to aerospace customers, cleanup, and other business impacts remain TBD.

- The worksheet alternates between itemized future-medical totals and higher “adjusted” life-care ranges for Trask. Obtain a signed life-care plan, updated medical records, vocational/economic opinions, and a consistent model. Quantify BWC benefits/subrogation and any employer statutory subrogation interests; the worksheet’s joint-and-several statutory citation should be separately verified before reliance.

Punitive exposure is materially increased by the February emails, especially the stated desire to avoid a “paper trail” and Brennan’s identification of the Dalton installation. The email record is powerful but not dispositive; confirm completeness, context, and whether the decision-makers knew the complaints implicated a life-threatening bladder/burst hazard. Analyze applicable Ohio statutory standards, limits and apportionment before including any punitive amount in the model.

# Bottom line

Proceed aggressively but with disciplined risk management. The current record supports a persuasive defective-component and unsafe-system narrative, with unusually probative pre-incident notice evidence against Ironclad. The principal threats to practical recovery are the incorporated arbitration/cap/consequential-loss terms, unresolved proof and expert issues, Dalton’s own cited safety deficiencies, and the absence of identified first-party property/BI insurance. Protect the OSHA and contractual deadlines first; preserve the evidence; separate Dalton’s corporate recovery from the employees’ independent cases; and quantify recoverable damages only after the press, dies, mitigation and coverage gaps are reconciled.
