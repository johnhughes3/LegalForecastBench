# Examination goals

- Establish Elliston’s firsthand operational role and how he identified the Q3 2023 shipment/sales mismatch while doing routine sales-operations work.

- Authenticate his contemporaneous spreadsheet and establish only what he personally saw in SAP: MidAmerican POs, ship-to destinations, account fields, and the SAP user ID recorded for account creation.

- Show that he reported concerns up Cascade’s chain of command, received the full responsive communications, and was told by General Counsel to stop inquiries.

- Present the termination circumstances for the limited purposes of consciousness of guilt and credibility—not as an independent wrongful-termination or retaliation claim.

- Confront foreseeable credibility points candidly; preserve the line between lay observations and Kowalski’s expert financial analysis.

# 3. Plaintiff’s direct examination

*Suggested sequence follows the factual chronology. Bracketed notes are counsel cues, not questions for the jury.*

## A. Identity, employment, and role

**Purpose:** Make clear why this witness routinely accessed the relevant sales, shipment, customer, and royalty-reporting information.

**Q.** Please state your name and current position.

**Q.** Where are you employed, and what is your role there?

**Q.** Were you employed by Cascade Supply Group? When did you work there?

**Q.** What was your title, and to whom did you report?

**Q.** What were your responsibilities as Vice President of Sales Operations?

**Q.** How many regional sales managers did you oversee?

**Q.** What did your responsibilities include regarding sales forecasting, shipment reconciliation, and royalty reporting?

**Q.** How often did you work with shipment and sales data? For how many years?

**Q.** What systems and tools did you use in that work? How long had you used SAP?

*Expected foundation: Cascade August 2018–January 12, 2024; VP of Sales Operations reporting to CEO Derek Voss; 22 regional sales managers; daily SAP and regular Excel use; routine comparison of warehouse shipments to invoiced sales and preparation of reports later finalized by Finance. Deposition refs: pp. 15–19, 120–21, 215–16.*

## B. Ridgeline relationship and reporting workflow

**Purpose:** Orient the jury to the account and Elliston’s personal role without turning him into a contract-law witness.

**Q.** What was your role, if any, in Cascade’s relationship with Ridgeline?

**Q.** What did you understand your operational responsibilities to be under the Ridgeline distribution relationship?

**Q.** What information did Cascade report quarterly to Ridgeline, and who in the company certified the reports?

**Q.** What was your role in reconciling shipment data and sales records before Finance finalized a report?

**Q.** As part of your work, did you maintain or use a list of authorized sub-distributors? How?

**Q.** What did your ordinary process require before a proposed sub-distributor could be treated as approved?

*If the EDA is used: mark/identify it; ask whether he recognizes the agreement and how he worked with it. Keep any reference to the 4.5% rate or prior written approval tied to his routine administration and the written document. Do not ask for a legal conclusion that Cascade “breached” the EDA.*

## C. Routine Q3 reconciliation and what he found

**Purpose:** Present a simple, perception-based comparison and show that ordinary adjustments did not explain it.

**Q.** What work were you doing in September 2023?

**Q.** How did you prepare the Q3 sales forecast and reconciliation?

**Q.** Which Cascade system records did you compare?

**Q.** What did the warehouse shipment records show for Series 700 gate valves in Q3 2023?

**Q.** What did the sales-reporting data show for sales to authorized customers in that quarter?

**Q.** What difference did you observe between those two figures?

**Q.** What did you do to check whether ordinary adjustments accounted for the difference?

**Q.** What did you find regarding returns, warranty replacements, damaged goods, and inventory adjustments?

**Q.** Based on your work, could you account for the unit gap through those categories?

**Q.** What was the approximate per-unit resale price you knew for Series 700 valves, and how did you know it?

**Q.** Did your work at that point tell you the total financial loss or total unpaid royalties?

*Permitted factual figures: 4,217 shipped; 3,104 reported sold to authorized customers; 1,113-unit gap; no returns, warranty replacements, or inventory adjustments accounting for it; approximate unit resale price \$1,271.34 from his sales-pricing/forecasting role. Do not ask him to multiply units by price or apply the royalty rate.*

**Purpose:** Lay the full Rule 901(a) foundation before publication; establish the file as his contemporaneous factual compilation.

**Q.** Did you create a spreadsheet relating to the Q3 2023 unit discrepancy?

**Q.** What software did you use, and when did you start and finish the work?

**Q.** What source records did you use to make it?

**Q.** How did you obtain the data from SAP and bring it into Excel?

**Q.** What does the Summary tab show?

**Q.** What information appears on the Shipment Detail and Sales Comparison tabs?

**Q.** How did the comparison identify the shipments associated with MidAmerican?

**Q.** Do you recognize the exhibit marked \[PX \_\_\] as the spreadsheet you created?

**Q.** Is it the same spreadsheet you attached to your September 28 email to Mr. Voss?

**Q.** Is the exhibit a fair and accurate copy of the spreadsheet you created and sent?

**Q.** Has it been altered by you since you created it? Is it in substantially the same condition as when you created it?

**Q.** Where did you save it during your employment, and what copy did you later provide to counsel?

| **COUNSEL NOTE:** Chronology must be handled accurately. The workbook metadata shows September 22; Elliston testified “around September 25” when first identifying the discrepancy, and the September 28 email says “identified last week.” Elicit that he worked over multiple days, began the spreadsheet around September 22, may first have noticed something earlier and confirmed it later, and cannot give one exact first-discovery date. Do not force false precision. |

**Q.** When you said in your deposition that you believed it was around September 25, were you claiming to remember an exact date?

**Q.** What do you mean by the phrase “identified last week” in the September 28 email?

**Q.** How does the September 22 creation date relate to your work on the spreadsheet over several days?

**Q.** At trial, can you give the jury a precise date for the first moment you noticed the issue?

*Impeachment preparation: deposition pp. 72–74 (around Sep. 25; “last week”; no precise date), p. 83 (spreadsheet started on/about Sep. 22); spreadsheet metadata; September 28 email. Be candid and distinguish first noticed, confirmed, and spreadsheet creation.*

## E. MidAmerican POs, destinations, account information, and user ID

**Purpose:** Confine testimony to records he personally inspected and what he knew from ordinary SAP use.

**Q.** After noticing the Q3 gap, what did you do to look for the shipments that did not match reported authorized sales?

**Q.** What did the purchase-order number on a shipment allow you to locate in SAP?

**Q.** What account name appeared on the POs you found?

**Q.** What did you observe in the POs’ “bill to” and “ship to” fields?

**Q.** Which destinations appeared in those fields?

**Q.** Before this review, had you dealt with MidAmerican, Great Lakes, or Summit in your Ridgeline sales-operations work?

**Q.** What was your responsibility concerning Cascade’s authorized sub-distributor list?

**Q.** Did you find Great Lakes or Summit on the list? Did you find written approval for either in the records you maintained or reviewed?

**Q.** What did you personally observe in SAP about when the MidAmerican customer account was created and the “created by” field?

**Q.** What did “DVOSS01” signify to you based on your own SAP experience at Cascade?

**Q.** How were SAP user IDs assigned and used at Cascade? Were they intended to be individual and unique?

**Q.** Did you ever personally see someone other than Mr. Voss use his credentials?

**Q.** Can you testify from personal knowledge who was physically at the keyboard when that account was created?

**Q.** What else, if anything, did you observe in the MidAmerican account record—such as contact, representative, or credit-application information?

**Q.** Was that consistent with the ordinary customer-account setup process you knew?

*Careful formulation: “The SAP field recorded DVOSS01; from my ordinary use that was Mr. Voss’s assigned user ID.” If asked, acknowledge the audit trail logs the user ID, not the physical person. Do not testify that Voss personally created it as a certainty, that the account was a shell, or to account transfers/bank deposits. Deposition refs: pp. 120–34.*

## Core “do not cross” lines

**Purpose:** Prevent an otherwise helpful fact witness from straying into expert territory or inadmissible hearsay.

- Do not ask total diverted revenue across Q2 2022–Q4 2023, total unpaid royalties (\$456,975), or any overall Ridgeline damages/financial-impact opinion.

- Do not ask Elliston to compute 1,113 × \$1,271.34, apply the 4.5% rate, or characterize bank deposits/transfers. The court expressly reserves those matters for expert testimony.

- Do not describe DVOSS01 as conclusive proof that Derek Voss personally acted; state what the field recorded and what his user-ID experience told him.

- Do not elicit Karen Cho’s alleged statement that Trimble ordered the Q3 report held unless admissibility is resolved; Elliston did not hear the instruction.
