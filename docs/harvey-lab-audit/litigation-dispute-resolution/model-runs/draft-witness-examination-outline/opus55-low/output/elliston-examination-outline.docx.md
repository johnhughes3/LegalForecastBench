# Part I. Objectives, Themes, and Strategy

## A. What this witness must accomplish

- **Discovery (fact witness, FRE 602/701):** Establish through personal perception the Q3 2023 gap: 4,217 Series 700 units shipped from the Dublin, Ohio DC vs. 3,104 reported sold to authorized customers = 1,113 units, not explained by returns, warranty replacements, or inventory adjustments.

- **The MidAmerican trail:** MidAmerican POs in SAP; “bill to” MidAmerican (440 Broad St., Suite 200, Wilmington, DE) but “ship to” Great Lakes Pipe & Valve (Detroit) and Summit Industrial Supply (Milwaukee); neither on the authorized sub-distributor list Elliston personally maintained; no written Ridgeline consent.

- **DVOSS01:** MidAmerican customer account created April 18, 2022 by SAP user ID DVOSS01; Elliston’s personal knowledge of the user-ID convention and that DVOSS01 was Voss’s login; no credit application, contact, or sales rep — contrary to standard onboarding.

- **Notice and suppression (party-opponent statements):** Three email chains, admitted complete (Order § III): Voss (“Don’t share this with anyone else for now”), Trimble (“this is a finance matter and is being handled. Please focus on your sales targets”), Greer memo (“reviewed and resolved”; “refrain from further inquiries”).

- **Consciousness of guilt:** First-ever late Q3 2023 royalty report (due Oct. 30; filed Nov. 17, 2023); termination 58 days after formal escalation under a “restructuring” that eliminated only his position; Voss’s “business decision, nothing personal” statement; severance conditioned on release and non-disparagement (limited purpose).

- **Credibility:** Present him as a careful, rule-following executive who went up the chain of command, and take the sting out of the USB drive, the commission dispute, and the severance certification before Stokes does.

## B. Themes for this witness

- “He did his job — and they told him to stop.”

- “The numbers came from Cascade’s own system.” (It is Cascade’s SAP data, not Elliston’s opinion; Kowalski independently confirmed 4,217 / 3,104 / 1,113 and DVOSS01.)

- “Up the chain, not out the door.” (CEO → CEO + CFO → CEO + CFO + GC before ever speaking to anyone outside Cascade.)

- “Fired for restructuring — of one.” (1 of 23 in the department; replaced within 24 days.)

# Part III. Pre-Trial Action Items and Red Flags (Resolve Before Direct)

*A close comparison of the case materials reveals problems that, if not addressed, could cause the spreadsheet to be excluded on renewed objection, or could let Cascade impeach Elliston with his own documents. Each item lists the problem and the recommended fix.*

## 1. Spreadsheet version / authentication (CRITICAL)

- **Post-creation commentary in the file.** The “Date Created” row on the Summary tab of the produced spreadsheet contains a “Notes” entry that discusses Elliston’s **deposition testimony (p. 72)** and the “last week” email wording. That text could not have existed in September 2023. As produced, the file is not “in substantially the same condition as when created” (Order § IX), and it contradicts Elliston’s testimony that the version he gave counsel is the same one he attached on September 28 (Dep. 88).

- **Shipments dated after the September 28 email.** The Shipment Detail tab includes 13 shipments dated **September 29–30, 2023** (rows 108–120), among them a MidAmerican shipment on 9/29 (PO-MDA-23-0082, 20 units). A file attached at 2:17 PM on September 28 cannot contain them. The Summary also covers the full quarter (“July 1 – September 30, 2023”).

- **Content that was new as of October 5.** The Summary tab’s “Key Finding” (MidAmerican; account created April 18, 2022 by DVOSS01) matches the **October 5** email (“I have now identified the source of the discrepancy”), which attached a different file (MidAmerican_PO_Analysis_100523.xlsx). By contrast, the September 28 email says: “At this point I do not have an explanation for where these units went.” Dep. 78 has Elliston saying he had identified the MidAmerican POs **before** the September 28 email. That is a real inconsistency Stokes can exploit.

- **Internal math that does not tie.** (a) Shipment Detail rows total **3,503** units (2,390 authorized + 1,113 MidAmerican), not 4,217; the authorized rows understate the 3,104 in the Summary by 714 units. (b) Monthly authorized figures on Sales Comparison (1,005/1,048/1,051) do not match the detail rows (725/742/923). (c) The line-item extended value of the MidAmerican rows is **\$1,500,395.00**, but the Summary and Sales Comparison use **\$1,415,001.42** (1,113 × \$1,271.34). (d) Row 106 is labeled the “final MidAmerican shipment in Q3 2023,” but row 110 (9/29) is a later one. (e) The September 28 email says the analysis breaks the data down by “warehouse lot number,” but the produced file has no lot-number column.

- **The number of MidAmerican POs does not match the expert.** The spreadsheet shows **36** Q3 MidAmerican POs (PO-MDA-23-0047 through -0082). Kowalski says the 1,113 units were “traced to eight specific MidAmerican purchase orders” (Kowalski § IV.D, § V.D). Cascade will use this against both witnesses.

- **Dollar and royalty calculations inside the exhibit.** The Summary and Sales Comparison tabs show estimated resale value (\$1,415,001.42), estimated unpaid royalties (\$63,675.06), and royalty variances. These are Q3 figures only and not the barred aggregate figures, but publishing them through Elliston invites a FRE 701/702 objection under the Order’s spirit (§ VII, § XII(1)).

**Recommended fixes:**

- Get the **native attachments** from Cascade’s production (the Sept. 28 attachment Q3_2023_Unit_Discrepancy_Analysis.xlsx to CASCADE_EMAIL_003857, and the Oct. 5 attachment MidAmerican_PO_Analysis_100523.xlsx to CASCADE_EMAIL_003853). Also get Elliston’s USB copy, with a forensic hash and metadata report. Offer the version **as it was attached to Cascade’s own email** — Cascade produced it, which also strengthens authentication (FRE 901(b)(4)).

- Identify who added the “Notes” commentary to the working copy, and strip all attorney or consultant annotations from any trial exhibit. Disclose the clean exhibit to opposing counsel.

- Meet with Elliston (without coaching) to learn the true history of the file. Did he keep refining one workbook after September 28? Are the September 28 and October 5 files separate? Which version is which? If the produced file is a later or merged version, **do not** have him testify that it is “the same version” he attached on September 28. Authenticate each version for what it actually is.

- Consider offering a **redacted/demonstrative version** (FRE 1006 summary or FRE 107 illustrative aid) limited to units: shipped / reported / gap / zero returns, zero warranty, zero transfers. Remove the dollar and royalty columns. Confirm with Kowalski that she will present the Q3 dollar value.

- Resolve the “8 POs vs. 36 POs” and “3,503 vs. 4,217” discrepancies with Kowalski before either witness testifies. The detail tab may be an excerpt of the full shipment log; if so, Elliston should say so.

## 2. Severance Agreement Exhibit A and § 9(c) (CRITICAL)

- On January 19, 2024, Elliston initialed and signed Exhibit A, certifying that he had returned all “USB drives” and “ha\[d\] not retained any copies ... of Company documents, data, or records in any form.” He also represented in § 9(c) that he had not violated the Data Security Policy. Yet he kept the November 20, 2023 USB copies and later gave them to Whitfield & Crane (Dep. 150).

- This is a signed, false written certification. Stokes can pursue it under FRE 608(b) as conduct probative of untruthfulness — much stronger than the policy-violation point in Cascade’s brief. The § 8(b) carve-out protects **testimony**; it does not cure the certification.

- **Fix:** Before trial, ask Elliston about his state of mind when he signed (e.g., he understood the “return” obligation to cover company devices; he believed the files were evidence of potential wrongdoing; he had been told by the General Counsel the matter was “closed”). Do not suggest answers. If there is a truthful explanation, consider fronting it on direct (Chapter 11). Research whether retaining evidence of suspected fraud is protected (e.g., public-policy limits on enforcing confidentiality provisions against evidence preservation). Prepare a bench brief on the limits of FRE 608(b): no extrinsic evidence; the questioning must be probative of truthfulness.
