# I. Summary and Bottom Line

I checked all 35 of Defendants' Proposed Jury Instructions against (a) the Order on Cross-Motions for Summary Judgment (Jan. 12, 2025) ("SJ Order"); (b) the Order on Motion for Sanctions for Spoliation (Sept. 5, 2024) ("Sanctions Order"); (c) the Minutes of the Feb. 10, 2025 Pretrial Conference ("Minutes"); (d) the Voss Employment Agreement dated March 14, 2016 ("Agreement"); and (e) our Trial Brief on Damages (Mar. 14, 2025). Under the Minutes, objections must be filed by **April 7, 2025**. Each objection must name the instruction number, state the legal basis, and propose alternative language. The charge conference is **April 14, 2025**.

**Four instructions directly violate express court rulings.** We should object to each as barred by a prior order, not just as a misstatement of law:

- **No. 21 (Spoliation):** tells the jury it "may not draw any adverse inference." Paragraph 4 of the Sanctions Order expressly forbids any party from proposing that kind of instruction.

- **No. 29 (Failure to Enforce / Apportionment):** this is the failure-to-mitigate defense under a new label. Defendants withdrew that defense on February 10, and the Court barred raising it "in proposed jury instructions."

- **No. 14 (Identification of Trade Secrets):** asks the jury to decide SignalSift (category 7). The SJ Order dismissed that category with prejudice and said the jury "will not be asked to consider" it.

- **No. 23 (Inevitable Disclosure), second paragraph:** says job similarity "is not evidence of misappropriation." The SJ Order held the opposite: it is admissible circumstantial evidence.

**Several other instructions misstate the substantive law in ways that favor Defendants on key issues.** The most important are:

- No. 16: defines misappropriation by acquisition only and leaves out the use/disclosure prong.

- No. 25: requires disclosure of "specific documents," which conflicts with Agreement §4.2 and the SJ Order.

- No. 30: forces the jury to choose between lost profits and unjust enrichment and requires unanimity on that choice.

- No. 32: sets the punitive standard as actual fraud plus specific intent to injure.

- No. 31: tells the jury to view Dr. Marchetti with "particular skepticism."

- No. 12: adds a "novel" requirement.

- No. 27: adds elements to tortious interference.

**We also found inconsistencies across the case documents, including our own filings.** These include the plaintiff's name, section numbering in the Agreement, spoliation dates, docket numbers, and several factual errors in our Trial Brief. They are collected in Part IV and should be fixed before we file objections or our own proposed instructions.

# III. Instruction-by-Instruction Analysis

## A. Instruction No. 21 — Spoliation / Adverse Inference (CRITICAL)

**Problem.** The instruction says the jury "may not draw any adverse inference from either party's handling of electronic data." It describes deletion as routine business practice and says the unavailability of data "does not permit you to presume that the missing data would have been favorable or unfavorable to any party." That is the opposite of what the Court ordered. It also restates the "routine file management" explanation that the Court found "wholly lacking in credibility." Defendants cite Fed. R. Civ. P. 37(e), but Rule 37(e)(2)(B) is the provision the Court used to authorize the inference.

**Controlling authority.** Sanctions Order ¶4: "At trial, the Court will give an adverse inference instruction permitting the jury to presume that the 347 deleted files contained information unfavorable to Dr. Voss. . . . No party shall submit a proposed jury instruction that purports to bar the jury from drawing adverse inferences from Dr. Voss's handling of electronic data." The Court also found intent to deprive under Rule 37(e)(2) (¶3). The SJ Order (§II.F and ¶6) states that the sanctions ruling "remains in full force and effect." The Minutes (§V) reconfirm it.

**Recommended objection / alternative.** Strike No. 21 in full as filed in violation of the Sanctions Order, and consider asking the Court to note the violation. Submit substitute language along these lines: "Before trial, the Court found that Dr. Nolan Voss deleted 347 files from his personal cloud storage account after he received notice of his obligation to preserve evidence in this case, and that he did so with the intent to deprive Oakvale of the use of that information in this litigation. You may presume that the deleted files contained information unfavorable to Dr. Voss." Two points to watch. First, the authorized inference runs against **Dr. Voss only**. Our Trial Brief (§V.A.2) overstates it as "unfavorable to Defendants"; that should not be repeated. Second, the dates in the substitute must match the Sanctions Order (hold received April 17, 2023; deletions April 19 – May 3, 2023). See Part IV.

## B. Instruction No. 29 — Plaintiff's Failure to Enforce / Apportionment (CRITICAL)

**Problem.** The instruction tells the jury to "reduce any damages award proportionally" for Oakvale's "delay or inaction" and for failing to "exercise reasonable diligence in protecting its own interests." That is the failure-to-mitigate / avoidable-consequences doctrine. The cited statute, O.C.G.A. §51-11-7, is Georgia's avoidance-of-consequences provision, which is the statutory basis for mitigation. The instruction also adds comparative apportionment, which has no pleaded basis in an intentional-misappropriation and contract case.

**Controlling authority.** Minutes §IV: Defendants "formally withdraw their Fifteenth Affirmative Defense, which asserted failure to mitigate damages," and the withdrawal was "unconditional and without reservation." The Court ruled: "Defendants may not raise failure to mitigate at any stage of the trial proceedings, including in proposed jury instructions or closing arguments." Delay is also not among the five defenses Defendants said they would pursue at trial.

**Recommended objection / alternative.** Strike in full, as barred by the February 10 ruling. Also ask for a limiting order on "delay" argument in closing. Note that Oakvale sued within about eight months of Dr. Voss's departure and about two months after the Prism launch. No substitute instruction is needed.

## C. Instruction No. 14 — Identification of Alleged Trade Secrets (CRITICAL)

**Problem.** The instruction lists seven categories, including "(7) a machine-learning feature selection methodology known as 'SignalSift,'" and tells the jury to decide whether "each category" is a trade secret. It cites the SJ Order as authority even though that order dismissed category 7.

**Controlling authority.** SJ Order §IV.B.3 and Order ¶1: SignalSift is "DISMISSED WITH PREJUDICE. . . . The jury will not be asked to consider whether SignalSift is a protectable trade secret, as that question has been resolved as a matter of law." Minutes §III: the ruling "is final, and the parties should govern themselves accordingly in preparing trial materials."

**Recommended objection / alternative.** Delete item (7). Change the lead-in to "the following six categories." Consider also asking for a sentence telling the jury that no other category is at issue. This ties in with our motion in limine (Minutes §V(i)). Leaving SignalSift in front of the jury invites an argument that one "lost" category undermines the others. Confirm that Defendants' proposed verdict form does not include category 7.

## D. Instruction No. 23 — Inevitable Disclosure Doctrine (CRITICAL, in part)

**Problem.** The first paragraph, which says Georgia does not recognize inevitable disclosure as an independent basis for liability, is consistent with the SJ Order. The second paragraph is not. It states flatly that "the fact that Dr. Voss took a similar position at Axial Systems Corp. is not evidence of misappropriation." The instruction also cites a "Court's Order on Daubert Motions, November 18, 2024." We found no such order in the materials. The Daubert rulings are in the January 12, 2025 SJ Order (§IV.F).

**Controlling authority.** SJ Order §IV.C: the similarity of Dr. Voss's positions "is one piece of circumstantial evidence the jury may consider as part of the totality of the evidence." The distinction is "between a legal presumption (which is impermissible) and an evidentiary inference drawn from the totality of the evidence (which is permissible)," and the Court said it would address that distinction at the charge conference. The Court prohibited only an instruction that misappropriation "may be inferred solely from Dr. Voss's competitive employment."

**Recommended objection / alternative.** Object to the second paragraph and to the citation. Proposed alternative: "You may not find misappropriation based solely on the fact that Dr. Voss accepted a similar position with a competitor. However, you may consider the nature of Dr. Voss's positions at Oakvale and Axial, together with all other evidence in the case, in deciding whether Dr. Voss or Axial actually acquired, used, or disclosed Oakvale's trade secrets." Ask Defendants to identify the "November 18, 2024" order or withdraw the citation.

## E. Instructions No. 16, 17 and 22 — Misappropriation / Improper Means / Use in New Employment (HIGH)

**Problem.** No. 16 defines misappropriation only as "acquisition . . . by a person who knows or has reason to know that the trade secret was acquired by improper means." It leaves out the statutory "disclosure or use" prong entirely, including use by someone who acquired the information under a duty to keep it secret. Dr. Voss obtained Oakvale's information lawfully while employed, so an acquisition-only definition would effectively take the core use/disclosure theory away from the jury. The elements paragraph and No. 22 repeat the problem by requiring proof that defendants acquired, used, or disclosed information "through improper means." No. 16 also duplicates the improper-means definition in No. 17, which risks confusing the jury.

**Controlling authority.** O.C.G.A. §10-1-761(2)(A)–(B). SJ Order §IV.B.1: the statute "protects against both the wrongful acquisition of trade secrets and their wrongful use or disclosure," including use or disclosure by a person who "knew or had reason to know that the knowledge of the trade secret was acquired under circumstances giving rise to a duty to maintain its secrecy or limit its use."

**Recommended objection / alternative.** Replace No. 16 with the full statutory definition covering both (A) acquisition and (B) disclosure or use, including the duty-based prong. Revise the last sentence of No. 22 to read: "The plaintiff must prove that the defendant actually misappropriated specific trade secret information, as I have defined that term." Consolidate No. 17 into No. 16, or keep it as a pure definition. We do not object to the "similarity alone is not sufficient" concept in No. 22, as long as it is paired with the SJ Order's permissible-inference language (see D).

## F. Instruction No. 25 — Breach of Confidentiality Provision (HIGH)

**Problem.** The instruction requires Oakvale to prove that Dr. Voss "disclosed specific documents belonging to Oakvale." It also requires that the disclosure be a "material breach." This narrows the contract to document disclosure. It leaves out (i) the non-use obligation, (ii) intangible and memorized information, and (iii) indirect use. It also adds a materiality requirement that the Agreement does not contain for a damages claim.

**Controlling authority.** Agreement §4.2 prohibits both use and disclosure. It provides that unauthorized use, "including the application of intangible knowledge, methods, processes, or know-how . . . in the course of employment with a subsequent employer, constitutes a breach of this Agreement regardless of whether such use involves the physical transfer or disclosure of specific documents or tangible materials." Under §4.1, Confidential Information includes "information retained in Employee's memory or recollection," and "the burden of establishing that any of the foregoing exclusions applies shall rest with Employee." SJ Order §IV.D: the obligation "is not limited to the physical transfer or copying of documents."

**Recommended objection / alternative.** Substitute: "Oakvale claims that Dr. Voss breached Section 4 of the Employment Agreement by using or disclosing Oakvale's Confidential Information without authorization. Confidential Information is defined in Section 4.1 and includes information in any form, including information retained in Dr. Voss's memory. A breach occurs if Dr. Voss used or disclosed Confidential Information for any purpose other than his duties for Oakvale, whether or not documents were transferred. If Dr. Voss contends that information falls within one of the Agreement's exclusions, he bears the burden of proving that exclusion." Delete "material." The section reference itself needs to be confirmed; see Part IV.2.

## G. Instruction No. 30 — Lost Profits and Unjust Enrichment (HIGH)

**Problem.** The instruction tells the jury it "must unanimously agree on which category of damages — lost profits or unjust enrichment — to award. You may not award both." That makes the two measures mutually exclusive and imposes a unanimity requirement on the choice of measure. The instruction also leaves out the reasonable-royalty alternative, which Oakvale has reserved.

**Controlling authority.** O.C.G.A. §10-1-763(a): a complainant "may recover damages for the actual loss" and "also may recover for the unjust enrichment . . . that is not taken into account in computing damages for actual loss." SJ Order §IV.F.1: the jury "may not award both lost profits and unjust enrichment **to the extent they compensate for the same injury**." Order ¶5(d) sends compensatory damages "including lost profits and unjust enrichment" to trial "subject to appropriate jury instructions to prevent double recovery." Fed. R. Civ. P. 48(b) requires unanimity on the verdict, not on each underlying theory (Trial Brief §II.A).

**Recommended objection / alternative.** Substitute a non-duplication instruction: "You may award damages for Oakvale's actual loss, including lost profits, caused by misappropriation. You may also award damages for any unjust enrichment of a defendant caused by misappropriation, but only to the extent that enrichment was not already taken into account in computing Oakvale's actual loss. You may not award damages twice for the same injury." Ask for separate verdict-form lines for actual loss and non-duplicative unjust enrichment. **Caution:** Dr. Marchetti's unjust-enrichment figure is \$22.0M × 70% = \$15.4M. Our Trial Brief says she excluded revenue from the 14 diverted customers from that base, but the Brief applies the margin to "total Prism revenue" of \$22.0M. The SJ Order also describes the calculation as \$22.0M × 70%. We need to reconcile this before the charge conference. If the \$22.0M base includes the diverted customers, Defendants will argue the model double-counts, and the anti-duplication argument becomes weaker.

## H. Instruction No. 32 — Punitive Damages (HIGH)

**Problem.** The instruction requires clear and convincing proof that the defendant "acted with actual fraud and specific intent to injure the plaintiff." It also substitutes a general "reasonable relationship" limit for the statutory cap. "Specific intent" language comes from the cap-exception provisions of the general punitive statute (O.C.G.A. §51-12-5.1(f)–(g)), and "actual fraud" is not an element under either statute. Neither is the GTSA liability standard.

**Controlling authority.** O.C.G.A. §10-1-763(b) authorizes exemplary damages for "willful and malicious misappropriation," up to twice the compensatory award. SJ Order §IV.G frames the jury question as "whether Defendants' conduct was 'willful and malicious' within the meaning of O.C.G.A. §10-1-763(b)" and reserved the cap for the charge conference. The Minutes (§VII) also reserve the §51-12-5.1(g) cap question.

**Recommended objection / alternative.** Object and substitute the "willful and malicious" standard, for example: "knowingly and deliberately misappropriated a trade secret in conscious disregard of Oakvale's rights." Object to any cap-related or "reasonable relationship" language in front of the jury, since the cap is a matter for the Court after the verdict. **Burden-of-proof risk:** §51-12-5.1(b) requires clear and convincing evidence for Georgia tort punitive damages generally. Our Trial Brief (§II.B) says that standard applies "most notably" to product liability, which overstates our position. Before the charge conference we should research whether clear and convincing applies to §10-1-763(b). We should not rely on the Brief's characterization.

## I. Instruction No. 31 — Expert Witness Testimony (HIGH)

**Problem.** The instruction tells jurors to view Dr. Marchetti's testimony "with particular skepticism given her financial relationship with the plaintiff." It then treats Dr. Webley, who was also retained and paid, neutrally. This is one-sided comment on the evidence. It also effectively revisits the Court's denial of Defendants' Daubert motion. The Eleventh Circuit pattern instruction it cites contains no such language.

**Controlling authority.** SJ Order §IV.F.1: Dr. Marchetti's methodology is "sufficiently reliable," and challenges go to weight, to be tested by cross-examination and competing expert testimony. The pattern instruction treats expert testimony like any other testimony.

**Recommended objection / alternative.** Strike the "particular skepticism" sentence. Keep a neutral statement that the jury may consider compensation "of any expert witness" in weighing credibility.

## J. Instruction No. 12 — Definition of "Trade Secret" (HIGH)

**Problem.** The first sentence defines a trade secret as information that is "novel." It also leaves out the statutory lead-in listing covered forms of information (compilations, methods, financial data, customer/supplier lists), which matters for categories 2, 3, and 6. Finally, it inserts "independent" before "economic value," which is not in the text of §10-1-761(4)(A) as quoted in the SJ Order.

**Controlling authority.** SJ Order §IV.B.1: "The GTSA does not require that the information claimed as a trade secret be novel or represent an advance over the prior art." The SJ Order quotes the statute in full.

**Recommended objection / alternative.** Delete "novel." Use the statutory text of §10-1-761(4) verbatim, including "a compilation" and "financial data." The last paragraph of No. 12, which says to find for defendants "on the misappropriation claim as to that category," is acceptable if it is limited to the GTSA claim and does not carry over to the contract claim.

## K. Instruction No. 27 — Tortious Interference with Contract (HIGH)

**Problem.** The instruction lists five elements. It adds (3) "an independently wrongful act, separate from the interference itself" and (4) "purpose of inducing the breach." Both go beyond the elements the Court identified.

**Controlling authority.** SJ Order §IV.E (citing Disaster Servs. and Valdosta Livestock): (1) a valid contract; (2) the defendant's knowledge; (3) intentional inducement of breach; (4) damages. The Court identified "knowing inducement" as the jury question.

**Recommended objection / alternative.** Substitute the four elements from the SJ Order. Consider a short statement that element 1 is undisputed and that the defendant's knowledge may be shown by Axial's possession of the Agreement from onboarding (Ex. S). If Defendants argue that Georgia requires "improper" or unprivileged conduct, we should be ready to address it at the charge conference. The authority for the Court's four-element formulation is its own order.

## L. Instructions No. 13 and 20 — Reasonable Measures; Combination of Known Elements (MEDIUM)

**Problem.** No. 13 asks whether security was "inadequate" as to any category and directs a finding against trade-secret status. It never says the measures need only be "reasonable under the circumstances" and not perfect. It also leaves out the size and sophistication of the business and the totality of the circumstances. No. 20 requires that a combination provide "sufficient added value beyond its individual components," a test that does not appear in the statute or the SJ Order.

**Controlling authority.** §10-1-761(4)(B). SJ Order §IV.B.2 (reasonableness is a totality-of-circumstances question under Hoshizaki, 338 Ga. App. at 44). On compilations, the SJ Order asks "whether the compilation as a whole . . . derives independent economic value from not being generally known."

**Recommended objection / alternative.** No. 13: replace "inadequate" with "not reasonable under the circumstances." Add that reasonable measures do not require perfect or absolute secrecy and that the jury may consider the nature of the information and the size and circumstances of the business. No. 20: delete the "sufficient added value" sentence and track the SJ Order's "compilation as a whole" language.

## M. Instructions No. 18, 19 and 15 — Independent Development; General Knowledge; Readily Ascertainable (MEDIUM/LOW)

**Problem.** No. 19 (general knowledge, skill, and experience) is a proper GTSA concept. As drafted, however, it is not limited to the trade secret claim, and jurors could apply it to the contract claim. The Agreement reaches a broader range of non-public Confidential Information, including memorized information, and puts the burden of proving exclusions on the employee (§4.1). No. 18 calls independent development a "complete defense." That is acceptable as to Axial's independently developed features, but it should be clear that the burden on misappropriation stays with Oakvale, which is consistent with our case, and that independent development by Axial does not excuse any use or disclosure by Dr. Voss. No. 15 lists "independent development" as a way information becomes "readily ascertainable." That confuses a defense to use with the definition of a trade secret. Also check the citation: No. 15 and the SJ Order cite Penalty Kick as 318 Ga. App. 586 (2012), while our Trial Brief cites 318 F.3d 1284 (11th Cir. 2003).

**Controlling authority.** Agreement §4.1 and §4.2; SJ Order §IV.D (Dr. Voss's general-skills contention is a fact dispute under the contract).

**Recommended objection / alternative.** Limit No. 19 to the GTSA claim, and add a companion contract instruction. In No. 18, state that the defense applies only to features developed "without use of or reference to" Oakvale information, "including information provided by Dr. Voss." Remove "independent development" from No. 15. Cite-check Penalty Kick in all filings.

## N. Instructions No. 11, 24, 28, 35 and Other Items (LOW)

**Problem.** No. 11 cites "Employment Agreement, Section 12" for choice of law. In the Agreement produced, the governing-law clause is §8.1 (see Part IV.2). No. 24 states that the parties "have stipulated" that the Agreement is valid and enforceable. Confirm there is a written stipulation, since Defendants argued unenforceability at summary judgment (SJ Order §IV.D, rejected). If there is none, ask that the instruction say the Court has determined enforceability. No. 28 (causation) is generally acceptable, but the "would have occurred regardless" sentence should not suggest that Defendants' conduct must be the sole cause. The instruction already says there may be more than one proximate cause. No. 35 (verdict form) should list the six categories only, include separate actual-loss and unjust-enrichment lines, and include the "willful and malicious" finding. No. 34 (injunctive relief for the Court) is consistent with the Minutes, and we do not object. Nos. 1–9, 26 and 33 appear to be standard and unobjectionable, subject to the party-name correction below.

**Controlling authority.** Agreement §8.1; SJ Order §IV.D; Minutes §§III, VIII.

**Recommended objection / alternative.** Make targeted corrections as noted.

# V. Omissions: Instructions We Should Affirmatively Request

- The **adverse-inference instruction against Dr. Voss** (Sanctions Order ¶4). Defendants offered only its opposite.

- The **permissible-inference / job-similarity instruction** the SJ Order said would be addressed at the charge conference (§IV.C).

- A **full statutory misappropriation definition** including use and disclosure (§10-1-761(2)(B)).
