**DEFENDANT VANTAGE MICRO SYSTEMS, INC.'S COUNTERCLAIMS AGAINST PLAINTIFF LUMENARA OPTICS CORPORATION**

Defendant and Counter-Plaintiff Vantage Micro Systems, Inc. ("Vantage"), by and through its undersigned counsel, and pursuant to Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 13, asserts the following Counterclaims against Plaintiff and Counter-Defendant Lumenara Optics Corporation ("Lumenara"). These Counterclaims are filed together with, and are a part of, Vantage's Answer and Affirmative Defenses to Plaintiff's Original Complaint (Dkt. \[\_\_\]). Except where expressly stated, the allegations below are made on Vantage's personal knowledge as to itself and its own acts, and on information and belief as to all other matters.

**I. NATURE OF THE COUNTERCLAIMS**

1\. Lumenara has sued Vantage for supposedly misusing Lumenara's coating technology during the parties' Project Meridian collaboration. The documentary record shows the reverse. Before Lumenara signed the Joint Development Agreement, its senior leadership privately agreed that the collaboration was a means to obtain Vantage's proprietary SensorCore™ substrate technology. Two weeks before signing, Lumenara's Project Meridian Program Manager wrote to its Chief Executive Officer that "\[t\]he real value here is getting our hands on their substrate tech." The CEO replied: "Agreed. Let's keep Meridian going long enough to extract what we need."

2\. Lumenara did what those emails describe. It signed the Joint Development Agreement and gave a written representation that it was entering the agreement "in good faith and with the genuine intent to perform." It delivered the first, easy milestone on time. It then gave its designated engineer full access to Vantage's Shared Technical Portal, from which she downloaded Vantage's complete 847-file, 2.3 GB SensorCore™ design package four times. The last download took place on December 12, 2023 (UTC), after the project had stalled because of Lumenara's own missed milestones and 27 days before Lumenara announced its LumiSense 400 LIDAR sensor at CES. Meanwhile, Lumenara failed to deliver three of its four contractual milestones.

3\. An independent teardown of a commercially purchased LumiSense 400 found that its substrate matches the SensorCore™ architecture on every parameter examined. The match includes non-functional features, such as Vantage's "cross-hair with quadrant notch" registration marks and aerospace-specific "dog-leg" routing paths that serve no purpose in a LIDAR product. Features like these indicate copying of Vantage's electronic design files, not independent development.

4\. Vantage asserts these Counterclaims for breach of the Joint Development Agreement, breach of the Mutual Non-Disclosure Agreement, trade secret misappropriation under federal and Texas law, fraudulent inducement, and patent infringement. It seeks compensatory, exemplary, and enhanced damages, injunctive relief, and attorneys' fees and costs.

**A. Vantage and Its SensorCore™ Technology**

12\. Vantage was founded in 2011. It has approximately 310 employees and reported annual revenue of approximately \$142 million for fiscal year 2023. Its products are used in unmanned aerial systems, satellite imaging platforms, and other aerospace and defense applications.

13\. Beginning in approximately 2016, Vantage invested more than seven years of research, design, simulation, prototyping, and testing in its proprietary SensorCore™ substrate-level sensor array architecture. SensorCore™ uses a 14-layer copper-pillar micro-bump interconnect structure. The industry norm for comparable optical sensor substrates is 6 to 10 layers. SensorCore™ uses a micro-bump pitch of 45μm, at the aggressive end of the design space; most commercial designs use 60μm or greater. It also uses a distinctive radial-array thermal via pattern developed through Vantage's computational fluid dynamics simulations and empirical thermal testing.

14\. Vantage owns U.S. Patent No. 11,234,567 (the "'567 Patent"), entitled "Multi-Layer Copper-Pillar Micro-Bump Interconnect Architecture for Integrated Optical Sensor Substrates," which the United States Patent and Trademark Office duly and legally issued on January 10, 2023. \[CONFIRM exact title from the face of the patent; the Langford report recites a different title.\] Two continuation applications claiming priority to the '567 Patent are pending.

15\. The '567 Patent claims certain aspects of the SensorCore™ architecture at the level of generality appropriate to patent claims. It does not publish, and Vantage has never publicly disclosed, the detailed design and manufacturing information that makes SensorCore™ work in practice (together, the "SensorCore™ Trade Secrets"), including:

> \(a\) the complete lithographic mask layouts for each of the 14 substrate layers, including Vantage's proprietary layer-to-layer registration alignment mark design (the "cross-hair with quadrant notch" pattern);
>
> \(b\) Vantage's proprietary semiconductor doping profiles, including the updated doping profile documentation (v3.3) uploaded to the Portal on October 5, 2022;
>
> \(c\) the precise coordinates and distribution of each thermal via in the radial-array pattern, as reflected in Vantage's thermal via schematics (v3.2), together with the simulation and empirical data from which that distribution was optimized;
>
> \(d\) Vantage's signal routing topology, including specific routing paths in layers 3–5 and 10–12 optimized for aerospace sensor signal integrity;
>
> \(e\) under-bump metallization (Ti/Cu/Ni) layer thicknesses, solder cap composition ratios, and related interconnect process specifications; and
>
> \(f\) process flow documentation, design verification data, and interface specifications contained in the SensorCore™ design package.

16\. The SensorCore™ Trade Secrets derive independent economic value, actual and potential, from not being generally known to, and not being readily ascertainable through proper means by, others who could obtain economic value from their disclosure or use. A competitor with this information could skip years of development and the associated cost. Lumenara's own Program Manager acknowledged as much, writing that the SensorCore™ designs "would leapfrog our LiDAR sensor program by 2+ years" and that Lumenara's own team had "been struggling to achieve \[the design\] independently for over a year."

17\. Vantage takes reasonable measures to keep the SensorCore™ Trade Secrets secret. Among other things, Vantage (a) disclosed them to Lumenara only after the parties executed a Mutual Non-Disclosure Agreement and, later, a Joint Development Agreement with strict use restrictions; (b) marked every file in the SensorCore™ design package "PROPRIETARY — VANTAGE MICRO SYSTEMS"; (c) made the files available only through a Vantage-hosted Shared Technical Portal limited to a short list of named, individually credentialed users, restricted to whitelisted IP address ranges for each party, and subject to automated activity logging; and (d) limited portal access on the Lumenara side to three authorized individuals. \[CONFIRM internal Vantage protections to add, e.g., employee confidentiality agreements, internal need-to-know access controls, and data-loss-prevention measures.\]

**B. The Mutual Non-Disclosure Agreement**

18\. On February 14, 2022, Vantage and Lumenara executed a Mutual Non-Disclosure Agreement (the "MNDA"). Lumenara's CEO, Martin DeLuca, signed it for Lumenara. The MNDA is governed by Texas law (MNDA § 11.1).

19\. The MNDA defines "Confidential Information" to include trade secrets, designs, specifications, and technical data, and it expressly includes "substrate designs, lithographic mask layouts, doping profiles, thermal management schematics, \[and\] sensor architectures" (MNDA § 1.1). Information qualifies as Confidential Information "whether or not marked." A receiving party that claims an exclusion bears the burden of proving it by "clear and convincing documentary evidence" (MNDA § 2.2).

20\. Section 3.2 of the MNDA bars the receiving party from using the disclosing party's Confidential Information "for any purpose other than the Purpose," defined as "evaluating and pursuing a potential business relationship and joint development activities between the Parties" (MNDA § 1.4). It specifically bars use "for the development, manufacture, marketing, sale, or distribution of any product or service of the Receiving Party." Section 3.4 bars any attempt to derive the structure or underlying technology of Confidential Information. Under Section 4.2, the receiving party is responsible for any breach by its Representatives.

21\. The MNDA's term runs for three years from February 14, 2022. Its confidentiality and non-use obligations survive for five years after termination or expiration, and for as long as the information remains a trade secret (MNDA §§ 7.1, 7.3). Lumenara agreed that a breach may cause irreparable harm and that Vantage may seek injunctive relief without posting a bond (MNDA § 8.1).

**D. The Joint Development Agreement**

28\. On June 1, 2022, Vantage and Lumenara executed the Joint Development Agreement (the "JDA") governing Project Meridian. The project's goal was to develop a next-generation integrated sensor-optics module combining Vantage's SensorCore™ substrate-level sensor arrays with Lumenara's anti-reflective coatings for aerospace and defense applications (JDA § 2.1). Mr. DeLuca signed for Lumenara, fifteen days after writing the May 16, 2022 email. The JDA is governed by Texas law (JDA § 13.1).

29\. **Good-faith representation.** In Section 9.3, Lumenara represented and warranted to Vantage that it "is entering into this Agreement in good faith and with the genuine intent to perform its obligations hereunder and to devote the resources and efforts necessary to achieve the Project Objective." It further represented "that it has no present intention or plan to use the collaboration contemplated by this Agreement for any purpose other than the Project."

30\. **Background IP.** The JDA defines Vantage's Background IP to include "its proprietary substrate-level sensor array designs, including all lithographic mask layouts, doping profiles, thermal management schematics, and related design data," all patents relating to the SensorCore™ architecture, and all related trade secrets and know-how (JDA §§ 1.2, 7.1). Background IP remains the sole property of the owning party (JDA § 7.1). Foreground IP, which is jointly owned, is limited to IP conceived or developed "jointly by personnel of both Parties" under the Project, and "does not include any Background IP of either Party" (JDA §§ 1.6, 7.2). The SensorCore™ architecture predates the JDA and was not jointly developed. It is Vantage's Background IP.

31\. **Use restrictions.** Section 7.3(a) bars use of the other party's Background IP "for any purpose other than the performance of work under the Project" without specific, signed, written consent from an authorized officer. Section 7.3(b) specifically bars each party from (i) incorporating the other's Background IP "into any product . . . other than the Module"; (iv) reverse-engineering or otherwise attempting to derive the structure of the other's Background IP; and (v) using it "to develop, design, manufacture, or market any product or technology that competes with the other Party's products or technologies." Any violation of Section 7.3 is a material breach and is not subject to cure (JDA §§ 7.3(c), 10.4(a)). Sections 2.4 and 4.5 likewise restrict all Background IP, Confidential Information, and Portal materials to Project use and prohibit downloading Portal materials "for any purpose unrelated to the Project." Section 8.2(b) prohibits use of the other party's Confidential Information for "product development outside the scope of the Project."

32\. **Milestones.** Exhibit A and Section 6.2 set four Lumenara milestones:

> \(a\) **M1** — complete coating composition specifications, due September 30, 2022;
>
> \(b\) **M2** — fifty (50) prototype coated lens assemblies, due March 31, 2023. The JDA provides that "\[d\]elivery of fewer than the specified quantity of fifty (50) units, or delivery after the specified date of March 31, 2023, shall constitute a failure to achieve M2, regardless of the quality or conformance of the units actually delivered";
>
> \(c\) **M3** — a complete data package showing full MIL-STD-810H compliance across all applicable test methods (including Methods 501.7, 502.7, 503.7, 507.6, 514.8, and 516.8) for all M2 assemblies, due September 30, 2023. "Partial test data . . . shall not constitute achievement of M3"; and
>
> \(d\) **M4** — complete, production-ready coating process documentation sufficient for a reasonably skilled manufacturer to reproduce the process at production scale, due March 31, 2024.

33\. The parties agreed that "timely achievement of Milestones is essential" and that each party's performance depended on the other's (JDA § 6.1). Under Section 6.4(c), failure to achieve two or more milestones "shall constitute a material breach of this Agreement." Under Section 10.4(c), such failures permit termination "regardless of any attempted cure."

34\. **Remedies.** The JDA's limitation-of-liability provisions do not apply to claims for breach of Section 7 or 8, for misappropriation or unauthorized use of Background IP or trade secrets, for breach of the MNDA's confidentiality obligations, for patent infringement, or for "fraud, willful misconduct, intentional misrepresentation, or bad faith" (JDA §§ 11.1–11.3). The prevailing party in any action or counterclaim to enforce the JDA recovers its reasonable attorneys' fees, expert witness fees, consulting fees, and costs (JDA § 13.5). Each party must also indemnify the other for losses, including attorneys' fees, expert fees, and investigation costs, arising from its breach of any representation, warranty, or covenant (JDA § 12.1(a)). Lumenara agreed that breach of Sections 7 or 8 may cause irreparable harm warranting injunctive relief without bond (JDA § 13.4).

35\. **Survival.** The JDA's initial term expired on May 31, 2024. The parties did not extend it. Sections 1, 2.4, 7, 8, 9, 11, 12, 13, and 14 survive expiration (JDA § 10.5(b)), and expiration does not waive any remedy for breach (JDA § 10.6).

**I. Teardown Analysis Confirms Copying**

50\. On April 3, 2024, Vantage purchased a LumiSense 400 unit (serial no. LO-LS4-00247) from an authorized distributor, Photon Source Distribution, Inc., for \$12,400. Vantage retained Dr. Franklin Ayers of Ridgeline Technical Consulting LLC, an expert in semiconductor packaging with more than 25 years of experience, to perform a teardown and comparative analysis. Dr. Ayers used X-ray computed tomography, focused-ion-beam cross-sectioning, scanning electron microscopy, and energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy, and he maintained chain of custody throughout.

51\. The analysis found that the LumiSense 400 substrate matches SensorCore™ on every parameter examined:

> \(a\) exactly 14 copper-pillar micro-bump interconnect layers;
>
> \(b\) a measured micro-bump pitch of 44.8μm ± 0.3μm across more than 50 measurement points, against SensorCore's 45μm specification;
>
> \(c\) a radial-array thermal via pattern with 93% positional correspondence to Vantage's thermal via schematic, with via diameters of 75μm ± 2μm and depths of 200μm ± 5μm (SensorCore™ specification: 75μm and 200μm);
>
> \(d\) an identical Ti/Cu/Ni under-bump metallization stack, with layer thicknesses matching SensorCore™ specifications within measurement tolerance, and identical SnAg solder cap composition;
>
> \(e\) Vantage's distinctive "cross-hair with quadrant notch" registration alignment marks. These are a non-functional design choice that appears in Vantage's proprietary mask layouts and has no bearing on device performance; and
>
> \(f\) the "dog-leg" routing paths from Vantage's aerospace-optimized design in signal layers 3–5 and 10–12. These paths serve no independent purpose in a LIDAR application.

52\. Dr. Ayers concluded that the LumiSense 400 substrate is "substantially derived from Vantage's SensorCore™ architecture." He further concluded that the presence of non-functional artifacts shows that Lumenara directly used Vantage's electronic design files, and that the probability of independent development of all the matching features is less than one in ten million. Registration marks and routing artifacts are not disclosed in the '567 Patent. They could have come only from the confidential SensorCore™ design files that Lumenara obtained through the Portal.
