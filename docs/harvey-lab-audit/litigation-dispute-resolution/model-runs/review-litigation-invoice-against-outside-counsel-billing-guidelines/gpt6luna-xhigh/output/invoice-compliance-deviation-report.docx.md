# 1. Executive Summary

The submission has several facially material deviations from the Guidelines and engagement terms. Most significantly, the detailed invoice and LEDES export sum to \$514,206.14, while the invoice claims \$487,329.14—a \$26,877.00 mismatch before any compliance adjustments. The detail contains \$269,554.00 in fees versus the \$242,687.00 fee subtotal, and \$244,652.14 in expenses versus the \$244,642.14 expense subtotal.

The highest-confidence billing issues are: (i) \$51,907.50 of detailed fees billed by Timothy Kwan, a summer associate never approved, and Elaine Cho, whose project approval expired September 30; (ii) a further \$56,948.74 of facially non-reimbursable or unapproved expenses (after-hour word processing/admin support, technology surcharge, Forrest Data Solutions charges, and unapproved San Francisco local counsel); and (iii) other capped, unapproved or over-limit expense lines. Separate potential exposure exists for travel outside the Litigation Forum, excess deposition attendance, an over-cap internal strategy meeting, deficient narratives, and block billing. These figures overlap with other defects and should not be mechanically added into one net adjustment.

The arithmetic and compliance issues should be addressed first. The fee amounts below are calculated from the 139 itemized fee lines, not the inaccurate displayed subtotals; expense amounts are calculated from the 22 itemized expense lines.

Correct all fee lines to VIH-approved task codes or supply a documented VIH-approved exception. The activity codes are populated in the export.

## 5.4 Block billing and narrative minimum

Guidelines §4.2 prohibits combining distinct tasks and authorizes a 30% reduction of each affected entry. Clear examples include entry 008 (privilege-log analysis and drafting a challenge letter); 014 (expert material review, internal conference, opposing-counsel correspondence); 054 (deposition preparation/review plus client conference); 061–063 (preparation/attendance plus debrief, coordination, summary or follow-up work); 069 (transcript review plus legal research); 088 (motion drafting, research, memorandum, exhibits and prior-order review); 105 (transcript review plus client conference); and 133 (billing/status review plus budget conference). These examples are not exhaustive; review all semicolon- and conjunction-separated entries for distinct tasks. Apply the 30% remedy to block-billed entries, without stacking it on charges disallowed in full.

The separate minimum-narrative rule in §4.1 requires at least 30 words per time entry. Only entry 008 meets that threshold (36 words). Entries 001–007 and 009–139 (138 entries) do not; their detailed line charges total \$266,794.00. Require revised, specific narratives identifying work and relevant materials/participants. The Guidelines permit reduction or disallowance for inadequate descriptions but prescribe no flat percentage for short narratives; the \$266,794.00 is gross affected billing, not an additional automatic write-off.

## 5.5 Prohibited administrative/billing time

# 7. Budget Controls and Matter Status

The annual budget is \$2,800,000; 75% is \$2,100,000. The invoice’s Matter Status tab reports year-to-date spend through October of \$2,230,941.14 (79.7%), \$130,941.14 above the 75% threshold. Section 10.1 requires written notice within five business days of the date cumulative invoiced fees and expenses reached \$2.1 million, including work completed, remaining tasks and a revised year-end estimate. No such contemporaneous notice or revised forecast is in the approval log or transmittal materials. Request the dated notice and reforecast; the present record shows a documentation gap, not conclusively when the threshold was crossed.

The reported invoice total of \$487,329.14 exceeds the \$350,000 monthly threshold by \$137,329.14. The transmittal identifies activity drivers (the Nexon inspection, depositions, expert preparation and motion work) and anticipated work, but does not provide the separate contemporaneous written explanation required by the engagement letter §7 and Guidelines §10.1 addressing why elevated spend was necessary and the expected spending trajectory. Request that explanation and a month-by-month forecast. Note that the underlying line detail totals even higher, at \$514,206.14.

The status report says fact discovery closed September 15, yet October time and expenses include three depositions and a third-party inspection. The supplied record does not include a court order extending fact discovery. This is not, by itself, a billing-rule violation; request the relevant case-management order or explanation to confirm the work was authorized and necessary.
