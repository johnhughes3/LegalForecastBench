# Executive assessment

The central defense is factual, not semantic: Prismavale admits the MSA, shipment dates/quantities, specifications, COA requirements, and that each COA reported conformance at the time of testing; it denies that either batch was out of specification when shipped, that a COA was inaccurate, that Prismavale caused the later test findings or recalls, and that Greenleaf’s claimed losses are proven. Discovery must test the reliability and chain of custody of Greenleaf’s retained-sample testing and the possibility of post-delivery contamination, while candidly investigating the highly adverse alleged quality records—CAR-2024-019, the February 2024 cleaning validation event, the June 10, 2024 environmental alert, release decisions, and the timing/content of root-cause investigations.

There are two separate incidents and different alleged mechanisms (1,4-dioxane in SB-102; Pseudomonas / elevated bioburden in PS-302). Avoid collapsing them into a single generalized quality narrative. The most promising defenses and leverage points are: (1) origin and timing of contamination; (2) actual testing and release records; (3) Greenleaf’s handling, storage, manufacturing, sampling, and recall decisions; (4) notice and the MSA’s 14-day inspection/rejection clause, particularly its application to latent defects; (5) whether PS-302 was included in the pre-suit notice and mediation; and (6) the characterization and contractual treatment of each damages category, including Section 12.4.

The attached response set is a counsel draft, not a representation that Prismavale has completed a reasonable inquiry, searched every source, or made a production. No response should be served until the named custodians and business owners verify facts, counsel tests factual positions against underlying records, privilege is reviewed, and production commitments are achievable. The answer was filed April 7, 2025; supplementation under Rule 26(e) remains important.

# 1. Case posture and governing discovery constraints

- Plaintiff alleges two contaminated shipments under the January 15, 2020 MSA and approximately \$14.7 million in damages: \$2.3 million recall costs; \$8.9 million lost retail revenue; \$2.5 million brand/reputation harm; and \$1.0 million increased insurance/regulatory costs. Defendant denies breach, contamination at shipment, causation, and damages, and asserts defenses concerning rejection/notice, pre-suit mediation as to PS-302, a consequential-damages cap, post-delivery conditions, mitigation, comparative fault, and speculative damages.

- Fact discovery closes October 31, 2025. Plaintiff served 25 RFAs and 30 RFPs on May 5, 2025. The Joint Discovery Stipulation provides 35 calendar days for written responses; on that assumption, responses and production are due June 9, 2025. Confirm actual service date and docket entry/order status before calendaring or asserting the extension.

- The stipulated limits are 30 RFAs, 50 RFPs inclusive of distinct subparts, and 25 interrogatories per side. The RFAs are numbered 1–25, but Nos. 14, 15, and 16 contain 3, 3, and 4 distinct factual propositions, respectively. If each discrete subpart is counted separately, the set comprises 32 matters. Section 2(a) permits the receiving party to count compound RFAs as multiple requests at its discretion, but does not automatically disqualify a request. Preserve a narrow numerical-limit objection, respond on the merits to avoid deemed-admission exposure, and promptly meet and confer.

- RFP numerical-limit review: the 30 numbered requests appear below the 50-request ceiling even counting the most clearly distinct categories in Nos. 4 and 15 separately. Maintain an internal count under Section 2(b), which counts only subparts seeking documents on a subject distinct from the other subparts; do not make an unsupported across-the-board numerical objection.

- Fact discovery must be served sufficiently early for responses and depositions to be completed by October 31. Written discovery responses cannot substitute for a timely third-party subpoena or deposition of Thornfield Analytical Labs, Greenleaf personnel, or any carrier with relevant records.

# 3. Immediate response and factual verification plan

Before the June 9, 2025 assumed deadline, complete these steps in parallel. A response that says “after reasonable inquiry” is proper only after an actual reasonable inquiry of information known or readily obtainable; counsel should not convert an unreviewed allegation into an admission, denial, or unsupported inability to answer.

1.  Collect the operative MSA, Exhibit A, purchase orders, shipping/receiving records, invoices, all COAs and supporting test records. Confirm specification limits and the exact Section 8.1, 12.4, and 14.3 language from the executed agreement.

2.  Conduct privileged interviews of Raymond Ortiz, Claudia Ferris, Harold Breckenridge, Timothy Wardell, the SB-102 and PS-302 batch owners, Line 3 sanitation/production personnel, environmental monitoring personnel, the COA signatory, and document/ERP administrators. Keep counsel notes and legal analysis separate from ordinary-course records.

3.  Review CAR-2024-019, EML-2024-06-10, complete batch files, relevant SOPs, QA hold/release criteria, raw data and full investigation files. Identify whether names, dates, batches, and conclusions match Plaintiff’s allegations. Determine which facts can be admitted, denied, or answered only after inquiry.

4.  Confirm receipt and contents of Greenleaf notices, Thornfield reports and samples; exact consumer-complaint notice; product recall communications; mediation notice/response/submissions; and the date PS-302 was first raised. Do not assume complaint allegations are established facts.

5.  Verify insurance coverage and Section 12.4 purchase-price calculations from policies, declarations, endorsements, invoices, and ledger data. Check whether the calendar-year 2024 coverage request asks for product-liability coverage specifically or a broader CGL policy.

6.  Map responsive sources and custodians; issue/refresh legal hold, suspend deletion and auto-delete, preserve email, Teams/Slack/texts, ERP, QA/QMS, lab systems, shared drives, mobile devices, backup sources as warranted, and third-party materials within control. Record collection dates and custodians.

7.  Finalize RFA responses request by request. Separate partial admissions from denials; identify exactly what is admitted in compound requests. For an inability to answer, state reasonable inquiry and why information known or readily obtainable remains insufficient. Have client representative review verification of facts, then counsel sign under Rule 26(g).

8.  Finalize RFP objections with concrete limits and production commitments. Identify records held by carriers/contractors and assess Prismavale’s practical or legal control, rather than categorically treating all third-party records as outside control. Confirm that promised sources can be searched and the June 9 production schedule is feasible.

# 4. RFA response posture

- Admit contract existence, term, contractual specifications/COA obligation, shipment facts that records confirm, and the fact that the COAs reported conformance. Those admissions match the Answer and narrow undisputed issues without admitting actual product condition.

- Deny Requests 9–11, 19–23 insofar as they ask Prismavale to concede accuracy of Greenleaf’s test results, nonconformity at shipment, causation, merchantability, or damages. Where a report’s contents are admitted, distinguish “the report states X” from “X is accurate” or “X existed at shipment.”

- For RFAs 12–13, do not conflate the date Prismavale learned of a complaint with whether notice was “reasonable” or satisfied MSA §8.1. Admit a supported receipt/timing fact, then clearly state the disputed characterization and the contractual defense.

- For compound RFAs 14–16, respond to every subpart separately even while preserving the 32-versus-30 limit issue. If the internal records establish an adverse fact, do not evade it through a compound objection. Address exactly whether a deviation occurred, its classification, batch/line relation, investigation timing, and who approved release.

- For RFA 18, admit the February 18 mediation occurred; maintain the Answer’s position that it addressed SB-102 but not PS-302, subject to checking the original notice and mediation record. For RFA 25, admit the contract language only; do not admit enforceability, application to each damage category, or the amount of any recoverable cap.

- RFA requests do not generally require an oath under Rule 36; they must be signed by counsel under Rule 26(g). The requesting set’s “under oath” instruction does not change the Rules. Use a verification only if counsel elects or a specific rule/order requires it.

Produce separable nonconfidential notices and administrative documents if appropriate; withhold only material protected by an applicable privilege, work product, statute, court order, or agreement, and describe the basis accurately.
