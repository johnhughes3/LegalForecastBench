# I. THE PARTIES AND THE PROCUREMENT

1.  Ridgeline is a Pennsylvania corporation with its principal place of business at 1400 Industrial Parkway, Butler, Pennsylvania 16001. It manufactures precision-machined industrial components for aerospace and automotive original equipment manufacturers at three facilities located in Butler, Pennsylvania; Erie, Pennsylvania; and Youngstown, Ohio. App. Ex. 1 (Master Services Agreement (“MSA”)), preamble & Recitals; *id.* § 2.1.

2.  Apex is a Delaware corporation headquartered at 2100 Cornerstone Drive, Suite 400, Reston, Virginia. Its primary line of business is implementing the Stratos ERP platform for mid-market manufacturers. At the time of the Ridgeline engagement it had approximately 140 employees and had completed approximately 14 Stratos ERP implementations. Kresch Dep. 7:3–8:3 (App. Ex. 7); App. Ex. 1, preamble.

3.  Jordan Kresch is Apex’s Chief Executive Officer and co-founder. Tara Bellingham has been Apex’s Vice President of Sales since 2019. Ryan Ostroff was the Apex Lead Project Manager assigned to the Ridgeline engagement from March 14, 2022 until January 12, 2023, and reported to Mr. Kresch. Kresch Dep. 7:3–5 (App. Ex. 7); Bellingham Dep. 8:3–7 (App. Ex. 6); Ostroff Dep. 14:3–16 (App. Ex. 5).

4.  Paul Szymanski, Ridgeline’s Vice President of Information Technology, drafted Ridgeline’s Request for Proposal for an ERP implementation (the “RFP”), which was issued to five vendors on January 10, 2022. Mr. Szymanski evaluated the responses, conducted reference checks, and recommended Apex to Ridgeline’s Chief Executive Officer, Margaret Calloway, and Chief Financial Officer, Dennis Hargrove. Szymanski Dep. 6:3–17 (App. Ex. 8); App. Ex. 1, Recitals.

10. The Proposal listed three client references. None of the three involved Teamcenter integration or AS9100D compliance configuration work. App. Ex. 2 at APEX-000185–187; Szymanski Dep. 14:1–14 (App. Ex. 8).

Szymanski Dep. 15:5–11 (App. Ex. 8); Bellingham Dep. 9:9–18 (App. Ex. 6) (admitting she “referenced them as clients where we had Teamcenter experience”).

# III. THE REPRESENTATIONS WERE FALSE

18. Before the Ridgeline engagement, Apex had never performed a Siemens Teamcenter integration for any client. Ostroff Dep. 25:12–21 (App. Ex. 5); Bellingham Dep. 20:4–9 (App. Ex. 6) (“\[W\]e never actually performed a Teamcenter integration for anyone before Ridgeline.”).

19. Apex did not perform a Teamcenter integration for Corridor Metals. Corridor Metals used SAP, not Stratos, and Apex did no Teamcenter work for it. Ostroff Dep. 23:14–24:11 (App. Ex. 5); Bellingham Dep. 18:12–19:11 (App. Ex. 6).

20. Apex did not perform a Teamcenter integration for PrimeTech Industries. Apex’s PrimeTech engagement was a consulting assessment involving, at most, early-stage scoping of what a Teamcenter integration might look like. Ostroff Dep. 24:12–25:10 (App. Ex. 5); Bellingham Dep. 19:12–20:3 (App. Ex. 6).

21. Asked whether Slide 7 was accurate, Ms. Bellingham testified: “I may have overstated our experience,” and “it wasn’t entirely accurate.” She also conceded that Apex never performed a Teamcenter integration for either client she identified to Mr. Szymanski on February 14, 2022. Bellingham Dep. 20:10–21:20 (App. Ex. 6). Mr. Ostroff testified that the listings of both Corridor Metals and PrimeTech on Slide 7 were inaccurate. Ostroff Dep. 24:9–11, 25:8–10 (App. Ex. 5).

22. On February 22, 2022, when the Capability Summary was sent, Apex had one AS9100D-certified employee on staff — Gerald Frisk — not six. Kresch Dep. 15:5–16:11 (App. Ex. 7); *see also* Bellingham Dep. 33:5–34:11 (App. Ex. 6) (identifying only Mr. Frisk and stating she “can’t confirm there were six certified specialists at that time”).

23. Mr. Kresch testified that the “6 certified specialists” statement “was aspirational — we were planning to hire more AS9100D people,” and agreed that when the slide was sent Apex “had one certified specialist, not six.” Kresch Dep. 16:1–11 (App. Ex. 7). Ms. Bellingham testified that “the intent was to reflect our planned team capacity.” Bellingham Dep. 34:4–11 (App. Ex. 6).

24. Neither Apex witness could identify any additional AS9100D-certified specialist hired before Mr. Frisk’s departure. Kresch Dep. 16:12–17 (App. Ex. 7); Bellingham Dep. 34:1–3, 34:16–20 (App. Ex. 6).

# VI. THE MASTER SERVICES AGREEMENT

34. The MSA was signed by Margaret A. Calloway for Ridgeline and by Jordan Kresch for Apex on February 28, 2022, and is stated to be entered into and effective as of March 1, 2022. App. Ex. 1, preamble, § 1.5 & signature page.

35. The MSA’s Recitals state that “Apex represents that it is an experienced ERP implementation services provider specializing in mid-market manufacturing companies and that it possesses the requisite skill, experience, personnel, and technical expertise to perform the implementation services described herein, including implementation of the Stratos Platform in multi-facility manufacturing environments.” The Recitals also identify the RFP and the Proposal. App. Ex. 1, Recitals.

36. Section 2.2(c) requires Apex to integrate the Stratos Platform with Ridgeline’s existing Siemens PLM Teamcenter system (version 13.2), “including bidirectional data exchange for engineering change orders, part specifications, revision control, and released-design synchronization,” enabling “automated propagation of engineering changes . . . without manual re-entry of data.” App. Ex. 1 § 2.2(c).

37. Section 2.2(d) requires Apex to configure the AS9100D aerospace quality-management compliance module, “including lot tracking, serial number tracking, non-conformance reporting (NCR), corrective and preventive action (CAPA) workflows, first article inspection (FAI) management, supplier quality management, and full aerospace traceability functionality meeting the requirements of AS9100D:2016.” App. Ex. 1 § 2.2(d); *see also id.* Ex. A, Phase 2 Deliverables 2–3.

38. Section 2.3 provides that Apex “shall not reassign, remove, or replace any key project personnel (including the project manager . . .) without providing Ridgeline at least fifteen (15) business days’ prior written notice and shall ensure that any replacement personnel possess qualifications and experience at least equivalent to the personnel being replaced.” App. Ex. 1 § 2.3.

39. The MSA fixes three milestone completion dates: Phase 1 (Design and Configuration) by July 15, 2022; Phase 2 (Integration and Testing, including the Teamcenter Integration and the AS9100D module) by November 30, 2022; and Phase 3 (Go-Live) by March 31, 2023. App. Ex. 1 §§ 3.2–3.4.

40. Section 3.5, captioned “Time Is of the Essence,” provides that “timely completion of the Project is material to this Agreement,” that “\[t\]he milestone dates . . . represent firm commitments by Apex, subject only to adjustment by mutual written agreement in the form of a Change Order,” and that “\[n\]o unilateral extension by either Party shall be effective to modify any milestone date.” App. Ex. 1 § 3.5.

41. Section 3.6 requires Apex, if it anticipates missing a milestone, to “promptly notify Ridgeline in writing.” It provides that Ridgeline-caused delays extend a milestone only if “(i) Apex gives Ridgeline timely written notice specifying the nature of Ridgeline’s failure and its impact on the schedule, and (ii) Apex uses commercially reasonable efforts to mitigate.” App. Ex. 1 § 3.6.

42. The MSA provides for a fixed fee of \$2,850,000, payable in five milestone installments: \$570,000 at signing; \$712,500 on written sign-off on Phase 1; \$855,000 on written sign-off on Phase 2; \$427,500 on written sign-off on UAT; and \$285,000 on Go-Live. App. Ex. 1 §§ 4.1–4.2 & Ex. B.

43. Section 4.4 makes each milestone payment “conditioned upon Apex’s completion of the corresponding Deliverables to Ridgeline’s reasonable satisfaction, as evidenced by Ridgeline’s written sign-off,” and provides that payment “shall not constitute acceptance” or “a waiver of any deficiency, defect, non-conformance, or breach,” absent a signed writing specifically referencing § 4.4. App. Ex. 1 § 4.4.

44. Section 4.5 provides that payment “accompanied by a contemporaneous written reservation of rights or notation of protest, shall not constitute acceptance, waiver, release, or estoppel of any claim.” Section 14.3 provides that no waiver is effective unless in a signed writing. App. Ex. 1 §§ 4.5, 14.3.

45. Section 4.7 provides that, absent an executed Change Order, Apex is not entitled to any compensation in excess of the fixed fee, which “encompasses all labor, materials, travel, and other costs required for Apex to perform the Services in their entirety.” Section 6.3 provides that neither party is obligated to accept a proposed Change Order and that, absent agreement, “Apex shall continue to perform the Services as originally specified.” App. Ex. 1 §§ 4.7, 6.3.

46. Section 5.1 contains Apex’s express warranties that: (a) Apex “possesses the requisite skill, expertise, and experience to perform the Services in a professional and workmanlike manner consistent with generally accepted industry standards for ERP implementation services provided to mid-market manufacturing companies”; (b) the Services “shall conform in all material respects to the specifications, requirements, and functional descriptions set forth in this Agreement” and Exhibit A; and (e) “\[a\]ll personnel assigned to the Project shall possess the qualifications, training, professional certifications, and experience necessary to perform their assigned tasks competently.” App. Ex. 1 § 5.1(a), (b), (e).

47. Section 8.2 permits termination for cause on thirty days’ written notice of an uncured material breach, and provides: “For the avoidance of doubt, a response to a notice of breach that conditions cure upon the non-breaching Party’s agreement to modify the terms of this Agreement, approve additional fees, or approve an extension of the project timeline shall not constitute cure of the identified breach.” App. Ex. 1 § 8.2.

48. Sections 8.4(d) and 8.5 preserve all rights and remedies for pre-termination breaches. Section 8.4(b) obligates Ridgeline upon termination to pay only “amounts due and owing for Services properly performed and accepted by Ridgeline,” subject to setoff and recoupment. App. Ex. 1 §§ 8.4(b), (d), 8.5.

49. Section 11.2 caps “either Party’s aggregate liability arising out of or related to this Agreement” at “the total fees paid or payable under this Agreement (i.e., . . . \$2,850,000).” App. Ex. 1 § 11.2.

50. Section 14.1 provides: “This Agreement, together with all Statements of Work and Change Orders, constitutes the entire agreement between the parties and supersedes all prior negotiations, representations, warranties, commitments, offers, and agreements, whether written or oral. Each Party acknowledges that it has not relied upon any statement, representation, promise, or agreement not expressly set forth in this Agreement in deciding to enter into this Agreement.” App. Ex. 1 § 14.1.

51. The MSA is governed by Pennsylvania law. App. Ex. 1 § 13.1.

# VII. APEX’S PERFORMANCE

## A. Phase 1

52. Project kickoff occurred on March 14, 2022. Ostroff Dep. 14:9–10 (App. Ex. 5); App. Ex. 1 § 3.1.

53. Gerald Frisk left Apex in April 2022, approximately one month after kickoff. He had done “some initial consulting on the AS9100D configuration” and “wasn’t a full-time resource on the project.” After his departure, Apex had no AS9100D-certified specialist assigned to the Ridgeline project, and Ms. Bellingham is not aware that Apex filled the position. Kresch Dep. 17:1–13 (App. Ex. 7); Bellingham Dep. 34:13–20 (App. Ex. 6).

54. On May 3, 2022, Mr. Ostroff wrote in an Apex internal Slack message: “We have zero experience with Teamcenter. I’ve been Googling the API docs for two weeks. We need to bring in a subcontractor or this is going to blow up.” The same message states: “We need someone who has actually built one of these integrations before, because right now we are flying blind,” and “Don’t raise this with the client yet — I want to figure out our options first.” App. Ex. 9 (APEX-00004782); Ostroff Dep. 38:1–18 (App. Ex. 5).

55. As produced by Apex, the May 3, 2022 message was posted to the private Slack channel “#ridgeline-project,” whose listed members included Ms. Bellingham and Dana Cho. App. Ex. 9 (APEX-00004782).

56. Apex never brought in a Teamcenter subcontractor, and Mr. Ostroff never shared his concerns about Apex’s lack of Teamcenter experience with anyone at Ridgeline. Ostroff Dep. 39:1–3, 40:1–6 (App. Ex. 5). Asked whether the message reflected Apex’s capabilities when the Proposal was submitted, Mr. Ostroff testified: “we didn’t have the experience we said we did.” *Id.* 39:4–12.

57. On June 10, 2022, Mr. Ostroff emailed Mr. Szymanski (copying Ms. Bellingham) that “Phase 1 is on track for completion by end of July,” that the Teamcenter integration architecture was “\[p\]rogressing well,” and “I don’t anticipate any issues meeting our Phase 1 milestone targets.” App. Ex. 10 (APEX-00005114).

58. When he sent that email, Phase 1 was “\[n\]ot really” on track; Apex “w\[as\] behind” specifically on the Teamcenter integration design, and Apex’s lack of prior Teamcenter experience “was a factor.” Ostroff Dep. 40:12–41:15 (App. Ex. 5). Mr. Szymanski relied on the June 10 status report. Szymanski Dep. 36:10–17 (App. Ex. 8).

59. Phase 1 was not signed off until October 3, 2022, approximately eleven weeks after the July 15, 2022 contractual date. The primary cause of the delay was the Teamcenter integration design. Ostroff Dep. 58:1–11 (App. Ex. 5); App. Ex. 11.

60. Ridgeline’s technical requirements were “fairly standard for that type of manufacturing environment.” The Teamcenter work proved more complicated than Apex anticipated “\[b\]ecause we had never done it before.” Ostroff Dep. 59:1–10 (App. Ex. 5).

61. During Phase 1, “Ridgeline was pretty responsive. The delays were on our side.” Any minor Ridgeline delays “weren’t the reason we missed the Phase 1 deadline. The problem was the Teamcenter integration.” Ostroff Dep. 59:11–14, 60:8–10 (App. Ex. 5); *accord* Szymanski Dep. 37:4–8 (App. Ex. 8).

62. On October 3, 2022, Mr. Szymanski authorized the \$712,500 Phase 1 payment by email copied to Apex’s CEO, stating: “We are paying this milestone to keep the project moving, but we reserve all rights regarding the delay and the incomplete Teamcenter integration design,” and that the payment “should not be construed as acceptance of the Phase 1 deliverables, satisfaction with Apex’s performance, or a waiver of any of Ridgeline’s claims, rights, or remedies.” App. Ex. 11 (RMC-00008231); Szymanski Dep. 27:1–29:3 (App. Ex. 8).

63. Ridgeline has paid Apex a total of \$1,282,500 under the MSA: \$570,000 at signing and \$712,500 for Phase 1. App. Ex. 17 (RMC-00013502); App. Ex. 16 (APEX-00011234) (Apex asserting that the remaining \$1,567,500 is unpaid).

## C. The AS9100D Module

72. By memorandum dated April 18, 2023, Ridgeline’s Quality Director, Anita Flores, documented that seven of twelve critical AS9100D traceability requirements in the module configured by Apex were non-functional or fundamentally misconfigured: (1) lot tracking and serialization (multi-plant lot genealogy broken); (2) non-conformance reporting (no Material Review Board disposition routing); (3) first article inspection (no AS9102 formatting); (4) supplier quality traceability; (5) calibration management; (6) process change control; and (7) customer-specific requirements flowdown. App. Ex. 18 (Flores Mem.) § 3.

73. Ms. Flores raised these deficiencies with Ms. Cho on March 7, March 22, and April 4, 2023. Apex delivered no remediation or remediation plan, and Ms. Cho could not identify any Apex employee with AS9100D expertise assigned to the project. App. Ex. 18 § 4.

74. In its May 15, 2023 response to Ridgeline’s notice of breach, Apex “acknowledge\[d\] that certain AS9100D configuration items remain in progress.” App. Ex. 16 (APEX-00011234).

## D. Uncontroverted Expert Testimony

75. Ridgeline’s technical expert, Marcus Tran, has completed four Stratos–Teamcenter integrations and is a certified AS9100D lead auditor. App. Ex. 19 (Tran Rep.) § II.A. Apex did not move to exclude his testimony. *Id.*

76. Mr. Tran opines that Apex’s Phase 1 design proposed a “direct database-level integration” between Stratos and Teamcenter — a deprecated and unsupported approach that no competent Teamcenter integrator would have proposed after 2018 — and that Apex’s design documents contained fundamental errors regarding Teamcenter’s data model, item revisions, BOM structures, and change-management workflows. App. Ex. 19, Opinion 1.

77. Mr. Tran opines that Apex configured lot tracking using a “batch-level” approach rather than the “serialized lot” tracking AS9100D aerospace traceability requires, that the NCR module lacked the required linkage to corrective-action workflows, and that these were “architectural choices that reflect a lack of understanding of what AS9100D requires,” not bugs. App. Ex. 19, Opinion 2.

78. Mr. Tran opines that Apex’s performance did not meet “generally accepted industry standards” for ERP implementation within the meaning of MSA § 5.1(a) and was not “professional and workmanlike.” App. Ex. 19, Opinion 3.

79. Mr. Tran found that, of more than forty Apex data requests, Ridgeline’s responses were late on two occasions, by three to five business days, with “no material impact on the project timeline.” App. Ex. 19 § II.E.

80. Apex’s expert, Dr. Raj Anand, does not address MSA § 5.1, does not offer any opinion that Apex’s performance met generally accepted industry standards, and does not respond to Mr. Tran’s findings regarding the Teamcenter design or the AS9100D configuration. App. Ex. 21 (Anand Rep.) *passim*.

81. Dr. Anand attributes, at most, three to four weeks of Phase 1 delay to Ridgeline (two late data deliveries and three rescheduled workshops). App. Ex. 21, Opinion 2. # VIII. NOTICE OF BREACH AND TERMINATION

82. On May 1, 2023, Ridgeline served written notice of material breach under MSA § 8.2 by certified mail and email, identifying four breaches: (1) failure to complete Phase 2; (2) failure to deliver a functional Teamcenter integration; (3) failure to properly configure the AS9100D modules; and (4) staffing the project with unqualified personnel. The notice gave Apex thirty days to cure. App. Ex. 15 (RMC-00012876); Szymanski Dep. 46:1–10 (App. Ex. 8).

83. Apex’s May 15, 2023 response disputed the breaches, stated that Apex was “prepared to cure any perceived deficiencies within sixty (60) days,” and stated that Apex’s “willingness and ability to dedicate the substantial additional resources necessary . . . is contingent upon Ridgeline’s approval of the revised scope and fee structure previously proposed by Mr. Kresch on February 6, 2023.” It requested that the cure period be extended to sixty days. App. Ex. 16 (APEX-00011234); Szymanski Dep. 47:1–4 (App. Ex. 8).

84. Apex did not cure the identified breaches within thirty days. By letter dated June 1, 2023, Ridgeline terminated the MSA under § 8.2. App. Ex. 17 (RMC-00013502); Szymanski Dep. 47:5–12 (App. Ex. 8).
