# 1. Executive findings

The best-supported baseline is the February 3, 2025 Engagement Letter (EL), supplemented by the detailed Matter Plan (MP), the March 28, 2025 Revision 1 CPSC Addendum (MP-R1), and Pinnacle’s Outside Counsel Guidelines (OCGs). The documents describe one defense engagement for two clients and two proceedings, with seven work streams after WS-4 was added. Several important details remain unresolved or internally inconsistent; they should not be treated as settled solely because they appear in a later summary or budget workbook.

- Core litigation scope: lead defense of Pinnacle Industrial Holdings, Inc. and PHS in the HX-9000 products-liability MDL and the parallel Cuyahoga County case, through trial; Phase 0 is closed and excluded from the \$4.25 million aggregate fee budget.

- Later product claims: EX-6’s MDL-consolidation exception does not line up with WS-1’s “as currently constituted” limit. The MP expressly says later consolidated non-HX-9000 claims are not authorized until review and a scope amendment. January emails record this as open, not resolved.

- Regulatory work: WS-4 is a post-engagement \$320,000 work stream and is described as coordinating counsel only, but its task list includes CPSC submissions, VCAP materials, and meetings. Meridian is primary counsel; the distinction between coordination/support and direct representation/submission needs to be made operationally precise.

- Budget: the \$4.25 million aggregate professional-fee control total is consistent, but detailed stream allocations differ between MP and workbook. Disbursements differ by \$20,000: EL says \$1.35 million / \$780,000 experts; MP and workbook say \$1.33 million / \$760,000 experts. Contract reviewer cost classification is also inconsistent.

- Success fee: 7.5% of savings below \$8.5 million is stated, but “Actual Aggregate Payments” has no definition. Emails document different proposed positions (indemnity-only versus broader enterprise payments), and the final documents do not resolve them.

- Effectiveness / priority: the extracted EL and MP show blank signature lines; MP-R1 prints signatory names and dates but also shows blank signature lines. Verify executed counterparts, especially because WS-4 exceeds the EL’s 5% threshold for a newly added stream.

## 3.5 Success fee and termination economics

Success fee formula: if all claims within the engagement are finally resolved for aggregate payments at or below \$8,500,000, W&C earns 7.5% × (\$8,500,000 − actual aggregate payments); no fee if aggregate payments are at or above \$8.5m, and no negative fee/refund. Example in EL/MP: \$6m resolution → \$187,500. Payable within 60 calendar days after final resolution of all claims (final non-appealable judgment, executed settlement, or dismissal with prejudice, whichever occurs last). EL says fee is additional to hourly fees/disbursements.

- Termination: either party may terminate on 30 calendar days’ written notice; files/work product due within 15 business days; accrued fees payable within 45 calendar days after final invoice. EL requires final invoice within 30 calendar days after termination. MP directs the Firm upon notice to stop discretionary tasks, complete only tasks needed to avoid prejudice, and support transition to successor counsel.

- Success fee forfeited on termination for cause or W&C voluntary withdrawal. Client termination without cause preserves only a pro-rata fee, calculated as professional fees billed through termination ÷ \$4.25m, and payable only on final resolution using the same formula.

- Wind-down fee: 2% of aggregate professional fees billed if Client terminates without cause after fees are “in excess of” \$2m; EL says payable within 45 days. MP paraphrases the trigger as “\$2m or more.”
