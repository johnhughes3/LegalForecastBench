# <u>SECTION 1 — JURISDICTIONAL STATEMENT (Proposed Joint)</u>

**Subject-matter jurisdiction.** The Court has subject-matter jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. § 1332(a)(1). Complete diversity exists between the parties, and the amount in controversy exceeds \$75,000, exclusive of interest and costs. *See* SJ Op. § III (Court "exercises diversity jurisdiction pursuant to 28 U.S.C. § 1332").

**Citizenship of Plaintiff.** Ridgeline is a corporation incorporated under the laws of the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania. Its principal place of business is 4500 Whitestown Road, Butler, Pennsylvania 16001. For diversity purposes, Ridgeline is a citizen of Pennsylvania only. 28 U.S.C. § 1332(c)(1).

**Citizenship of Defendant.** Corbin Supply is a Delaware limited liability company with its principal offices at 1200 Tryon Tower, Suite 800, Charlotte, North Carolina 28202. For diversity purposes, a limited liability company takes the citizenship of each of its members. *Zambelli Fireworks Mfg. Co. v. Wood*, 592 F.3d 412, 420 (3d Cir. 2010). The parties will state in this Section the identity and citizenship of each member of Corbin Supply, including Theodore Corbin III, its Managing Member, and will confirm that no member is a citizen of Pennsylvania. **\[Defendant to provide its members’ identities and citizenship. Ridgeline asks Defendant to confirm that no member, including any member tracing through upstream entities, is a citizen of Pennsylvania.\]**

**Amount in controversy.** Ridgeline seeks compensatory damages well above \$75,000. The undisputed Minimum Annual Purchase Commitment ("MAPC") shortfalls alone total \$4,010,000. Ridgeline’s damages claims are described in Sections 3 and 5.

**Venue.** Venue is proper in this District under 28 U.S.C. § 1391(b)(2). A substantial part of the events giving rise to the claims occurred here: the November 9, 2020 presentation at Ridgeline’s Butler headquarters, the negotiation and execution of the EDA, and Ridgeline’s performance from Butler County. In addition, EDA § 14.5 provides that each party "irrevocably and unconditionally submits to the exclusive jurisdiction of the federal and state courts located in the Western District of Pennsylvania" and waives any venue or forum non conveniens objection.

# <u>SECTION 2 — STIPULATED FACTS (Proposed by Plaintiff)</u>

Ridgeline proposes the following stipulated facts. Each is drawn from the EDA, from Defendant’s admissions in its Responses to Plaintiff’s Statement of Material Facts (Def.’s Resp. to Pl.’s SMF ¶¶ 7, 16, 18–22), from the parties’ prior Joint Stipulation (ECF No. 71), from the Court’s November 15, 2024 ruling, or from sworn testimony that no party has contradicted. Ridgeline further asks that Stipulations 22 through 26 be read to the jury at the start of trial as facts established by the Court.

### A. The Parties

1\. Plaintiff Ridgeline Manufacturing, Inc. is a Pennsylvania corporation formed in 1987 with its principal place of business at 4500 Whitestown Road, Butler, Pennsylvania 16001. It manufactures precision hydraulic cylinders, control valves, and pump assemblies for heavy-equipment original equipment manufacturers ("OEMs"). It employs approximately 310 people at two manufacturing facilities in Butler County, Pennsylvania.

2\. Margaret ("Peggy") Hausman is Ridgeline’s founder and Chief Executive Officer. David Rinaldi has been Ridgeline’s General Counsel since 2015.

3\. Defendant Corbin Supply Group, LLC is a Delaware limited liability company with its principal offices at 1200 Tryon Tower, Suite 800, Charlotte, North Carolina 28202. It is a national distributor of industrial components. Its fiscal year 2023 revenue was approximately \$285 million.

4\. Theodore ("Ted") Corbin III has been Corbin Supply’s Managing Member since 2012. Marcus Findlay has been Corbin Supply’s Vice President of Sales since 2018 and reports directly to Mr. Corbin III. Janine Koppel is Corbin Supply’s General Counsel.

### B. Pre-Contract Communications

5\. The parties negotiated the terms of their distribution relationship from approximately September 2020 through February 2021. Mr. Corbin III was Corbin Supply’s lead negotiator.

6\. On October 14, 2020, Mr. Corbin III sent Ms. Hausman an email (PX-002), copied to Mr. Findlay and Ms. Koppel, stating that "Corbin Supply has firm relationships with over 300 OEM service centers across the twelve-state southeastern territory."

7\. On November 9, 2020, a Corbin Supply team led by Mr. Corbin III delivered a PowerPoint presentation (PX-003) to Ridgeline’s leadership at Ridgeline’s headquarters in Butler, Pennsylvania.

8\. On December 3, 2020, Mr. Corbin III signed and sent Ms. Hausman a letter on Corbin Supply letterhead (PX-004). The letter proposed the MAPC schedule later incorporated into the EDA.

9\. On February 12, 2021, Mr. Corbin III sent Ms. Hausman an email (PX-006), copied to Ms. Koppel, Mr. Rinaldi, and Mr. Findlay, stating that the draft EDA was "substantially acceptable to Corbin Supply."

10\. On January 15, 2021, Mr. Findlay wrote an internal Corbin Supply memorandum (PX-007) addressed to "Senior Leadership." The Senior Leadership distribution list included Mr. Corbin III, Ms. Koppel, and Corbin Supply’s then-CFO, Harold Eastbrook. The memorandum was not provided to Ridgeline before the EDA was executed.

### C. The Exclusive Distribution Agreement

11\. On March 1, 2021, Ridgeline and Corbin Supply executed the Exclusive Distribution Agreement, Contract No. CSG-RM-2021-0301 (PX-001). Ms. Hausman signed for Ridgeline and Mr. Corbin III signed for Corbin Supply. The EDA is a valid and enforceable contract containing all essential terms. (Jt. Stipulation ¶ 1, ECF No. 71.)

12\. The EDA appointed Corbin Supply as the exclusive distributor of Ridgeline’s hydraulic product line in a twelve-state territory: Alabama, Arkansas, Florida, Georgia, Kentucky, Louisiana, Mississippi, North Carolina, South Carolina, Tennessee, Virginia, and West Virginia (EDA §§ 1.15, 2.1). The five-year term ran from March 1, 2021 through February 28, 2026 (EDA § 3.1).

13\. EDA § 4.1 and Schedule B set these MAPCs: Year 1 (Mar. 1, 2021–Feb. 28, 2022), \$6,000,000; Year 2 (Mar. 1, 2022–Feb. 28, 2023), \$7,500,000; Year 3 (Mar. 1, 2023–Feb. 28, 2024), \$9,000,000; Year 4 (Mar. 1, 2024–Feb. 28, 2025), \$10,500,000; Year 5 (Mar. 1, 2025–Feb. 28, 2026), \$12,000,000. The five-year total is \$45,000,000.

14\. In EDA § 6.2, Corbin Supply represented and warranted that, as of March 1, 2021, it (a) maintained active business relationships with not fewer than 300 OEM service centers in the Territory; (b) operated not fewer than 11 regional warehouses in the Territory, each with climate-controlled storage; and (c) employed not fewer than 42 dedicated sales representatives in the Territory whose primary responsibility was selling hydraulic products. Section 6.2 also states that these representations "are material to Manufacturer’s decision to enter into this Agreement." The EDA recitals restate the same three representations.

15\. The EDA includes a cure-notice provision (§ 8.2), a remediation-plan requirement for MAPC shortfalls (§ 8.3), a termination-for-material-breach provision (§ 8.4), an anti-waiver clause (§ 8.5), a force majeure clause requiring written notice within 30 days of the event (§ 12.1), and a Pennsylvania choice-of-law clause (§ 14.5).

### D. Performance, Breach, and Termination

16\. In Year 1, Corbin Supply purchased \$5,120,000 of Ridgeline products, which was \$880,000 below the \$6,000,000 Year 1 MAPC. (Def.’s Resp. to Pl.’s SMF ¶ 18.)

17\. On March 15, 2022, Ridgeline sent Corbin Supply a written cure notice under EDA § 8.2 (PX-014) identifying the Year 1 shortfall and requiring a remediation plan within 60 days. The cure period expired on May 14, 2022. Corbin Supply did not submit a written remediation plan, did not make supplemental purchases, and did not otherwise cure the breach. (Def.’s Resp. to Pl.’s SMF ¶¶ 19–20.)

18\. In Year 2, Corbin Supply purchased \$4,370,000 of Ridgeline products, which was \$3,130,000 below the \$7,500,000 Year 2 MAPC and \$750,000 less than it purchased in Year 1. (Def.’s Resp. to Pl.’s SMF ¶ 21.)

19\. The combined Year 1 and Year 2 MAPC shortfall is \$4,010,000.

20\. On March 10, 2023, Ridgeline terminated the EDA under § 8.4, effective immediately (PX-017). Ridgeline filed this action the same day. (Def.’s Resp. to Pl.’s SMF ¶ 22.)

21\. Corbin Supply never gave Ridgeline written notice invoking the force majeure provision in EDA § 12.1. (Corbin Dep. 92:1–97:15; Rinaldi Dep. 78:2–81:14.)

24\. The Court has determined as a matter of law that Ridgeline did not waive its rights under the EDA, and that Corbin Supply’s defenses of waiver and unclean hands to the breach-of-contract claim fail.

# <u>SECTION 3 — PLAINTIFF’S CONTESTED FACTUAL ISSUES</u>

Ridgeline contends that the jury must resolve the following factual issues. For each issue, Ridgeline states (a) the factual proposition, (b) the supporting record evidence, and (c) why the issue is material. Issues 1 through 8 concern Count II (fraudulent inducement), including Defendant’s statute-of-limitations defense. Issues 9 through 13 concern damages on Count I and Count II and Defendant’s mitigation defense. Issue 14 concerns Defendant’s force majeure defense, to the extent the Court allows it to reach the jury.

### Issue 1: Corbin Supply represented three specific, quantified capabilities as existing facts

**(a) Factual proposition.** Before the EDA was signed, Corbin Supply, through Mr. Corbin III, told Ridgeline as present facts, not goals or projections, that Corbin Supply (i) had "firm relationships with over 300 OEM service centers" in the Territory; (ii) operated 11 regional warehouses in the Territory, all "Operational" and "Climate-Controlled"; and (iii) employed 42 "dedicated" hydraulic territory representatives who would "focus exclusively" on the Ridgeline line. Corbin Supply repeated these representations after its own Vice President of Sales had documented in writing that they were not accurate, and it then restated them as warranties in the EDA.

**(b) Supporting evidence.**

> • PX-002 (Oct. 14, 2020 email): "firm relationships with over 300 OEM service centers . . . . These are active, purchasing accounts that we service on a regular call cycle."
>
> • PX-003 (Nov. 9, 2020 presentation): Slide 4 ("11 Regional Warehouses . . . All facilities equipped with full cold-chain and climate-controlled storage"); Slide 5 (lists all 11 warehouses by city, each labeled "Operational \| Climate-Controlled"); Slide 7 ("42 Dedicated Hydraulic Territory Representatives . . . Specialized exclusively in hydraulic components"); Slides 8 and 11 ("Firm relationships with over 300 OEM service centers"); Slide 15.
>
> • PX-004 (Dec. 3, 2020 letter): "Corbin Supply maintains 11 regional warehouses . . . each equipped with full cold-chain and climate-controlled storage"; "Our dedicated hydraulic sales force consists of 42 territory representatives . . . . This sales force is separate from our general industrial distribution team"; "These are not dormant accounts or legacy entries in a database. These are purchasing customers."
>
> • PX-006 (Feb. 12, 2021 email): "With our 300-plus service center relationships and our dedicated 42-person hydraulic sales team, we are well-positioned to exceed the minimum purchase targets."
>
> • PX-001 (EDA), Recitals and § 6.2(a)–(c) (representations and warranties "as of the Effective Date").
>
> • Corbin Dep. 45:3–68:14 (admits sending PX-002 and signing PX-004; concedes the email said "firm relationships," not "prospects"; concedes Slide 4 "didn’t say ‘plans’"); Corbin Dep. 72:8–86:19.
>
> • Hausman Dep. 18:4–38:22; Rinaldi Dep. 10:8–27:15.

**(c) Materiality.** This issue establishes the "representation" element of fraudulent inducement. *Gibbs v. Ernst*, 647 A.2d 882, 889 (Pa. 1994). Corbin Supply contends the statements were "aspirational" or forward-looking (SJ Op. § IV.C.3). Whether they were statements of existing fact is disputed and must be decided by the jury.

### Issue 2: The representations were false when made and when the EDA was signed

**(a) Factual proposition.** When the representations were made, and again on March 1, 2021, Corbin Supply had (i) 187 active OEM service center accounts in the Territory, not "over 300"; (ii) 7 fully operational warehouses with dedicated climate control, not 11 (two sites were still under lease negotiation, and two were shared third-party spaces without dedicated climate control); and (iii) 23 representatives assigned to hydraulic products on a primary or exclusive basis, not 42. The other 19 representatives sold fasteners, seals, and gaskets, and they were never meaningfully redeployed to hydraulic sales during Years 1 or 2.

**(b) Supporting evidence.**

> • PX-008 (Corbin Supply CRM export, Dep. Ex. 23) (187 accounts with "active" status); PX-009 (warehouse status report); PX-010 (sales-force roster, Dep. Ex. 24) (23 hydraulic representatives).
>
> • PX-007 (Findlay Memo) § 3.1 ("187 OEM service centers . . . . I recognize that our external materials reference over 300"; the 300-plus figure included lapsed accounts, unconverted prospects, and third-party sub-distributor relationships); § 3.2 (7 operational warehouses, of which only 5 had full climate control); § 3.3 ("dedicated hydraulic sales force currently consists of 23 territory representatives").
>
> • Findlay Dep. 48:7–64:21 (7 warehouses; 23 representatives; the 19 others "weren’t dedicated to hydraulics" and redeployment "never happened in a meaningful way").
>
> • Corbin Dep. 45:3–68:14 (7 warehouses is "approximately correct"; does not dispute 7 as of March 1, 2021; does not dispute 23 from the roster; 187 "in the ‘active’ category").

**(c) Materiality.** This issue establishes falsity, the third element of *Gibbs*, 647 A.2d at 889. It also rebuts Corbin Supply’s "aspirational" defense: its own records show the numbers were not true on the dates it made the representations.

### Issue 3: Corbin Supply made the representations knowingly or with reckless disregard for their truth

**(a) Factual proposition.** Mr. Corbin III and Corbin Supply knew the representations were false, or made them recklessly without regard to their truth. Mr. Corbin III personally ran the negotiations and helped prepare the November 9 presentation. He received the Findlay Memo, read it, and discussed it with Mr. Findlay in late January 2021. He then reaffirmed the 300-plus and 42-representative figures to Ms. Hausman on February 12, 2021, and signed the EDA warranting those figures on March 1, 2021. Mr. Findlay, who had the true figures, was copied on the October 14, 2020 email and on the later correspondence and never corrected them.

**(b) Supporting evidence.**

> • PX-007 (Findlay Memo) at Closing: "our external negotiations should continue to reflect our full capabilities and market position"; § 3.1 (acknowledging that "external materials reference over 300").
>
> • Findlay Dep. 22:3–44:18 (Mr. Corbin III "said he’d seen the memo," discussed it with Findlay in late January 2021 "before the contract was signed," and did not direct that the concerns be shared with Ridgeline); Findlay Dep. 48:7–64:21 (the presentation was prepared by "Ted and our marketing team").
>
> • Corbin Dep. 72:8–86:19 (he is on the Senior Leadership list; Findlay reports to him; "I don’t deny that it was sent to me"; he "may have seen it"). The Findlay testimony directly contradicts this testimony, and Ridgeline will offer it for impeachment.
>
> • PX-002 (cc: Findlay and Koppel); PX-006 (Feb. 12, 2021, four weeks after the Findlay Memo, reaffirming "300-plus" and "dedicated 42-person hydraulic sales team").
>
> • The size of the discrepancies: the service center count was overstated by about 60% (113 accounts), the warehouse count by 4 of 11, and the dedicated sales force by about 83% (19 representatives). SJ Op. § II.E.

**(c) Materiality.** Scienter is the central contested element of Count II. The Court found it to be a genuine jury question. SJ Op. §§ IV.C.3, IV.D. Ridgeline must prove it by clear and convincing evidence. *Gibbs*, 647 A.2d at 889–90. The Findlay deposition testimony that Mr. Corbin III read and discussed the memo before signing was not addressed in the summary judgment record as the Court described it, and it bears directly on this issue.

### Issue 4: Corbin Supply intended Ridgeline to rely on the representations and did not intend to perform as represented

**(a) Factual proposition.** Corbin Supply made the representations so that Ridgeline would grant exclusivity and accept Corbin Supply’s own proposed MAPC schedule. Mr. Corbin III proposed that schedule himself, calling the minimums "conservative targets given our market penetration." Internally, Corbin Supply projected Year 1 purchases of only \$4.5–\$5.5 million, directed that operational resources not be committed to the \$6.0 million contractual figure, valued the Ridgeline line mainly as a door-opener for cross-selling fasteners, and planned to "renegotiate or exit after year one."

**(b) Supporting evidence.**

> • PX-004 (Corbin Supply proposes the MAPC schedule; "I characterize these as conservative targets"); PX-003 Slide 13 ("Conservative targets based on our 300+ service center network demand analysis"); PX-006 ("I view the Year 1 commitment of \$6.0 million as a floor, not a ceiling").
>
> • PX-007 § 2 ("These minimums are aggressive but we need the line to cross-sell our fastener inventory. We can renegotiate or exit after year one if hydraulic margins don’t hit 18%"); § 4; § 5, Rec. 5 ("a realistic Year 1 purchase target is \$4.5–\$5.5 million . . . . We should . . . not commit operational resources based on the \$6.0 million contractual figure").
>
> • Findlay Dep. 22:3–44:18 ("I didn’t think we could hit those numbers in the first two years"); Corbin Dep. 72:8–86:19.
>
> • Findlay Dep. 71:3–78:15 (Year 1 tracking at about \$4.8 million annualized by November 2021, consistent with the internal pre-signing forecast).

**(c) Materiality.** This issue goes to the fourth *Gibbs* element (intent to mislead another into relying). It also rebuts any claim that the MAPC shortfalls were caused by unforeseen market conditions.

### Issue 5: The representations were material to Ridgeline’s decision to enter the EDA and to the MAPC levels

**(a) Factual proposition.** A reasonable manufacturer would treat Corbin Supply’s service-center reach, warehouse network, and dedicated sales force as important in deciding whether to grant a five-year exclusive in twelve states. Ridgeline did treat them that way. Ridgeline’s acceptance of the MAPC schedule, its grant of exclusivity, and its southeastern capital commitments all depended on those capabilities.

**(b) Supporting evidence.**

> • PX-001 § 6.2 (the representations "are material to Manufacturer’s decision to enter into this Agreement and to Manufacturer’s planned capital investments"); PX-001 Recitals ("Manufacturer would not enter into this Agreement absent such representations").
>
> • Hausman Dep. 18:4–38:22 (the representations were "central to my decision"; "If they only had 187 accounts . . . those minimum purchase levels were always going to be out of reach").
>
> • Rinaldi Dep. 10:8–27:15 (Ridgeline evaluated the Year 1 MAPC at roughly \$20,000 per account across 300 accounts).
>
> • PX-024 (Oct. 19, 2020 Hausman memorandum) ("If their numbers are accurate — 300-plus active service center accounts . . . this could be transformative"; "The economics only work if we have a distribution partner with the scale Corbin Supply is claiming").

**(c) Materiality.** This issue establishes the materiality element of *Gibbs*, 647 A.2d at 889.

### Issue 6: Ridgeline justifiably relied on the representations

**(a) Factual proposition.** Ridgeline actually and justifiably relied on the representations. They were specific, quantified, written, and repeated by the Managing Member of a company with \$285 million in revenue. When Ridgeline sought corroborating information, Corbin Supply declined to provide audited financials. Ridgeline then protected itself in the way sophisticated commercial parties customarily do, by requiring that the representations be made as express warranties in the EDA. Independent verification of hundreds of third-party customer relationships was not practical.

**(b) Supporting evidence.**

> • Rinaldi Dep. 10:8–27:15 ("These were specific, quantified representations from the Managing Member of a \$285 million company. We had no reason to disbelieve them").
>
> • Testimony of Mr. Rinaldi that Ridgeline requested audited financial statements and warehouse data in October 2020, and that Corbin Supply’s General Counsel declined to provide audited financials (to be elicited at trial).
>
> • PX-001 § 6.2 and Recitals; § 11.1 (reliance damages excluded from the limitation of liability).
>
> • Hausman Dep. 18:4–38:22; Hausman Dep. 42:6–58:19. *See* SJ Op. § IV.D (reasonable jury could find justifiable reliance).

**(c) Materiality.** This issue establishes the fifth *Gibbs* element. Corbin Supply is expected to argue that Ridgeline’s due diligence was inadequate, which the Court identified as a jury question. SJ Op. § IV.D.

### Issue 7: Ridgeline’s reliance proximately caused its reliance losses

**(a) Factual proposition.** Because of its reliance on the representations, Ridgeline entered the EDA and made two expenditures it would not otherwise have made: (i) the \$1,850,000 Greenville shipping hub lease, and (ii) \$475,000 in custom packaging equipment configured for Corbin Supply’s warehouse network. Corbin Supply knew before signing that Ridgeline was planning a Greenville shipping facility. The packaging equipment had no alternative use and has been written off.

**(b) Supporting evidence.**

> • PX-005 (Jan. 8, 2021 email to Corbin Supply: "Ridgeline is already exploring options for a dedicated southeastern shipping facility . . . in the Greenville, South Carolina area").
>
> • PX-018 (Lease recitals tying the facility to the EDA and to "Corbin Supply’s regional warehouse network"); PX-019 (rent payments); PX-021, PX-022 (equipment invoices and write-off records); PX-023 (internal approval memorandum).
>
> • Hausman Dep. 42:6–58:19 ("We had no business reason to be in Greenville without the Corbin Supply relationship"; the equipment "wasn’t compatible with our existing packaging lines. We’ve written it off").
>
> • Rinaldi Dep. 84:7–98:19; Prescott Rpt. ¶¶ 64–74; Prescott Dep. 76:2–90:18.

**(c) Materiality.** This issue establishes the sixth *Gibbs* element (injury proximately caused by reliance) and the amount of Count II damages.

### Issue 8: Ridgeline did not know, and could not reasonably have discovered, the falsity of the representations more than two years before asserting Count II

**(a) Factual proposition.** Ridgeline did not know, and through reasonable diligence could not have learned, that the representations were false until Corbin Supply produced its internal CRM data, warehouse records, sales roster, and the Findlay Memo in November and December 2023. The Year 1 shortfall was consistent with ordinary start-up problems. Corbin Supply answered the cure notice by asserting its commitment to the relationship and citing general supply-chain challenges. It never disclosed that its infrastructure was smaller than represented. The facts showing falsity were in Corbin Supply’s exclusive possession.

**(b) Supporting evidence.**

> • Hausman Dep. 62:1–74:16 ("A shortfall in Year 1 didn’t necessarily mean Corbin Supply’s representations were false . . . . What concerned me was the Year 2 decline"; the documents "came through in November and December of 2023").
>
> • Rinaldi Dep. 32:7–52:22 and 78:2–81:14 (the March 28, 2022 Koppel letter promised to "increase efforts"; Ms. Koppel referred only to general supply-chain challenges in the April 2022 calls); PX-015.
>
> • Corbin Dep. 98:7–108:22 (attributing the decline to "market conditions" and soft demand).
>
> • PX-007 (marked "CONFIDENTIAL — INTERNAL USE ONLY"); PX-008 through PX-010 (internal records produced only in discovery).

**(c) Materiality.** Corbin Supply has pleaded the two-year limitations period of 42 Pa. C.S. § 5524(7) (Answer ¶ 57, Third Affirmative Defense). When Ridgeline knew or should have known of the fraud is a question of fact under the discovery rule. *Fine v. Checcio*, 870 A.2d 850, 858–59 (Pa. 2005); SJ Op. § IV.C.2. This issue should be submitted to the jury by special interrogatory if Defendant continues to press the defense.

### Issue 9: Ridgeline’s lost profits on the Year 1 and Year 2 MAPC shortfalls

**(a) Factual proposition.** Had Corbin Supply purchased the contractually required minimums, Ridgeline would have made additional sales of \$4,010,000 (\$880,000 in Year 1 and \$3,130,000 in Year 2) and earned its historical 34% gross margin on distributor-channel sales. Ridgeline’s lost profits on those shortfalls are \$1,363,400 (\$299,200 for Year 1 and \$1,064,200 for Year 2). Ridgeline had the capacity to manufacture and ship the shortfall volume. No additional selling, general, or administrative costs would have been needed.

**(b) Supporting evidence.**

> • Stipulated Facts 16–19 (purchase figures and shortfalls); PX-011, PX-012, PX-031 (summary).
>

### Issue C: Failure to mitigate (Count I and Count II damages)

### Issue D: Relationship between expectation damages (Count I) and reliance damages (Count II); prevention of double recovery

**(a) Legal question.** May Ridgeline recover (i) contract expectation damages on Count I and (ii) fraud damages measured by its out-of-pocket reliance expenditures on Count II? How should the verdict be structured and molded to prevent duplicative recovery?

**(b) Burden of proof.** Ridgeline bears the burden of proving each category of damages. The Court decides as a matter of law whether any awards overlap and how to mold the verdict.

**(c) Plaintiff’s position.** Count I (breach) and Count II (fraudulent inducement) are separate wrongs with separately measured damages. A party fraudulently induced into a contract may affirm the contract, recover its contract damages, and also recover in tort the pecuniary losses caused by its reliance on the misrepresentation. Ridgeline recognizes that it may not recover twice for the same loss. It therefore proposes a special verdict form that asks the jury to find (1) Year 1–2 lost profits, (2) Years 3–5 net lost profits, and (3) reliance losses on Count II, stated both as total expenditures and as post-termination unrecouped expenditures. The Court can then mold the judgment. To the extent any reliance expenditure would have been recouped from the gross profit Ridgeline recovers on Count I, the Court should reduce the Count II award by the overlap only. Defendant’s expert admits he cannot identify any actual overlap. Holt Dep. 62:3–78:19. In the alternative, if Ridgeline does not prove Count II, it may recover its reliance expenditures on Count I as an alternative to expectation damages, subject to Corbin Supply’s right to prove that the contract would have been a losing one.

### Issue F: Force majeure

### Issue H: Statute of limitations and the discovery rule (Count II)

**(b) Burden of proof.** Corbin Supply bears the burden of proving its affirmative limitations defense. When the plaintiff reasonably should have discovered the injury and its cause is ordinarily a jury question.

**(c) Plaintiff’s position.** Count II is timely. Corbin Supply gave the defense only "a single perfunctory paragraph" at summary judgment. SJ Op. § IV.D. If the defense is submitted, Ridgeline asks for a special interrogatory on the date of discovery.

### Issue I: Elements of fraudulent inducement; effect of the EDA’s integration clause and the gist-of-the-action doctrine

**(b) Burden of proof.** Ridgeline must prove each element by clear and convincing evidence. Corbin Supply bears the burden on any contractual or doctrinal bar it asserts.

The integration clause does not bar the claim. The parol evidence rule bars fraudulent-inducement claims based on prior representations that the parties omitted from a fully integrated writing. Here, each representation at issue was written into the EDA itself, in the Recitals and in § 6.2. Fraud in the inducement concerns a pre-contract duty not to deceive that exists independently of the contract, and the Supreme Court of Pennsylvania looks to the nature of the duty breached. Finally, specific, quantified statements of present capability (such as 300 accounts, 11 operational warehouses, and 42 dedicated representatives) are statements of fact, not puffery or opinion.

# <u>SECTION 10 — MOTIONS IN LIMINE</u>

## A. Plaintiff’s Anticipated Motions in Limine (to be filed by August 25, 2025)

### Plaintiff’s MIL No. 1: To exclude or limit the testimony of Warren Holt, CPA/ABV (Fed. R. Evid. 702; \*Daubert\*).

Ridgeline will move to exclude three sets of opinions. First, Mr. Holt’s opinions that (a) Ridgeline should have engaged a replacement distributor within 60 to 90 days, and (b) future damages must end after twelve months. Mr. Holt admits he is "not a distribution operations expert," has never been involved in replacing a distributor, and relies on no empirical data or industry study. Holt Dep. 42:7–58:14. He also conceded that the six-month search "was within a reasonable range," which undercuts his own opinion. Under amended Rule 702, Defendant must show by a preponderance that each opinion rests on sufficient facts and a reliable application of reliable methods. The mitigation opinions also rest on factual premises that the deposition record does not support: that outreach began "sometime around May" and that Ms. Hausman was "evaluating \[its\] legal options." Holt Rpt. §§ VII.C, X. Compare Hausman Dep. 78:4–94:17 and Rinaldi Dep. 56:3–74:18. His report also misidentifies the witnesses and documents on which it relies (for example, calling Ms. Hausman Ridgeline’s "Chief Financial Officer" and Mr. Rinaldi its "VP of Sales," and attributing the October 14, 2020 email to Mr. Findlay).

Second, his unsupported 40–60% residual-value opinion on the packaging equipment, which rests only on his "experience" without inspection, appraisal, or market data. Third, his opinions that reliance and expectation damages are "impermissible" and "duplicative" as a matter of "well-established principles of contract damages law." Those are legal conclusions reserved to the Court, and he admits they are "a legal argument" he is "not qualified to render." Holt Dep. 62:3–78:19. In the alternative, Ridgeline will ask that Mr. Holt be precluded from offering these opinions as legal conclusions to the jury.

### Plaintiff’s MIL No. 2: To preclude evidence and argument supporting a force majeure defense.

EDA § 12.1 requires written notice within 30 days of a force majeure event and provides that failure to give that notice "shall constitute a waiver of the right to claim relief." Corbin Supply admits it gave no notice (Corbin Dep. 92:1–97:15), and the Court found the record "devoid of any evidence" of compliance (SJ Op. § IV.A.2). The Court reserved the defense only to the extent Corbin Supply could proffer evidence of compliance or grounds for excusing the notice requirement. No such evidence was disclosed during discovery, which closed in 2024.

Ridgeline will ask the Court to preclude any evidence, argument, or instruction suggesting that COVID-19 or supply-chain disruption excused the MAPC shortfalls. Because liability is established, such evidence is also irrelevant to damages and would confuse the jury (Fed. R. Evid. 401–403). In the alternative, Ridgeline asks that Corbin Supply be required to make a proffer outside the jury’s presence before mentioning the defense.

### Plaintiff’s MIL No. 3: To preclude relitigation of liability on Count I and of the waiver and unclean-hands defenses.

The Court entered judgment on liability for breach of the MAPC provisions, found the cure notice and termination proper, and rejected waiver and unclean hands. SJ Op. § V.1.a. Ridgeline will move to preclude Corbin Supply from suggesting to the jury that it did not breach, that the breach was excused, that Ridgeline waived its rights by continuing the relationship after the cure period, or that Ridgeline acted inequitably. Fed. R. Civ. P. 16(e), 56(g).

Ridgeline will also ask that the jury be told of the established liability findings at the outset (Stipulated Facts 22–26) and that the preliminary and final instructions so state.

### Plaintiff’s MIL No. 4: To preclude argument that the EDA’s integration clause, the parol evidence rule, or the gist-of-the-action doctrine bars Count II.

These are questions of law. Corbin Supply did not obtain summary judgment on them, and they would mislead the jury if argued as reasons to disregard the pre-contract representations. The representations at issue are expressly incorporated in the EDA Recitals and § 6.2, and § 14.2’s non-reliance clause excepts representations "expressly set forth in this Agreement." *See* Section 5, Issue I.

Ridgeline will ask the Court to rule before trial that the pre-contract communications (PX-002 through PX-006) are admissible on Count II. It will also ask that Corbin Supply be precluded from telling the jury that Ridgeline "agreed not to rely" on them.

### Plaintiff’s MIL No. 5: To preclude references to settlement communications and to the parties’ relative size, litigation funding, or attorneys’ fees.

Evidence of compromise offers and negotiations is inadmissible to prove or disprove the validity or amount of a disputed claim (Fed. R. Evid. 408). References to litigation costs or fees are irrelevant and unfairly prejudicial (Fed. R. Evid. 401–403).

Ridgeline will ask for a reciprocal order binding both parties.
