*(Plaintiff’s proposed contribution in the Court-ordered twelve-section format)*

Defendant-specific sections and settlement/jurisdiction facts that cannot be verified from the supplied materials are expressly identified for counsel’s completion or confirmation. The Court has set jury trial to commence September 15, 2025, for an estimated seven trial days. The November 15, 2024 Memorandum Opinion and Order granted Ridgeline partial summary judgment on liability for Corbin Supply’s breach of the Minimum Annual Purchase Commitment (“MAPC”) provisions of the parties’ Exclusive Distribution Agreement (“EDA”). Count I proceeds for damages only. Count II (fraudulent inducement) and Corbin Supply’s remaining affirmative defenses identified by the Court—including the statute-of-limitations defense to Count II and force majeure defense to Count I—remain for trial to the extent set forth below. Count III (negligent misrepresentation) was dismissed. (Pretrial Order § I; Memorandum Opinion and Order, Nov. 15, 2024 (“SJ Order”) §§ I, V.)

# SECTION 1. JURISDICTIONAL STATEMENT

Subject-matter jurisdiction is based on diversity of citizenship under 28 U.S.C. § 1332(a)(1). Ridgeline Manufacturing, Inc. is a Pennsylvania corporation with its principal place of business in Butler, Pennsylvania, and is therefore a Pennsylvania citizen. Corbin Supply Group, LLC is organized under Delaware law and has its principal offices in Charlotte, North Carolina. Because Corbin is an LLC, its citizenship is that of each of its members—not its state of organization or principal office. The supplied materials do not identify every member and each member’s citizenship. The SJ Order states that the Court exercises diversity jurisdiction under § 1332, and the provided materials disclose no jurisdictional challenge; before filing, counsel should confirm from the jurisdictional record that complete diversity exists and insert/verify the citizenship of each Corbin member. (SJ Order §§ II.A, III; Corbin III Dep. Tr. 12:4–17:22.)

The amount in controversy exceeds \$75,000, exclusive of interest and costs. Ridgeline’s expert has calculated economic damages of \$14,325,000 before any allocation or adjustment to avoid duplicative recovery. (Prescott Report §§ X, XII; SJ Order § II.F.) Venue is proper in this District under 28 U.S.C. § 1391(b)(2), including because the EDA selects the federal and state courts located in the Western District of Pennsylvania, and the negotiations and Ridgeline’s principal place of business are tied to Butler, Pennsylvania. EDA § 14.5; Pretrial Order caption and § I. No jurisdictional or venue objection is identified in the materials supplied; counsel should confirm that statement with Defendant before filing.

# SECTION 3. PLAINTIFF’S CONTESTED FACTUAL ISSUES

### 1. Falsity and scope of the pre-contract and contractual capability representations

> **Proposition.** Ted Corbin and Corbin Supply represented that Corbin had 300-plus active OEM service-center relationships, 11 operational climate-controlled warehouses, and 42 dedicated hydraulic representatives; those representations were materially inaccurate when made and/or as of the EDA’s March 1, 2021 Effective Date.
>
> **Supporting evidence.** Corbin’s October 14, 2020 email described “firm relationships with over 300” active service centers (Pre-Contract Correspondence Ex. A, CSG-001204–001207; proposed PX-004); the November 9, 2020 presentation stated 11 warehouses with full climate control (slides 4–6), 42 dedicated hydraulic reps (slides 7–8), and 300-plus service centers (slide 11) (proposed PX-005); Corbin’s December 3 letter repeated 11 warehouses, 42 dedicated reps, and 300-plus active accounts (Ex. C, CSG-001210–001215; proposed PX-006). The EDA itself warrants, as of the Effective Date, at least 300 service centers, 11 suitable warehouses, and 42 dedicated sales representatives. (EDA § 6.2.) Corbin’s CRM reflected 187 active accounts; the record identifies seven operational warehouses at signing, not eleven, with internal records describing five as fully climate-controlled and two as requiring upgrades (while Findlay’s deposition described seven operational climate-controlled locations); and the hydraulic roster listed 23 representatives. (Proposed PX-008–PX-010; Findlay Dep. Tr. 48:7–64:21; Corbin III Dep. Tr. 45:3–68:14; Findlay Memo § 3.2.)
>
> **Materiality.** Truth, falsity, and materiality of these representations are elements of Count II and bear on justifiable reliance, causation, and the damages claimed.

### 2. Corbin’s knowledge, recklessness, and intent to induce the EDA

> **Proposition.** Corbin Supply, acting through senior leadership including Ted Corbin III, knew or recklessly disregarded that its represented capabilities and ability to meet the MAPCs were materially overstated, and used the proposed relationship and MAPCs to pursue a cross-selling strategy rather than disclose its actual limits.
>
> **Supporting evidence.** Findlay’s January 15, 2021 internal memo to “Senior Leadership” states the minimums were “aggressive,” describes a plan to “renegotiate or exit after year one” if hydraulic margins did not reach 18%, and records 187 active centers, seven operating warehouses (five with full climate control and two with limited environmental controls), and 23 hydraulic-dedicated representatives. (Findlay Memo at 2–4, CSG-00004721; proposed PX-007.) Findlay testified that Corbin III was on the distribution list, reported directly to him, and discussed the memo with him before execution; Findlay recalled Corbin III saying he had seen it and that Corbin would “grow into the numbers.” (Findlay Dep. Tr. 8:12–16:5, 22:3–44:18.) Corbin testified he “may have seen” the memo and could not specifically recall reading it; he also testified that he believed Corbin could meet the targets and that some representations reflected what it was building toward. (Corbin III Dep. Tr. 72:8–86:19; SJ Order § IV.C.3.)
>
> **Materiality.** Scienter and intent are required elements of fraudulent inducement and are disputed for the jury; the Court held the circumstantial evidence creates a genuine dispute, not that scienter is established.

### 3. Materiality, reliance, and causal inducement

> **Proposition.** The scope of Corbin’s service-center network, warehouse infrastructure, and dedicated sales force was material and actually induced Ridgeline to execute the EDA, accept the exclusive-territory arrangement and MAPCs, and make the Greenville and packaging expenditures; Ridgeline’s reliance was justifiable.
>
> **Supporting evidence.** Hausman testified that the 300-plus network, 11 warehouses, and 42-person force were central to the decision and that Ridgeline used Corbin’s stated infrastructure to set the MAPCs. (Hausman Dep. Tr. 18:4–38:22.) She testified Ridgeline would not have entered the EDA or made the capital commitments had it known the actual figures. (Hausman Dep. Tr. 142:8–150:12; SJ Order § IV.D.) The EDA independently states the § 6.2 capabilities are material to Ridgeline’s decision and planned investments and that Ridgeline entered in reliance on them; the recitals likewise identify reliance and planned capital investments. (EDA recitals; § 6.2.) Rinaldi testified the MAPCs were modeled on Corbin’s representations and those written, quantified statements gave Ridgeline no reason to disbelieve them. (Rinaldi Dep. Tr. 10:8–27:15.)
>
> **Materiality.** Fraud Count II requires proof of materiality, justifiable reliance, and proximate causation. The jury must resolve these disputed elements, including the effect of the EDA’s integration language and its express § 6.2 representations.

### 4. Count I damages: direct shortfall damages and lost profits

> **Proposition.** The MAPC shortfalls and the future contract term provide a sufficiently certain basis to determine Ridgeline’s contract damages; the proper amount depends on the legal measure and the jury’s assessment of the disclosed expert evidence.
>
> **Supporting evidence.** The contract establishes annual MAPCs totaling \$45 million and specifically states that direct damages, including lost profits calculated by reference to MAPCs, are not subject to the limitation in § 11.1. (EDA §§ 4.1, 4.3, 11.1.) Actual Year 1 and Year 2 purchase shortfalls are \$880,000 and \$3,130,000, respectively. (SJ Order § II.D; proposed PX-011–PX-012.) Prescott calculates \$4,010,000 in shortfall amounts and \$7,990,000 in net future lost profits for Years 3–5, using MAPCs and a 34% margin, less a \$2,720,000 mitigation credit. (Prescott Report §§ VII–VIII; Prescott Dep. Tr. 18:4–30:22, 34:8–56:19.) Holt proposes \$1,972,000 future loss for a 12-month period and challenges use of future MAPCs; he admits contractual minimums are not inherently speculative and MAPC/gross-margin methods fall within accepted approaches. (Holt Report §§ VI–VII; Holt Dep. Tr. 18:3–38:22, 42:7–58:14.)
>
> **Materiality.** The Court left all Count I damages for trial, including future lost profits, mitigation, and reliance. (SJ Order §§ IV.A.3, V.)

### 5. Reasonableness and timing of mitigation

> **Proposition.** Ridgeline acted with reasonable diligence in locating and vetting a replacement exclusive distributor for a 12-state territory, and the approximately six-month period from termination to Allegheny’s effective date was commercially reasonable.
>
> **Supporting evidence.** Hausman and Rinaldi testified that Ridgeline began identifying candidates shortly after termination, contacted five candidates, two declined, vetted three, visited facilities, checked finances and references, selected Allegheny in July, and completed negotiations, systems integration, and training before the September 1, 2023 effective date. (Hausman Dep. Tr. 78:4–94:17; Rinaldi Dep. Tr. 56:3–74:18; proposed PX-020.) Prescott opines that four to nine months is typical and six months is on the faster end. (Prescott Dep. Tr. 60:3–72:14.) Holt contends a replacement should have been secured in 60–90 days and asserts a \$272,000 avoidable-loss credit, but acknowledges a reasonable manufacturer could take up to a year and that the search itself, once underway, was within a reasonable range; he is not a distribution-operations expert and cites no empirical study for his timetable. (Holt Report §§ VI–VII; Holt Dep. Tr. 42:7–58:14.)
>
> **Materiality.** The Court expressly held that mitigation diligence and the reasonable replacement period present jury questions. Erie Ins. Exch. v. Claypoole, 673 A.2d 348, 354 (Pa. Super. Ct. 1996); SJ Order § IV.A.3.

### 6. Reliance expenditures, value received, and potential overlap

> **Proposition.** Ridgeline incurred the Greenville lease and custom packaging-equipment expenditures because of the EDA and the capability representations; the amount of any recoverable fraud reliance loss must account for value received, residual/redeployment value, and any overlap with expectation damages without a double recovery.
>
> **Supporting evidence.** The lease states a total 36-month rental obligation of \$1,850,000, from June 1, 2021 to May 31, 2024, with a \$308,333.34 early-termination penalty. (Lease §§ 2.1, 3.1–3.2, 7.1–7.3; proposed PX-016.) Hausman testified the facility and \$475,000 equipment were specifically for Corbin’s network and would not have been acquired absent the EDA; she testified the equipment was written off and not practically reusable. (Hausman Dep. Tr. 42:6–58:19.) Rinaldi testified Ridgeline explored termination, determined the penalty exceeded the expected savings, marketed the facility for sublease without success, and used the facility only in limited fashion for Allegheny after September 2023. (Rinaldi Dep. Tr. 84:7–98:19.) Prescott calculated \$2,325,000 but acknowledged that limiting lease loss to the post-termination period would reduce the lease portion to approximately \$770,833.35 and the total reliance amount to approximately \$1,245,833.35. (Prescott Report § IX; Prescott Dep. Tr. 76:2–90:18.) Holt asserts equipment residual value of 40–60% and a theoretical double-counting concern but did not conduct a line-item allocation analysis and could identify no specific duplicate line item. (Holt Report § VII.D; Holt Dep. Tr. 62:3–78:19.)
>
> **Materiality.** This evidence bears on fraud causation and damages, the proper allocation of damages between Counts I and II, and the Court’s instruction preventing duplicative recovery. (SJ Order § IV.A.3; Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 349 cmt. a.)

### 7. Discovery and reasonable diligence for statute-of-limitations purposes

> **Proposition.** Ridgeline did not know, and through reasonable diligence could not reasonably have known, the specific falsity of Corbin’s infrastructure representations more than two years before filing; a Year 1 shortfall or generalized concerns did not reveal the concealed internal account, warehouse, sales-force, and Findlay-memo facts.
>
> **Supporting evidence.** Hausman testified that the Year 1 shortfall could reflect ordinary onboarding or market factors; concerns about the worsening Year 2 trend arose in fall 2022, but Ridgeline did not learn the actual 187/7/23 figures and Findlay memo until Corbin’s document productions in November–December 2023. (Hausman Dep. Tr. 62:1–74:16.) The SJ Order recounts Ridgeline’s position that specific falsity was not discovered until discovery and holds that when Ridgeline knew or should have known is a factual question; Corbin did not support its limitations argument with evidence of earlier discovery. (SJ Order § IV.C.2; Pl.’s Opp. Br. at 28–31, ECF No. 89.)
>
> **Materiality.** The Court denied summary judgment on limitations and directed that the discovery-rule issue be addressed if the defense remains. The date of discovery or reasonable discoverability bears on the two-year fraud limitations period. (42 Pa. C.S. § 5524(7); Fine v. Checcio, 582 Pa. 253, 266–67 (2005); SJ Order §§ IV.C.2, V.)

### 8. Force majeure and causal relation to MAPC nonperformance

> **Proposition.** Corbin did not provide the written notice required by EDA § 12.1(b), and the current record does not establish that a qualifying force majeure event prevented or materially impaired performance of the MAPCs or caused the specific shortfalls.
>
> **Supporting evidence.** Corbin III testified that he could not identify any force majeure notice, did not direct one to be sent, and did not believe Corbin sent one. (Corbin III Dep. Tr. 92:1–97:15.) Rinaldi testified that Ridgeline’s records contained no written notice and that Corbin did not invoke force majeure during their conversations. (Rinaldi Dep. Tr. 78:2–81:14.) Corbin cited COVID-related supply-chain challenges and hesitant customers as general explanations for declining purchases, but the supplied record identifies no notice complying with § 12.1(b). (Corbin III Dep. Tr. 98:7–108:22; EDA § 12.1.)
>
> **Materiality.** The Court reserved the defense only to the extent Corbin may proffer compliance or argue excuse of the notice condition. The notice, qualifying event, causation, and excuse are material to that remaining defense. (SJ Order §§ IV.A.2, V.)

# SECTION 4. DEFENDANT’S CONTESTED FACTUAL ISSUES

*Plaintiff’s summary of disputes Corbin raised in the summary-judgment record and expert disclosure; Defendant must confirm, revise, and supply its own final Section 4.*

> *The following is not offered as Defendant’s approved statement. It identifies known defense contentions so the joint statement can be completed without implying Plaintiff’s agreement.*

### 1. Nature and truth of capability statements

> **Proposition.** Corbin contends that “300-plus,” 11 warehouses, and 42 sales representatives reflected aspirational plans, pipeline relationships, national resources, or personnel who could be redeployed, rather than false statements about current capabilities.
>
> **Supporting evidence.** Corbin III testified that the 300-plus figure may have included prospects, that warehouses were at different stages of readiness, and that representatives from other product lines could be redeployed; he testified that Corbin was building toward the represented capabilities. (Corbin III Dep. Tr. 45:3–68:14, 72:8–86:19, 98:4–102:11; SJ Order § IV.C.3.) Corbin points to two additional warehouses under negotiation and hiring/cross-training plans. (Findlay Memo at 3–4; Corbin III Dep. Tr. 98:4–102:11.)
>
> **Materiality.** The competing evidence bears on falsity and the meaning of the statements, but the EDA § 6.2 expressly states the capabilities as of the Effective Date and the pre-contract statements use present-tense descriptions.

### 2. Scienter and corporate knowledge

> **Proposition.** Corbin contends that Findlay’s memo reflected his individual business assessment and contingency planning, and that Corbin III’s “may have seen” testimony does not establish Corbin III’s actual knowledge or intent to mislead.
>
> **Supporting evidence.** Corbin III testified he could not specifically recall reading the memo and believed the parties could meet the MAPCs; Corbin asserts its business expected to grow into the targets. (Corbin III Dep. Tr. 72:8–86:19; SJ Order § IV.C.3.) Findlay testified Corbin discussed the memo with him before execution and knew his concerns. (Findlay Dep. Tr. 22:3–44:18.)
>
> **Materiality.** Scienter is an element of fraud, to be proven by clear and convincing evidence, and the Court identified this as a jury issue. (Gibbs v. Ernst, 647 A.2d 882, 889–90 (Pa. 1994); SJ Order § IV.C.3.)

### 3. Reasons for purchase shortfalls and market conditions

> **Proposition.** Corbin contends that weak market demand, customer hesitation, COVID-era supply-chain disruption, and the ramp-up of a new product line—not any pre-contract misrepresentation—explain the Year 1 and Year 2 shortfalls.
>
> **Supporting evidence.** Corbin testified that market conditions were difficult, customers hesitated to commit to large orders, and demand in the Southeast was soft; he also referenced pandemic-related supply-chain challenges. (Corbin III Dep. Tr. 92:1–97:15, 98:7–108:22.) Findlay testified that the targets were aggressive relative to then-current capacity. (Findlay Dep. Tr. 22:3–44:18.)
>
> **Materiality.** The explanation bears on causation for Count II and on Corbin’s remaining force-majeure defense; it does not reopen Count I liability already adjudicated.

### 4. Amount and duration of future lost profits

> **Proposition.** Corbin’s expert disputes projecting full MAPC performance through Years 3–5 after actual purchases missed and declined in Years 1–2, and contends the future-loss period should be limited to Year 3 or a shorter mitigation period.
>
> **Supporting evidence.** Holt calculates \$1,972,000 for Year 3 using \$9 million less \$3.2 million projected Allegheny revenue, times a 34% margin; he proposes limiting the period to 12 months and disputes Prescott’s \$7.99 million net projection. (Holt Report §§ VI–VII; Holt Dep. Tr. 18:3–38:22, 42:7–58:14.)
>
> **Materiality.** The Court denied summary judgment on future damages and left the period, reliability, and amount for trial. (SJ Order § IV.A.3.)

### 5. Mitigation diligence and avoidable loss

> **Proposition.** Corbin contends Ridgeline delayed initiating a replacement-distributor search, could have secured a replacement within 60–90 days, and thereby avoided at least \$272,000.
>
> **Supporting evidence.** Holt’s report asserts active solicitation began around May 2023 and calculates a \$272,000 credit. (Holt Report §§ VI–VII.C.) Hausman and Rinaldi testify that candidate identification began in late March/April and describe the multi-stage search and due diligence. (Hausman Dep. Tr. 78:4–94:17; Rinaldi Dep. Tr. 56:3–74:18.)
>
> **Materiality.** The issue affects the future-loss period and damages; the Court has held mitigation reasonableness is for the jury. (SJ Order § IV.A.3.)

### 6. Reliance damages and residual value

> **Proposition.** Corbin contends that expectation and reliance measures cannot be cumulated; that much lease cost was incurred while the EDA remained in effect; that Ridgeline should have exercised the lease termination option; that the hub had value for Allegheny; and that the equipment retained salvage or redeployment value.
>
> **Supporting evidence.** Holt offers these opinions in his report and acknowledges at deposition that he did not fully analyze whether the hub lease was included in Prescott’s gross-margin calculation and cannot identify a specific duplicate line item. (Holt Report § VII.D; Holt Dep. Tr. 62:3–78:19.) Rinaldi describes the early-termination economics, unsuccessful sublease attempt, and limited Allegheny use. (Rinaldi Dep. Tr. 84:7–98:19.)
>
> **Materiality.** The evidence goes to the amount and allocation of Count II damages and the prohibition on double recovery; the Court denied summary judgment on this subject. (SJ Order § IV.A.3.)

### 7. Statute of limitations and reasonable discoverability

> **Proposition.** Corbin pleaded a two-year statute-of-limitations defense and may contend Ridgeline should have discovered the alleged falsity before the fraud claim was filed.
>
> **Supporting evidence.** The alleged communications began October 14, 2020; the Complaint was filed March 10, 2023. The SJ Order notes Corbin’s brief asserted untimeliness but did not cite evidence that Ridgeline knew or should have known of falsity earlier or address the discovery rule. (SJ Order §§ IV.C.2, IV.D.)
>
> **Materiality.** The defense remains unresolved; when Ridgeline knew or should have known is material under the discovery rule. (42 Pa. C.S. § 5524(7); Fine, 582 Pa. at 266–67.)

# SECTION 5. CONTESTED LEGAL ISSUES

> *Plaintiff’s positions are stated below. Defendant’s positions are summarized from its summary-judgment submissions and expert report where available; counsel must verify that these remain Defendant’s trial positions.*

### A. Measure of Count I damages, including MAPC shortfalls and future lost profits

> **Question and burden.** What measure places Ridgeline in the position it would have occupied on performance, and whether the MAPC shortfalls are recoverable at face value or as lost profits after costs; whether Years 3–5 lost profits are reasonably certain. Ridgeline bears the burden to prove damages and amount with reasonable certainty; liability is established and is not for trial.
>
> **Plaintiff’s position.** The MAPCs are bargained-for minimums and the EDA expressly recognizes direct damages, including lost profits calculated by reference to MAPCs, outside the § 11.1 limitation. Plaintiff will seek the damages the jury finds legally supported by the MAPCs, purchase records, audited historical margins, and the disclosed expert opinions. Corbin’s expert characterizes the Year 1–2 shortfall amounts as revenue rather than profit and proposes a 34%-margin adjustment; the corresponding alternative calculation appears in Prescott’s deposition, not her written report. Plaintiff will not offer any expert opinion beyond those properly disclosed under Rule 26 and the Pretrial Order, and submits that the contractual language and disclosed evidence support the appropriate MAPC-based measure. Plaintiff does not seek duplicate recovery.
>
> **Defendant’s position (as reflected in the record).** Corbin contends the \$4.01 million Year 1–2 shortfalls are revenue, not profit, and argues for a 34% margin adjustment; it further contends the future MAPCs are not reliable forecasts given prior shortfalls and proposes limiting future loss to 12 months (\$1.972 million).
>
> **Authority.** EDA §§ 4.1, 4.3, 11.1; Ware v. Rodale Press, Inc., 322 F.3d 218, 225–26 (3d Cir. 2003); CoreStates Bank, N.A. v. Cutillo, 723 A.2d 1053, 1058 (Pa. Super. Ct. 1999); AM/PM Franchise Ass’n v. Atlantic Richfield Co., 526 Pa. 110, 118–19 (1990); SJ Order § IV.A.3.

### B. Failure to mitigate / replacement-distributor period

> **Question and burden.** Whether Ridgeline acted reasonably in finding and onboarding a replacement distributor and what losses, if any, could reasonably have been avoided. Corbin bears the burden on its affirmative mitigation defense; Ridgeline must prove the amount of its claimed damages.
>
> **Plaintiff’s position.** Ridgeline’s six-month replacement process was commercially reasonable for a vetted exclusive partner across 12 states, especially after Corbin’s capability claims proved inaccurate. Ridgeline will prove actual search steps, rejected candidates, site visits, diligence, integration, and training. Any credit should be based on the actual reasonable replacement timeline and proven replacement revenue, not a hypothetical 60–90-day period.
>
> **Defendant’s position (as reflected in the record).** Corbin contends Ridgeline should have begun earlier, secured a substitute in 60–90 days, and should receive a further \$272,000 mitigation credit; it also challenges the Allegheny projection.
>
> **Authority.** Erie Ins. Exch. v. Claypoole, 673 A.2d 348, 354 (Pa. Super. Ct. 1996); SJ Order § IV.A.3; Holt Report §§ VI–VII.C.

### C. Availability and allocation of reliance damages on Count II; no double recovery

> **Question and burden.** Whether and to what extent the Greenville lease and specialized equipment costs are recoverable as fraud reliance damages in addition to distinct contract expectation damages, and what credit is required to avoid overlap. Ridgeline bears the burden to prove fraud damages and causation; the factfinder may not award duplicative damages.
>
> **Plaintiff’s position.** The fraud claim concerns out-of-pocket commitments induced by the pre-contract representations, while Count I concerns breach of the purchase commitments. They are distinct theories and categories if the evidence establishes separate loss; the verdict should allocate any award and exclude any proven overlap. The full lease and equipment costs are the expert’s quantified expenditures, with the post-termination lease period, actual limited use, and value questions for the jury.
>
> **Defendant’s position (as reflected in the record).** Corbin argues reliance and expectation damages are alternative remedies and that the lease and equipment were costs of performance or retain value; its expert proposes excluding reliance damages or materially reducing them.
>
> **Authority.** Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 349 & cmt. a; SJ Order § IV.A.3; Prescott Report §§ IX–X; Holt Report § VII.D.

### D. Elements and proof standard for fraudulent inducement

> **Question and burden.** Whether Ridgeline proves a material false representation, scienter/intent to induce, justifiable reliance, and proximately caused injury. Plaintiff bears the burden by clear and convincing evidence as to scienter and by the applicable civil burden as to the remaining elements.
>
> **Plaintiff’s position.** Corbin made specific present-tense statements and then executed § 6.2 representations as of the Effective Date. The express representations in § 6.2 and the EDA’s carve-outs for fraud and warranty breaches distinguish this case from reliance on merely informal or superseded statements. Findlay’s memo, the actual records, and Corbin’s awareness create a jury question on scienter; Hausman’s testimony and § 6.2 establish materiality and reliance evidence.
>
> **Defendant’s position (as reflected in the record).** Corbin contends the statements were aspirational/forward-looking, the network figures included prospects and planned facilities, Corbin III’s awareness of Findlay’s memo is equivocal, and Ridgeline’s reliance was not justifiable without additional verification.
>
> **Authority.** Gibbs v. Ernst, 647 A.2d 882, 889–90 (Pa. 1994); EDA §§ 6.2, 11.1, 14.2; SJ Order §§ IV.C–D.

### E. Statute of limitations and discovery rule on Count II

> **Question and burden.** Whether the fraud claim is time-barred under Pennsylvania’s two-year period or tolled until Ridgeline knew or reasonably should have known of the alleged fraud. Defendant bears the burden on its limitations affirmative defense; the discovery-rule trigger is a jury issue on this record.
>
> **Plaintiff’s position.** Ridgeline’s evidence is that a Year 1 shortfall and later declining sales did not disclose the concealed internal figures; the specific falsity evidence was learned from Corbin’s internal discovery productions in late 2023. Corbin has not shown that reasonable diligence would have revealed those facts more than two years before suit.
>
> **Defendant’s position (as reflected in the record).** Corbin pleaded the defense and asserted that the October 2020–February 2021 statements preceded suit by more than two years; its summary-judgment presentation did not establish earlier actual or constructive knowledge. The Court denied summary judgment and left the issue for trial if pursued.
>
> **Authority.** 42 Pa. C.S. § 5524(7); Fine v. Checcio, 582 Pa. 253, 266–67 (2005); SJ Order §§ IV.C.2, IV.D.

### F. Force majeure under EDA § 12.1

> **Question and burden.** Whether COVID-19 or related disruption qualifies, caused or materially impaired the relevant performance, and whether the 30-day written-notice condition can be excused. Defendant bears the burden on this affirmative defense and must establish the contractual prerequisites or a basis for excuse.
>
> **Plaintiff’s position.** Written notice within 30 days is an express condition to relief; Corbin’s managing member could identify no notice and Ridgeline’s General Counsel searched and received none. General awareness of pandemic disruption is not the contractually required notice. Plaintiff will seek to preclude an unsupported defense absent competent evidence of compliance or a legally sufficient excuse.
>
> **Defendant’s position (as reflected in the record).** Corbin asserts COVID-19 and supply-chain/customer disruption. The SJ Order did not finally adjudicate whether the notice condition may be excused and reserved the defense only if Corbin proffers evidence of compliance or argues excuse.
>
> **Authority.** EDA § 12.1; Corbin III Dep. Tr. 92:1–97:15; Rinaldi Dep. Tr. 78:2–81:14; SJ Order §§ IV.A.2, V.

### G. Expert admissibility and scope

> **Question and burden.** Whether Prescott’s and Holt’s opinions satisfy Federal Rule of Evidence 702 and Daubert, including reliability, fit, and the basis for the challenged assumptions. The proponent of expert testimony bears the Rule 702 burden.
>
> **Plaintiff’s position.** Prescott’s disclosed opinions use contract MAPCs, audited financial data, a disclosed 34% gross margin, and an express mitigation credit; her opinions are within the disclosed report. Plaintiff anticipates a targeted motion to limit unsupported Holt opinions on transition time and residual value, not a request to preclude proper damages testimony.
>
> **Defendant’s position (as reflected in the record).** Corbin has challenged Prescott’s future-loss methodology and may seek to exclude or limit her testimony; Holt offers the alternative damages methodology. The Court deferred Daubert issues for pretrial motions or a fuller record.
>
> **Authority.** Fed. R. Evid. 702; Daubert v. Merrell Dow Pharms., Inc., 509 U.S. 579 (1993); Padillas v. Stork-Gamco, Inc., 186 F.3d 412, 418 (3d Cir. 1999); SJ Order §§ IV.A.3, V.

### H. Contractual exclusion of consequential and incidental damages

> **Question and burden.** Whether EDA § 11.1 bars any category of incidental or consequential damages, and whether the claimed damages fit the agreement’s direct-damages and express carve-out language. Ridgeline bears the burden to prove the nature and amount of each claimed loss.
>
> **Plaintiff’s position.** Plaintiff seeks direct expectation damages for MAPC shortfalls/lost profits and reliance damages tied to express § 6.2 representations and fraudulent inducement; Plaintiff does not seek a separate award of incidental, consequential, special, exemplary, or punitive damages. Section 11.1 expressly preserves direct lost profits referenced to MAPCs and liability for fraud and breach of the § 6.2 representations.
>
> **Defendant’s position (as reflected in the record).** No separate developed defense position on consequential or incidental damages is identified in the supplied summary-judgment materials; Defendant should confirm whether it contends § 11.1 bars any particular claimed category.
>
> **Authority.** EDA §§ 6.2, 11.1; SJ Order § IV.A.3.

### I. Waiver and unclean hands as to Count I

> **Question and burden.** Whether waiver or unclean hands remains a trial defense to Count I liability.
>
> **Plaintiff’s position.** No. The Court has resolved both issues against Corbin as to Count I liability. They may not be submitted to the jury or used to reopen the established liability finding.
>
> **Defendant’s position (as reflected in the record).** The SJ Order held the waiver defense foreclosed by EDA § 8.5 and the timely cure notice, and found no evidentiary support for unclean hands. Defendant must identify any position it contends remains open, consistent with the ruling.
>
> **Authority.** EDA § 8.5; SJ Order §§ IV.A.2, IV.B, V; Pretrial Order § I.

# SECTION 10. MOTIONS IN LIMINE

Plaintiff anticipates filing the following separate motions in limine by August 25, 2025. Plaintiff’s positions on any opposing motions are unknown because no Defendant motions were provided; Plaintiff will state support/opposition in the filed joint statement or subsequent required response after receiving Defendant’s list.

### A. Preclude relitigation of Count I liability and resolved defenses

Plaintiff anticipates moving to preclude evidence or argument inviting the jury to reconsider Corbin’s MAPC-breach liability, waiver, or unclean hands. The Court conclusively decided Count I liability for Ridgeline and held waiver and unclean hands do not defeat liability; only damages remain. Authority: SJ Order §§ IV.A.2, IV.B, V; Fed. R. Civ. P. 56.

> **Plaintiff’s position:** preclude such evidence and argument and request an instruction that liability is established. Defendant’s position is not supplied and should be completed after conferral.

### B. Limit Holt’s opinions on distributor-transition timing and mitigation

Plaintiff anticipates a targeted Rule 702 motion concerning Holt’s 60–90-day replacement-distributor standard and quantified avoidable loss. Holt admitted he is not a distribution-operations expert, has never operationally replaced a multi-state exclusive distributor, and has no empirical study for the 12-month/60–90-day standard; he also acknowledged that the search duration, once underway, was within a reasonable range. Authority: Fed. R. Evid. 702; Daubert v. Merrell Dow Pharms., Inc., 509 U.S. 579 (1993); Holt Dep. Tr. 42:7–58:14; Holt Report §§ VII.C, X.

> **Plaintiff’s position:** exclude or limit unsupported assertions of an industry standard while permitting properly supported financial analysis. Defendant’s position is not supplied and should be completed after conferral.

### C. Exclude or limit Holt’s generalized equipment residual-value opinion

Plaintiff anticipates seeking to exclude or limit Holt’s opinion that custom-configured packaging equipment retained 40–60% of acquisition cost absent a case-specific appraisal, market evidence, or reliable evaluation of its configuration and re-use. Hausman testified that the equipment was incompatible with Ridgeline’s other lines and was written off. Authority: Fed. R. Evid. 702; Daubert, 509 U.S. 579; Holt Report § VII.D.3; Hausman Dep. Tr. 42:6–58:19.

> **Plaintiff’s position:** exclude or limit the unsupported residual/salvage opinion. Defendant’s position is not supplied and should be completed after conferral.

### D. Limit Holt from giving legal conclusions on duplicative damages

Plaintiff anticipates moving to bar an accounting expert from instructing the jury that expectation and reliance damages are legally mutually exclusive or resolving the EDA’s legal effect. Holt acknowledged the legal permissibility of recovery on separate claims is for the Court and identified only a theoretical double-counting risk, without a cost-allocation analysis identifying a specific duplicate line item. Authority: Fed. R. Evid. 702; Holt Dep. Tr. 62:3–78:19; Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 349 cmt. a.

> **Plaintiff’s position:** bar legal conclusions while permitting properly supported economic analysis. Defendant’s position is not supplied and should be completed after conferral.

### E. Preclude unsupported force-majeure defense absent notice or proof of excuse

Plaintiff anticipates moving to preclude Corbin from presenting force majeure absent competent evidence of compliance with EDA § 12.1(b) or a legally sufficient basis to excuse its notice condition. Corbin III could identify no notice and Rinaldi’s records search found none; general pandemic conditions alone do not establish contractual notice or causation of the MAPC shortfalls. Authority: EDA § 12.1; Corbin III Dep. Tr. 92:1–97:15; Rinaldi Dep. Tr. 78:2–81:14; SJ Order §§ IV.A.2, V.

> **Plaintiff’s position:** preclude the defense absent a proffer addressing the Court’s stated reservation. Defendant’s position is not supplied and should be completed after conferral.
