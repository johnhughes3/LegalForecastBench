**DEFENDANT CALDWELL INDUSTRIAL TECHNOLOGIES, LLC'S ANSWER AND AFFIRMATIVE DEFENSES TO PLAINTIFF'S COMPLAINT**

Defendant Caldwell Industrial Technologies, LLC ("Caldwell" or "Defendant"), by and through its undersigned counsel, hereby files its Answer and Affirmative Defenses to the Complaint for Breach of Contract and Unjust Enrichment (the "Complaint") filed by Plaintiff Meridian Supply Chain Solutions, Inc. ("Meridian" or "Plaintiff"), and states as follows:

**PRELIMINARY STATEMENT**

This action concerns two Purchase Orders issued under a Master Supply Agreement dated March 15, 2023 (the "MSA"), for valve components intended exclusively for the Savannah River Water Reclamation Project (the "Project"). Meridian's Complaint rests on a series of contractual and factual mischaracterizations. First, Meridian's Tranche 1 shipment of 800 butterfly valves was not "deemed accepted": Caldwell delivered a detailed, written, fully documented notice rejecting the entire shipment within the fifteen-business-day Inspection Period, after its inspection revealed a 21.25% defect rate—more than ten times the 2% Acceptable Quality Level to which Meridian agreed—and Meridian's Project Manager acknowledged, reviewed, and responded substantively to that notice the same day. Second, Caldwell never repudiated the Purchase Orders; it gave timely notice of the Project suspension, requested cooperation, and then exercised its express, bargained-for right under MSA Section 13.2 to terminate the undelivered portions of the Purchase Orders for convenience. Third, Meridian's attempted "tender" of Tranche 2 was made to a location that the Purchase Order expressly forbade absent Caldwell's prior written authorization. Finally, Meridian's demand for the full contract price of goods that were never delivered, never accepted, and in many cases never manufactured—together with contractual interest on amounts that never became due—has no basis in the MSA or the Georgia Uniform Commercial Code.

This Preliminary Statement is not a pleading to which a response is required and is provided for context only. Caldwell further states at the outset that, as set forth in its First Defense, this Court lacks subject matter jurisdiction because complete diversity of citizenship does not exist between the parties.

**DEFENSES AND AFFIRMATIVE DEFENSES**

Without assuming any burden of proof, persuasion, or production that it would not otherwise bear under applicable law, and without conceding that any of the following must be pleaded as an affirmative defense, Caldwell asserts the following defenses. Each defense is pleaded in the alternative to the extent necessary under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 8(d)(2)–(3). **FIRST DEFENSE  
(Lack of Subject Matter Jurisdiction)**

This Court lacks subject matter jurisdiction over this action, and the Complaint should be dismissed pursuant to Federal Rules of Civil Procedure 12(b)(1) and 12(h)(3).

Meridian invokes only diversity jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. § 1332(a). Meridian bears the burden of establishing complete diversity. Under 28 U.S.C. § 1332(c)(1), a corporation is "deemed to be a citizen of every State and foreign state by which it has been incorporated and of the State or foreign state where it has its principal place of business." Meridian alleges that it is incorporated in Delaware and that its principal place of business is at 2200 Commerce Park Drive, Suite 400, Savannah, Georgia (Compl. ¶¶ 1, 7), where it maintains its headquarters and its manufacturing facility. Meridian is therefore a citizen of both Delaware and Georgia.

Caldwell is a limited liability company and takes the citizenship of each of its members. Each of Caldwell's members, including its Managing Member, Theodore R. Caldwell III, is a citizen of Georgia, as Meridian itself alleges (Compl. ¶¶ 7–8).

Because Meridian and Caldwell are both citizens of Georgia, complete diversity does not exist, and no other basis for federal jurisdiction is alleged or exists. Subject matter jurisdiction cannot be conferred by consent, waiver, or the forum selection clause of MSA Section 15.2, which is fully satisfied by the state courts located in Fulton County, Georgia. Caldwell's filing of this Answer is without waiver of this defense, which Caldwell intends to raise by separate motion.

**SECOND DEFENSE  
(Failure to State a Claim)**

The Complaint fails, in whole or in part, to state a claim upon which relief can be granted. Without limitation, Count IV fails as a matter of law because Meridian alleges, and Caldwell admits, the existence of valid and enforceable express contracts—the MSA and the Purchase Orders—governing the identical subject matter, and under Georgia law a party may not recover in unjust enrichment where an express contract governs the parties' rights. Counts I and II further fail to state a claim for the full contract price because Meridian does not allege facts that would satisfy any of the conditions for an action for the price under O.C.G.A. § 11-2-709.

**THIRD DEFENSE  
(Proper and Timely Rejection of Tranche 1 (No Deemed Acceptance))**

Caldwell did not accept, and is not deemed to have accepted, the Tranche 1 goods. Caldwell delivered written notice of rejection on September 27, 2024, within the fifteen-business-day Inspection Period prescribed by MSA Section 6.1. The notice described each non-conformity in detail, identified every rejected unit by serial number, specified the Specifications the goods failed to meet, and was supported by a full inspection report, photographic documentation, and calibrated measurement data, as Section 6.1 requires.

MSA Sections 6.1 and 6.2 require only "written notice of rejection" within the Inspection Period; they do not prescribe any method of transmission. Caldwell's notice was in writing, and was actually and indisputably received by Meridian within the Inspection Period, as Meridian's Project Manager confirmed in writing the same day after reviewing the notice and all attachments, with a copy to Meridian's Vice President of Sales, the designated notice recipient under MSA Section 19.1.

To the extent MSA Section 19.1 is construed to apply to notices of rejection, Caldwell substantially complied with it, and the purpose of Section 19.1—to ensure that the recipient actually receives notice—was fully served. Meridian suffered no prejudice from the method of transmission. Construing Section 19.1 to nullify a timely, detailed, and actually-received rejection notice would work a forfeiture disfavored under Georgia law. Caldwell's rejection was also seasonable and effective under O.C.G.A. § 11-2-602(1), which requires only that the buyer "seasonably notif\[y\]" the seller.

Independently, Caldwell did not engage in any act inconsistent with Meridian's ownership of the goods, and therefore did not accept them under O.C.G.A. § 11-2-606.

**FOURTH DEFENSE  
(Right to Reject Entire Shipment for Exceedance of Acceptable Quality Level; Non-Conforming Installment)**

The Tranche 1 shipment contained 170 defective units out of 800—a defect rate of 21.25%—comprising 127 units with micro-cracking at the disc-to-stem weld joints (a safety-critical defect in a valve rated to ANSI Class 150) and 43 units with bore diameters outside the 6.000 ± 0.010-inch tolerance expressly specified in PO-147. MSA Section 4.4 and PO-147 Section III establish an AQL of 2% per shipment, and Section 4.4(a) grants Caldwell the right, in its sole discretion, to reject the entire shipment, "including both the Defective Goods and the non-defective Goods," when the AQL is exceeded. Caldwell validly exercised that right.

Independently, the Tranche 1 non-conformity substantially impaired the value of that installment and could not be cured without replacement, entitling Caldwell to reject it under O.C.G.A. §§ 11-2-601 and 11-2-612(2).

**FIFTH DEFENSE  
(Latent Defects)**

In the alternative, to the extent any Tranche 1 defect was not reasonably discoverable through ordinary inspection methods—including micro-cracking at weld joints detectable only through dye penetrant testing and magnification—Deemed Acceptance does not apply pursuant to the express terms of MSA Section 6.2, and Caldwell's written notice was timely as to any such latent defect. Further, and in the alternative, even if any Tranche 1 goods were accepted, Caldwell timely revoked any such acceptance under O.C.G.A. § 11-2-608, and Caldwell retains all rights for breach of warranty under MSA Section 4.3 and O.C.G.A. §§ 11-2-714 and 11-2-717.

**SIXTH DEFENSE  
(Waiver, Estoppel, and Acquiescence)**

Meridian's claims are barred, in whole or in part, by the doctrines of waiver, estoppel, quasi-estoppel, and acquiescence. With respect to Tranche 1, Meridian received and acted upon Caldwell's rejection notice without objecting to its form; treated it as a rejection notice by proposing a joint inspection, undertaking an internal quality review, and asking Caldwell to hold all 800 units in place; referred in its October 15, 2024 letter to the "quality concerns raised in Caldwell's September 27, 2024 communication"; and first asserted that the notice was defective in form only on January 8, 2025, long after the Inspection Period had closed and after Caldwell had relied on Meridian's conduct by holding the units as requested. Meridian likewise transmitted its own acceptance of the Purchase Orders by email and routinely conducted contractual communications with Caldwell by email. Having induced and accepted Caldwell's reliance, Meridian is estopped from asserting that the Tranche 1 goods were deemed accepted and is estopped from recovering for goods it asked Caldwell to retain.

To the extent Meridian relies on MSA Section 18.4 (anti-waiver), Meridian's conduct constitutes an independent basis for estoppel and, further, Meridian's September 27, 2024 written response constitutes a writing by Meridian recognizing Caldwell's rejection.

**SEVENTH DEFENSE  
(Meridian's Prior Material Breach and Failure to Cure)**

Meridian's claims are barred, in whole or in part, by Meridian's own prior material breaches of the MSA and PO-147, including its delivery of non-conforming Tranche 1 goods in breach of MSA Sections 4.2, 4.3, and 4.4 and PO-147; its failure to repair or replace the non-conforming goods within thirty calendar days as required by MSA Section 6.3; its failure to provide return or disposition instructions for the rejected goods within fifteen business days as required by MSA Section 6.4; and its attempted delivery of Tranche 2 to a location that PO-147 expressly prohibited. A party that has materially breached a contract may not recover for the other party's subsequent non-performance.

**EIGHTH DEFENSE  
(Valid Termination for Convenience (MSA § 13.2))**

Caldwell validly terminated Tranches 2 and 3 of PO-147 and the entirety of PO-163 for convenience pursuant to MSA Section 13.2 by written notice dated November 12, 2024, delivered by certified mail and overnight courier in accordance with MSA Section 19.1, effective December 12, 2024. Section 13.2 grants either party an unconditional right to terminate any individual Purchase Order for convenience on thirty days' notice; it contains no requirement that the terminating party be "in good standing," and no such requirement may be read into it.

By reason of that termination, Caldwell's liability, if any, with respect to Tranches 2 and 3 of PO-147 and PO-163 is limited exclusively to the amounts payable under Section 13.2: payment for conforming Goods already manufactured or in the process of manufacture as of November 12, 2024, plus reasonable, documented, non-cancellable raw-material costs procured specifically for the terminated orders, net of Meridian's commercially reasonable mitigation, and in no event exceeding the total value of the terminated Purchase Orders. Meridian bears the burden of proving the quantity, stage of completion, and conformity of any such goods, and has never provided the accounting and quality documentation Caldwell requested on November 12, 2024. Caldwell has at all times acknowledged, and continues to acknowledge, its obligations under Section 13.2 upon proper verification.

**NINTH DEFENSE  
(No Anticipatory Repudiation; Retraction)**

Caldwell did not anticipatorily repudiate PO-147 or PO-163. Caldwell's October 9, 2024 force majeure notice did not express a clear, unequivocal, and unconditional refusal to perform; it described a temporary inability to receive deliveries at the Project jobsite caused by the Tidewater suspension, requested cooperation, and expressly reserved Caldwell's contractual rights under MSA Sections 12.3 and 13.2. The good-faith assertion of a contractual right, even if ultimately disputed, is not a repudiation.

Further and in the alternative, Meridian did not treat the October 9 notice as a repudiation: it elected to await performance, demanded continued performance, announced that it would proceed with manufacturing and delivery, and did not cancel or otherwise indicate that it considered any repudiation final. Under O.C.G.A. §§ 11-2-610 and 11-2-611, any repudiation (which Caldwell denies) was retractable, and Caldwell's subsequent exercise of its express contractual termination right under Section 13.2 was valid and effective. Meridian may not both demand continued performance under the Purchase Orders and deny Caldwell the benefit of the Purchase Orders' termination provisions.

**TENTH DEFENSE  
(Force Majeure)**

Caldwell's performance of its non-monetary obligations to receive and accept delivery of Tranches 2 and 3 of PO-147 and of PO-163 was excused, suspended, or delayed by a Force Majeure Event under MSA Section 12.1. The indefinite suspension of the Project, the owner-level dispute that caused it, and Tidewater's directive that "\[n\]o further material deliveries shall be made to the Project jobsite" were events beyond Caldwell's reasonable control that rendered the sole contractually designated delivery location unavailable. Section 12.1's enumeration of Force Majeure Events is expressly non-exhaustive ("including, without limitation"), and Caldwell gave timely written notice within ten business days as Section 12.1 requires. Because the suspension continued for more than ninety consecutive days, MSA Section 12.3 further limits Meridian's recovery with respect to the affected Purchase Orders to payment for Conforming Goods delivered and accepted prior to termination.

**ELEVENTH DEFENSE  
(Impossibility, Impracticability, and Frustration of Purpose)**

In the alternative, Caldwell's performance with respect to Tranches 2 and 3 of PO-147 and PO-163 was excused, in whole or in part, by the doctrines of impossibility, commercial impracticability, and frustration of purpose, including under O.C.G.A. § 13-4-21 and, to the extent applicable, principles embodied in O.C.G.A. § 11-2-614 and § 11-2-615. Both Purchase Orders were issued solely for installation on the Project, as each expressly recites, and each designated the Project jobsite as the sole permitted delivery location. The indefinite suspension of the Project and Tidewater's prohibition on further jobsite deliveries eliminated the only contemplated place of delivery and destroyed the purpose of the orders, which both parties understood at the time of contracting.

**TWELFTH DEFENSE  
(Improper Tender; Non-Conforming Delivery of Tranche 2)**

Meridian never made a proper tender of delivery of Tranche 2, Tranche 3, or PO-163. The November 15, 2024 attempted delivery to Caldwell's Atlanta offices was not a tender at the place designated by the contract as required by MSA Section 5.1, PO-147 Section II.A, and O.C.G.A. §§ 11-2-503 and 11-2-507. PO-147 expressly provides that any delivery to an alternative location without Caldwell's prior written authorization "shall be deemed a non-conforming delivery" which Caldwell has no obligation to accept. Caldwell's refusal of that delivery was therefore rightful, and Meridian's unilateral decision to redirect the shipment cannot give rise to liability. No tender of Tranche 3 or PO-163 goods was ever made.

**THIRTEENTH DEFENSE  
(Failure to Mitigate Damages)**

Meridian's claims are barred or must be reduced to the extent Meridian failed to exercise reasonable care and diligence to mitigate its alleged damages, as required by O.C.G.A. § 13-6-5 and the Georgia Uniform Commercial Code. Among other things, after receiving Caldwell's October 9, 2024 notice of the Project suspension and Caldwell's express request that Meridian suspend further manufacturing, Meridian elected to continue—and in the case of Tranche 3 and PO-163, to commence—manufacturing goods for a suspended Project, rather than exercising reasonable commercial judgment to cease manufacture and salvage or resell materials as contemplated by O.C.G.A. § 11-2-704(2). Meridian also failed to resell or attempt to resell completed goods under O.C.G.A. § 11-2-706, failed to redirect raw materials to other orders or return them to vendors as MSA Section 13.2 contemplates, and failed to repair or replace the non-conforming Tranche 1 units. Meridian cannot recover any damages that reasonable mitigation would have avoided.

**FOURTEENTH DEFENSE  
(Seller's Remedies Limited; No Action for the Price)**

Meridian's recovery, if any, is limited to the remedies available to a seller under the MSA and Article 2 of the Georgia Uniform Commercial Code. Meridian is not entitled to recover the full contract price of goods that were never accepted by Caldwell, that were not lost or damaged after risk of loss passed to Caldwell, and for which Meridian has not shown that it is unable after reasonable effort to resell them at a reasonable price. See O.C.G.A. § 11-2-709. Under the FOB Destination terms of MSA Section 5.1 and PO-147, title and risk of loss never passed to Caldwell as to any goods in Tranches 2 or 3 or PO-163. Any recovery must be measured, if at all, under O.C.G.A. §§ 11-2-706 or 11-2-708, less expenses saved, and in any event cannot exceed the amounts payable under MSA Section 13.2.

**FIFTEENTH DEFENSE  
(Contractual Limitation of Liability)**

Meridian's recovery is barred or limited by MSA Section 16. Section 16.1 bars recovery of any indirect, incidental, special, or consequential damages, including lost profits and lost revenue. To the extent Meridian's claim for the full contract price of terminated, undelivered, and unmanufactured goods seeks recovery of lost profits, lost revenue, or other excluded damages, such recovery is barred. Meridian's recovery, if any, is further subject to the aggregate liability cap of MSA Section 16.2 and to the cap in MSA Section 13.2 limiting any amount payable upon termination for convenience to the value of the terminated Purchase Order.

**SIXTEENTH DEFENSE  
(No Entitlement to Contractual Interest)**

Meridian is not entitled to interest under MSA Section 7.3, or is entitled to substantially less than it claims, because (a) Section 7.3 applies only to amounts "not paid when due"; (b) under Section 7.1, payment becomes due only forty-five days after acceptance or Deemed Acceptance, and no Goods were accepted or deemed accepted; (c) the MSA contains no acceleration clause and does not permit Meridian to accelerate the price of undelivered goods or charge interest from the date of an alleged repudiation; (d) Section 7.3 does not provide for monthly compounding; (e) under Section 7.4, disputed amounts do not accrue interest during the pendency of a good-faith dispute; and (f) Meridian's claimed figure of \$148,927.80 is internally inconsistent, having been asserted both as interest through January 8, 2025 and as interest through the March 5, 2025 filing date.

**SEVENTEENTH DEFENSE  
(Failure to Satisfy Conditions Precedent)**

Meridian's claims are barred, or this action must be stayed or dismissed, because Meridian failed to satisfy the mandatory pre-suit conditions of MSA Section 15.1. As stated with particularity pursuant to Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 9(c) in response to Paragraph 58, Meridian did not designate a senior representative, did not participate in any meeting of designated senior representatives in person or by teleconference, and did not negotiate in good faith, but instead demanded payment in full and failed to respond to Caldwell's written compromise proposal and invitation to discuss.

Meridian's claims are further barred to the extent payment for any goods was conditioned on acceptance or Deemed Acceptance, on proper tender at the designated delivery location, on conformity of the goods, or on Meridian's submission of proper invoices referencing the applicable Purchase Order and tranche as required by MSA Section 7.2 and PO-147 Section IV, none of which conditions was satisfied.

**EIGHTEENTH DEFENSE

**NINETEENTH DEFENSE

To the extent Caldwell possesses the rejected Tranche 1 goods, Caldwell holds a security interest in those goods for any payments made and expenses reasonably incurred in their inspection, receipt, transportation, care, and custody, pursuant to O.C.G.A. § 11-2-711(3), and Meridian's claims are subject to that interest.

**TWENTIETH DEFENSE  
(Adequate Remedy at Law; Express Contract Bars Unjust Enrichment)**

Meridian's claim for unjust enrichment is barred because an express written contract governs the subject matter of the claim, because Meridian has an adequate remedy at law, and because Caldwell has not been enriched: the rejected Tranche 1 goods have been held at Meridian's risk, at Meridian's request, and pursuant to MSA Section 6.4, and have conferred no benefit on Caldwell. **TWENTY-SECOND DEFENSE
