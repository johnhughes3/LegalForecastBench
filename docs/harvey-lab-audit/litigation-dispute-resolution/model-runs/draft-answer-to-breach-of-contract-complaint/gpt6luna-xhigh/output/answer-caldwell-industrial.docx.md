**AFFIRMATIVE AND OTHER DEFENSES**

Without assuming any burden of proof that would otherwise rest on Plaintiff, and reserving the right to amend as permitted by the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure and the evidence developed in discovery, Caldwell asserts the following defenses:

**First Defense — Lack of Subject-Matter Jurisdiction (No Complete Diversity).**

> The Complaint fails to establish diversity jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. § 1332. Plaintiff alleges that it is incorporated in Delaware and has its principal place of business in Savannah, Georgia. A corporation is a citizen of both its state of incorporation and the state of its principal place of business. Caldwell is a Georgia citizen, including because its members are citizens of Georgia. The parties therefore share Georgia citizenship, and complete diversity is absent. The amount in controversy cannot cure that jurisdictional defect. The Court should dismiss the action without prejudice under Rule 12(h)(3).

**Second Defense — Failure of a Contractual Condition Precedent.**

> Section 15.1 of the MSA requires the parties, before suit, to engage in good-faith negotiations for at least 30 days; each party must designate a senior representative within five business days, and the representatives must meet at least once. Meridian alleges that the only response was Mr. Caldwell’s January 15 email and that no meeting occurred. Caldwell offered to discuss resolution; Meridian did not designate a representative or arrange the required meeting. Meridian therefore did not satisfy the contractual pre-suit procedure, and its claims are premature. Caldwell gives notice under Rule 9(c) that the condition precedent did not occur.

**Third Defense — Force Majeure.**

> The October 2, 2024 Tidewater suspension halted all Project work, required demobilization, and prohibited further material deliveries to the jobsite indefinitely. Caldwell gave written notice to Meridian by overnight courier on October 9, within the MSA’s 10-business-day period, described the affected obligations and event, and requested a pause in further production. The suspension and resulting inability to receive materials at the only authorized jobsite were beyond Caldwell’s reasonable control within the broad terms of Section 12.1. To the extent applicable, the provision excuses or suspends affected non-monetary performance obligations during the event. This defense does not seek to excuse a payment obligation for goods accepted or otherwise properly due.

**Fourth Defense — Valid Termination for Convenience; Contractual Limit on Termination Damages.**

> Caldwell gave written notice on November 12, 2024, terminating the remaining portions of PO-147 and PO-163 under Section 13.2, with an effective date of December 12, 2024. The MSA grants either party the right to terminate an individual Purchase Order on 30 days’ notice and does not condition that right on the terminating party being “in good standing.” The notice expressly preserved Caldwell’s force majeure position and exercised termination alternatively. Following termination, any payment is limited to conforming goods actually manufactured or in process as of the notice date and reasonable, documented, non-cancellable raw-material costs, subject to mitigation and the applicable Purchase Order value cap. Caldwell requested the supporting inventory, conformity records, and accounting.

**Fifth Defense — No Anticipatory Repudiation.**

> The October 9 notice did not unequivocally and unconditionally renounce Caldwell’s contractual obligations. It invoked the MSA’s force majeure clause based on an indefinite jobsite suspension, described the resulting temporary inability to receive deliveries, requested that Meridian suspend further production, and reserved rights. A conditional request for suspension during a force majeure event is not an absolute refusal to perform. The Complaint therefore cannot treat the October notice as a repudiation that extinguished Caldwell’s subsequent Section 13.2 termination right.

**Sixth Defense — Failure to Tender at Contractually Required Destination.**

> PO-147 specifies delivery to the Savannah River Project jobsite and expressly provides that no delivery may be made to an alternative location without Caldwell’s prior written authorization. Caldwell did not authorize delivery to its Atlanta warehouse. Meridian’s November 15, 2024 attempt to deliver Tranche 2 to that warehouse was not a conforming tender under PO-147 and did not pass title or risk of loss, constitute acceptance, or trigger payment. PO-163 likewise required delivery to the Project jobsite, and no conforming tender of those goods is alleged.

**Seventh Defense — Nonconforming Tranche 1 Goods; Valid Rejection and Warranty Breach.**

> Caldwell’s inspection identified micro-cracking at the disc-to-stem weld joints in 127 units and bore dimensions outside the express PO tolerance in 43 different units, for 170 defective units out of 800 (21.25%). That rate exceeds the 2% AQL. MSA Section 4.4 allows Caldwell, in its discretion, to reject the entire shipment when a shipment’s defect rate exceeds the AQL. Caldwell provided a detailed written rejection within the 15-business-day inspection period and expressly rejected the entire shipment. Meridian’s actual receipt and substantive response, the PO’s designated quality contact, and the parties’ conduct support substantial compliance, waiver, and/or estoppel as to the notice method, to the extent that issue is disputed. Plaintiff is not entitled to the price of the rejected goods.

**Eighth Defense — Breach by Plaintiff / Failure of Performance.**

> Plaintiff’s alleged failure to supply conforming goods under PO-147, including the weld and dimensional defects identified in Caldwell’s timely rejection notice, and its failure to tender Tranche 2 at the specified destination, constitute breaches or failures of performance that bar or reduce the relief Plaintiff seeks. Caldwell reserves all rights to rely on the MSA’s warranty, inspection, replacement, repair, and rejection provisions.

**Ninth Defense — No Mature Payment Obligation; Disputed Amounts and Invoicing.**

> Under Sections 6 and 7 of the MSA, payment is due only for accepted goods (or goods deemed accepted) following the contractually applicable payment period and proper invoicing. PO-147 further requires invoices to identify the PO and tranche and provides that the payment period does not begin until a corrected invoice containing required references is received. Plaintiff must prove valid invoices, acceptance or deemed acceptance, and the due date for each amount. Amounts for rejected goods and undelivered goods are not accepted invoice balances. Any bona fide disputed amount is subject to Section 7.4, including its interest and payment terms.

**Tenth Defense — Failure to Mitigate; Avoidable Loss; UCC Remedies.**

> Plaintiff seeks the full contract prices for custom goods that it says remain in its possession or were never delivered, without adequately accounting for resale, reuse, salvage, costs saved, raw materials recoverable or usable for other orders, or other mitigation. Under the MSA and Georgia’s Uniform Commercial Code, including O.C.G.A. §§ 11-2-703, 11-2-706, 11-2-708, and 11-2-709, any recovery must be based on the applicable remedy and proved loss, reduced by amounts avoided or reasonably avoidable. Recovery of the price is available only under the statutory conditions; Plaintiff may not recover the full contract price where an alternative remedy applies or obtain a double recovery while retaining goods with value.

**Eleventh Defense — Contractual Exclusions and Liability Cap.**

> Any recovery is subject to MSA Section 16.1, which excludes indirect, incidental, special, and consequential damages, and Section 16.2, which limits aggregate liability to the stated contractual cap. Plaintiff may not recover excluded damages or amounts beyond the applicable limitation, and must establish any asserted exception to those provisions.

**Twelfth Defense — Interest Not Due as Claimed.**

> Section 7.3 permits interest only on amounts not paid when due, from the date payment was due, at 1.5% per month or the maximum rate permitted by law, whichever is less; it does not authorize monthly compounding. The MSA does not provide for automatic acceleration of the entire price of undelivered goods or interest on amounts not accepted, not properly invoiced, or not yet due. Section 7.4 also addresses good-faith disputed invoice amounts. Plaintiff has not established the claimed \$148,927.80, the applicable due dates, any contractual basis for acceleration or compounding, or a basis for continuing interest on the entire alleged principal.

**Thirteenth Defense — Unjust Enrichment Barred by Express Contract; No Double Recovery.**

> The MSA and accepted Purchase Orders govern the parties’ dealings and provide express rights and remedies, precluding an unjust-enrichment recovery where the contracts are enforceable and applicable. In all events, Count IV is expressly pleaded in the alternative and cannot yield a duplicative recovery of the same Tranche 1 value sought under Count III or a recovery exceeding the value, if any, of a benefit actually retained.

**Fourteenth Defense — Offset and Recoupment.**

> To the extent Plaintiff proves any amount due, Caldwell is entitled to all applicable credits, offsets, and recoupment for amounts attributable to nonconforming goods, the cost of inspection, repair or replacement, amounts avoided or recovered through resale or reuse, and any other direct contractual or statutory credits established in discovery, subject to the MSA’s limitations and applicable law.
