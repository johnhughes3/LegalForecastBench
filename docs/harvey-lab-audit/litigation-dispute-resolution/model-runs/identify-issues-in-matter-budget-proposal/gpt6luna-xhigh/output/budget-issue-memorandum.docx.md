# Executive conclusion

Return the proposal for revision and do not approve the stated \$2,397,450 total, proposed rates, or unapproved staffing/vendor commitments as submitted. The budget’s fee schedules do not reconcile; several rates exceed the OCG caps or lack timely written approval; Margaret Hsu’s addition is not shown as approved; and the proposal includes a restricted L700 reserve and expenses that are prohibited or require advance GC approval. The trial plan also budgets four attorneys in court, above the two-attorney default absent written approval.

The most material quantified issue is a \$116,050 gap between the phase fee estimate (\$1,708,950) and the staffing-table fees (\$1,825,000), before adding any unbudgeted Boyd Whitaker time. Separately, the proposal includes \$92,000 in L700 and \$15,000 for expressly non-reimbursable internal copying/printing. These amounts should not be treated as authorized spend.

# 1. Budget construction, reconciliation and scope

## 1.1 Fee estimates fail the required reconciliation

OCG §5.2 requires the phase fee total to reconcile to the staffing table (hours multiplied by rates), with any difference identified and explained. The proposal provides matching total hours (3,740) but not matching fees. Applying the rates printed in the proposal to the phase-by-phase staffing allocation yields the following:

|-----------------|----------------------|-------------------------------------|----------------|
| L200            | \$148,800            | \$150,000                           | \$1,200        |
| L300            | \$589,250            | \$646,250                           | \$57,000       |
| L400            | \$266,800            | \$318,000                           | \$51,200       |
| L500            | \$348,100            | \$350,250                           | \$2,150        |
| L600            | \$264,000            | \$265,500                           | \$1,500        |
| L700            | \$92,000             | \$95,000                            | \$3,000        |
| TOTAL           | \$1,708,950          | \$1,825,000                         | \$116,050      |

The proposal’s staffing-table total is arithmetically consistent with its stated hours and rates, while its phase fees sum to \$1,708,950. Thus, the stated combined budget uses phase fees and expenses (\$1,708,950 + \$688,500 = \$2,397,450), but the staffing-based combined amount would be \$2,513,500, a \$116,050 difference. This comparison uses the proposed rates and does not adjust for disallowed rates, remove L700, or add Whitaker. No explanatory reconciliation is provided.

**Action:** Require one revised fee schedule in which each phase total equals the allocated hours times the applicable approved rates, and the grand total rolls through to expenses. Do not approve either fee total until corrected.

## 1.2 Boyd Whitaker is a disclosed but unpriced/unreconciled resource

The proposal describes approximately 60 hours of possible partner time in L400, but expressly excludes Mr. Whitaker from the staffing totals and fee estimates. His rate, detailed hours by phase, and any GC approval are not provided. OCG §§2.1 and 5.2 require the anticipated team and associated rates/hours to be budgeted. OCG §4.2 permits at most two partners (including partner-level of counsel), but requires advance approval to add a second partner if the approved plan included only one. Croft plus Whitaker would be two partners—not automatically a numerical breach—yet the prior-year materials show Croft as the only partner and no Whitaker approval is included here. Add and price him only if needed and approved in writing; otherwise remove the contingency. Any third partner-level timekeeper would be written off.

## 1.3 Budget period and historical bridge need clarification

The submission is dated May 12, 2025 but starts its forecast on May 1. The supporting actuals end December 31, 2024, leaving January–April 2025 unreported. The proposal therefore does not establish current spend, remaining amounts, or a cumulative phase forecast from inception. OCG §5.1 requires quarterly updates (and updates when scope or anticipated cost materially changes), submitted with the quarterly status report. Obtain a January–April 2025 actuals/forecast bridge and identify whether this submission is the required quarterly update; include phase-to-date actuals, remaining forecast and total matter projection.

# 3. Staffing and billable-task compliance

## 3.1 Hsu/Hu addition does not meet notice/approval requirements

The April 25, 2025 email requests approval to add “Margaret Hsu” effective April 28—approximately one business day later—and asks the GC to confirm. The budget instead names “Margaret Hu.” No written approval appears in the supplied materials. OCG §4.3 requires at least ten business days’ advance notice and written approval; compressed notice is limited to genuinely exigent circumstances, supported by a contemporaneous explanation and expressly approved. The request does not provide a full experience/qualification profile, and \$225 exceeds the \$200 paralegal cap. The budget assigns 740 hours/\$166,500 at that rate. Do not authorize/bill those hours until the timekeeper identity, qualifications, role, capped rate and GC approval are documented; unapproved time is at risk of nonpayment.

## 3.2 Trial staffing exceeds default attendance limit

The L600 allocation assigns hours to four attorneys—Croft (140), Okeke (120), Yoon (110) and Novak (40)—and states that the “full trial team” will attend throughout. OCG §6.5 allows no more than two attorneys at a trial session without advance written GC pre-approval. The plan therefore requires a written request identifying which attorneys will attend, why more than two are needed, and the sessions/duration; otherwise reduce the attendance plan and its hours. Separately, only one attorney may attend a deposition or other proceeding absent advance written approval. The 12–15 deposition forecast should identify attendance assumptions and avoid duplicate attendance.

## 3.3 Administrative work is not billable even at paralegal rates

The April 25 email describes Hsu/Hu as a way to shift “administrative and organizational tasks” from associates to paralegal billing rates. OCG §6.4(c) makes administrative tasks—including filing, organizing, indexing and cataloguing—non-billable regardless of who performs them; moving the work to a paralegal does not make it billable. The proposed role also includes document management, deposition logistics and maintaining binders/files, which should be screened task-by-task. Bill only substantive, permitted paralegal services (for example, eligible exhibit preparation) at an approved rate, and exclude clerical/administrative time entirely. The prior-year audit reduced \$7,100 for administrative/clerical work, reinforcing this control.

## 3.4 Attorney cite-checking is expressly non-billable

Ms. Novak’s biography assigns cite-checking to a junior associate. OCG §6.4(g) disallows attorney-rate proofreading and cite-checking; assign eligible cite-checking to paralegal/legal support at an approved lower rate. Do not bill attorney time for this task. Separate substantive exhibit preparation from non-billable filing, organizing, indexing and logistics under §6.4(c).

## 3.5 Staffing metrics largely pass on the forecast shown

The proposed-period efficiency calculation is correct: Yoon and Novak total 1,400 of 3,000 attorney hours (46.67%), above the 40% threshold; paralegal hours are appropriately excluded. Okeke is budgeted at 980/3,000 attorney hours (32.67%), above the 15% significant-role threshold. However, §4.4 requires the efficiency ratio to be measured cumulatively and reviewed quarterly. Known 2024 actuals plus this forecast yield approximately 45.9% (1,810/3,943) before January–April 2025, which is missing. Confirm the cumulative ratio in each quarterly report. Continue diversity reporting under §4.5 and ensure any category information is voluntarily disclosed and treated confidentially.

# 4. Phase restriction: L700 must be removed

The proposal includes 200 hours/\$92,000 for post-trial motions and potential appellate proceedings as L700, describing it as prudent planning. OCG §5.4 and Appendix A prohibit post-trial/appellate reserves unless the Company expressly requests appellate planning in writing; any reserve also requires a separate line item, written supporting memorandum and independent GC approval. The prior-year actuals specifically state that no appellate planning was requested and no L700 budget existed. No subsequent written client request or approval is included. Remove the \$92,000 L700 phase (and all corresponding L700 staffing hours) unless the GC first requests appellate planning in writing and separately approves a supported L700 budget. The current generic coordination language is not authorization.

The Phase Detail also uses L800 for case administration, while OCG Appendix A lists L100 (Case Assessment, Development, and Administration) and does not list L800. Request corrected phase history and confirm all prospective budgets/invoices use the applicable Appendix A codes.
