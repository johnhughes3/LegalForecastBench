# III. Arbitration Waiver: A Threshold Strategic Issue

The January 20 strategy memo relies on the three-factor Second Circuit test in \*Thyssen\* and \*Louisiana Stadium\*, which includes prejudice. That framework has been superseded in part. In \*Morgan v. Sundance, Inc.\*, 596 U.S. 411 (2022), the Supreme Court held that federal courts may not require prejudice to find waiver of the right to arbitrate. The question is whether the party knowingly relinquished the right by acting inconsistently with it. Post-\*Morgan\* courts focus on the moving party's own conduct, especially how long it litigated on the merits and whether it took advantage of court discovery.

- As of March 5, 2025, we will be about six months past the complaint (Sept. 12, 2024). By then we will have answered, attended the Rule 26(f) conference, negotiated the CMO, served initial disclosures, and (if we answer on the merits) served sworn interrogatory answers. Each step adds to the waiver record, whatever reservation language we use.

- **Favorable contract text we should use.** The actual § 9.1 is broader than both parties' pleadings suggest. It covers disputes "arising out of or relating to this Agreement, or the breach, termination, or validity thereof, **including any dispute regarding the scope or applicability of this agreement to arbitrate**." JAMS Comprehensive Rule 11(b) also gives the arbitrator jurisdiction over arbitrability. Together, these give a strong argument that the arbitrability questions Trident raises (material breach, whether the tort claims are in scope, unconscionability) are delegated to the arbitrator (\*Henry Schein, Inc. v. Archer & White Sales, Inc.\*, 586 U.S. 63 (2019)). Separately, Trident's "material breach vitiates the clause" theory runs into the separability rule (\*Prima Paint\*; \*Buckeye Check Cashing\*). One caution: whether we waived arbitration by litigating is generally for the court to decide, not the arbitrator. The delegation clause does not protect us on waiver.

- **Complaint ¶ 91 misquotes § 9.1.** It says the arbitrator may "award any remedy that would be available in a court." The actual clause says the arbitrator has "no authority to award damages in excess of the limitations set forth in Article VIII." This matters for the cap defense and for any unconscionability argument. It should be raised in the motion to compel, not in interrogatory answers.

**Recommendations:** (1) VC should decide, before March 5, whether to file the motion to compel arbitration and stay under 9 U.S.C. §§ 3–4. If we file, simultaneously request a stay of discovery, or an extension of the response deadline, pending decision (CMO ¶¶ 17, 28 contemplate this). (2) If we do not file now, include the standing reservation in the preamble and in Nos. 11, 18, 25 and 26 as the strategy memo directs. Draft every answer to avoid any suggestion that this Court is the chosen forum. Frame answers as compelled participation. (3) Do not seek affirmative merits relief from the Court (for example, a motion to compel against Trident) while the arbitration decision is pending. That is the kind of conduct post-\*Morgan\* courts weigh most heavily.
