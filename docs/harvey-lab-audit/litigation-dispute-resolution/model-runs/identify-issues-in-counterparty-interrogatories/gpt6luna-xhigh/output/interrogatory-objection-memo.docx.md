# I. Immediate response issues and global positions

**Response deadline.** The set was served February 3, 2025. Under Rule 33(b)(2) and Case Management Order (“CMO”) ¶ 5, responses and specific objections are due March 5, 2025 absent stipulation or court-ordered extension; the CMO sets August 15, 2025 as the fact-discovery cutoff. Untimely objections risk waiver under Rule 33(b)(4). Each answer should be separate and complete to the extent not objected to; an officer or agent must verify the answers under Rule 33(b)(3), (5).

**Numerical limit.** The CMO permits no more than 25 interrogatories, including discrete subparts, absent stipulation or leave. Plaintiff numbered 30. Object specifically to Nos. 26–30 as exceeding the limit; also assess whether compound subparts in Nos. 1–25 independently consume multiple interrogatories (especially Nos. 2, 5, 9, 10, 12, 15, 17, 20, 21, and 24). Meet and confer promptly to obtain withdrawal/designation of 25 or a stipulation/leave. Do not silently omit answers or assume a reservation cures a numerical-limit objection. Counsel should decide whether to answer excess requests subject to objection or seek protective relief before the deadline.

**Contention timing.** Nos. 11, 18, 25, and 26 seek contentions, legal positions, or “each and every fact.” CMO ¶ 9 permits the Court, on motion, to defer contention interrogatories until designated discovery is complete; it does not automatically suspend the response deadline. Provide a focused answer based on presently known nonprivileged facts, seek stipulation or court deferral where appropriate, and supplement under Rule 26(e). Expert-related No. 20 is also governed by CMO ¶ 13 and Rule 26(b)(4).

**Standing arbitration reservation.** Consistent with the existing defense strategy, include the following in the response preamble and refer to it in Nos. 25–26:

> *“Whitmore Capital Advisors LLC reserves all rights under Section 9.1 of the Fund Administration Services Agreement to compel arbitration of all disputes arising under or related to the Agreement. **Important qualification.** A reservation is not a safe harbor from waiver; counsel should assess promptly whether and when to move to compel arbitration. Route Nos. 25–26, and any response touching Section 9.1, to lead counsel before service. The CMO ¶ 17 preserves contractual dispute-resolution mechanisms but does not decide enforceability or waiver.

**Privilege / work product.** Do not invoke privilege as a blanket bar to facts or business records. Separate the June–August 2022 ordinary-course business review from the investigation directed by litigation counsel beginning in September 2022. Log withheld documents/ESI under Rule 26(b)(5); the interrogatories’ broader log instruction does not convert discoverable facts into privileged material.

**Other clients / confidentiality.** FASA § 11.2 expressly protects other clients’ identities and client-specific information and requires notice and an opportunity to assert rights before compelled disclosure. Confidentiality alone is not an evidentiary privilege or an automatic discovery bar. For Nos. 2, 12, 15, and 24, seek narrowing, anonymization/aggregation, client notice, and an appropriate protective order; do not disclose identifiable client data absent consent or court direction.

**General definitions / instructions.** Object to “Relevant Period” (2009–present), “You” insofar as it sweeps in attorneys, unaffiliated affiliates, and persons outside Whitmore’s possession, custody, or control, and “Identify” insofar as it demands personal contact information unnecessary to the dispute. The instruction to restate each interrogatory is not required by Rule 33; it may be followed for clarity without conceding the instruction. Use Rule 33(d) only where the answer can be derived from specified business records and the burden is substantially the same for both sides; identify the records with enough detail to locate the answer, preferably by Bates range after production.

# II. Contract provisions and factual guardrails

- Valuation allocation: FASA §§ 2.2(b)–(d) directs Whitmore to incorporate valuation inputs provided by the General Partner, says Whitmore need not independently verify or audit the valuations, and requires reasonable-care consistency checks, mathematical verification, reconciliation, and prompt communication of questions/apparent discrepancies. Do not describe Whitmore as the independent valuation decision-maker unless contemporaneous records support that characterization.

- Reporting/error correction: § 5.2 requires quarterly NAV reports within 45 calendar days after quarter-end. § 5.5 requires written notice within five business days after discovery of a material error and a corrected report within 15 business days after notification. Verify actual discovery, notice, correction, and delivery dates from source records.

- Technology costs: § 4.7(b) permits documented third-party technology/data vendor costs incurred specifically for the Client; shared services must be allocated reasonably and equitably, with the methodology and Trident’s portion disclosed. It contains no \$10,000 prior-written-approval threshold. Advance approval appears in § 4.7(a) for other out-of-pocket costs. Reconcile the vendor contracts/invoices and allocation records against the allegation that “WhitmoreConnect” was proprietary and shared before asserting the factual basis for the \$47,500 quarterly charge.

- Liability cap: § 8.3 uses fees actually paid in the 12 months before the first event giving rise to liability, not simply an annualized fee. § 8.4 expressly excludes fraud, willful misconduct, gross negligence, and confidentiality breaches from the Article VIII limitations. Verify the trigger date, payment ledger, adjustments, and pleaded allegations before stating \$496,000 as a definitive cap.

- Arbitration: actual § 9.1 covers disputes arising out of or relating to the Agreement, its breach, termination, or validity, including scope/applicability disputes; it specifies JAMS, Hartford, and New York substantive law. Use the executed FASA text. Do not import language from summaries (including any supposed arbitrator subject-matter expertise requirement) that is not in the agreement.

- Committee / approval theories: distinguish any Whitmore internal review committee from Trident’s General Partner/Fund valuation committee. The FASA refers to values provided or approved by the General Partner or Fund-designated valuation committee; it does not itself establish that Trident approved each report or that an internal Whitmore committee met. Verify minutes, workflow, and sign-offs before repeating allegations or defenses about committee approval.
