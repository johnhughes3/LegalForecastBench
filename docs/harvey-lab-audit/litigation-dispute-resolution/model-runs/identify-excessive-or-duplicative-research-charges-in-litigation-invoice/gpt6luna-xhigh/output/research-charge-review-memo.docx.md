## 2.1 Partner and senior-associate research

Section 6.3 bars partner research at partner rates. If exceptional partner research is retained, it must be written off or billed at no more than the \$545 senior-associate rate, and the entry must explain why delegation was not possible and why partner involvement was necessary. The six partner-coded RES entries contain no such notation: Diane Hargrove, 5.5 hours/\$4,922.50; Nathan Briggs, 7.0 hours/\$5,075.00. If retained rather than disallowed as duplicative, reduce the combined \$9,997.50 to \$6,812.50 at \$545/hour — a \$3,185.00 reduction. Do not also apply this rate reduction to any partner entry fully disallowed under the duplication rule below.

Samantha Ortega’s July 8 (3.5 hours) and July 22 (2.0 hours) research entries total 5.5 hours/\$2,997.50 for deposition procedure and witness-preparation ethics. These are routine procedural research topics, with no explanation why a junior associate could not perform them. Under Sections 4.1 and 6.3, re-rate to the first-year associate rate of \$295/hour (or disallow if the firm cannot justify the staffing level): recommended reduction \$1,375.00.

## 2.3 Cross-matter MTCA research

Section 6.6 requires research that benefits multiple TerraVerde matters to be billed only once, to the primary matter, with a notation identifying the other applicable matter. Neither July research schedule contains such a notation or a prior written request to conduct the same research independently. The Cascade team had already researched and briefed the MTCA contractor-liability issue in June; the June 28 email specifically noted the parallel DOE matter and reminded H&L not to bill shared research twice.

The clearest overlap is between Cascade’s July 1–5 Takahashi/Wendt MTCA contractor-liability and CERCLA-analog entries and the DOE invoice’s Takahashi entries: July 1 (3.0h/\$1,020), July 2 (2.5h/\$850), and July 8 (2.0h/\$680). Those three DOE entries total \$2,550 and substantially track the same MTCA contractor-liability/CERCLA questions billed on Cascade. Treat Cascade as the primary matter for the shared liability research, given the June summary-judgment work, and credit the duplicate \$2,550 on the DOE bill (or on whichever invoice has not yet been paid). Review the additional DOE July 15 and July 22 Takahashi entries (combined \$1,700) for overlap; allow only time H&L can show was genuinely DOE-specific, separately allocated, and not useful to Cascade. DOE-specific administrative-hearing and penalty research by Osei (\$1,622.50) appears distinct on its descriptions and is not included in the proposed cross-matter credit.
