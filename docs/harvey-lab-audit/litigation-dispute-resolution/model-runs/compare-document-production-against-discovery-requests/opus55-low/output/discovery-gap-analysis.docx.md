# I. Executive Summary

We reviewed Meridian's August 12, 2024 production (Bates MLC-000001 – MLC-004217), its production index, its 43-entry privilege log (PRIV-000001 – PRIV-000043), its June 28, 2024 Objections and Responses, and the August 19 – September 15, 2024 meet-and-confer correspondence, against the served First Set of RFPs (May 15, 2024), the pleadings, and the April 22, 2024 Scheduling Order. Bottom line: the production is materially deficient on every element of the fraud claim and on key breach and counterclaim issues. Much of the most probative evidence has not been lost. Meridian has it and is withholding it on its privilege log under privilege claims that are facially improper.

**The most significant findings are:**

> 1\. **Meridian responded to a rewritten set of requests, not the RFPs we served.** Its responses "restate" each request, and for at least 14 of the 27 RFPs the restated text is narrower or covers a different subject altogether. As a result, **RFP 2** (the due-diligence package, including the FY2020 unaudited financials), **RFP 10** (drafts and source data for the Fleet Capacity Report), **RFP 12** (certificates of insurance) and **RFP 18** (lease liabilities / ASC 842) have never actually been answered. Meridian's production index uses the same altered numbering, so its RFP coding cannot be relied on.
>
> 2\. **The privilege log withholds the core fraud evidence under privilege claims that cannot be sustained.** Entries 33–40 are eight e-mails between CFO Rebecca Cho and Pinnacle Fleet underwriter James Whitfield (Sept. 2020 – Mar. 2021), withheld as "work product." They include Cho's October 28, 2020 direction to cut cargo coverage to \$2M effective December 1, 2020, the resulting endorsement, and her **January 11, 2021 request for a certificate "showing \$5M per-occurrence cargo coverage for Pacific Corridor proposal."** Entries 12–18 are seven e-mails between Brisk and Cho with no lawyer involved, withheld as attorney-client. They cover truck-count assumptions, the "gap between owned fleet and total capacity representation," the draft EBITDA schedule, and "adjustments to capitalized maintenance figures." Entry 31 is GC Delaney's January 10, 2021 advice on "potential exposure if actual fleet count disclosed," sent two days after the Fleet Capacity Report went out. It is a strong candidate for in camera review under the crime-fraud exception.
>
> 3\. **Zero documents were produced for RFP 5 (board materials) or RFP 8 (subcontractor/affiliate carrier arrangements)**, even though Meridian agreed to produce both. RFP 8 goes to the heart of the 347-vs.-189-truck misrepresentation. PRIV-000018 ("subcontractor availability estimates") shows responsive material exists.
>
> 4\. **Seven months of daily dispatch logs (October 2021 – April 2022) are missing**, which is the period of the most acute performance failures. Meridian attributes the gap to a RouteCast Pro → FleetBridge migration. Its litigation hold was not issued until August 25, 2023 (MLC-002859). Yet Meridian itself asserts work product over documents dating to September 2020 and logs legal analyses of our breach notices from June 2021. That supports an argument that the duty to preserve attached before the migration (FRCP 37(e)).
>
> 5\. **Custodial e-mail is limited to two of six agreed custodians.** Every RFP 23 document comes from Brisk (19) or Sato (39). None comes from Alderton, Cho, Hale or Delaney, even though other produced documents and log entries show each of them communicating about the account. No text messages or messaging-app data were produced from anyone.
>
> 6\. **Financial and insurance records are selectively incomplete.** The following were not produced: the FY2020 unaudited statements that are the actual alleged misrepresentation (only the Crestline audited version is produced); audited FY2022–2023 statements; any Crestline management letters or adjusting entries; general-ledger detail for the \$5.1M capitalized maintenance (only a two-page summary); any lease-liability documents for the \$3.6M omitted leases; any Pinnacle policy other than the 2022 policy year; and the December 1, 2020 downgrade endorsement, which appears only on the privilege log.
>
> 7\. **Meridian flatly refused RFP 16 (and its version of RFP 18) as irrelevant, while its Counterclaim affirmatively relies on the same material.** Counterclaim ¶¶ 46–48, 52, 59 and 65 invoke management reports, board presentations and account-level profitability analyses to support the \$3.2M claim. Meridian cannot use those documents as a sword and withhold them as a shield.
>
> 8\. **The production index has integrity problems.** It lists 332 entries against a stated total of 312 documents. Nine RFP 27 documents concern an unrelated customer, Greystone Distribution Partners. The termination notice and response it indexes (July 1 and July 15, 2023) do not match the August 22 and September 5, 2023 letters pleaded by both parties. Neither of those letters, nor most of the 11 breach notices, appears in the production.

**Timing is the immediate risk.** The Scheduling Order (§ 3) requires a direct telephonic or in-person meet-and-confer at least 14 days before any discovery motion, and email alone does not qualify. To file by the October 1, 2024 deadline (§ 4.1), the call must take place by **September 17, 2024**. Meridian's proposed "week of September 23" call would make a timely motion impossible. We recommend demanding a call on September 16–17 and documenting the demand in writing. In parallel, we recommend preparing a joint stipulation or ex parte application to extend the motion deadline in case Meridian refuses.

# III. Threshold Defects in Meridian's Written Responses

## A. Meridian Responded to Requests It Rewrote

Each of Meridian's responses begins with a "Request:" paragraph that purports to quote the RFP. Compared side by side with the RFPs as served, the restated text often differs in substance: it is narrowed, its date range is changed, or in several places it is replaced with a different request (often the subject of an adjacent RFP). Rule 34(b)(2)(B)–(C) requires a response to each request **as served**. Where Meridian answered a different question, there is effectively no response to the request we actually served, and a motion to compel should treat those as unanswered. The production index uses the same altered numbering (for example, driver-training records are coded "RFP 18," and counterclaim emails are coded "RFP 25"), so the index cannot be used to measure compliance.

## B. Timeliness, Boilerplate Objections, and Rule 34(b)(2)(C)

- **Untimely service.** Responses were due June 14, 2024 and were served June 28 (44 days after service). Unless a written extension exists, which we should confirm in our file, Meridian's objections are subject to a waiver argument (FRCP 34(b)(2)(A); \*Richmark Corp. v. Timber Falling Consultants\*, 959 F.2d 1468, 1473 (9th Cir. 1992)).

- **General objections.** The twelve boilerplate General Objections are "incorporated" into every response, contrary to our Instruction 8, and are disfavored in this District. General Objection 8 unilaterally converts every "all documents" request into "documents sufficient to show." That is not a valid Rule 34 objection and was applied in practice to RFPs 7, 9/10 and 17.

- **No statement of withholding.** Rule 34(b)(2)(C) requires a response to state whether responsive materials are being withheld on the basis of an objection. No response does so. The "subject to and without waiving" formulation leaves us unable to tell what was withheld, particularly for RFPs 6, 7, 9, 11, 15, 17 and 19.

- **Relevance objections contradicted by Meridian's own pleading.** RFP 16 was refused on the basis that enterprise financial reporting is "not probative." Yet Counterclaim ¶ 45 attributes Meridian's \$215M → \$162M revenue decline to Pacific Corridor, and ¶¶ 46–48, 52, 59 and 65 rely on "internal management reports," "board-level presentations" and "account-level profitability" analyses. The FY2020 financial condition is also an express element of the fraud claim (Compl. ¶¶ 30–35, 72–73). The objection is not tenable.

- **Protective Order inconsistency.** The responses reference a Protective Order "entered … on May 8, 2024," while Ms. Farouk's September 15 email references a "Stipulated Protective Order entered July 10, 2024." We should confirm the operative order from the docket. Either way, confidentiality is not a basis to delay production of RFP 8 documents; the order already provides for designations.

# VI. Missing Dispatch Logs (Oct. 2021 – Apr. 2022): Preservation and Spoliation

- **What is missing.** Daily dispatch logs, monthly dispatch summaries and the Q4 2021 on-time report for October 2021 – April 2022. This window covers several breach notices and the period PC identifies as the worst performance. Load-level data for the months before and after this window was produced.

- **Meridian's shifting explanations.** On Sept. 2, Meridian said records "may not have been preserved in the ordinary course." On Sept. 15, it said certain RouteCast Pro data "was not migrated" to FleetBridge in approximately Q4 2021, and that it will not undertake "extraordinary recovery efforts." Meridian has not answered any of the five questions in our Sept. 9 letter (system names confirmed only; no dates, migration scope, backups, or hold date and scope).

- **Trigger date.** The only produced hold document is Brisk's August 25, 2023 memo (MLC-002859), almost two years after the migration. But the duty to preserve arises when litigation is reasonably anticipated. Meridian's own log supports an early trigger: legal analysis of the first breach notice (PRIV-19, June 22, 2021), advice on "potential breach exposure" (PRIV-20, Sept. 15, 2021), and assertions of work product for communications dating back to Sept. 2020. Meridian cannot claim it anticipated litigation in 2020 for privilege purposes and then deny it for preservation purposes.

- **Alternative sources to demand.** Legacy RouteCast Pro database backups or archives; vendor-hosted copies from the RouteCast vendor; migration project files; ELD/telematics data (FMCSA-mandated ELD records); driver HOS logs (Meridian's own quarterly HOS reports for Q4 2021 and Q1 2022 were produced at MLC-003601 and MLC-003611, so the underlying data existed); fuel card data; customer invoices with load detail (MLC-001306–001410 cover Oct. 2021 – Apr. 2022); PRIV-6 compilation; and e-mailed dispatch reports in the Hale and Sato mailboxes.

- **Remedy path.** First demand a verified declaration (from IT and a 30(b)(6) designee) covering systems, migration dates, data scope, backups, hold dates and custodians. If the ESI was lost and cannot be restored or replaced, FRCP 37(e)(1) permits curative measures on a showing of prejudice. If intent to deprive is shown, 37(e)(2) permits an adverse inference. The Scheduling Order (§ 4.2) expressly lists adverse-inference and preclusion sanctions. In the meantime, PC's own records of missed pickups for this period should be assembled as the primary proof.
