Plaintiff Vantage Industrial Holdings, Inc. (“Vantage”), pursuant to Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 37(a), respectfully moves for an order compelling Defendant Corbin Machining & Fabrication, LLC (“Corbin”) to provide targeted supplemental responses and production on three interrogatories and five requests for production identified in the attached required summary chart (Exhibit A). The supporting memorandum is within the Case Management Order’s 20-page limit, exclusive of the exhibits and required summary chart.

**MEMORANDUM OF LAW IN SUPPORT OF PLAINTIFF’S MOTION TO COMPEL**

# I. INTRODUCTION

Vantage seeks a focused order requiring Corbin to answer three interrogatories and produce documents responsive to five RFPs. These requests concern the evidence at the center of this case: whether Corbin’s Type 347 stainless steel turbine housings conformed to Vantage Engineering Specification VES-4400, Revision C; whether quality-control tests identified nonconformities before shipment; whether Corbin nevertheless shipped and certified the parts as conforming; and whether the records required by the parties’ MSA exist. The Case Management Order recognizes the substantial amount in controversy, the technical complexity of the manufacturing issues, and that broad discovery is appropriate. (Case Management Order ¶¶ 2, 4.1.)

Corbin’s responses do not answer those questions. Its interrogatory objections do not identify the claimed burden with useful particularity and leave core questions unanswered. Most notably, its answer to Interrogatory No. 18 says Corbin is unaware of any failed parts shipped to Vantage, while Corbin’s own email from Gerald Foss, its Quality Control Manager, states that 15 housings failed CMM inspection, were cleared for shipment at Raymond Corbin’s instruction, and were certified as conforming. (Ex. E, Resp. to Interrog. No. 18; Ex. H.) The email does not alone establish whether that particular batch was ultimately delivered; it makes a substantive, verified answer about the batch, shipment, decision, and CoC indispensable.

Corbin likewise refused to produce relevant quality records, production communications, retention policies, or AMT communications, and has not provided the privilege log required by Rule 26(b)(5)(A) and the Case Management Order. The requests are tied to a single customer, product, specification, and (where applicable) defined time period. Vantage is willing to proceed under a reasonable protective order for properly identified confidential information. The motion should be granted as narrowed in Exhibit A.

# II. RELEVANT FACTS

Vantage alleges that Corbin supplied nonconforming precision-machined turbine housings under the March 15, 2021 MSA, which incorporated VES-4400, Rev. C, and that quality-control failures and false certifications contributed to 38 turbine failures. Corbin has denied the allegations. (Case Management Order ¶ 2.) Corbin’s interrogatory responses acknowledge approximately 4,280 units shipped across approximately 47 purchase orders between April 2021 and December 2023, for approximately \$23.6 million. (Ex. E, Resp. to Interrogs. Nos. 3, 25.)

The MSA requires strict conformance to VES-4400; performance and maintenance of documented QC testing; complete testing records; no shipment of nonconforming products; a CoC with each shipment; seven-year retention of quality records; and maintenance and production of subcontractor test records. (Ex. G §§ 4.2.1–4.2.4, 9.4.1–9.4.4, 14.3.3.)

Corbin served its responses on July 31, 2024, after a two-week extension. Fact discovery closes March 31, 2025, and timely access to these records is necessary before depositions and expert discovery. (Case Management Order ¶ 3.3.) Corbin produced 312 pages overall, but no documents under RFP Nos. 5, 14, 31, or 37. It produced 12 CoCs and stated that the remaining 35 of 47 could not be located after a “diligent search.” (Ex. C, responses to RFP Nos. 5, 14, 22, 31, 37 and summary of production.) The underlying test records requested with the CoCs were not identified as produced. Corbin has not served a privilege log. (Ex. F.)

Vantage sent detailed letters on August 14, September 9, and October 7, and counsel conferred by telephone for approximately 90 minutes on August 28. Corbin declined to withdraw its objections or supplement, except for a commitment to produce a privilege log by approximately September 28. It did not meet that commitment or the October 14 final deadline. The disputes remain unresolved. (Ex. F.)

# III. LEGAL STANDARD

Rule 37(a)(3)(B)(iii)–(iv) authorizes a motion to compel when a party fails to answer an interrogatory or produce documents requested under Rule 34. Rule 33 requires each interrogatory to be answered separately and fully under oath, subject to stated objections; an objection must be specific, and the responding party must answer the unobjectionable portion. Fed. R. Civ. P. 33(b)(3)–(4). A party may use Rule 33(d) for business records only by specifying the records in sufficient detail and providing a reasonable opportunity to examine them.

A Rule 34 objection must state its grounds with specificity and whether responsive materials are being withheld; an objection to only part of a request does not excuse producing the unobjectionable part. Fed. R. Civ. P. 34(b)(2)(B)–(C). The Case Management Order is more explicit: general or boilerplate objections are disfavored; the responding party must identify whether documents are withheld; responsive nonprivileged documents outside an objection must be produced; and privilege logs must be served with responses or within 14 days thereafter. (Case Management Order ¶¶ 4.3–4.4.) The party resisting discovery bears the burden of substantiating a specific objection, not merely invoking burden or confidentiality in the abstract. See Malibu Media, LLC v. Doe, No. 12-cv-2078, 2013 WL 3038025, at \*2 (E.D. Pa. June 18, 2013); Fed. R. Civ. P. 26(b)(1), 34(b)(2)(B)–(C).

Relevant, proportional discovery includes nonprivileged information tied to the claims and defenses, considering the importance of the issues, amount in controversy, relative access to information, and the importance of the discovery. Fed. R. Civ. P. 26(b)(1). Trade-secret or confidential status is addressed through appropriate protection under Rule 26(c)(1)(G), not a categorical refusal to produce relevant material. A discovery motion must also comply with the Local Rule 37.1 meet-and-confer requirement and the Case Management Order’s summary-chart and exhibit requirements. (Case Management Order ¶¶ 5.2–5.3.)

# IV. ARGUMENT

## A. Corbin must provide substantive answers to Interrogatory Nos. 7, 12, and 18.

### 1. Interrogatory No. 7 seeks the test information and records required by the MSA.

Interrogatory No. 7 asks Corbin to identify the QC testing on the Vantage housings, the test methods and dates, who performed and reviewed the testing, the results, and the records documenting those results. It is confined to the Vantage Components during the Relevant Period. Corbin provided no substantive answer. Its response paraphrases a different, shortened version of the question—listing four subparts, whereas the interrogatory as served has six—and objects that compiling the information is burdensome and proprietary. (Ex. D, Interrog. No. 7; Ex. E, Resp. to Interrog. No. 7.)

The request is central and proportional. The MSA expressly requires these very records—including methods, personnel, dates, and results—and traceability from the finished product to the raw material and test data. (Ex. G § 4.2.3.) Corbin has exclusive practical access; it identifies roughly 4,280 units and 47 purchase orders, not an unbounded universe of products. (Ex. E, Resp. to Interrog. No. 3.) Its generalized claim of burden provides no estimate of the volume of Vantage-specific records or explanation of what cannot be retrieved. The subparts all address one subject—the QC testing of the contracted housings—and Corbin has not shown why the common-theme details should be withheld wholesale.

The Court need not require Corbin to create a narrative database. It should order a full verified response based on information reasonably available, or a Rule 33(d) response that identifies the exact produced records, Bates ranges, or data sources from which each requested answer can be derived and affords Vantage a reasonable opportunity to examine them. A generic reference to the production is not enough. Corbin should also produce the responsive inspection and test records under RFP No. 5.

### 2. Interrogatory No. 12 is bounded by the Vantage product and specified quality subjects.

Interrogatory No. 12 asks Corbin to identify internal communications during the Relevant Period concerning specified subjects: quality issues or nonconformities in Vantage turbine housings; complaints; decisions to ship components that had not passed testing or exhibited nonconformities; and investigations of the product’s quality. Corbin objected that the request is vague, overbroad, burdensome, and may encompass privileged communications, but supplied no answer. (Ex. D, Interrog. No. 12; Ex. E, Resp. to Interrog. No. 12.) The defined product, recipient/customer, time period, and subject matter make the request understandable and material.

The request seeks facts about communications, not privileged legal advice. Corbin should identify responsive nonprivileged communications with date, participants, method, subject, and substance, as requested. To avoid unnecessary burden, Vantage seeks the answer for communications about Vantage Components and the listed quality subjects, focused on relevant Corbin QC, manufacturing/production, and management custodians. If Corbin withholds particular communications as privileged or work product, it must log them with the details required by Rule 26(b)(5)(A) and the Case Management Order; a blanket assertion is not a document-specific showing.

### 3. Interrogatory No. 18 requires a verified answer addressing the Foss email.

Corbin objected that Interrogatory No. 18 assumes a failure and uses an ambiguous term, then answered that it was “not aware of any parts” that failed internal quality inspection and were shipped. (Ex. E, Resp. to Interrog. No. 18.) Corbin’s response also restates the question as asking about parts “intended for shipment” and omits the seven specific details requested by the interrogatory as served. The answer cannot stand without a complete explanation in light of Corbin’s own email. On September 14, 2022, Gerald Foss told Raymond Corbin: “we had another batch of 15 housings that didn’t pass CMM inspection but I’ve cleared them for shipment per your instruction.” Foss stated that the bore and sealing surfaces were outside the ±0.002-inch tolerance; that he had signed the CoC as conforming; and that this was the third time that quarter housings with CMM failures had been shipped to Vantage. (Ex. H; see also Ex. G §§ 4.2.1, 4.2.3–4.2.4.)

The email does not itself prove that the September 2022 batch was ultimately delivered; it establishes that Corbin’s QC Manager knew of 15 failed housings and cleared them for shipment, and it directly contradicts the unqualified “not aware” answer. Corbin must state under oath whether the batch was shipped, identify the purchase order, parts/serial numbers if available, the dates and measurement results, the person(s) authorizing shipment, and the CoC issued; if any information is unavailable after reasonable inquiry, Corbin must say so and describe the inquiry. It must also identify any other instances encompassed by Foss’s reference to prior shipments. The “failed” objection is not a reason to avoid answering: the email supplies the test (CMM), a numerical tolerance, and the observed deviations. This is a targeted supplement based on Corbin’s own records, not an invitation to assume facts.

## B. Corbin must produce nonprivileged inspection and QC records under RFP No. 5 and interdepartmental ESI under RFP No. 14.

RFP No. 5 requests internal QC, inspection, and test records for turbine housings shipped to Vantage from April 2021 through December 2023, including CMM and dimensional data, metallurgical tests, NDE, first-article, in-process, final inspection, and SPC records. Corbin refused all production, asserting that the request would require review of “millions of pages,” seek trade secrets, and reach products not limited by part or purchase order. (Ex. B, RFP No. 5; Ex. C, Resp. to RFP No. 5.) But the request is expressly limited to components shipped to one customer in a defined 32-month period. Corbin has not supported the “millions of pages” assertion with a declaration, an estimate, an explanation of the repositories involved, or a proposed narrower search. And the MSA independently requires complete testing records for these products. (Ex. G § 4.2.3.)

RFP No. 14 seeks communications between QC and manufacturing/production personnel relating to Vantage, its turbine housings, VES-4400, CoCs, or quality issues for the specified period. Corbin likewise produced nothing, invoking privilege/work product and burden without identifying any particular communication or custodian. (Ex. B, RFP No. 14; Ex. C, Resp. to RFP No. 14.) The responding party’s characterization of this request misstated its time scope: the request as served runs from January 1, 2021 through the present. It is not a request for every internal email; it is limited to the defined departments and the specified Vantage/quality topics. At a minimum, Corbin must search the pertinent QC and production custodians, including Foss and the personnel responsible for the relevant shipments, and produce nonprivileged responsive communications. Vantage will confer in good faith about reasonable search terms and a protective order.

The trade-secret objection does not excuse wholesale withholding. The Case Management Order requires production of responsive nonprivileged documents outside an objection and encourages a protective order; Corbin has neither identified the particular sensitive material nor sought protection under Rule 26(c). (Case Management Order ¶¶ 4.3, 4.7.) Vantage has repeatedly offered a reasonable stipulated protective order. (Ex. F.) Any genuinely privileged communication may be withheld only if Corbin makes a document-specific claim and serves a compliant log. Ordinary business communications among quality-control and manufacturing personnel are not privileged merely because the dispute is now in litigation.

## C. The 35 missing CoCs and underlying records require a documented search and explanation under RFP No. 22.

RFP No. 22 requests all CoCs for the 47 Vantage purchase orders and the records supporting each certification. Corbin produced CoCs for only 12 purchase orders and says it cannot locate the other 35 after a “diligent search.” It did not identify the supporting test records, search methodology, repositories, or custodians, and its only later description was that a “thorough search” had been conducted. (Ex. B, RFP No. 22; Ex. C, Resp. to RFP No. 22; Ex. F.)

The MSA requires a CoC with each shipment and retention of CoCs and all quality records for at least seven years after shipment; it also prohibits premature destruction and requires production upon request. (Ex. G §§ 4.2.4, 9.4.1–9.4.4.) All shipments identified by Corbin occurred between 2021 and 2023, so the seven-year retention period had not expired when Corbin responded in July 2024. The missing certificates are especially important because they certify compliance with dimensional and metallurgical requirements. Their absence does not, without more, establish spoliation; it does warrant a meaningful account of the search and the status of each record.

The Court should compel production of any located CoCs and supporting inspection/test records, and a declaration from a knowledgeable corporate representative describing (for the 35 missing CoCs) the records searched, relevant custodians and systems, search steps, whether a certificate was never created or is lost or destroyed, and the facts known about any destruction. The declaration should also state what preservation steps have been taken and confirm that responsive quality records remain on litigation hold. This limited information is necessary to assess completeness and to determine whether further relief is warranted.

## D. Relevant retention and preservation policies are discoverable under the narrowed RFP No. 31.

Corbin withheld all retention/destruction and litigation-hold materials under RFP No. 31, asserting irrelevance and overbreadth. (Ex. B, RFP No. 31; Ex. C, Resp. to RFP No. 31.) The policies that governed retention of CoCs, inspection records, and testing data are directly relevant to Corbin’s inability to locate 35 of 47 CoCs despite the MSA’s seven-year retention requirement. Corbin’s interrogatory response that it follows “industry standards” and has taken preservation steps is not a substitute for producing the governing policy or explaining its application. (Ex. E, Resp. to Interrog. No. 22.)

Vantage does not seek unrelated generic corporate policies. It asks for nonprivileged policies and procedures in effect from 2020 to the present that govern retention, destruction, preservation, or retrieval of quality records, CoCs, inspection/test records, and related ESI, plus nonprivileged records sufficient to show when the litigation hold was issued and which relevant categories/custodians it covered. Any litigation-hold communication withheld as work product can be logged; Corbin cannot use privilege as a basis to withhold the nonprivileged retention rules or facts necessary to assess preservation.

## E. Corbin must produce responsive AMT materials under RFP No. 37.

RFP No. 37 requests communications between Corbin and AMT about metallurgical testing of material or components for Vantage, VES-4400, and the relevant test results. Corbin withheld all such material because it says AMT’s information is confidential, the request is broad, and it cannot disclose materials without AMT’s consent. (Ex. B, RFP No. 37; Ex. C, Resp. to RFP No. 37.) Third-party confidentiality is not a discovery privilege and does not support a categorical refusal. Any genuine confidentiality concern can be addressed by a protective order or, if necessary, a targeted request for protection under Rule 26(c).

The MSA specifically requires Corbin to maintain test results from subcontractors as Quality Records and to ensure subcontractor record access; if a subcontractor refuses, Corbin must use commercially reasonable efforts and, if necessary, obtain and produce relevant records. Its obligations are not excused by a confidentiality agreement. (Ex. G §§ 4.2.2, 14.3.3.) Corbin has therefore not shown that it lacks possession, custody, or control, nor explained what efforts it made to obtain the records. The Court should order production of responsive Corbin-AMT communications and test deliverables in Corbin’s possession, custody, or control, including records Corbin has a contractual right to obtain; if any category cannot be obtained, require a specific supplemental statement describing the efforts and result.

## F. Corbin must serve a compliant privilege log; the Court should consider waiver only as appropriate.

Corbin asserted attorney-client privilege or work-product protection in answers to Interrogatory No. 12 and RFP No. 14 and in other discovery responses, but served no privilege log. Rule 26(b)(5)(A) requires a party withholding otherwise discoverable information on that basis to expressly make the claim and describe each withheld item in a manner that permits assessment of the claim. Paragraph 4.4 of the Case Management Order requires a log with the responses or within 14 days thereafter and warns that failure may be deemed a waiver. Corbin’s original responses were served July 31; the log was due no later than August 14 under the Order. Counsel agreed on August 28 to serve it by approximately September 28, but did not do so. (Ex. F.)

The Court should order a compliant log promptly for any documents responsive to the disputed requests that Corbin continues to withhold, identifying the date, author/sender, recipients, general subject, and specific protection asserted. Given the missed court-ordered deadline and the additional unfulfilled commitment, the Court may alternatively deem the privilege claims waived as to responsive items for which no timely log was provided. See Pham v. Hartford Fire Ins. Co., No. 09-cv-0140, 2010 WL 1253481, at \*3 (W.D. Pa. Mar. 26, 2010). Vantage requests that alternative relief only to the extent the Court finds waiver warranted; it does not ask the Court to declare that any particular document is nonprivileged without the necessary facts.

# V. EXPENSES AND CONCLUSION

Vantage exhausted the required meet-and-confer process, narrowed the disputes presented, offered a protective order, and gave Corbin an additional deadline to supplement. Corbin did not supplement, produce its promised log, or provide a reasonable explanation for its refusals. If the motion is granted, Rule 37(a)(5)(A) directs an award of reasonable expenses after an opportunity to be heard unless an exception applies. Vantage requests its reasonable expenses and attorney’s fees, with a supporting submission in the form and on the schedule directed by the Court.

For the foregoing reasons, Vantage respectfully requests that the Court grant the motion and enter the targeted relief set forth above and in Exhibit A.

SECTION 4.2 — QUALITY REQUIREMENTS AND CERTIFICATES OF CONFORMANCE

4.2.1 Conformance to Specifications

Supplier shall manufacture, test, and deliver all products, parts, assemblies, and other items ordered by Buyer under this Agreement (collectively, "Products") in strict conformance with the engineering specifications, drawings, and quality requirements set forth in the applicable Purchase Order and referenced Vantage Engineering Specifications, including without limitation Vantage Engineering Specification VES-4400, Rev. C ("VES-4400, Rev. C"), as such specification may be amended from time to time by written notice from Buyer to Supplier. All Products shall conform to the dimensional tolerances specified in VES-4400, Rev. C, including a tolerance of ±0.002 inches on all critical surfaces as designated in the applicable engineering drawings. In the event of any conflict between the requirements of a Purchase Order and VES-4400, Rev. C, the more stringent requirement shall govern unless Buyer provides written direction to the contrary. Supplier acknowledges that the Products are intended for use in safety-critical gas turbine applications, and that strict conformance to all specifications is essential to the safety, reliability, and performance of Buyer's finished turbine systems.

4.2.2 Material Requirements

All Type 347 stainless steel used in the manufacture of Products shall meet or exceed the following minimum metallurgical requirements:

\(a\) tensile strength of not less than 75 ksi; and

\(b\) Charpy V-notch impact toughness of not less than 40 ft-lbs at -20°F.

Supplier shall obtain material certifications from its raw material suppliers and, where applicable, from its metallurgical testing subcontractors, and shall maintain such certifications as part of its quality records for the duration of the retention period specified in Section 9.4 of this Agreement. Supplier may utilize third-party testing laboratories approved by Buyer for the purpose of conducting metallurgical testing required under this Agreement, including but not limited to tensile testing, Charpy V-notch impact testing, and chemical composition analysis. Any such third-party testing laboratory shall be qualified and approved in writing by Buyer prior to performing testing on Products or materials intended for use in Products. All test results generated by any third-party metallurgical testing subcontractor shall be maintained by Supplier as part of Supplier's quality records and shall be subject to the same retention, accessibility, and production requirements as all other Quality Records under this Agreement.

4.2.3 Quality Control Testing

Supplier shall implement and maintain a documented quality management system consistent with the requirements of ISO 9001:2015 or an equivalent quality standard approved by Buyer, and shall perform all quality-control inspections and tests required by the applicable engineering specifications and Purchase Orders prior to shipment of Products to Buyer. Quality-control testing shall include, without limitation:

\(a\) dimensional inspection using coordinate measuring machines ("CMM") or equivalent precision measurement instruments traceable to NIST standards;

\(b\) metallurgical testing as specified in VES-4400, Rev. C, including tensile testing and Charpy V-notch impact testing performed in accordance with applicable ASTM standards;

\(c\) visual inspection for surface defects, including but not limited to cracks, porosity, inclusions, tool marks, and surface finish non-conformities; and

\(d\) such other testing as may be specified in the applicable Purchase Order or as Buyer may reasonably require by written notice to Supplier.

Supplier shall maintain complete and accurate records of all quality-control testing performed on Products under this Agreement, including the test methods employed, the identity of each person who performed or reviewed the testing, the dates of testing, and the results obtained. Such records shall be maintained in sufficient detail to permit traceability from the finished Product back to the raw material lot, the applicable test results, and the identity of personnel involved in manufacturing and inspection.

Products that do not conform to the applicable specifications shall not be shipped to Buyer. Supplier shall segregate, quarantine, and disposition all nonconforming Products in accordance with Supplier's documented nonconformance procedures, and shall maintain records of all nonconforming Products, including the nature of the nonconformity, the disposition (whether rework, scrap, or return to vendor), and the authorization for such disposition. Supplier shall ensure that no nonconforming Product is reworked or re-inspected without documented authorization from Supplier's Quality Control Manager, and that all reworked Products are re-inspected and re-tested to the original specification requirements prior to shipment.

4.2.4 Certificates of Conformance

Supplier shall deliver to Buyer, concurrently with each shipment of Products, a Certificate of Conformance ("CoC") in the form attached hereto as Exhibit D (or such other form as Buyer may approve in writing from time to time). Each Certificate of Conformance shall certify that:

\(a\) the Products included in the shipment have been manufactured, inspected, and tested in accordance with all applicable specifications, including VES-4400, Rev. C, and the requirements of the applicable Purchase Order;

\(b\) the Products conform to all dimensional tolerances specified in the applicable engineering drawings, including ±0.002 inches on all critical surfaces;

\(c\) the materials used in the Products meet all applicable metallurgical requirements, including minimum tensile strength of 75 ksi and Charpy V-notch impact toughness of not less than 40 ft-lbs at -20°F; and

\(d\) the Products are free from defects in material and workmanship and are fit for their intended use in Buyer's gas turbine systems.

Each Certificate of Conformance shall be signed by Supplier's Quality Control Manager or another authorized representative of Supplier designated in writing to Buyer. The signature of Supplier's representative on a Certificate of Conformance shall constitute a representation and warranty by Supplier that the certifications contained therein are true, accurate, and complete as of the date of the Certificate.

Supplier shall retain the original or a true copy of each Certificate of Conformance in accordance with the document retention requirements of Section 9.4 of this Agreement. Buyer's receipt or acceptance of a Certificate of Conformance shall not relieve Supplier of any obligation under this Agreement or constitute a waiver of Buyer's right to reject nonconforming Products upon subsequent inspection or testing.

4.2.5 Notification of Nonconformities

In the event Supplier becomes aware, at any time, that Products previously shipped to Buyer may not conform to the applicable specifications or may contain defects in material or workmanship, Supplier shall promptly notify Buyer in writing of such nonconformity, including the identity of affected Products by serial number and Purchase Order number, the nature of the suspected nonconformity, and the corrective actions being taken or proposed by Supplier. Such notification shall be made within forty-eight (48) hours of Supplier's discovery of the nonconformity or suspected nonconformity, and shall be directed to Buyer's Vice President of Quality Assurance. Failure to provide such notification in the time and manner required by this Section 4.2.5 shall constitute a material breach of this Agreement.

SECTION 9.4 — DOCUMENT RETENTION

9.4.1 General Retention Obligation

Supplier shall maintain complete, accurate, and legible records of all documents, data, correspondence, test results, inspection reports, Certificates of Conformance, nonconformance reports, corrective action records, material certifications, subcontractor test reports, and other quality records relating to Products manufactured and supplied under this Agreement (collectively, "Quality Records") for a minimum period of seven (7) years following the date of shipment of the applicable Products to Buyer, or such longer period as may be required by applicable law or regulation. This retention obligation shall survive the expiration or termination of this Agreement and shall remain in full force and effect with respect to each Quality Record until the applicable seven (7) year retention period has expired. The obligation to retain Quality Records is an essential obligation of this Agreement, and Supplier's compliance therewith is a material condition of Buyer's willingness to enter into this Agreement.

9.4.2 Format and Accessibility

Quality Records may be maintained in original paper form or in electronic format, provided that electronic records are stored in a manner that preserves their integrity, legibility, and accessibility throughout the applicable retention period, and that Supplier can produce complete and accurate copies of such records in paper or electronic format upon request by Buyer. Supplier shall implement and maintain a document management system or filing system adequate to ensure the organized storage, retrieval, and production of Quality Records throughout the retention period. Supplier shall take reasonable steps to protect Quality Records from loss, damage, unauthorized access, or deterioration, including implementing appropriate backup procedures for electronically stored records.

9.4.3 Destruction Prohibited During Retention Period

Supplier shall not destroy, discard, delete, or otherwise dispose of any Quality Records prior to the expiration of the applicable retention period set forth in Section 9.4.1 without the prior written consent of Buyer. Any destruction of Quality Records prior to the expiration of the applicable retention period without Buyer's written consent shall constitute a material breach of this Agreement.

In the event of any claim, dispute, or litigation arising out of or relating to this Agreement or the Products supplied hereunder, Supplier shall immediately preserve all Quality Records and other potentially relevant documents, data, and electronically stored information, and shall suspend any routine document destruction or disposal practices with respect to such records, regardless of whether the applicable seven (7) year retention period has expired. This preservation obligation shall arise upon Supplier's receipt of notice of any such claim, dispute, or litigation, or upon Supplier's becoming reasonably aware that such a claim, dispute, or litigation is anticipated, whichever occurs first.

9.4.4 Production Upon Request

Upon written request by Buyer, Supplier shall produce copies of any Quality Records within fifteen (15) business days of receipt of such request. Supplier shall bear all costs associated with the production of such records, including costs of copying, scanning, and delivery. Failure to produce requested Quality Records within the time period specified herein shall entitle Buyer to exercise any and all remedies available under this Agreement or at law or in equity.

14.3.3 Subcontractor Audit Rights

Supplier shall ensure that its subcontractors, including without limitation any metallurgical testing laboratories or other third-party testing facilities utilized by Supplier in connection with the manufacture, testing, or inspection of Products, maintain records relating to the Products in accordance with the requirements of this Agreement, including the retention requirements of Section 9.4. Supplier shall ensure that its agreements with all such subcontractors include flow-down provisions requiring the subcontractor's compliance with this Section 14.3, and Supplier shall permit Buyer to conduct audits and inspections of such subcontractor records upon reasonable prior written notice. In the event any subcontractor refuses or fails to permit such audits or inspections, Supplier shall use commercially reasonable efforts to obtain the subcontractor's cooperation and, if necessary, shall obtain and produce to Buyer copies of all relevant subcontractor records. Supplier's obligations under this Section 14.3.3 shall not be excused by any confidentiality or non-disclosure agreement between Supplier and its subcontractors.

14.3.4 No Limitation on Discovery Rights

Nothing in this Section 14.3 shall be construed to limit either Party's rights to obtain discovery in any legal proceeding arising out of or relating to this Agreement, whether under the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure, applicable state rules of civil procedure, or otherwise. The inspection and audit rights granted under this Section 14.3 are in addition to, and not in lieu of, any discovery rights that may be available to either Party in connection with any such legal proceeding.
