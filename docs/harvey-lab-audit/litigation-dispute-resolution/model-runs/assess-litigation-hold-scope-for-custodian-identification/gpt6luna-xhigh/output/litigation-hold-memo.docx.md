# Executive Summary and Immediate Direction

Nexfield should issue one coordinated preservation hold immediately covering (1) Darren T. Kovach’s threatened whistleblower-retaliation / wrongful-termination claims and (2) the SEC Division of Enforcement’s October 28, 2024 informal inquiry into channel sales and revenue recognition. The factual overlap is substantial, but the two matters must remain separately tracked for scope, access, privilege review, and any production decisions. The hold should cover the common revenue-recognition evidence and the distinct employment, Audit Committee, and regulatory materials described below.

The record reviewed does not establish that a formal hold notice or effective technical preservation controls were implemented before November 4. IT affirmatively reported that no eDiscovery hold had been placed on Kovach’s mailbox or Teams data, the 90-day Teams chat deletion policy remained active, and Salesforce inactive-record deletion remained active. Legal should verify the hold log and all system settings immediately; do not infer that preservation occurred merely because routine retention existed or an investigation was underway.

Preservation likely became necessary no later than August 5, 2024, when Kovach escalated a written accounting-misconduct complaint to Audit Committee Chair Marcus Ainsley. The internal investigation beginning August 20 and Kovach’s September 12 termination reinforced the foreseeability of claims; the October 3 demand letter made threatened litigation explicit. The SEC’s October 28 letter separately expressly requested preservation. The August 5-to-November 8 interval is 95 days. During this interval, at least some relevant Teams chats may have been automatically purged; Salesforce records may have been permanently deleted; and archive completeness for key custodians remains unknown.

The most urgent steps are: (i) issue the hold and suspend the Teams 90-day chat purge; (ii) suspend Salesforce inactive-record deletion and preserve exports, audit logs, and recovery sources; (iii) place M365 and Enterprise Vault data on hold and reconcile the 421-mailbox migration remediation log; (iv) freeze deletion/decommissioning activity on NXF-FS01 and arrange a forensic image before January 31, 2025; and (v) have Pinnacle Hartwell promptly send the agreed preservation demand to Kovach’s counsel concerning his personal iPhone. Do not wait for collection to begin before stopping routine destruction.

# 1. Matter, Trigger, and Preservation Gap

The Kovach matter concerns alleged retaliation for internal complaints about accelerated channel shipments and revenue recognition, including a SOX Section 806 claim and Texas-law claims. The SEC inquiry concerns channel distribution arrangements, quarter-end shipments, revenue recognition, and returns/credits during Q1–Q3 FY2024. Both matters require preservation of communications and business records concerning channel sales, and the Kovach matter additionally requires employment, performance, complaint, investigation, and termination evidence.

Trigger assessment. Based on the documents reviewed, August 5 is the prudent conservative trigger date for the Kovach dispute: Kovach’s written letter to the Audit Committee Chair alleged material misstatements and requested an independent investigation. The June verbal complaints, the Audit Committee-directed investigation commencing August 20, and the September 12 termination are additional facts bearing on when claims were reasonably anticipated. At the latest, the termination of an employee who had complained about accounting practices and escalated those complaints to the Audit Committee made litigation reasonably foreseeable. The SEC preservation obligation was explicit upon receipt of its October 28 inquiry letter; the overlapping data should already have been preserved under the earlier events.
