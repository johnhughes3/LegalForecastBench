# I. Bottom Line

We have two overlapping preservation obligations: the Kovach SOX § 806 / Sabine Pilot claims and the SEC Division of Enforcement informal inquiry. As of today, November 8, 2024, no legal hold has been issued to any custodian, and no eDiscovery hold or suspension of automated deletion is in place in any Nexfield system. That is true even though the duty to preserve attached at least eight weeks ago and probably thirteen. Four automated processes that destroy data are still running: the Teams chat 90-day purge, the Teams channel 1-year purge, the Salesforce 18-month inactive-record deletion, and the 90-day rolling backup rotation. A fifth event, the NXF-FS01 decommissioning with deletion of "stale" files, is scheduled for January 31, 2025.

My conclusions:

- **Unified hold.** One consolidated hold covering both matters, issued today. It should use one custodian list and one data-source inventory, each tagged by matter (Kovach, SEC, or both). You approved this approach in your November 5 and 7 emails.

- **Custodians.** We need 18 custodians right away (Tier 1 and Tier 2), plus a Tier 3 group of about 20 more. Four interviewees from Nathan Cross's investigation who had nothing to do with the termination should be on the list: Collings, Muñoz, Patwardhan, and Jessup. Marcus Ainsley must be a custodian, under the special protocol in Section VI.

- **Spoliation exposure.** Data has almost certainly already been lost. Every Teams 1:1 and group chat created before about August 10, 2024 has been permanently purged. That includes the whole period covering Kovach's three verbal complaints (June 14, June 28, July 2), the July 2–15 window between his second complaint and the PIP (which Stadler Raines specifically demanded we preserve), and the Q2 quarter-end. If the hold had issued on August 5, all of those chats would have survived. Other losses are likely or possible: Salesforce records deleted by the nightly batch since August, on-premises backups, and anything a custodian hard-deleted. Section V quantifies this.

- **The loss is still happening.** Q3 2024 quarter-end chats from the last ten business days of September are still on the server. They will start purging around December 16, 2024, and some of them are directly responsive to SEC Request 5. If the Teams hold slips by a few more weeks, we will have made the problem worse ourselves after receiving both the demand letter and the SEC letter. That would be very hard to defend under FRCP 37(e) and in front of the Staff.

- **Immediate asks.** Section VIII lists 12 actions for the next 72 hours. The most urgent is authorizing Priya Dasgupta (M365) and Ridgeway to place tenant-level preservation on Teams and custodian holds in the M365 Compliance Center today.

## B. Has Data Already Been Lost During the Gap? (Your November 5 Request)

Short answer: yes, almost certainly for Teams chats. Probably for some Salesforce records and backups. Possibly for user-deleted email and some Kovach-specific sources. Details follow, ordered from most to least severe.

**1. Teams 1:1 and group chats — confirmed permanent loss.**

- As of today, chats created before about August 10, 2024 are gone. Purged content can't be recovered. Novotny confirms no hold or retention label was ever applied, so nothing was retained in the Substrate layer. Kovach's Teams use during Q1–Q3 2024 was substantial.

- Content lost during the gap, by trigger date. With an Aug 5 trigger, the gap swallowed chats from May 7 to Aug 10. With a Sep 12 trigger, from Jun 14 to Aug 10. Either way that includes Kovach's June 14 / June 28 / July 2 complaint period, any Sokolova / Ellicott / Purdy chats in the July 2–15 window, PIP planning, late-June Q2 quarter-end sales and finance chats, and chats around the Aug 5 letter. With an Oct 3 trigger, chats from Jul 5 to Aug 10 were still lost after an express written demand. That includes the tail of the demanded window (July 5–15) and the PIP rollout.

- Stadler Raines has already said it will seek "the strongest possible inference" for any loss in the July 2–15 window. Under FRCP 37(e)(1), prejudice-curing measures are available on a showing of prejudice. An adverse inference under 37(e)(2) requires intent to deprive. The facts point to negligence, since the purge was a 2023 cost-saving configuration. Even so, the 36-day delay after the demand letter and the August 29 Teams disclosure will be used to argue recklessness. In SEC proceedings the concern is credibility and cooperation credit rather than Rule 37.

- Partial substitutes: (a) M365 unified audit log metadata (who, whom, when) for purged chats. Export it now, because audit log retention is limited. (b) Chats cached on Kovach's iPhone and on custodians' own phones and desktops (the Teams client cache). (c) Emails or files that reference the chats. (d) Screenshots Kovach may have kept. Ridgeway should assess client-side caches on custodians' company laptops before any device refresh.

**2. Salesforce inactive records — probable loss, scope unknown.**

Records deleted in the last 15 days are still in the recycle bin and must be restored today. Most Q1–Q3 2024 records are not yet at risk; the earliest would reach 18 months around mid-2025.
