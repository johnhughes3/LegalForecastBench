# I. Case posture and issues discovery must resolve

Pinnacle alleges that 48 HV-4400 high-pressure valves delivered from April 2022 through November 2023 were defective, asserting contract, express- and implied-warranty, fraud/fraudulent-concealment, and negligent-misrepresentation claims. It seeks \$12.8 million in compensatory damages and \$1.9 million in punitive damages. Hartwell denies liability and asserts a \$612,400 counterclaim for unpaid invoices under PO-2023-011, PO-2023-012, and PO-2023-013. The Court’s December 2, 2024 Initial Case Management Order governs the schedule and expressly calls for discussion of phasing, ESI, preservation, third-party discovery, and proportionality.

# IV. Proposed production protocol and search approach

- Use a rolling, source-by-source production schedule, with an early priority production of the July 2023 communications and attachments, related quality records, the 48-valve traceability / test package, and PO-2023-011 through -013 invoice and delivery records. Agree to reasonable written-discovery response deadlines and phased rolling productions well before the October 31, 2025 cutoff.

- Email and ordinary office documents: propose TIFF (or reasonably usable near-native) with extracted text, standard load files and agreed metadata (custodian, sender/recipient, sent/received/created dates, subject, file name, hash, attachment relationship, and source path where available). Preserve email families and attachments. Produce spreadsheets, structured data, high-resolution images, video, CAD or other function-dependent material in native format with reasonably available metadata. Do not flatten content that needs formulas, filters, layers or context.

- SAP / QualTrack: propose structured production that is searchable and reasonably usable, with a data dictionary, field mapping, date/time conventions, unique row/record IDs, stable serial/lot/PO keys and related-record context. For QualTrack, a processed, fielded load-file or equivalent row-level export is preferable to an unexplained raw CSV. Preserve and make available the original CSV exports and source database; test a sample with Pinnacle and discuss any genuinely necessary additional export before claiming undue burden. The system is used operationally, so do not rely on its limitations alone to label it inaccessible under Rule 26(b)(2)(B).

- Agree on exact deduplication (including attachment families), preservation of custodian/source provenance, date/time normalization, OCR quality control, Bates numbering, exception reporting, and treatment of embedded/linked files. No metadata stripping; no unilateral destruction of originals. Use a Rule of Evidence 502(d) order and a practical privilege-log protocol (categorical logging for agreed categories, with sufficient identifying information), with final logs by November 30, 2025 as ordered.

- Use targeted date ranges: (1) January 1, 2021–March 12, 2022 for MSA negotiations and specifications; (2) January 1, 2022–November 30, 2023 for production, supplier and delivery records; (3) July 1, 2023–present for irregularities, complaints, testing, quality decisions, mitigation and litigation; and (4) the relevant invoice dates–present for the counterclaim. Preserve all relevant material from January 1, 2021 forward and supplement for later-created responsive records.

- Propose iterative search terms rather than a rigid exhaustive list, with hit counts and sampling. Seed terms should include variants of “HV-4400” / “HV 4400”; Pinnacle / Blackridge / Marsh / Reeves / Alcott; Great Lakes / GLF; relevant lot and heat numbers (including GL-2023-0618, GL-2023-0625, GLF-4417 through GLF-4459, as applicable); “porosity,” “void,” “irregularity,” “pitting,” “NDE,” “UT,” “radiograph,” “heat treatment,” “hardness,” “NCR,” and “corrective action.” Refine based on test results and confirm potentially important names/records are captured. Evaluate TAR only after sample volumes and complexity are known; agree to validation before use.

- Use an agreed confidentiality / attorneys’-eyes-only tier for sensitive manufacturing, pricing, customer, and supplier material, subject to a protective order. The CMO requires the parties to submit the proposed protective order and Rule 502(d) order by February 24, 2025.

# VI. Third-party evidence and physical examination

Great Lakes Foundry Partners, LLC (Erie, Pennsylvania) is central to origin, treatment and lot traceability; Hartwell’s own MSA §§ 7.6 and Exhibit E, however, state that use/approval of a sub-tier supplier does not relieve Hartwell of responsibility. Discovery should develop supplier facts without treating the vendor as a substitute for Hartwell’s contract obligations. The CMO requires at least 14 days’ written notice to all parties before any Rule 45 subpoena, including the proposed subpoena, and a meet-and-confer before motion practice.

- Prepare a narrowly tailored Rule 45 subpoena to Great Lakes after notice to Pinnacle: castings supplied for the 48 valves and the identified production/heat lots; order/specification, MTRs, furnace time-temperature records, NDE (RT/UT) raw data and interpretations, foundry process controls, NCR/CAPA, correspondence with Hartwell, audits/qualification, and complaints or returns involving the same casting process during the relevant period. Seek a knowledgeable witness only if records leave material questions, to avoid burden and conserve deposition slots.

- Before destructive or additional nondestructive examination, exchange proposed protocols, serial/heat identification, methods/acceptance criteria, calibration and qualifications, notice and observation rights, chain of custody, data/imagery, and specimen disposition. The 11 sectioned valves and the remaining 37 valves should be inventoried; the 19 UT-positive valves are not confirmed defective by destructive analysis. Consider agreed independent phased-array UT or radiography and a limited, representative destructive sample if technically justified.

- Seek Whitmore’s complete case file for any testifying expert: instructions, data considered, raw measurements, photos/micrographs, calibration, chain-of-custody, test procedures, drafts/communications to the extent discoverable under Rule 26(b)(4), and the data supporting the 19 indications. The report is marked prepared at counsel’s direction, but is affirmatively relied on in the Complaint; assess any privilege/work-product position carefully and separately from discoverable expert facts and test data.

- Consider, before the April 30, 2025 pleading-amendment/joinder deadline, whether facts support any claim against Great Lakes or another party. Evaluate contractual supplier remedies and contribution/indemnity; MSA § 7.6 and Exhibit E expressly allocate sub-tier quality responsibility to Hartwell.

# VII. Phasing, depositions, experts, and case schedule

Recommended approach: agree to a modest first-stage sequence, not a blanket stay. Stage 1 should promptly resolve core MSA/specification issues, Hartwell’s lot-level quality / warning evidence, test reliability and preservation, invoice liability, and a focused damages record. After an initial 60–90 days of targeted document exchange, counsel can assess whether a partial motion on contract interpretation / liability cap, warranty remedy, fraud independence, or counterclaim can narrow later discovery. Do not defer discovery of the July 2023 warning chain, raw quality records, or evidence necessary to evaluate the alleged fraud while litigating a cap issue: those facts overlap and a stay could prejudice either side. The Court may entertain discrete early motions but may stay issues only by order.

Proposed deposition planning (subject to conference and the 10-fact-deposition cap): prioritize a Hartwell Rule 30(b)(6) witness on manufacturing, supplier oversight, MSA testing requirements, QualTrack/SAP and preservation; Kowalski; Fenton; Ostrowski; Brecker; a Pinnacle Rule 30(b)(6) witness on testing, shutdown, damages and mitigation; Reeves and/or Alcott; Marsh for procurement/invoices and communications; and a Great Lakes witness only if necessary. Avoid duplicative corporate/individual examinations; define focused 30(b)(6) topics and preserve slots for unexpected needs. Expert depositions are separately governed by the CMO’s expert schedule and should not be presumed to count as fact depositions.

Technical experts likely include metallurgical/foundry quality and NDE, valve manufacturing / standards, installation and operations, and damages / lost profits. Hartwell should identify and retain its experts early enough to meet the affirmative report deadline. Expert reports must be served by August 1, 2025; rebuttal reports by September 15, 2025; all expert depositions by October 31, 2025. Any supplemental testing or inspection must be completed early enough for the experts to analyze it and meet those dates.
