# I. Purpose and Bottom Line

This memorandum proposes Hartwell’s discovery plan and our negotiating positions for the Rule 26(f) conference. The Case Management Order (“CMO”) requires the conference by **February 10, 2025**. The joint discovery plan, Rule 26(a)(1) disclosures, stipulated protective order, and Rule 502(d) order are all due **February 24, 2025**. We reviewed the Complaint, Answer and Counterclaim, CMO, Master Supply Agreement (“MSA”), pre-suit correspondence, Pinnacle’s preservation notice, the Whitmore report, Hartwell’s October 30, 2024 hold memo, the vendor’s November 15, 2024 e-discovery assessment, and the July 2023 Ostrowski/Kowalski/Fenton email chain.

**Bottom line.** Our review turned up facts that should change our positions for the conference:

1.  **The July 2023 internal emails give Pinnacle the fraud facts it now pleads “on information and belief.”** Ray Ostrowski warned Diane Kowalski and James Fenton in writing four times (July 8, 15, and 22, 2023). He reported porosity in about 12 of 26 castings and a dime-sized void. He recommended supplemental UT, which Hartwell had the equipment to perform. Ms. Kowalski told him to keep going. On July 23, 2023, Mr. Fenton forwarded the chain to Tom Brecker with the note “keep an eye on this with the customer.” These emails are responsive to almost any request Pinnacle will serve, and we will have to produce them. They also make a Rule 9(b) motion or a “fraud-first” phasing proposal unwise. Either would focus early discovery and the Court’s attention on our worst documents (Part IV).
2.  **Hartwell’s preservation position is weaker than the vendor memo suggests. Under the CMO, we must disclose it in writing by the conference.** The duty to preserve arguably began with Pinnacle’s April 5, 2024 demand, which expressly asked Hartwell to preserve text messages. Hartwell promised on April 22, 2024 to take “appropriate steps.” The hold did not issue until October 30, 2024. As of November 15, 2024, the hold also left out Ostrowski, text messages on personal devices, the hardcopy QA binders, and QualTrack. No platform-level Microsoft 365 hold had been applied. The Brecker phone had not been imaged. Before February 10, we need confirmation that every remediation step has been completed (Part III).
3.  **The MSA says something different from what both sides pleaded.** Both sides call Section 12 a “consequential damages cap” of \$5,811,300. It is two provisions:
    - **§ 12.1** excludes consequential damages, lost revenue, loss of use, cost of substitute goods, and punitive damages altogether. The exception is gross negligence or willful misconduct.
    - **§ 12.2** caps each party’s **aggregate** liability on all theories, expressly including “misrepresentation,” at 150% of the Total Contract Price.

- Other provisions cut against defenses Hartwell has pleaded:
  - § 9.2 defines subsurface porosity as a latent defect. That defeats our acceptance/waiver defense.
  - § 7.6 and Exhibit E.4 make Hartwell fully responsible to Pinnacle for foundry defects.
  - § 8.4 puts the burden of proving the torque exclusion on Hartwell.
  - Exhibit A specifies **ASTM A217 WC6/WC9 alloy steel** for HV-4400 bodies. Whitmore and Ms. Kowalski both describe the castings as **ASTM A216 WCB carbon steel**.
  - Exhibit D.3 required **100% volumetric NDE and hardness testing of every HV-4400 casting** before shipment.

  Whether those tests were performed, and what the purchase orders specified for material, may be the most important liability facts in the case. We need to know the answers before we negotiate scope (Parts II and VI).

4.  **Recommended overall posture:**
    - Agree to a single-track discovery schedule for all claims and the counterclaim, with a narrow agreed exception for early briefing of pure contract-interpretation questions.
    - Negotiate a detailed ESI protocol that deals with QualTrack openly. Do not argue that QualTrack is “not reasonably accessible.”
    - Press hard on damages, mitigation, and physical-evidence discovery from Pinnacle. Those are Hartwell’s strongest ground.
    - Preserve the right to bring a third-party claim against Great Lakes Foundry Partners before the **April 30, 2025** joinder deadline.

# III. Preservation: Status, Required Disclosures, and Immediate Actions

## A. CMO Requirement

CMO § IV requires each party to confirm to opposing counsel **in writing, no later than the Rule 26(f) conference**:

- the date the hold was implemented;

Known losses must also be disclosed to the Court “promptly.” CMO § V.B separately requires a written **ESI source list** by the conference.

## B. Exposure Analysis

- **When the duty arose.** Pinnacle will argue the duty arose on April 5, 2024, with the demand letter. It threatened litigation and specifically demanded preservation of “text messages.” Hartwell’s April 22 response committed to “take appropriate steps to preserve.” Hartwell’s hold issued October 30, 2024. That is roughly **seven months** after the demand, not the 37 days the vendor measured from the Complaint. Our preservation letter should state accurately when the hold issued. It should not concede when the duty arose.
- **Email.** Microsoft 365 keeps deleted items for 365 days, with no auto-purge of inbox or sent items. Items deleted before about late October 2023 may be permanently gone. Ask IT whether any Tier 1 custodian’s deleted items were purged, and whether audit logs exist.
- **Teams and SharePoint.** Retention settings have not been verified. The “Pinnacle Project” Teams channel (opened April 2022) is high-value. Get the Purview retention policy report now.
- **Brecker phone.** His texts with Daniel Marsh were frequent and covered delivery, pricing, and at least some early-2024 quality issues. These texts are the likely Rule 37(e) battleground. Whether messages were deleted after April 5, 2024, and whether an iCloud backup exists, must be determined forensically **before** we sign the CMO § IV letter.
- **Ostrowski.** He was not on the hold as of November 15, 2024. He also says he took **cell phone photos** of the affected castings (July 15, 2023 email). The vendor memo does not address his phone. His device and any photos must be preserved.
- **Physical specimens at Hartwell.** Determine whether any Great Lakes castings from the 2023 lots, machining scrap, retained samples, or rejected parts remain at Toledo or Findlay. CMO § IV expressly covers “physical specimens.”
- **QA binders.** Hartwell’s five-year binder retention policy conflicts with **MSA § 7.4 and Exhibit D.1**, which require Hartwell to keep QA/QC records for **seven** years. That is a contract obligation independent of the litigation, and Pinnacle can rely on it.
- **Findlay.** MSA § 5.1 designates Findlay as a possible FOB shipping/distribution point, and the Complaint alleges manufacturing there. Findlay was not assessed.

# IV. Phased Discovery and Early Motions

CMO §§ II.J and VII invite proposals for phased discovery. The Answer (¶54) previews early summary judgment on the fraud claim and the cap. We recommend **against** asking for fraud-first or liability-first phasing, for four reasons:

1.  **Rule 9(b) is no longer a good vehicle.** The Complaint names Kowalski, Fenton, and Brecker and gives a July–November 2023 window. The Ostrowski chain matches those allegations closely. A motion would probably be denied or produce an amended complaint, and it would put the emails in front of the Court early.
2.  **Whether the fraud exception to the cap applies depends on facts.** The § 12.1 carve-out for gross negligence and willful misconduct, and Ohio’s fraud exception to limitation clauses, both turn on what Hartwell knew and did in July–November 2023. The Court will not stay fraud discovery to decide the cap in the abstract.
3.  **Phasing damages second would hurt us.** Our best issues are mitigation, the downtime calculation, and Whitmore’s own fitness-for-service and return-to-service recommendations. We want that discovery to run early, alongside liability discovery.
4.  **The schedule is tight.** The May 4, 2026 trial date and August 1, 2025 expert deadline leave little room for phases.

**Proposed alternative (to raise at the conference):**

- A single fact-discovery track closing October 31, 2025, covering all claims and the counterclaim.
- An agreed **early partial summary judgment window** (e.g., opening briefs by July 15, 2025) limited to pure contract-interpretation questions that do not depend on the fraud facts:
  - 1.  § 8.6 disclaimer of the implied warranty of merchantability (Count III);

  - 2.  whether § 12.2’s aggregate cap reaches negligent misrepresentation, and the economic loss doctrine (Count V);

  - 3.  § 12.1’s exclusion of lost revenue and cost of substitute goods absent willful misconduct or gross negligence;

  - 4.  counterclaim liability under §§ 4.3–4.4 (no set-off; waiver of undisputed invoices).
- Discovery would not be stayed during the briefing.
- The **counterclaim** is a good early motion candidate: it is document-driven, and Pinnacle has effectively admitted withholding payment (Compl. ¶60). First confirm which HV-4400 serials were on the unpaid POs, because Pinnacle will argue it rejected or revoked acceptance of those valves.

**Expected Pinnacle position:** oppose phasing and seek immediate, broad fraud discovery. Our proposal gives the Court a reasonable, efficient middle path and shows cooperation.

## B. Key Protocol Terms to Propose

- **Production format.**
  - Email and e-documents: TIFF with extracted text and standard metadata load files.
  - Native production for spreadsheets, CAD/drawings, audio/video, and structured data.
  - QualTrack and SAP: delimited fielded data with a data dictionary. QualTrack free-text notes kept in their own field. Each record gets a unique control number.
  - Photographs: native with EXIF metadata.
  - Texts: RSMF or conversation-threaded PDF, with sender, recipient, and timestamp fields.
- **Metadata fields.** A standard set (custodian, all-custodians, from/to/cc/bcc, dates sent/received/created/modified, file name, file path, MD5 hash, BegBates/EndBates, attachment range, conversation index, redaction flag, confidentiality designation).
- **De-duplication.** Global MD5/SHA-1 de-duplication with an “all custodians” field. Email threading allowed only if lesser-included emails are logged or produced on request.

The vendor estimates a 30–40% cut in review cost, bringing review from \$85–120K to \$50–75K. If we use TAR, disclose it with validation by elusion sampling (recall target around 75%). Keep seed-set details proportionate; the CMO asks for transparency. Structured data is produced by field filters (model HV-4400; customer Pinnacle; Great Lakes lots), not keywords.
