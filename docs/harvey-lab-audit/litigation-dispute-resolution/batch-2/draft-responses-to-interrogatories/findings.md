## Confirmed defects

- **C-006 / C-008 (high, penalizes truthful responsive facts):** No. 3(c)-(d) expressly asks whether notice/cure were provided; No. 4(d)-(e) asks actions on the 2020 shortfall and why performance continued for 2.5 years; No. 19 explicitly asks whether there was any pre-termination written breach notice and, if none, why. Client-interview-memo paragraphs 29–31/121/135 acknowledges no 2020/2021 written notice and continued performance, and expressly says the fact cannot be hidden and responses should acknowledge it honestly. Spreadsheet Annual Summary row 7 likewise records no notice. The rubric fails candid disclosure of those facts because they could support waiver. Facts can be admitted while disputing waiver, cure timing, or the legal validity of termination. Rule 33(b)(3) requires full answers to the extent not objected to; Rule 37(a)(4) treats evasion as failure: https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/frcp/rule_33 ; https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/frcp/rule_37 . Replace the negative-admission tests with truthful factual answers preserving nonfrivolous legal positions.
- **C-011 (moderate, evasive passing alternative):** Correctly rejects Hauck's late-2022 memory contradicted by the May 17 email, but expressly permits vague “communications in 2022” wording. No. 12 asks the actual first-awareness date and circumstances. A qualified answer such as “no later than May 17, 2022, subject to ongoing investigation” is defensible; merely obscuring the known date is not a complete response. Reward evidence-based qualification, not strategic vagueness.
- **C-013 (moderate, optional procedure made mandatory):** Requires at least one Rule 33(d) invocation and fails a complete direct answer to all transactional questions. Rule 33(d) is optional and has an equal-burden condition; a solver may reasonably summarize the supplied annual purchase table directly. Guidelines identify it as an available option. Accept complete direct answers.
- **C-014 (moderate, insufficient passing threshold):** Allows any single record type/custodian/date range to establish adequate Rule 33(d) identification. Rule 33(d)(1) requires enough detail to locate records as readily as the responding party, plus equal burden and access. “Purchase histories” alone can pass literally while being legally inadequate. Grade actual identifiability and conditions.
- **C-015 (moderate, mandated discretionary objection):** Requires a premature-contention objection. Rule 33(a)(2) permits contentions and authorizes court-ordered deferral; an open discovery period alone does not make a factual contention unanswerable. Existing pleaded defenses and source facts allow present answers with supplementation. Accept such answers or a supported request for deferral. This does not establish that any particular deferral motion would fail.
