*Adapted from the [beta results report](/findings/beta-results/).*

GPT-6 Astra did not beat its smaller siblings. GPT-5.6 Sol had better observed scores on all three metrics. GPT-5.6 Luna had better micro Brier and unit accuracy, while Astra had better equal-case Brier. With 91 cases, the simplest explanation is sampling noise: only Muse Spark 1.3 was statistically separable from the rest.

I audited the cases with the largest negative relative impact on Astra's score. In addition to some overconfident predictions, Astra sometimes appeared to underweight the possibility that the motion would become moot because the plaintiff would amend the complaint.

## Why amendment matters to the label

The benchmark asks about the motion's **first written disposition**, not the ultimate merits outcome. That creates two situations that look alike but score differently:

| What happens | Counts as |
| --- | --- |
| The judge dismisses the claim with leave to amend | Dismissal |
| The plaintiff amends first, and the judge denies or terminates the old motion as moot | Not dismissal |

After an amendment, the defendant can often file a renewed motion, and the amended complaint may ultimately be dismissed. A model reasoning about the merits could therefore forecast dismissal and be "right" about the case while being wrong about the benchmark's target.

In some cases, Astra, Sol, and Luna explicitly considered that an amendment could moot the motion. In others, Astra seemed to ignore the possibility. The prompt did ask for the first written disposition, but spelling out the distinction above more clearly might yield more consistent forecasts in future versions.
