Brier score is the squared distance between a forecast and the outcome. A 97% forecast that turns out wrong costs 0.94, while a 70% forecast that turns out wrong costs 0.49. A model that is sharp but slightly overconfident can therefore trail a more cautious model even when it gets more calls right.

Claude Fable 5.1 and Claude Opus 5.5 were never wrong among their high-confidence predictions (81 and 65), and Grok 4.6 and Kimi K3 missed only one each. GPT-6 Sol was confident more often (169 units) and missed 8. By contrast, GPT-5.6 Luna missed 20, GPT-6 Astra missed 21, and GPT-5.6 Sol missed 26. Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview made the most high-confidence predictions of any model (320 of 387 units) and missed 57.

The GPT-5.6 models and GPT-6 Astra had thoughtful and perceptive analyses of the underlying cases, but several overly confident predictions cost a lot of points: their mean stated confidence within these subsets was roughly 95–97%, while accuracy was roughly 89–90%.

## Reading this carefully

- "90% confidence" means at least 90% probability of dismissal or at most 10%.
- These are scored units, not independent cases. Several misses can come from one case, so a handful of cases can move the ordering materially.
- This compares mean stated confidence with realized accuracy inside one slice. It is not a full calibration curve.
