*Adapted from the [first-release report](/findings/first-release/).*

On Vals AI's September 10 leaderboard for Harvey's Legal Agent Benchmark (LAB), Muse Spark 1.2 ranked first and Muse Spark 1.3, the version tested here, ranked third. On LegalForecastBench, Muse Spark 1.3 had the lowest accuracy of the seventeen configurations (72.1%) and the second-worst Brier scores. It was the only configuration that did not beat simply predicting dismissal every time (73.6%).

The two benchmarks measure different things, and their scores are not on a common scale. LAB scores long-form legal work product against expert-written rubric criteria. LegalForecastBench scores calibrated probabilities against what judges actually decided. A model can reasonably do well on one and poorly on the other.

Still, the gap suggests a hypothesis worth testing. LAB rewards models that give specific kinds of answers, which might make it more vulnerable to benchmark overfitting: models could improve at satisfying rubric requirements without comparable gains in generalizable abilities. LAB's headline metric, the all-pass rate, rewards recall against its rubrics, essentially requiring models to flag the large number of issues LAB tests. That may not be the best optimization target. Practicing lawyers often prefer precision, focusing on a small number of dispositive points rather than a large number of technically accurate but minor issues.

## What this does not show

- It does not establish contamination or deliberate optimization for LAB. Harvey describes a held-out set, and its rubric criteria cover facts, conclusions, citations, structure, and analytical moves, not merely issue recall.
- It rests on one retained forecast per unit across 91 cases, and on the contributor-tier endpoint for Muse Spark 1.3. Standard-tier execution has not been measured.
- Vals AI's leaderboard has been updated since September 10. The ranking cited here is the one checked on that date.
